# Proposal Resources — Real NGO & CSR Proposal Documents

A collection of **real, publicly available proposal/project documents** — actual proposals, real project descriptions from Annual Reports, or a funder's own official required format, rather than blank generic templates. Now **9 documents** across a wide range of funder types. Each entry gives: **which organisation wrote it, exactly who/what they wrote to, a profile of that funder (what they actually fund, where, and how much), the sector, and a detailed description with source.**

### 1. AchieversGhana_Education_Proposal_GlobalGiving.pdf
- **Source URL:** https://www.globalgiving.org/pfil/28934/projdoc.pdf
- **NGO/Organisation whose proposal this is:** Achievers Ghana Education (Accra, Ghana)
- **Written to:** GlobalGiving's donor/crowdfunding community (individual and corporate donors browsing the platform)
- **Funder Profile — GlobalGiving:**
  - A US-registered **501(c)(3) nonprofit**, founded **2002** by former World Bank executives **Mari Kuraishi and Dennis Whittle**
  - **Not a funder itself in the traditional sense** — it is a **crowdfunding intermediary**: money comes from **1.9 million individual donors** who have collectively given **over $1 billion** to more than **40,000 projects in 175+ countries** since launch
  - Funds virtually **any development sector** as long as the implementing nonprofit passes GlobalGiving's own **vetting process** (including site visits for many organisations)
  - Takes a **5–13% "nonprofit support fee"** plus a 3% payment-processing fee on every donation, varying by how much an org has already raised and where it's registered
  - Also runs flexible, unrestricted funds (Flex Fund, Hope in Crisis Fund) for orgs already vetted into its network
- **Sector:** Girls' education / anti-child-marriage
- **Short description:** A proposal seeking sponsorship for girls' education in Accra's slum communities. Opens with a strong narrative (a named girl's personal story of escaping forced child marriage to stay in school), backed by a real problem statement with legal/statistical context, followed by a detailed line-item budget (school fees, uniforms, health insurance, learning materials).
- **Additional details:**
  - **Origin story:** the NGO itself was co-founded in **May 2011** by Amina (the girl in the founding story, then still a student) together with friends and a teacher, **Amadu Mohammed** — it started life simply as a girls' reading club before expanding into a full organisation.
  - **Exact locations:** Nima and Maamobi, two specific slum communities in Accra, Ghana.
  - **5 named project activities:** (1) Education/scholarships — school fees, books, uniforms, with selection criteria and regular grade/attendance tracking; (2) Technology — an "Achievers Coding and ICT" programme teaching computer science to girls; (3) Leadership & Mentorship — sessions with female professional role models; (4) Career Skills Development — vocational training in batik, tie-and-dye, liquid soap making, bead making, and painting; (5) Community Advocacy against child marriage.
  - **Cited statistic:** "more than a third — 34% — of young women in developing countries are jobless" (World Bank, 2014), used to justify the career-skills component.
  - **5 explicitly listed organisational challenges:** social bias against girls' education, parents lacking confidence/resources to contribute, lack of scholastic materials, poor access to nutrition/sanitary products, and the NGO's own limited resources/finances.

### 2. UNICEF_Somalia_FoodSecurity_Proposal.pdf
- **Source URL:** https://mptf.undp.org/sites/default/files/documents/25000/699_unicef_project_proposal.pdf
- **Source platform:** UNDP Multi-Partner Trust Fund (MPTF) Office
- **NGO/Organisation whose proposal this is:** UNICEF (United Nations Children's Fund) — note this proposal was written by a UN agency, not an NGO, but follows the same pooled-fund application format NGOs must also use
- **Written to:** The **Somalia Humanitarian Fund (SHF)**, at the time called the Common Humanitarian Fund (CHF) Somalia
- **Funder Profile — Somalia Humanitarian Fund / Country-Based Pooled Funds (CBPFs):**
  - A **multi-donor, country-based pooled fund**, established **2010** for Somalia specifically, **managed by OCHA** under the oversight of the UN's **Humanitarian Coordinator** for Somalia
  - Governments and private donors contribute **unearmarked** money into one common pot, allocated to the highest-priority projects in that country's **Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP)**
  - Funds mainly **international and national NGOs, plus some UN agencies** — NGOs are the primary intended recipients even though this specific sample happens to be from UNICEF
  - Part of a much larger global system: OCHA's **Country-Based Pooled Funds worldwide allocated US$1.01 billion across 20 different crises in 2021 alone**
  - Explicitly designed to push more money to **local/national responders** (aligned with the "Grand Bargain" commitment to channel 25% of humanitarian funding as directly as possible to local actors)
- **Sector:** Food security / humanitarian cash transfers
- **Short description:** A proposal for unconditional cash transfers to ~8,682 food-insecure people in Bardheere, Somalia, with a full Logical Framework, month-by-month work plan, and detailed M&E plan.
- **Additional details:**
  - **CHF Code:** CHF-DDA-3485-699 (Somalia Common Humanitarian Fund tracking code); part of the broader Humanitarian Response Plan (**HRP Code SOM-15/A/71824**, total HRP budget of **$17,679,870**), and ranked **"A – HIGH"** priority within that plan.
  - **Exact beneficiary breakdown:** 1,447 men + 1,447 women + 2,894 boys + 2,894 girls = **8,682 total**, further broken down into 6,078 Internally Displaced People, 5,788 children under 18, and 2,604 people in host communities.
  - **Named funder-facing contact:** Sarah Ng'inja, Donor Relations Specialist, UNICEF (with direct phone number and email listed in the proposal).
  - **Transfer mechanism specifics:** cash set at **60% of the local Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB)**, currently equivalent to **US$70/month**, delivered via Mobile Money Transfer or hawala agents depending on phone/network access.
  - **Delivery scale context:** UNICEF states it already runs ongoing cash support for **10,000+ vulnerable households** across four regions (Gedo, Galgaduud, Hiraan, Lower Shabelle), with this specific proposal funding **two additional months** of support in Baardheere for households already registered.
  - **Explicit numeric indicator targets:** e.g., "Percent of targeted households with acceptable Food Consumption Score: **50%**" and "Percent decrease in households using 3+ harmful coping strategies: **20%**" — precise, auditable KPI targets rather than vague goals.

### 3. FAO_Jordan_Agriculture_Proposal.pdf
- **Source URL:** https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/user_upload/faoweb/plant-treaty/Project-Proposals/W3B-PR-02-Jordan-Technical_Proposal.pdf
- **NGO/Organisation whose proposal this is:** Led by **ICARDA** (International Center for Agricultural Research in the Dry Areas, a CGIAR Center), in a 4-country consortium with Jordan's NCARE, Ethiopia's EIAR, and national Agricultural Research Centers in Egypt and Sudan
- **Written to:** The **Benefit-sharing Fund (BSF)** of the **International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture**, administered by the **FAO**
- **Funder Profile — Benefit-sharing Fund:**
  - Established **2009**, under a Treaty adopted in 2001 and in force since 2004
  - Has received **almost USD 30 million in total contributions since its 2009 launch**; has supported projects in **67 developing countries to date**
  - Funds distributed through **periodic, competitive "Calls for Proposals"** (this proposal was submitted to the **Third Call**, deadline 5 December 2014)
  - Specifically funds work to **conserve and sustainably use crop genetic resources**, with a strong emphasis on **climate change adaptation** for farming communities — a narrow, thematically-specific funder rather than a general-purpose one
  - The Treaty's "Multilateral System" (which the BSF is funded partly through) has facilitated the certified transfer of **over 6.6 million plant genetic samples** globally since 2006
- **Sector:** Agriculture / food security (wheat cultivation)
- **Short description:** A technical proposal requesting $500,000 (matched by $500,000 co-funding) to identify climate-resilient wheat genetic material across 4 countries.
- **Additional details:**
  - **Full project title:** "An Integrated Approach to Identify and Characterize Climate Resilient Wheat for the West Asia and North Africa Region."
  - **Lead applicant:** ICARDA (International Center for Agricultural Research in the Dry Areas), a **CGIAR Center**; named project contact is **Dr. Ayed Al-Abdallat, Senior Biotechnologist**.
  - **Actually 4 countries involved, not 3:** Egypt, Ethiopia, Jordan, and Sudan — via partner institutions EIAR (Ethiopia), NCARE (Jordan), and two separate national Agricultural Research Centers/Corporations (Egypt and Sudan, both abbreviated "ARC").
  - **Project duration:** 36 months. **Geographic extension:** 296,540 km².
  - **Funding call context:** submitted under the **"Third Call for Proposals"** of the **Benefit-sharing Fund** of the **International Plant Treaty** (deadline 5 December 2014); this call had 4 possible project "Windows" (single- or multi-country, Immediate Action vs. Co-development/Technology Transfer).
  - **Strict formatting rules disclosed in the guidelines:** max **15 pages of text** (excluding appendices/cover sheet), Times New Roman 12, submitted in both PDF and Word, physically signed by the project coordinator; proposals missing even one required appendix are automatically disqualified.
  - **Scientific rationale given:** wheat yields are projected to drop **~5% for every 0.5°C rise in temperature**; wheat landraces resilient to drought/heat are disappearing and less than **8% of existing genebank wheat material is currently utilised** in breeding — the project aims to characterise this material molecularly to feed future climate-resilient breeding programmes.
  - **Cited country statistic:** Ethiopia's per-capita income is **$470**, with **~40% of its population below the poverty line** (World Bank 2014) — used to justify the regional food-security stakes.

### 4. AdaptationFund_Congo_ClimateAdaptation_Proposal.pdf
- **Source URL:** https://www.adaptation-fund.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/12093CongoClean.pdf
- **NGO/Organisation whose proposal this is:** Republic of Congo's **Ministry of Tourism and Environment** (Executing Entity), with the **World Food Programme (WFP)** as the accredited Multilateral Implementing Entity, and NGO partners (e.g., Ordre de Malte) involved in delivery
- **Written to:** The **Adaptation Fund**
- **Funder Profile — Adaptation Fund:**
  - Established in **2001 under the Kyoto Protocol** (governed under the UNFCCC), to finance **concrete, ground-level climate adaptation projects** in the most climate-vulnerable developing countries
  - Financed by a **2% share of proceeds from Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) credits**, plus **voluntary contributions from governments**; total contributions since inception now around **USD 2.14 billion** (as of Dec 2024)
  - **Funding ceilings have risen dramatically over time** — at the time this Congo proposal was submitted (~2019), the **per-country cap was US$10 million**, which is exactly why the proposal requested **$9,999,909** — right at the ceiling. The Board has since **doubled the cap twice**: to $20 million, then to **$40 million per country** (single-country project caps now $25M, regional caps $30M)
  - Funds are channeled only through **accredited "Implementing Entities"** — Multilateral (like WFP, UNDP), or National/Regional entities under a "Direct Access" model
- **Sector:** Climate change adaptation / environment
- **Short description:** A ~$10 million, 5-year proposal titled "Building adaptive capacity to climate change in vulnerable communities living in the Congo River Basin" — the largest document in this set (166 pages).
- **Additional details:**
  - **Amount requested: exactly USD 9,999,909**, over 5 years.
  - **3-tier structure:** Fund → UN Implementing Entity (WFP) → national Executing Entity (Congo's Ministry of Tourism and Environment) — the standard structure for large international climate finance.
  - **Extensive country-context data used to justify the proposal**, e.g.: national poverty rate fell from 50.7% (2005) to 35% (2014) but rebounded to an estimated **54%** amid a later economic crisis; life expectancy is **51.9 years**; **1.4 million people** are malnourished and **31% of children are stunted**; the country was hosting **54,000 refugees** (as of end-2016); roughly **8% of the population (~400,000 people) is indigenous**, concentrated in the Likouala and Sangha regions and disproportionately exposed to food insecurity.
  - **Explicit gender-inequality data cited:** Gender Inequality Index of 0.578 (ranked 143rd of 160 countries); only 14% of parliamentary seats held by women; maternal mortality of 442 deaths per 100,000 live births.
  - **Environmental context used as justification:** Congo hosts part of the **Cuvette Centrale**, the world's largest tropical peatland (discovered 2017), estimated to store **30 billion tons of carbon** — used to argue for the ecological stakes of the proposed adaptation work.

- **Written to:** **The World Bank**, specifically to support **India's National AIDS Control Project II (NACP-II)**
- **Funder Profile — The World Bank:**
  - The world's largest **multilateral development bank**, owned/governed by its member-country governments
  - Primarily lends/grants money to **national governments** for large development programmes rather than funding individual NGOs directly — here, the money for NACP-II flowed to the **Government of India**, and this document is an NGO-executed **research/technical-assistance sub-component** nested inside that larger government-to-government loan/grant
  - Operates across virtually every development sector globally (health, infrastructure, education, governance, agriculture, etc.)
  - This particular record is now a **declassified World Bank archival document**, digitised and publicly disclosed on 10 March 2021
- **Sector:** Public health (HIV/AIDS) / India
- **Short description:** A research/assessment proposal (not service delivery) — proposing a desk review plus ~100 field case studies (Mumbai, Chennai, Manipur) to evaluate NGO effectiveness in reaching high-risk groups.
- **Additional details:**
  - **This is a declassified World Bank archival record**, not a live document — catalogued as **Folder ID G217927**, **Project ID P045051**, spanning dates **14 Sept 1999 – 31 March 2006**, part of the "Records of the South Asia Regional Vice Presidency," digitised and publicly disclosed on **10 March 2021**.
  - **Submitting unit:** the Social Services Area of the Administrative Staff College of India (ASCI), based in Bella Vista, Hyderabad – 500082.

### 6. BharatPetroleum_CSR_ProposalFormat_India.pdf
- **Source URL:** https://www.bharatpetroleum.in/images/files/csr-proposal-formats(2).pdf
- **NGO/Organisation whose proposal this is:** N/A — this is **BPCL's own official required format**, not a submitted proposal
- **Written to:** Any NGO applying for funding **from BPCL**
- **Funder Profile — Bharat Petroleum Corporation Limited (BPCL):**
  - A **Government of India-owned "Maharatna" public sector oil & gas company**
  - Like all large enough Indian companies, **legally mandated under Section 135, Companies Act 2013** to spend at least **2% of average net profit of the preceding 3 financial years** on CSR
  - BPCL's own stated CSR thrust areas: **Education, Environmental Sustainability, Health/Hygiene, Skill Development, Community Development**
  - Funds **only Indian-registered NGOs**, with unusually heavy compliance requirements (mandatory dedicated bank account per project; document checklist covering Trust Deed, CSR-1, 12A, 80G, PAN, 3 years audited financials, FCRA)
  - Distinctive payment structure: **milestone-based tranches**, with **10% of the total budget withheld** until final completion report + audited fund-utilisation certificate are submitted
- **Sector:** Cross-sector
- **Short description:** A bilingual (English + Hindi), 16-page official proposal format from BPCL's website, with strict compliance and documentation requirements.
- **Additional details:**
  - **Full required section list (numbered exactly as in the format):** 1.0 About the Organisation, 2.0 Background (incl. 2.1 Thematic Area/Schedule VII mapping), 3.0 Proposal (incl. 3.1 Methodology, 3.2 Target Group, 3.3 Project Team, 3.4 Monitoring Mechanism), 4.0 Outcomes (incl. 4.1 Expected Results, 4.2 Execution Plan/Gantt chart, 4.3 Impact Assessment), 5.0 Financial Implications (incl. 5.1 detailed line-item budget, 5.2 Tentative Payment Schedule), 6.0 Sustainability Plan, 7.0 Conclusion, 8.0 Enclosures (Forms A–F).
  - **Budget table format is highly specific:** requires per-unit cost × number of units × duration, split into three numbered sub-totals (I, II, III), plus a separate Administrative Cost line, a Grand Total, and — notably — a mandatory **"Cost Per Beneficiary"** figure.
  - **Payment is milestone-linked, not upfront:** payments are split into tranches (1st/2nd/3rd/final) tied to specific deliverables, and **10% of the total budget is explicitly withheld** until the NGO submits its project completion report *and* an audited fund-utilisation certificate.
  - **6 separate annexed Forms (A–F)** must be attached, including a specific **Form B required only for infrastructure-type projects** — showing the format is adapted depending on project type, not one-size-fits-all.
  - **Preference criteria explicitly stated:** BPCL says it will favour proposals demonstrating "capability in gathering real time data, technological intervention, project implementation support and impact assessment" — i.e., tech-enabled M&E capacity is a stated competitive advantage, not just a nice-to-have.
  - **A dedicated financial-variance disclosure table** requires applicants to explain, in a specific "Major Reasons for variation" column, why their Assets/Liabilities changed year-on-year across the last 3 financial years — a due-diligence mechanic not seen in any of the other 5 documents in this set.

**Why so few hits:** actual submitted funding proposals are typically **confidential documents exchanged directly between an NGO and a specific funder** — they are not usually published publicly (unlike Annual Reports, which many NGOs do publish for transparency/compliance reasons). The two genuine hits found here (ASC, PRDA) both came from **Annual Reports**, not from a leaked or published "proposal" per se — this is the realistic ceiling of what's discoverable via public search for small/mid-size grassroots NGOs, as opposed to the larger UN/multilateral/foundation-grantee proposals in Documents #1–8, which get published because of institutional transparency/disclosure policies (World Bank Archives, FAO's own site, Adaptation Fund's own site, etc.).
