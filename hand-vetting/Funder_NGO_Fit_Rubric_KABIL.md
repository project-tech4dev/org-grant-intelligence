# Funder–NGO Fit Rubric — Research-Backed, Applied to KABIL

**Purpose:** A research-grounded scoring rubric to assess how well each of KABIL's 4 current funders (Axis Bank Foundation, Azim Premji Foundation, SBI Foundation, Tata Trusts) actually fits KABIL. Rather than picking criteria arbitrarily, this rubric is built on real-world grant-fit and donor-prospecting frameworks used by nonprofits, grant consultants, and CSR-compliance practitioners in India. This is a testing/experimental rubric, not a final one.

---

## Part 1: Research — How Do Real Frameworks Score Funder Fit?

I reviewed several real-world grant-fit and donor-prospecting frameworks. Key findings:

### 1.1 FundRobin's "Grant Fit Score Framework" (nonprofit grant-seeking tool)
- Uses a **1-5 scale across 7 criteria**, summed to a score out of 50 (>35/50 = "strong fit, worth pursuing"): **Mission Alignment, Geographic Focus, Funding Area Match, Grant Size Appropriateness, Eligibility, Relationship Potential, Reporting Requirements**.
- Their more advanced AI-driven version collapses this into **3 core pillars**: (1) **Hard Eligibility** (legal status, geography, income thresholds — a pass/fail gate), (2) **Thematic Alignment** (semantic matching of project goals to funder priorities), (3) **Funder Capacity** (the funder's historical/financial ability to fund the request size).
- Explicitly states: **"the single greatest reason for rejection is a fundamental misalignment between the nonprofit's mission, project, or capacity and the funder's stated priorities"** — i.e., thematic/mission alignment is the top-weighted factor in practice.
- **Source:** https://www.fundrobin.com/articles/how-to-guide/funding-application-foundations/grant-fit-score-framework-nonprofits; https://www.fundrobin.com/articles/how-to-guide/funding-application-foundations/science-of-selection-grant-fit-score-nonprofit-efficiency

### 1.2 GrantAssistant.ai's "Funder Scoring Rubric"
- A simpler 4-dimension model (scale 1-12): **Mission Alignment, Program Fit, Geographic Match, Grant Size**.
- **Source:** https://www.grantassistant.ai/resources/guides/funder-scoring-rubric

### 1.3 The "10-Minute Funder Fit Test" (grant strategy scorecard)
- Frames funder fit as multi-dimensional alignment across: **organization, program, mission, population, location, budget, documentation/compliance readiness, and outcomes** — explicitly warning that "a grant can say 'community impact' and still not be interested in the kind of work your organization does," and that documentation gaps (e.g., missing audited financials, insufficient years of track record) sink otherwise-good-looking matches.
- **Source:** https://faithbasedgrants.substack.com/p/the-10-minute-funder-fit-test-a-simple

### 1.4 Classic donor/prospect research model: "Linkage – Ability – Interest" (L-I-A)
- A long-standing model used in major-gift/institutional fundraising: score each prospect 1-5 on **Linkage** (existing relationship/connection — "warmth"), **Interest** (affinity/mission alignment), and **Ability** (financial capacity to give) — typically summed to a score out of 15.
- Crucially, this model treats an **existing relationship/warm connection ("Linkage") as its own scored dimension**, distinct from thematic fit.
- **Source:** https://summitfundraising.co.uk/prioritise-how-to-use-the-l-i-a-model; https://kindsight.io/resources/blog/what-is-propensity-affinity-and-capacity; https://www.donorsearch.net/prospect-research-ultimate-guide

### 1.5 India-specific CSR-NGO partner due-diligence checklist (2026 guide for corporate CSR teams)
This is the most directly relevant to KABIL's context, since 2 of the 4 funders (Axis Bank Foundation, SBI Foundation) are **corporate CSR arms bound by Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013**, not just general foundations. Key legally-mandated eligibility gates for any NGO receiving Indian CSR funds:
- **Form CSR-1 registration with the MCA — mandatory since 1 April 2021, "no exceptions."** An NGO without CSR-1 **cannot legally receive CSR funds** as an implementing agency.
- **12A registration** (income-tax exemption / confirms non-profit status).
- **80G registration** (enables the donor to claim tax deduction; also a "marker of legitimacy and compliance discipline").
- **NGO Darpan (NITI Aayog) registration** — not strictly mandatory for CSR but "enhances credibility" and is required for many government grants.
- **PAN and bank account in the organization's own legal name** (not an individual's).
- **A minimum 3-year track record of similar activity** — required specifically for *independent* NGOs (this requirement is waived only if the NGO was itself established by the funding company or by government).
- **Financial health assessment** (audited financials, key ratios) and **governance structure review**.
- **Alignment with Schedule VII** of the Companies Act (the specific list of permissible CSR activity categories).
- **Source:** https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-select-ngo-csr-partnership-india-due-diligence-checklist-tzvof

### 1.6 What this research means for rubric design
Two dimensions are consistently present across serious frameworks that are easy to miss in a simple checklist:
1. **A "Relationship/Warmth" dimension** — every serious framework reviewed (FundRobin's "relationship potential," the classic L-I-A model's "Linkage") treats an existing/active relationship as its own scored factor, separate from thematic fit.
2. **A "Funder Capacity/Trend" dimension** — FundRobin's 3-pillar AI model explicitly includes "Funder Capacity" (is the funder financially able and *currently* growing its giving, or shrinking?) as a top-level pillar. We already have detailed year-over-year funding-trend research for all four funders (see each funder's individual file, Section 8: Funding Trends) — this criterion puts that research to direct use.
3. Additionally, the **India-specific CSR-1 registration requirement** is a hard, legally-mandated gate for CSR-arm funders (ABF, SBIF) that a generic "Eligibility" criterion could easily miss if not probed specifically — and it turns out to be a **real, flagged gap for KABIL** (see Part 3 below).

## Part 2: The Rubric (100 points, 7 criteria)

| # | Criterion | Points | Guiding Question | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Thematic/Sector Alignment** | 25 | Does the funder's stated priority area(s) match the NGO's actual program work? | The single highest-weighted factor — FundRobin and National Council of Nonprofits research both identify mission/thematic misalignment as "the single greatest reason for rejection." |
| 2 | **Eligibility & Compliance** | 20 | Does the NGO hold all legally-required registrations for this funder type (12A, 80G, FCRA if relevant, **CSR-1 if the funder is CSR-bound**), and does it meet minimum track-record/financial-documentation requirements? | Made specific rather than generic, directly incorporating the India CSR due-diligence checklist (CSR-1 is a hard legal gate for corporate CSR funders, not just a "nice to have"). |
| 3 | **Geography** | 15 | Does the funder operate in Assam/Northeast India, ideally the specific district? | Important, but most frameworks (FundRobin, GrantAssistant.ai) treat geography as one of several roughly-equal-weighted factors rather than a top-tier one — it's often closer to a "does it pass the filter at all" gate than a nuanced scoring dimension once a funder is already active in a broad region like "Northeast India." |
| 4 | **Target Population** | 15 | Does the funder's stated beneficiary group match the NGO's actual beneficiaries? | Kept as a distinct criterion (per the "10-Minute Funder Fit Test"'s "population" dimension), weighted to reflect that it substantially overlaps with Thematic Alignment in practice. |
| 5 | **Relationship/Warmth (Linkage)** | 10 | Does an existing, active, or historical relationship already exist between the NGO and funder? | Directly modeled on the classic Linkage-Ability-Interest (L-I-A) prospect-research framework and FundRobin's "relationship potential" factor. An active multi-year grantee relationship is a fundamentally different (and stronger) position than a cold approach. |
| 6 | **Funding Size & Type Fit** | 10 | Does the grant size, duration, and structure (project vs. core, multi-year vs. one-off) match the NGO's actual need and scale? | Validated by GrantAssistant.ai and FundRobin both including "grant size" as a scored factor, consistently one of the lower-weighted ones relative to mission/eligibility. |
| 7 | **Funder Capacity & Trend** | 5 | Is the funder's overall giving currently growing, stable, or shrinking — i.e., is now a good time to seek/renew funding from them? | Directly modeled on FundRobin's "Funder Capacity" pillar. Puts the year-over-year funding-trend research (already compiled for all 4 funders) to direct use. |
| | **Total** | **100** | | |

**Key takeaways:**
- KABIL's strongest, most secure funder relationship is with **Axis Bank Foundation** — a near-perfect thematic match, an active multi-year grant, and a rapidly growing funder.
- **Tata Trusts is the clear outlier and the relationship most in need of direct clarification** — the only evidence of a link is a historical (2013-14) connection via Sir Dorabji Tata Trust that is not confirmed as currently active, despite being listed as a current funder on KABIL's own website.
- A genuinely new, actionable finding from this exercise: **KABIL's CSR Form 1 (CSR-1) registration status was recorded as "Not Available"** by a third-party NGO-profile aggregator. Since CSR-1 is a mandatory, no-exceptions legal requirement for any NGO receiving funds from a Section-135-bound CSR foundation (which both Axis Bank Foundation and SBI Foundation are), **this is worth KABIL confirming/resolving directly** — it may already be resolved, or the specific grant structures may route funds through a different registered implementing agency (e.g., ASRLM/BRLF), but it should not be left unverified.

## Limitations (Honesty Notes)

1. **This is a single-rater, manually-scored exercise.** A second scorer, or a structured scoring session with KABIL's own team, would improve reliability.
2. **The CSR-1 "Not Available" data point is from a single third-party aggregator (give.do)** and was not independently re-verified on the official MCA CSR-1 database for this exercise — it should be treated as a flag to investigate, not a confirmed fact.
3. **The Relationship/Warmth and Funder Capacity criteria are only worth 10 and 5 points respectively** — deliberately kept smaller than Thematic Alignment and Eligibility, consistent with how the reviewed frameworks weight them, but this is a design choice worth revisiting once more real-world outcome data (which funders actually renewed/expanded KABIL's grants) is available.
4. **This rubric was applied only to KABIL** in this pass — a natural next step would be to apply the same rubric to BDF's prospective (non-active) funder relationships for comparison.
5. **No new primary-source fetching was done specifically to re-verify the Section 8/Schedule VII/CSR-1 nuances for APF and Tata Trusts** — the distinction drawn (private foundation vs. Section-135-bound CSR arm) is based on their known legal structures (private trust / Section 25 company vs. corporate CSR foundation) rather than a funder-published statement confirming they are exempt from CSR-1 requirements.
