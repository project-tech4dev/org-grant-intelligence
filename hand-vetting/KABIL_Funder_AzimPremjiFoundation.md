# KABIL Funder Profile — Azim Premji Foundation (APF)

**Part of KABIL's current funder research series.** This file covers only **Azim Premji Foundation**. See separate files for Axis Bank Foundation, SBI Foundation, and Tata Trusts.

---

## KABIL Connection (confirmed)
APF funds **"Strengthening the Livelihood of Rural Poor in Mazbat Block of Udalguri District"** — a KABIL-implemented project, evaluated via a 2-day APF field visit on 11–12 February 2026. APF leadership also jointly visited **DWIMU** project sites in Udalguri Block (with BRLF), reviewing fishery/poultry/piggery/MGNREGA-arecanut livelihood diversification work.

## 1. Funder Basic Information
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Funder name | Azim Premji Foundation (APF) |
| Parent organization | Founded/endowed by Azim Premji (Founder-Chairman, Wipro Ltd.) — funded by his personal wealth, not a corporate CSR arm |
| Funder type | Private/family philanthropic foundation (one of the largest in the world by endowment) |
| Headquarters | Bengaluru, Karnataka |
| Year established | 18 February 2000 (formally registered 9 March 2001, under Section 25 of the Companies Act) |
| Brief history | Azim Premji signed the Giving Pledge in 2013 (first Indian to do so); irrevocably donated ~66% economic stake in Wipro to the Foundation's endowment; named to TIME100 Philanthropy list (2025) |
| Main purpose/mission | To contribute to a "just, equitable, humane and sustainable society" in India, organized around "needs" (dignity, health, food, livelihood, education, shelter) and "values" (freedom from injustice, harmony, empathy) |

## 5. Organization Eligibility
- **Types accepted:** Grassroots civil society organisations of all sizes — "from small and very early-stage ones to those that are large and mature" (APF's own words)
- **12A/80G/FCRA/CSR-1 requirements, minimum turnover, minimum operating history:** **Not explicitly published** as a checklist on APF's Grants page — the Foundation emphasizes relationship-building and issue-understanding over a formal eligibility gate, though standard Indian NGO compliance (12A/80G) would be assumed baseline for any registered grantee
- **Confirmed early-stage/first-time applicant pathway:** APF has run a specific **"Call for Applications for Early-stage CSOs"** — open grant applications for new, early-stage not-for-profits in specific regions, with grant sizes up to $100,000 — direct proof it funds first-time/new organizations, not just established partners
**Resource:** https://azimpremjifoundation.org/who-we-are/how-we-are-organised/grants (general eligibility framing); https://triple-funds.com/tf-entry/call-for-applications-for-early-stage-csos ("The Azim Premji Foundation is issuing a call for grant applications from early-stage, not-for-profit organizations... Grant size: Small - up to $100,000")

## 6. Funding Size
- **Minimum/Maximum grant:** "Grant sizes vary from **a few lakh rupees to a few tens of crores**" (APF's own published statement)
- **Typical grant:** Commonly cited range ₹10 lakh – ₹5 crore (per broader sector reporting, not an APF-published figure)
- **Duration:** **1–10 years, with most grants being 3–4 years** (APF's own published statement) — explicitly multi-year, relationship-based
- **One-time funding:** Not the primary model
- **Core vs. project funding:** APF selectively provides **corpus/core grants** in addition to program-specific grants — notably flexible compared to many funders
- **Capital expenditure / operational costs / staff salaries:** **Not explicitly itemized publicly**, but APF's willingness to give corpus grants suggests more flexibility than strictly project-tied funders
**Resource:** https://azimpremjifoundation.org/who-we-are/how-we-are-organised/grants (direct quote: "Our grant sizes vary from a few lakh rupees to a few tens of crores; the tenures of these grants are from 1-10 years, with most being between 3-4 years"); https://triple-funds.com/tf-entry/call-for-applications-for-early-stage-csos (early-stage grant ceiling of $100,000)

## 8. Funding Trends (Is Giving Increasing, Decreasing, or Shifting?)

**Verdict: Strongly INCREASING (spend roughly doubled in 2 years), with broadening themes and geography — even as Azim Premji's own new personal cash donations have shrunk.**

### Year-over-year total spend
| Fiscal Year | Direct Spend | Grants Disbursed | **Total** | YoY Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023-24 | ₹594 cr | ₹934 cr | **₹1,528 cr** | — |
| FY2024-25 | ₹735 cr | ₹1,639 cr | **₹2,374 cr** | **+55%** |
| FY2025-26 | ₹1,542 cr | ₹1,664 cr | **₹3,206 cr** | **+35%** |

Total spend has **more than doubled in two years** (₹1,528 cr → ₹3,206 cr). Grants **committed** for future years exceed what's actually disbursed each year (e.g., ₹3,664 cr committed vs. ₹1,639 cr disbursed in FY24-25), pointing to a growing multi-year funding pipeline rather than one-off giving.

### An important nuance: endowment growth vs. Premji's "new" personal donations
- **Endowment size over time:** ~$5B (Jan 2015) → ~$21B (Mar 2019, after a fresh $7.5B commitment) → ~$29B/₹2.4 lakh cr (Jan 2023) → ~₹3.1 lakh cr (June 2025) → ~₹3.0 lakh cr (June 2026, fluctuating with Wipro's share price).
- **But Premji's own newly-reported annual cash donations have fallen sharply** on philanthropy league tables (EdelGive-Hurun): ₹7,904 cr (FY2019-20, "India's Most Generous") → ~₹9,713 cr (FY2020-21, a large one-off transfer year) → just **₹147 cr (FY2024-25)** — he has fallen out of the top ranks, overtaken by newer donors like Shiv Nadar and Mukesh Ambani.
- **Why both can be true:** Premji front-loaded almost his entire wealth transfer to the Foundation's endowment between 2001–2023. Now that the bulk of the transfer is done, "new money" flowing in each year is small — but the **Foundation's own program spending keeps growing** because it's drawing on returns/share-sale proceeds from the already-massive corpus (managed via an internal "Endowment Office" and vehicles like Premji Trust, which periodically executes Wipro block trades, e.g., a ₹5,057 crore block deal in mid-2025).

### Thematic shift: broadening beyond education
- **Education remains the largest historic focus** (59 District Institutes, ~2.8 lakh teachers, ~63 lakh students reached directly) — but the Foundation is visibly diversifying:
  - **Creches scaling rapidly**: 814 creches (mid-2025) → **1,784 creches** covering 30,000+ children (FY25-26) — expanding into new states (Nagaland, UP, Uttarakhand) and cities (Chennai, Delhi, Mumbai).
  - **Health**: expanding to ~30 new hospitals planned; public-health partnerships now in 12 districts/6 states.
  - **Livelihoods**: natural farming and income-support programs for small/marginal farmers scaling to ~22.5 lakh farmers across 265 districts.
  - **New large commitments**: ~$175 million (Aug 2024) mid-day-meal/egg-nutrition expansion for 5M+ children; Azim Premji Scholarship for girls scaled from **21,500 recipients (2024-25) to 1,61,000 (2025-26)** — a >7x jump in one year.
- **University expansion**: student enrollment grew from ~3,255 to ~3,806; a third campus (Ranchi, 150 acres) is opening, with a fourth planned specifically for the **Northeast** — a direct signal of increasing NE-region investment.

### Geographic shift: expanding into underserved states
Grant-making priority geography has **broadened** from an original core of 6 states (Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Karnataka, MP, Rajasthan, Uttarakhand) to explicitly include **Eastern UP, Bihar, Odisha, West Bengal, Northeast India, Telangana, and Andhra Pradesh** — i.e., a deliberate expansion into some of India's most underserved regions, of which KABIL's Assam/Udalguri grant is a direct example.

### Partner-count growth
NGO/CSO partners funded grew from **~1,320 (FY24-25) to ~1,640 (FY25-26)** — roughly **24% growth in partner count in a single year**, consistent with the rising total grant spend.

### Overall takeaway
APF's trend is one of **accelerating, compounding scale** (spend roughly doubled in 2 years) combined with **deliberate diversification** — from an education-only foundation into health, livelihoods, urban issues, gender justice, and a growing Northeast India footprint — funded by a still-appreciating multi-billion-dollar endowment rather than fresh annual fundraising.
