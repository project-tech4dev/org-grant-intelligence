# SATRA — Program Areas

> This document is updated continuously as new information is found. See `SATRA_Organization_Profile.md` for organization identity, leadership, and governance. Built primarily from the **FY2023-24 Annual Activity Report** (read in full, 32 pages) plus website program pages. SATRA's website hosts a **17-year Annual Report archive (FY2008-09 through FY2024-25)**; several years were downloaded and year-identified in this pass (see the reading log at the end), though most remain unread — a strong resource for future deep-dives.

### 1. Community Mental Health
- **Status:** Active, described as ongoing "since 2006" (per FY2023-24 Chairperson's Note) — one of SATRA's longest-running and most quantified programs.
- **Description:** A bio-psychosocial-model mental health program in Darrang district — identification/registration of patients, screening camps, follow-up home visits, counseling (patient and family), caregiver training, livelihood support for affected individuals, and a "suicide gatekeeper" village-volunteer network.
- **Sector:** Health (Mental Health)
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 3
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None
- **Geography (as stated):** Darrang district, Assam
- **Named Funder:** **Azim Premji Foundation / Azim Premji Philanthropic Initiatives**
- **Metric Trend Across Years (FY2023-24, the only year with detailed figures read so far):**
  - **2,879 patients registered** in total, including **688 new patients** and **414 recovered patients** that year.
  - **24 mental health screening camps** held, attended by **1,334 patients**.
  - **15,120 follow-up visits** conducted by SATRA staff.
  - **674 counseling sessions** delivered (364 for family members, 310 for patients directly).
  - **50 individuals supported with livelihood assistance.**
  - **268 caregivers trained.**
  - **16 village volunteers** promoted as "Suicide Gate-keepers."
  - Additional training breakdown: 52 ASHA workers trained, 50 caregivers trained (a slightly different figure from the "268" headline — likely this specific 50 refers to one particular training batch/cohort, not the cumulative 268), 16 suicide-gatekeeper trainees, 15 village leaders trained.
  - **Diagnosis breakdown among registered patients:** Schizophrenia (98), Depression (97), Intellectual/Developmental Disability + Behavioural Disorder (41), Psychosis (38), Epilepsy (36), Dementia (26), Anxiety (24), Substance Use Disorder (24), Bipolar Disorder (16), ADHD (7), Conversion Disorder (3), OCD (3), PTSD (2).
  - **Gender split:** 45% male, 55% female among total patients.
- **Open Questions:** Is the "268 caregivers trained" figure cumulative-to-date or FY2023-24-specific — it sits awkwardly next to a separate "50" caregiver-training figure in the same report's infographic, suggesting possible double-counting or two different measurement points not clearly reconciled in the source document.

### 2. Social and Economic Empowerment of Women and Youth (SATRA-EDGH-India / Sericulture-Weaving Livelihoods)
- **Status:** Active, a 3-year project
- **Description:** Economic empowerment of women through Eri silk farming/rearing and weaving, and skill development of youth in eco-friendly agro-based/alternative livelihoods (vermi-composting, beekeeping), in Assam's flood-prone "Char Area" (river-island/riverine tracts).
- **Sector:** Livelihoods / Women's Economic Empowerment
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 5, SDG 8
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Retail, Commerce & Craft → Creative & Traditional Trades (Eri silk weaving), 🔴 Informal (Women); Agritech & Allied Ecosystem → Livestock/Sericulture, 🔴 Informal (Youth)
- **Geography (as stated):** 8 villages in the "Char Area," Darrang District, Assam
- **Named Funder:** **EDGH Luxembourg**, in association with **TDH Germany**
- **Target groups:** Minority Scheduled Caste (SC), Other Backward Communities (OBC), and general communities — economically/socially vulnerable
- **Metric Trend Across Years (FY2023-24):**
  - **300 total women** engaged with the project — of whom **250 engaged in hand-spinning & Eri rearing** and **50 engaged in weaving activities**.
  - **150 total youth** targeted (100 girls, 50 boys) — **87 trained in vermi-compost management**; **8 youth clubs** formed.
  - **Women's income reportedly increased by 30%.**
  - Gender-equity workshops conducted, increasing household/community discussion on gender equity.
  - Families linked to government departments (Agriculture, Sericulture, KVK).
- **Named beneficiary story:** **Namita**, an Eri silk weaver, taught her husband to weave — described by SATRA as breaking traditional gender norms around who performs weaving work in the community.
- **Challenges (self-reported):** floods and soil erosion threatening agricultural activities; youth (especially boys) migrating for jobs, limiting programme participation; poor road/communication infrastructure in remote Char areas.
- **Open Questions:** No sales/income figures in absolute rupee terms were found — only the relative "30% increase" claim, which (as with similar claims from other NGOs in this project) cannot be independently verified from the text alone.

### 3. Livelihood — "Strengthening Community Action for Development in North East India"
- **Status:** Active, though explicitly described as significantly disrupted by COVID-19 and flooding
- **Description:** Formation of people's institutions (unions/associations) to advocate for rights/entitlements, SHG formation, skill training for unemployed youth/farmers (organic farming, vermicompost, resilient seeds, FPO formation), and linkage to government livelihood schemes.
- **Sector:** Livelihoods / Institutional Capacity Building
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 2, SDG 8, SDG 16
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied Ecosystem → Cultivation & Sourcing, 🔴 Informal (Small/Marginal Farmers, Unorganised Labour)
- **Geography (as stated):** Not specified beyond SATRA's general Darrang-area operating footprint
- **Named Funder:** **Bread for the World**
- **Metric Trend Across Years (FY2023-24):**
  - **2 people's organizations established:** "Jana Jagaran Adhikar Sangha" and "Bahumukhi Sramik Union" (a multi-purpose labour union).
  - **20 SHGs formed**, trained in bookkeeping/livelihood mapping/income generation — **16 of the 20 actively engaged** in income-generating activities.
  - **130 youths/farmers trained** in organic farming, vermicompost, resilient seeds, and FPO formation — **36 of the 130 engaged** in income-generating activities post-training.
  - Government schemes accessed by community members: bee-keeping, banana plantation, mustard cultivation, old-age pension, Kisan Credit Card (KCC), labour cards, **PM Vishwakarma**, **PM-SYM** (Pradhan Mantri Shram Yogi Maandhan pension scheme).
  - Self-reported outcome percentages: 38.4% of unemployed youth/farmers "mobilised," 34.4% "trained," 18.9% of unorganised labour "organised into unions," 6.5% "engaged in income generating activities" — these look like a funnel/conversion-rate breakdown (i.e., of everyone initially mobilized, a shrinking percentage progress to each next stage), though the base denominator is not explicitly stated.
- **Challenges (self-reported):** fund unavailability affecting planned activities; severe flooding requiring emergency response diversion of resources.
- **Open Questions:** The stated percentages (38.4% → 34.4% → 18.9% → 6.5%) suggest a conversion funnel, but without a stated total population/target base, these percentages cannot be converted into absolute headcounts — worth clarifying against the underlying denominator.

### 5. Conflict Management and Peace Education
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Peacebuilding and conflict-resolution work in the ethnically-diverse and historically conflict-affected **Bodoland Territorial Council (BTC)** region — village action plans, children/youth centers, children's councils and youth peace-volunteer groups, school safety/inclusion work, community peace-building committees, sports/literary/film/theatre clubs, and youth-as-reporters advocacy.
- **Sector:** Peacebuilding / Conflict Resolution / Education
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 16, SDG 4, SDG 10
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None
- **Geography (as stated):** 10 villages in the Bodoland Territorial Council (BTC) area, Assam
- **Named Funder:** **Misereor**, in association with **TDH Germany**
- **Metric Trend Across Years (FY2023-24):**
  - **10 village action plans** developed; **10 children/youth centers** established.
  - Children's councils and youth peace-volunteer groups formed in each of the 10 villages, with regular training and monthly peace sessions.
  - **10 community-based peace-building committees** formed (monthly meetings).
  - **10 sports clubs** established across the villages.
  - Literary, film, and theatre clubs formed — including weekly film screenings, book readings, and an annual student/youth story publication.
  - Youth engaged as "reporters" advocating for their communities in local planning meetings with legislators/public authorities.
- **Open Questions:** No headcount figures (e.g., number of children/youth actively participating in the councils/centers) were found — only counts of institutions/structures established, not people reached.

### 6. Child Protection (CHILDLINE + "Access to Justice" / KSCF-funded project)
- **Status:** Active — two related but distinct sub-initiatives documented.
- **Description:**
  - **(a) CHILDLINE Project, Darrang District** — running since **1 March 2020**, funded by the **Ministry of Women and Child Development, Government of India**, monitored by the **CHILDLINE India Foundation (CIF)**. Provides an emergency-response safety net (the CHILDLINE 1098 helpline) for children in distress.
  - **(b) "Access to Justice" (Phase 2)** — a distinct, ten-month project supported by **KSCF US**, focused on child labor, trafficking, marriage, sexual abuse, education access, and strengthening Village Level Child Protection Committees (VLCPCs) and Child Marriage Prohibition Officers (CMPOs).
- **Sector:** Child Protection / Rights
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 5, SDG 16
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None — protection program, out of scope for a livelihoods occupation taxonomy
- **Geography (as stated):** Darrang District, Assam (CHILDLINE); "450 villages" reached for stakeholder consultation under the KSCF project
- **Named Funders:** Ministry of Women and Child Development, GoI (CHILDLINE); **KSCF US** (Access to Justice Phase 2)
- **Metric Trend Across Years (FY2023-24, specifically April-August 2023 reporting window for CHILDLINE):**
  - **CHILDLINE case breakdown by issue type:** Child labour (66.7%), Child marriage (20%), Child sexual abuse (2.7%), Runaway child (2.7%), Cyber security/online safety (1.3%), Right to Education (1.3%), Trafficking for marriage (1.3%), Trafficking for sexual work (1.3%).
  - **68 days of outreach**, **1,600.45 hours**, reaching **2,871 individuals**.
  - **6 awareness programmes** (3 school-level, 3 community-level), April-August 2023.
  - **World Day Against Child Labour collaboration** with DCPU, Darrang Labour Department, Darrang Police, and **Bachpan Bachao Andolan** (the well-known national child-rights NGO) — resulted in **21 children rescued** from workplaces in Dolgaon town.
  - **1 training/orientation programme** for ASHA/Anganwadi workers (20 participants).
  - **Access to Justice (KSCF) project, cumulative results:** **15 FIRs registered**, **148 children rescued** from labour/trafficking; **33 child marriages intervened/stopped**, with **290 additional cases** where persuasion was used; **182 children supported** in sexual-abuse cases; **54 school dropouts re-enrolled**; extensive stakeholder engagement across **450 villages**; **519,132 group pledges**, **19,231 family pledges**, and **276,651 individual pledges** collected against child marriage/labour/abuse.
- **Open Questions:** The very large pledge counts (over 500,000 group pledges) seem disproportionate relative to the 450-village engagement scope — worth clarifying whether "pledges" are counted as individual signature/thumbprint instances or some other unit that could explain the scale.

### 7. Sericulture Development
- **Status:** Active, ongoing "since 2006" for the mulberry component
- **Description:** Three parallel silk-sector interventions in Darrang and Udalguri districts: **Mulberry silk (Paat)**, **Eri silk (non-violent/peace silk)**, and **Muga silk ("golden silk")** — each with scientific-management training to raise both productivity and rural women's/farmers' incomes.
- **Sector:** Livelihoods / Sericulture
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 8
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied Ecosystem → Sericulture, 🔴 Informal (Rural Women & Farmers)
- **Geography (as stated):** Darrang and Udalguri districts, Assam
- **Named Partner:** **Central Silk Board (CSB), Guwahati Regional Office** (technical/skill-training partner for the Muga component)
- **Metric Trend Across Years:**
  - **Mulberry silk:** **2,000 mulberry silk rearers trained since 2006** — reportedly **all 2,000 still practicing** the trade, with self-reported productivity/income gains and higher market prices for improved-quality silk.
  - **Eri silk:** No specific headcount given; describes formation of specialized groups along the value chain (cocoon producers, spinners, weavers) to strengthen collective production quality/marketability.
  - **Muga silk:** **80 to 120 farmers mobilized** in Darrang district (the report gives both figures in different places — "mobilized 80 farmers" in the methodology text vs. an objective of "mobilize 120 farmers," suggesting 80 is the actual achievement against a 120 target, though this isn't explicitly labelled as such).
- **Open Questions:** Is the "2,000 mulberry rearers, all still practicing" figure a genuinely current, re-verified count, or a legacy/cumulative figure carried forward in report text over multiple years (an "boilerplate" risk similar to that identified for PESCH)? This would benefit from checking an older SATRA annual report for comparison.

### 8. Bridging Gaps for Unorganised Labour in Assam
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Awareness-raising and registration facilitation for unregistered construction-sector laborers in Assam, who face barriers to accessing government social-security benefits — includes promotion of a labour union in Sipajhar, with SATRA providing office space until the union could operate independently.
- **Sector:** Labour Rights / Social Protection
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 8, SDG 10
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: None specified — general unorganised/informal labour, primarily construction sector
- **Geography (as stated):** Sipajhar and surrounding areas, Assam
- **Named Funders:** Not specified in the source text reviewed (may overlap with the Bread for the World-funded Livelihood programme, Program 3 above, given similar unionization themes)
- **Metric Trend Across Years (FY2023-24):** **86 labour cards** facilitated/provided; **491 people reached with direct awareness**; **1,767 people reached with indirect awareness**.
- **Open Questions:** Is this program a distinct funding line, or is it the same "Bahumukhi Sramik Union" initiative already counted under Program 3 (Livelihood/Bread for the World)? The two program write-ups in the same annual report describe very similar unionization activities without clearly cross-referencing each other.

### 9. Digital Learning Through the Chimple App
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** A CSR-funded digital/gamified early-learning initiative for young children (Classes 1-2, ages 5-6), using the **"Chimple" app** (developed by technology partner **Sutara Learning Foundation**) to teach Maths and Science through gamified, screen-based activities, aiming to shift screen time from unproductive to productive use.
- **Sector:** Education / EdTech
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 4
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None
- **Geography (as stated):** Mangaldai area — **5 schools**: Ramharil LP School, Ghorabandha LP School, Mangaldai Balak LP School, Saloijara LP School, and Chandowalpara LP School
- **Named Funder:** **North East Nutrient Pvt. Ltd.** (CSR)
- **Metric Trend Across Years (FY2023-24):** **500 students** selected across the 5 schools; **100 tablets** distributed (a 5:1 student-to-tablet ratio) with headphones/device covers; wireless internet installed in all 5 schools; teacher training on app usability conducted; target of **3,000+ minutes (50+ hours)** of student app-usage per student for the following (2022-23) session.
- **Open Questions:** The report references a "next session 22-23" target while reporting FY2023-24 activities — a possible dating inconsistency in the source document (i.e., is this project's content actually describing FY2022-23 activity, reused/carried into the FY2023-24 report?), echoing the boilerplate-reuse caution already flagged for PESCH's reports.

## Cross-Cutting Observations

1. **SATRA's FY2023-24 report is unusually rich in hard numbers compared to most NGOs researched in this project** (rivaling PESCH) — the Community Mental Health and Child Protection programs in particular provide detailed, granular breakdowns (diagnosis categories, case-type percentages, pledge counts).
2. **Two potential "boilerplate reuse" flags identified** (though less definitively proven than the PESCH case): the Chimple project's reference to "next session 22-23" inside an FY2023-24 report, and the Mulberry Silk programme's static "2,000 rearers, all still practicing" figure, which reads as a legacy claim rather than a freshly re-verified annual count.
3. **A dense, diversified funder base** — at least 15 distinctly-named institutional funders/partners are credited in a single annual report, reflecting two decades of relationship-building (RGVN in year 1, growing to Oxfam India, Tata Trust, Dasra, Azim Premji Foundation, and multiple European faith-based donors like TDH Germany/Switzerland and Misereor by FY2023-24).
4. **SATRA is one of the few NGOs in this research project to publish a detailed internal HR/governance breakdown** (staff counts by employment type and salary band, board meeting dates/attendance, volunteer counts) directly within its annual report, rather than only in a separate audited financial statement.
