## Organization Overview (diyafoundation.in) — Assam/Meghalaya-based NGO

- **Founded:** 2011, emerging from humanitarian relief work during the 2010-2011 Rabha–Garo ethnic conflict in Assam's Kamrup and Goalpara districts (the founder organized relief — food, clothing, hygiene items, paddy seeds — for displaced families in camps before formally registering the organization).
- **Legal status:** Registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860, in Meghalaya (Reg. No. SR/DF-4/19); PAN: AABAD3470F; 12A No. 191/12AA/CIT(TECH)/SHG/2012-13/3043-46; 80G: CIT (Exemption), Kolkata/80G/2020-21/A/10249; FCRA No. 020780156; also holds CSR-1 registration, TAN, and Professional Tax registration (per its own Reports page document list).
- **Vision:** "A society of self-reliant, sustainability and dignity" (current site) / earlier phrasing: "To be a changed agent especially for the underprivileged and deprived class/society of the rural and urban areas, building self reliant and self sustaining community, empowering them for total growth towards a civil society."
- **Mission:** "Serving the community to enhance their capacities, participation, institution-building and sustainable use of resources."
- **5-Year Goal (2025-2030, current site):** Reach 20,000 households through livelihood enhancement, community mental health, and education programs, impacting 100,000 lives, via 7 numbered sub-goals (income increases of ₹25,000–₹1,00,000/household/year for 6,500 households; 60% of households gaining financial/market/entitlement access; 14,000-16,000 households in community institutions; DRE/climate-resilient tech for 2,000 households; mental health services for 500+ individuals; FLN skills for 2,500 children in Kamrup).
- **Earlier organizational goal (as stated in the FY2021-22 report):** "To impact the lives of 60,000 by 2023 through livelihood, health and education programs."
- **Three core thematic areas (as self-described in FY2020-21 report):** (a) Livelihood Promotion, (b) Health, (c) Education.
- **Target communities:** Scheduled Tribes (notably the **Rabha** tribal community), Scheduled Castes, Tea Tribes, and other backward/marginalised communities; operates within areas under the **Rabha Hasong Autonomous Council (RHAC)**.
- **Geography of intervention (as stated, combining the 2020-21 report and the current Milestones page):**
  - **Assam:** Kamrup Rural District (Rani, Chayani Barduar, Rampur, Chhaygaon, Bongaon, Boko, Kamalpur Development Blocks); Goalpara District (Balijana, Krishnai, Jaleswar, Matia, Lakhipur, Kuchdhowa, Rongjuli); Darrang District (Kushdhuwa/Kuchdhowa, Sipajhar Development Blocks) — Darrang added from FY2021-22 for the new DRR programme.
  - **Meghalaya:** Ri-Bhoi District (Jirang Development Block); East Jayantia Hills District (per an older Commutiny.in profile, not confirmed on the current site).
  - **North Garo Hills District** (Resubelpara, Kharkutta, Bajengdoba, Adokgre) — appears only on the current Milestones page, not in the older reports reviewed so far — **flagged as an open question below (possible recent geographic expansion).**
  - **UPDATE — resolved:** **Dima Hasao District of Assam and West Jayantia Hills District of Meghalaya** — flagged as unconfirmed in the previous pass — are now confirmed as genuine, historical (not fabricated) operating geographies: the **FY2017-18 Annual Activity Report** explicitly names a "Sustainable Sanitation Project in Assam and Meghalaya" implemented in "Dima Hasao of Assam and West Jayantia Hills, Meghalaya" (73 sanitary latrines constructed, funded by **Habitat for Humanity India, New Delhi**). This appears to have been a **time-bound, single-project geography** rather than a core operating area — it does not appear in the FY2020-21 or FY2021-22 board-geography lists, nor on the current website, suggesting Diya's footprint contracted back to its Kamrup Rural/Goalpara/Ri-Bhoi/Darrang core sometime after this 2017-18 sanitation project ended.

## Program Areas (detailed, one section per program)

Each program below covers the same set of points as before — Status, Description, Sector, SDG Tags, Occupation Taxonomy, Geography, Named Funders, Metric Trend, and Open Questions — just laid out as readable sections instead of a cramped wide table.

### 1. MITA (Mental Illness Treatment Initiative) / Mental Health
- **Status:** Active, ongoing since ~2020
- **Description:** Community mental health camps and doorstep medicine delivery for conditions including epilepsy, schizophrenia, schizotypal/delusional disorders, somatoform disorders, common mental disorders (CMD), severe mental disorders (SMD), and PTSD; run in partnership with **The Ant** (an NGO based in Bongaigaon, Chirang, Assam).
- **Sector:** Health (Mental Health)
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 3
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: None
- **Geography (as stated):** Kamrup Rural District of Assam; patients also come from Meghalaya
- **Named Funders:** **The Ant** (implementation partner, not a funder per se)
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** 280+ patients registered cumulatively, 55-60% attending monthly camps regularly (FY2020-21 report) → 80 patients covered via COVID-19 doorstep medicine delivery specifically (FY2020-21) → 150 mental patients supported with doorstep medicines during COVID lockdown (also FY2020-21 milestones list — possibly the same or an overlapping figure to the "80 patients" above) → ~80 patients covered via doorstep delivery, described across "Assam and Meghalaya State" (FY2021-22) → **315 registered patients** in the "Mental Illness Treatment Initiative (MITA)" program (current website Milestones page, year unstated, but presented as the most recent figure)
- **Open Questions:** Two different COVID-era doorstep-medicine figures (80 vs. 150) appear in the same FY2020-21 report/milestones list — same cohort counted twice, or two distinct sub-activities? Also: the program is called "Mental Health" in the annual reports through at least FY2021-22, but branded "MITA" only on the current website — when did the MITA name get formally adopted, and is 315 a cumulative or point-in-time figure?

### 2. Maternal Health / Respectful Maternity Care
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Awareness-raising and advocacy program for respectful maternity care; links Village Health Sanitation & Nutrition Days (VHSNDs) with medical institutions; collects data on maternal fatalities and maternity care quality for stakeholder advocacy.
- **Sector:** Health (Maternal Health)
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 3, SDG 5
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: None
- **Geography (as stated):** Not geographically specified beyond the org's general Assam/Meghalaya operating area
- **Named Funders:** **White Ribbon Alliance India** (Diya is a member and manages **The White Ribbon Alliance Assam's secretariat**); related national initiative also involves **C3 (Centre for Catalysing Change), India** as the lead on a 3-year "Promoting Respectful Maternity Care in India" project that includes Diya Foundation in Assam
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** No beneficiary counts found in any source reviewed — purely qualitative description on the website.
- **Open Questions:** No metrics at all are given for this program anywhere reviewed — is it primarily an advocacy/secretariat role (which may not generate simple headcounts) rather than a direct-service program? Needs the annual report text (not yet reviewed in full) to check for numbers.

### 3. Menstrual Health & Hygiene (Adolescent Girls Program)
- **Status:** Active since 2019
- **Description:** Digital-media-based reproductive and menstrual hygiene awareness program for adolescent girls; includes joyful learning sessions, personal reflection and creativity-development sessions, entrepreneurship-thinking sessions, and tuition support for girls in classes IX-X preparing for board exams.
- **Sector:** Health / Gender & Rights / Education
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 3, SDG 5, SDG 4
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: None
- **Geography (as stated):** 10 villages, Kamrup District of Assam; "major inhabitants are ST, SC, Tea Tribe and other General community"
- **Named Funders:** Not attributed in the source text reviewed
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** 10 adolescent groups formed, **850 adolescent girls** covered (single figure given, on the Health page, undated — presumably a cumulative/current total since the 2019 launch)
- **Open Questions:** Only one point-in-time figure exists (850 girls) — no year-by-year trend is available from the sources reviewed so far; needs the annual reports (2019-20 onward) to establish a trend line.

### 4. Education & Literacy (incl. Foundational Literacy and Numeracy)
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Addresses low literacy, high absenteeism, and school dropouts among Rabha, Bodo, Garo, Tea Tribe and Assamese-general children in forest villages and tea gardens; includes sensitization meetings with Panchayat leaders/retired teachers/youth clubs, direct work in primary schools, teacher capacity-building workshops on pedagogy, and (per current site) a dedicated Foundational Literacy and Numeracy (FLN) push.
- **Sector:** Education
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 4
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: None
- **Geography (as stated):** 23 primary schools across 25 villages within a 13 km radius of the org's office, Kamrup District (figure's vintage unclear — appears on a program page without a year)
- **Named Funders:** Not attributed in the source text reviewed for this specific program (KKHSOU — Krishna Kanta Handique State Open University — appears as an education-related network/alliance partner in the FY2018-19 report's table of contents, exact role unclear without full report text)
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** 931 children aged 6-11 (grades 1-5) covered (undated program-page figure) → 550 students of govt. primary schools receiving remedial classes/creative learning (FY2020-21 milestones) → 631 children in 10 villages (FY2021-22 milestones) → 765 children in 11 villages (current Milestones page, one instance) → **645 children and youth in 11 villages** (current Milestones page, a second, different instance elsewhere on the same page) → Goal (2025-2030): FLN skills for 2,500 children in Kamrup
- **Additional Details Found (beyond the standard fields):** The FY2018-19 report's table of contents lists a sub-item under Education called **"Sambidhan Live! _ Be a JAGRIK"** — this is not an in-house Diya invention; it is a nationally-run constitutional-literacy toolkit (debuted 2016, developed under "The Jagrik Project," and now stewarded by Commutiny – The Youth Collective) that "facilitates the balancing of rights and duties through lived action" for adolescents, aimed at nurturing "values based leadership" and spreading constitutional literacy. This indicates Diya Foundation was a **local implementation partner/licensee** of this national citizenship-education program as of FY2018-19, layered on top of its core literacy/FLN work — a distinct educational sub-program not mentioned anywhere on the current website's Education page.
- **Open Questions:** The current Milestones page states **two different child counts (765 vs. 645) for the same "11 villages"** figure without distinguishing them by year or sub-program — likely one is "children only" and the other "children and youth," but this is not labelled clearly; also the jump from 931 (an undated but seemingly early figure) down to the 550-765 range in recent years is unexplained — real contraction in scope, or was 931 always a "schools in range" figure rather than an active-beneficiary count? Also: is "Sambidhan Live! Be a JAGRIK" still active — it does not appear in either the FY2020-21 or FY2021-22 report excerpts reviewed, nor on the current website, suggesting it may have been a time-bound partnership that ended after FY2018-19.

- **Sector:** Institutional Capacity Building / Livelihoods / Gender
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 5, SDG 17

- **Named Funders:** **Assam State Rural Livelihood Mission (ASRLM)** (institutional linkage partner, not necessarily a direct funder)
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** **85 women's SHGs** promoted and transformed into Village Organizations (consistent figure across the Institution Building page and the FY2020-21 report snippet); **2 FPOs** established — **Rangsang Agro Producer Company Limited** (641 shareholders, horticulture/spices: black pepper, turmeric, ginger) and **Mapakkai Agro Producer Company Limited** (450 shareholders, sericulture value chain) — combined ~1,091 shareholders (an earlier snippet rounds this to "600 comprising" in one source, which is inconsistent with the 641+450=1,091 figure given elsewhere)

## Cross-Cutting Observations (Preliminary)

1. **Recurring identical metrics across reporting periods.** At least three figures — 9,000 horticultural plants, 60,550 Black Pepper plants, and 55,000 Arecanut plants — appear **identically worded** in both the FY2021-22 Annual Activity Report and the current website's "Milestones (2024-2025)" page. This is a strong signal that the current website may be reusing older annual-report figures rather than presenting genuinely updated FY2024-25 data, similar to patterns seen in other small NGOs' self-reporting.
2. **Two different, seemingly conflicting figures appear side-by-side on the same Milestones page** for education reach (765 vs. 645 children in "11 villages") without any distinguishing label — a direct, current-website-level version of the "which metric is real" problem.
3. **Document repository appears broken.** Every individual report/audit "view" page on diyafoundation.in/reports (e.g., the Annual Activity Report 2022-2023 page) embeds a PDF viewer that resolves to a generic `sample.pdf` placeholder file bundled with the "Happy Elementor Addons" WordPress plugin, **not the actual report**. The real, working PDF files that could be found for this research were instead located via direct `/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Annual-Activity-Report-YYYY-YYYY.pdf` URLs surfaced through web search indexing, or via a third-party mirror (Yale School of Management's case-study library hosts the FY2020-21 report). This means Diya Foundation's own website currently does **not** actually deliver any of its 9 listed annual reports or 12 listed audit reports to a visitor who clicks "View"/"Download" — a significant transparency/access gap.
4. **Consistent institutional partners across years:** The Ant (mental health), White Ribbon Alliance India / C3 (maternal health), and ASRLM (SHG-to-VO linkage) appear as multi-year, named collaborators. COVID-era funders (Paul Hamlyn Foundation, RCRC Coalition, C3, Azim Premji Philanthropic Initiative) and 2022-flood funders (Azim Premji Foundation, Goonj, CBM) are each tied to a specific, time-bound humanitarian response rather than the core ongoing livelihoods programs, which mostly have **no named funder at all** in the sources reviewed so far.
5. **No SDG tags or occupation taxonomy are published by Diya Foundation itself** — as with the earlier SNEHPAD research, all SDG/taxonomy tags above are this analyst's inference from program descriptions, not the organization's own classification.
