## 3. Geographic Fit

- **Core geography:** Udalguri district, Assam (BTAD/BTR) — Mazbat, Udalguri, Harisinga blocks
- **Udalguri district is an official NITI Aayog "Aspirational District"** (1 of 112 nationally, 1 of 7 in Assam alongside Darrang, Barpeta, Dhubri, Goalpara, Baksa, Hailakandi) — confirmed via both the Assam government's own Transformation & Development portal and NITI Aayog's official list. **This is a strong, concrete funder-matching signal**, directly comparable to Kiphire district's Aspirational District status found for Better Life Foundation earlier in this series.
  - Udalguri has a **demonstrated track record of strong government-programme performance**: awarded ₹7 crore for a top Delta Ranking (Jan-Feb 2021), ₹3 crore for Financial Inclusion & Skill Development ranking (Dec 2021), and ₹2 crore for Delta Ranking (March 2022) — this indicates Udalguri is a "success story" Aspirational District, which could be a positive framing for funders wanting to associate with a well-performing (not struggling) government priority geography.
- **DWIMU project geography (Udalguri, Tamulpur, Baska)**: **Baksa (likely "Baska") district is ALSO an official Aspirational District** — meaning 2 of DWIMU's 3 target districts carry this official priority designation. Tamulpur is a newer district (carved from Baksa in 2022) and does not appear to carry its own separate Aspirational District status.
- **Bodoland Territorial Region (BTR/BTAD)** status itself is also a distinct, government-recognized special-category geography (autonomous tribal council area) — relevant for funders with a specific tribal-development or Northeast-India mandate, separate from the Aspirational District framework.
- **Multi-state footprint** (Assam, West Bengal, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan) is unusual for an org this size and **signals a distinctive positioning as a technical-assistance/replication partner rather than a single-geography grassroots implementer** — attractive to funders wanting a partner who can scale/replicate a model across states rather than being tied to one region.

## 6. Distinctive "Selling Points" for a Funder Pitch

1. **Founder/leadership pedigree:** Founding ED Achintya Ghosh's 28-year PRADAN career (including PRADAN's own Executive Directorship, 1997-2002) is a strong, verifiable sector-credibility signal — PRADAN is one of India's most respected livelihood-promotion NGOs, and KABIL is explicitly modeled on translating that same rigor to Northeast India. IIT Kharagpur Distinguished Alumnus Award (2020) adds further individual credibility.
2. **Proven ability to leverage government funds many times over:** The MGNREGS/IBS convergence model means every rupee of philanthropic/CSR funding to KABIL can unlock multiples in government scheme money (e.g., ₹70 lakh leveraged in Tikamgarh alone) — a compelling "multiplier effect" argument for funders focused on cost-efficiency/leverage.
3. **Official Aspirational District alignment (Udalguri, Baksa)** — concrete, government-recognized geographic priority tags, useful for CSR Schedule VII compliance narratives and government-aligned Foundation mandates.
4. **Demonstrated large-scale delivery capacity:** the concluded 24,765-family Axis Bank Foundation project is strong proof of KABIL's ability to competently absorb and deliver a large, multi-year, multi-crore grant — a key due-diligence reassurance for any large funder.
5. **Multi-state replication capability:** unlike most single-district grassroots NGOs, KABIL already operates a technical-assistance model across 5 states (Assam, WB, MP, UP, Rajasthan) — attractive to funders wanting a partner capable of scaling a proven model geographically.
6. **Governance continuity + institutional maturity:** Board members with 13-year tenures (Thomas Mathews, Asif Zaidi), formal Governing Board + General Body structure, and a clean leadership succession (Achintya Ghosh → Har Gobinda Boro, Nov 2025) all signal institutional stability rather than founder-dependency risk — a meaningful positive differentiator versus single-founder-dependent organizations.
7. **Tea garden worker niche:** the Oak Foundation-funded Dooars project gives KABIL a distinctive, high-narrative-value specialization in an under-served, historically neglected labor population (tea garden workers) — a strong fit for labor-rights/social-justice-oriented funders specifically.

## 7. Gaps a Funder Would Likely Flag

1. **FCRA status unconfirmed** — the same structural blocker found across other orgs in this research series; blocks/complicates the international/multilateral/bilateral/diaspora funder universe, and raises a genuine question about how the (already-received) Oak Foundation funding is structured.
2. **Not listed on GuideStar India, NGO Darpan, or CSR-1** — despite a strong actual funding track record, this makes independent third-party verification harder for a *new* funder doing first-time due diligence; likely an easy fix (self-registration) rather than a deep compliance problem.
3. **No audited financial statements found publicly** — only two Annual Report-cited topline figures (₹169.84 lakh / ₹164.62 lakh), which are **identical across two different fiscal years** — a data-quality red flag that a rigorous funder's due diligence would likely catch and query directly.
4. **Leadership transition risk (recent):** Founding ED Achintya Ghosh's departure in November 2025 (after 13 years) is very recent — funders may reasonably want to understand the new ED Har Gobinda Boro's track record and confirm continuity of relationships/institutional knowledge, especially with funders who worked closely with Ghosh personally (per Axis Bank Foundation's own 2020-21 Annual Report, which named him directly).
5. **Reporting inconsistencies across years:** household/family figures for the same or similar projects don't always reconcile cleanly year-to-year (see full discussion in `KABIL_Programs_and_Reports.md`) — best proactively clarified in any new funder proposal rather than left for a funder to discover independently.
6. **No independent press coverage found** — unlike several other orgs in this series, all data on KABIL's programmatic claims traces back to KABIL's own reports/website or its funders' own confirmations (which is reassuring) rather than independent journalism — a funder seeking fully independent, third-party impact verification (e.g., an external evaluation) would need to request this directly, as none was found publicly.
