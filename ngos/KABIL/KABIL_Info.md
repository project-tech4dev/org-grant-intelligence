## History & Origin

KABIL was registered as a society in **December 2012**. It was founded by a group of seasoned development professionals:
- **Mr. Tapas Kumar Datta** (Chairperson)
- **Ms. Smita Rawat** (Vice Chairperson)
- **Mohd. Asif Zaidi** (Member)
- **Mr. Bismaya Mahapatra** (Member)
- **Dr. Dulal Chandra Goswami** (Member)
- **Ms. Jyanthi Gangana** (Member)
- **Dr. Partha Ranjan Das Gupta** (Member)
- **Mr. Thomas Mathew** (Treasurer)
- **Mr. Vijay Kumar Sardana** (Member)

**Founding Executive Director: Mr. Achintya Ghosh**, who led KABIL from its inception (Dec 2012) until **November 2025**. Verified biographical details (official KABIL site + PRADAN's own publications + Axis Bank Foundation's own Annual Report 2020-21, which independently lists him as "Achintya Ghosh, Executive Director, KABIL"):
- Joined **PRADAN (Professional Assistance For Development Action)** in **1983/1984**, and served in progressively senior roles there for **~28-29 years (until 2012)**, including as **Executive Director of PRADAN from 1997–2002** and Programme Director both before and after that term.
- Brought **35+ years of large-scale livelihood-promotion experience**, including international work in **Ethiopia and Kenya**.
- Alumnus of **IIT Kharagpur** — received the institute's **Distinguished Alumnus Award (2020)** for his contribution to rural poverty alleviation.
- Founded KABIL to build a **long-term institutional support system** (not one-time charity) that could deploy field-level "know-how" and "do-how," anchored via a regional office in Guwahati to specifically address Northeast India's rural challenges.
- Pioneered KABIL's early interventions: low-cost lift irrigation, protected cultivation (rain shelters) for high-value off-season crops, and livelihood-asset creation in convergence with MGNREGS.

**Notable governance-network link:** Current KABIL Governing Board member **Narendranath Damodaran** also served as **PRADAN's own Executive Director from 2017–2022** (per PRADAN's official "Who We Are" leadership timeline) — indicating KABIL's governance draws directly from the same professional network as its founder's alma mater institution, PRADAN.

## Vision, Mission & Core Objectives

- **Mission (per official website):** "To enable the most marginalized, especially rural women, to earn a dignified livelihood and take charge of their own lives."
- **Core issues KABIL addresses (as stated):**
  1. Focus on rural women, tribal communities, and **tea garden workers** — among the most economically vulnerable groups
  2. Tackling low-productivity livelihoods and seasonal income insecurity
  3. Bridging limited access to government schemes, financial services, and reliable markets
- **What makes KABIL different (as stated):**
  - Continuous handholding (not one-time training)
  - Blending tradition with technology (e.g., traditional **Dong-Bund water systems** combined with modern technology)
  - Convergence with government schemes (e.g., MGNREGS) to create durable livelihood assets
  - Context-specific strategies: non-farm/livestock-based livelihoods for marginal landholders vs. agriculture/horticulture-based interventions for households with more land
- **Core principles (as stated):** In-situ engagement; Know-how and do-how; Long-term commitment; Systemic approach
- **Core beliefs (as stated):** Human Dignity; Participatory Development; Ecological Harmony; Inclusion (Women, Tribals, Adivasis, and other vulnerable groups at the center); Knowledge as Power

### Strategic Framework — "The Livelihood Triangle"
KABIL's central strategic model, integrating three interdependent pillars simultaneously:
1. **People** — building capabilities, skills, confidence, and entrepreneurial mindset (especially women and youth)
2. **Resources** — equitable access to and sustainable management of land, water, forests; strengthening productive assets
3. **Institutions** — strengthening government systems, panchayats/VCDCs (Village Council Development Committees), SHG-VO-CLF networks, and markets

### Four Thematic Pillars (per official website)
1. **Livelihood Promotion** — building "know-how"/"do-how" in agriculture, livestock, non-farm enterprises; improving access to inputs, irrigation, technology; strengthening market/financial-service/governance linkages
2. **Natural Resource Management (NRM)** — soil and water conservation, water security systems, productive land development; combines community knowledge (e.g., Dong-Bund systems) with technical support and government programme leverage
3. **Social Mobilisation** — strengthening the **SHG-VO-CLF network** (Self-Help Group – Village Organisation – Cluster Level Federation), participatory planning, leadership development, and promoting **Producer Groups (PGs)**
4. **Health and Nutrition** *(newest/expanding pillar)* — focused on women and children, particularly in **tea garden communities**, targeting maternal/child health in the "first 1,000 days" to break the cycle of undernutrition

## Verification / Discrepancy Notes

1. **Founding year discrepancy:** The user-supplied data table states the organization was established "**~2000**." However, **every official source found — the organization's own website and its own Annual Reports — states registration occurred in December 2012**, and founder Achintya Ghosh's LinkedIn independently corroborates "Oct 2012 – Present." This is a **significant, ~12-year discrepancy** that could not be resolved. Possible explanations (not confirmed): the "~2000" figure may reflect when founder Achintya Ghosh's broader livelihood-promotion career began (he joined PRADAN in 1983, decades earlier) rather than KABIL's own founding; or it may be an error in the original data source. Flagged rather than silently corrected.
2. **Finance figures possibly duplicated in source PDFs:** Both the FY2022-23 and FY2023-24 Annual Reports state identical finance figures — "KABIL received grants amounting to Rs 169.84 lakh and allocated Rs 164.62 lakh towards its activities" — word-for-word identical across two different fiscal years. This is almost certainly a copy-paste artifact in KABIL's own report drafting rather than a genuine repeat, but it means **neither year's finance figure should be treated as fully reliable** without further corroboration (e.g., audited financial statements, which were not found publicly).
3. **12A/80G/FCRA status:** Could not be independently verified via public registries (no NGO Darpan ID, GuideStar listing, or Credibility Alliance record found for KABIL specifically). Given KABIL's substantiated track record of receiving grants from major institutional Foundations (Axis Bank Foundation, Azim Premji Foundation, Tata Trusts, SBI Foundation) and a Government body (BTC/BTR), it is highly plausible that 12A/80G are in place, but this remains **unconfirmed by direct evidence** rather than assumed.
