# KABIL — Funder Fit Scores (Rubric v1, Hand-Vetted)

Fit scores for KABIL against its four shortlisted funders, scored on the v1 funder–NGO fit rubric
and then **manually verified against the underlying funder research**. Every score below has been
re-read against the source evidence; where the evidence did not support the original score, the
score was revised and the reason recorded.

- **Rubric:** v1 — 7 criteria, 100 points
- **Scored:** AI-generated first pass
- **Hand-vetted:** 10 September 2026, single rater, desk verification
- **Source research:** `hand-vetting/` (funder profiles, fact-check, rubric)
- **Companion file:** `KABIL_Funder_Matching_Profile.md` (see the compliance correction in §5 below)

## 3. What the hand-vetting changed

Of 28 criterion scores, **23 were confirmed and 5 were revised.** All four original totals were
arithmetically correct — the errors were in reading the evidence, not adding it up. Ranking order
was unaffected, but the top of the table tightened and the strategic reading changed.

| Funder | Criterion | Was | Now | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ABF | Relationship | 10 | **7** | Scored as an "active, ongoing" partnership. The project was 4 years from April 2018 — it ended ~March 2022, and KABIL's own Annual Report 2022-23 lists it as **concluded**. Recently lapsed, not live. |
| SBIF | Relationship | 9 | **10** | The freshest live grant of the four — KABIL ran field training under SBIF-CONSERW Jaldhara on **11 June 2026**, against APF's February 2026 evaluation visit. |
| Tata | Eligibility | 12 | **15** | Conflated compliance with whether the application route is open. Tata Trusts is not Section-135-bound, so the CSR-1 gap does not apply here at all — KABIL's compliance position is *stronger* here than with ABF, not weaker. |
| Tata | Geography | 12 | **10** | **NEIDA**, Tata's nodal North East *livelihoods* agency, covers Nagaland, Arunachal and Mizoram — **not Assam**. Tata's Assam work is education (in Baksa, Bongaigaon, Goalpara, Nalbari — not Udalguri) and cancer care. |
| Tata | Funding Size | 6 | **5** | SDTT small grants cover only **80–90% of project cost** (NGO co-finances the rest), and Tata's scale-up is explicitly going to *fewer, much larger* flagship projects. |

Tata Trusts' total is unchanged at 64, but three of its seven scores moved — the original total was
right by coincidence.

## 4. Per-funder findings

### Axis Bank Foundation — 93/100

The strongest thematic match of the four and the only perfect 25/25: ABF's flagship Sustainable
Livelihoods Programme (NRM, agriculture, livestock, SHG collectivisation, skill development) maps
almost exactly onto KABIL's core work. Geography, target population and funding structure all score
full or near-full marks. ABF's capacity is growing steeply — **₹91 cr (FY20-21) → ₹427 cr
(FY24-25), roughly 4.7x in four years**.

The single deduction that matters is **relationship**: this is a lapsed grant, not a live one. The
renewal case is strong — the project **delivered 24,765 families against a 15,000 target**, roughly
65% over — and that over-delivery is the argument to lead with in any re-approach.

Compliance costs 4 points: ABF is Section-135-bound, and KABIL's CSR-1 shows as "Not Available"
(see §5).

### Azim Premji Foundation — 90/100

The most flexible funder of the four and the best all-round compliance fit. APF is a private
foundation funded by a personal endowment, **not** a Section-135 CSR arm, so the CSR-1 gate does not
apply — which is why it scores highest on eligibility (18/20). Northeast India is an explicitly named
grant-making priority, and a fourth university campus is planned for the region.

Terms are unusually accommodating: **1–10 year grants, most 3–4 years**, sizes from a few lakh to a
few tens of crores, and APF will make **corpus/core grants**, not only project grants. That last
point is rare among KABIL's funders and worth pursuing deliberately.

Thematic alignment is capped at 20/25 only because school education, not livelihoods, remains APF's
flagship. Capacity is strong — total spend **₹1,528 cr → ₹3,206 cr in two years**.

### SBI Foundation — 87/100

The **most recently evidenced live grant** — SBIF-CONSERW Jaldhara, with KABIL field training
documented as recently as June 2026 — hence the only 10/10 for relationship in this table.

SBIF is also the only one of the four that **publishes an itemised eligibility checklist**
(12AA/80G/FCRA plus a minimum 3-year track record), and KABIL clears every published criterion
comfortably. The 15/20 reflects the statutory CSR-1 gate plus SBIF's specific request for Asset &
Liability statements, against which no public audited financials for KABIL were found.

Thematic alignment caps at 20/25: environment/water (the vertical funding KABIL) is real and
growing, but health takes ~40% of SBIF's spend. Grant band ₹50 lakh – ₹5 crore over 1–3 years.

### Tata Trusts — 64/100

The clear outlier, and the relationship most in need of direct clarification rather than a proposal.

The only evidence linking KABIL to Tata Trusts is a **cached snippet from a page that now returns
404**, naming **Sir Dorabji Tata Trust** — a different entity from the "Tata Trusts" that KABIL's
own website lists as a current funder — and dating to KABIL's 2013-14 founding year. No project,
amount, or recent date was found. Under a Linkage–Ability–Interest reading this is a cold-to-lukewarm
prospect despite the website listing.

Fit is genuinely weaker too, not just less documented: livelihoods/NRM is a secondary vertical for
Tata Trusts, its North East livelihoods agency does not cover Assam, and its current scale-up is
flowing into large flagship institutions rather than mid-size NGO grants.

The counterweight is capacity — disbursements are on track to roughly triple-and-a-half from
₹575 cr (FY23-24) to a ₹2,000 cr target (FY26-27), and the Trusts are shifting **from direct
implementation toward grant-making**, which structurally favours external partners like KABIL.
Enough reason to clarify the relationship before writing it off.

## 5. Compliance correction

`KABIL_Funder_Matching_Profile.md` in this folder records KABIL's FCRA status as
**"Not found/confirmed anywhere"** and 12A/80G as unverified. **That is incorrect.** Registration
numbers were subsequently located and are recorded in `hand-vetting/KABIL_Fact_Check_Verified.md`:

| Item | Status | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Legal form | ✅ Confirmed | Registered Society, Societies Registration Act | rcrc.in |
| Registration date | ✅ Confirmed | 21 December 2012 (13+ years) | rcrc.in; kabilindia.org |
| Registration No. | ✅ Confirmed | SOCIETY/WEST/2012/8900710 | give.do; myngos.in |
| PAN | ✅ Confirmed | AACTK0965G | give.do |
| 12A | ✅ Confirmed | AACTK0965GE20171 | give.do |
| 80G | ✅ Confirmed | AACTK0965GF20138 | give.do |
| **FCRA** | ✅ **Confirmed** | **231661741** | give.do |
| CSR-1 | ⚠️ "Not Available" | — | give.do (single source, not checked against MCA) |
| NGO Darpan ID | ❌ Not retrieved | — | Portal is CAPTCHA-gated |
| Audited financials | ❌ Not public | — | — |

This matters beyond bookkeeping: the earlier profile's claim would have ruled out **every foreign
and international funder** for an organisation that is in fact FCRA-registered.

**CSR-1 is the one real open flag.** It costs KABIL points against both Section-135-bound funders
(ABF and SBIF), it rests on a single third-party aggregator, and it was never checked against the
official MCA register. It may already be resolved. It is the highest-value item to verify.

## 7. Limitations

- **Single rater**, desk verification only. A second scorer, or a session with KABIL's own team,
  would improve reliability.
- The vetting pass re-read scores against the existing research files; it **did not re-fetch primary
  sources**. It catches misreading of evidence, not errors inside the evidence.
- **No KABIL-specific grant amount** is public for any of the four funders. All size and duration
  figures are funder-wide bands or project durations.
- Rubric **v1 only.** A v2 (sub-criteria) draft exists in `hand-vetting/` and has not been vetted
  or applied.
