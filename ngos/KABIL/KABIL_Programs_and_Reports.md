# KABIL — Programs & Reports Deep-Dive

This file documents KABIL's projects/programmes in depth, drawing on the official website and **three full Annual Reports (FY2022-23, FY2023-24, FY2024-25)** downloaded directly from KABIL's official site and saved locally as PDFs:
- `KABIL_Annual_Report_2022-23.pdf`
- `KABIL_Annual_Report_2023-24.pdf`
- `KABIL_Annual_Report_2024-25.pdf`

Unlike several other organizations researched in this series, **KABIL publishes genuine, detailed, multi-year Annual Reports with real activity-level metrics** — this allows for actual year-over-year trend analysis rather than single undated snapshots.

## 1. Sustainable Livelihoods for Rural Poor — Udalguri & Harisinga Blocks (Axis Bank Foundation)
- **Status:** **Concluded** (4-year project; no-cost extension ran April–June 2022; fully closed out in FY2022-23 report)
- **Description:** A 4-year sustainable-livelihoods project across "undivided Udalguri" block (Udalguri + Harisinga), working with both SHG and non-SHG families through agriculture and livestock interventions, and Individual Beneficiary Schemes (IBS) under MGNREGS (e.g., farm ponds).
- **Sector:** Livelihood / Agriculture & Livestock / MGNREGS Convergence
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1 (No Poverty), SDG 2 (Zero Hunger), SDG 8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth)
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied — Agriculture, Livestock; 🔴 Informal transitioning toward credit/scheme-linked livelihoods
- **Geography (as stated):** 20 Village Council Development Committees (VCDCs, equivalent to gram panchayats), Udalguri and Harisinga blocks, Udalguri district, Assam
- **Named Funders:** **Axis Bank Foundation (ABF)**
- **Target groups:** Rural poor households, SHG and non-SHG families
- **Metric Trend Across Years:**
  - Final cumulative reach: **24,765 net families** (also cited as "~24,000 families" in the following year's report)
  - FY2021-22 (final full year): 499 MGNREGS IBS plans sanctioned and implemented; 20 farm ponds constructed under MGNREGS
- **Open Questions:** No breakdown of the 24,765 families by specific activity type (agriculture vs. livestock vs. MGNREGS-only); no cost-per-family or total grant amount specifically attributed to this project (KABIL's finance reporting is organization-wide, not project-disaggregated in the reports reviewed).

## 3. DWIMU — "Diversification with Integrated Management for the Upliftment of People's Lives" (formerly "TLLB — Transforming Lives and Livelihoods in Bodoland")
- **Status:** **Active** — sanctioned/launched July 2024, described as a "high-impact," 4-year project
- **Description:** A large-scale livelihood and government-scheme-convergence project spanning Udalguri, Tamulpur, and Baska districts within the Bodoland Territorial Region (BTR). Involves block-level orientation meetings, Participatory Rural Appraisal (PRA) exercises producing 5 planning maps (Social Map, Water Resource Map, Land Use Land Cover Map, Land Classification Map, Flood Map) plus a Proposed Activity Map, and deep convergence work across multiple government line departments.
- **Sector:** Livelihood / Multi-sectoral Government Scheme Convergence / NRM
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 2, SDG 8, SDG 15 (Life on Land — via NRM/forest department work)
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied — Agriculture, Horticulture, Animal Husbandry, Fisheries; 🔴 Informal transitioning toward institutionally-linked livelihoods
- **Geography (as stated):** 20 VCDCs, 105 villages, across Udalguri, Tamulpur, and Baska districts, BTR, Assam
- **Named Funders:** **Azim Premji Foundation (APF)** (primary funder); **Bharat Rural Livelihoods Foundation (BRLF)** (technical support, district & state level); **Bodoland Territorial Region (BTR) Government** (collaborating government partner). **Independently confirmed via BRLF's own official Facebook page:** "On 13th June 2025, ASRLM signed an MoU with Bharat Rural Livelihoods Foundation (BRLF) to implement the DWIMU (Diversification with Integrated...)" — this cross-confirms the project from BRLF's own side and adds the **Assam State Rural Livelihood Mission (ASRLM)** as a formal government partner via tripartite MoU, not previously named in KABIL's own Annual Report text for this project. **BRLF itself is a Government-of-India-established entity** (formed by Union Cabinet decision, 3 September 2013, under the Ministry of Rural Development) — meaning DWIMU has substantial government backing, not purely private-foundation funding.
- **Target groups:** 10,000 households — specifically **ST, SC, and other vulnerable communities**
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** Single-year data so far (launched July 2024, reported in FY2024-25 Annual Report):
  - **Target: 10,000 households**, 20 VCDCs, 105 villages
  - Government department convergence achieved in first reporting year:
    - **Agriculture Dept:** ~4,000 households' vegetable/horticulture plans submitted; farmers being enrolled under PM-KISAN
    - **Forest Dept:** 356 households completed sapling transplanting (Package of Practices) under "Amrit Brikshya Andolan"
    - **Animal Husbandry & Veterinary Dept:** ~250 households covered for PPR (Peste des Petits Ruminants) vaccination; hatchery-unit access being arranged for ~200 households; swine fever vaccination list being compiled
    - **Irrigation Dept:** Engaged to form/strengthen Water User Associations for canal maintenance and equitable water distribution
    - **Fishery Dept:** Training organized for farmers with MGNREGS-excavated fish ponds
- **Open Questions:** No mid-project outcome data yet (household income change, adoption rates) — project is still in the institutional-convergence/planning phase as of the most recent report; "DWIMU" name change from "TLLB" is confirmed but the reason for the rename is not stated.

## 5. Teagarden Wellbeing Project — Dooars Region, Jalpaiguri District, West Bengal (Oak Foundation)
- **Status:** Active — launched **October 2022**, planned as a 4-year initiative
- **Description:** Livelihood and wellbeing project for tea garden workers in **closed and sick tea gardens** — a distinctly vulnerable population working outside KABIL's core Assam geography. Activities include SHG formation/strengthening, MGNREGS convergence, and diversified agriculture/livestock activities.
- **Sector:** Livelihood / Tea Garden Worker Welfare / MGNREGS Convergence
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 2, SDG 8, SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities — tea garden workers are a historically marginalized labor population)
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied — Black Pepper, Vegetable Cultivation, Ginger & Turmeric, Fish Cultivation, Pigsty/Goat rearing; Plantation/Tea-estate labor (target population's primary occupation)
- **Geography (as stated):** 4 tea gardens — **Charmuchi (Chamurchi), Katalguri, Red Bank, and Surendernagar (Surendranagar)** — in Banarhat block, Jalpaiguri district, West Bengal (Dooars region, foothills of the Eastern Himalayas), ~65 km from Jalpaiguri town
- **Named Funders:** **Oak Foundation**
- **Target groups:** 2,500 families from closed/sick tea gardens
- **Metric Trend Across Years:**
  | Metric | FY2022-23 (launch year) | FY2023-24 | FY2024-25 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | SHGs engaged | 51 SHGs (4 tea gardens) | 109 SHGs (89 in planning/implementation/monitoring; 86 with active plans/supervision) | 111 SHGs |
  | Total Net Families | — | 436 | 561 |
  | MGNREGS Annual Action Plan activities | — | 348 | 348 |
  | SHG/PRI members trained on MGNREGS | — | 19 | 19 |
  | Black Pepper plantation | — | 57 | 94 |
  | Vermi Compost preparation | — | 23 | 26 |
  | Vegetable cultivation | — | 101 (also "73 families" cited separately for a specific winter-vegetable seed initiative) | 188 |
  | Ginger & Turmeric cultivation | — | 14 | 95 |
  | Fish Cultivation | — | 61 | 61 |
  | Low-cost Pigsty model | — | 127 | 127 |
  | Low-cost Goat Shelter model | — | 1 (trial) | 1 |
  | Livestock Vaccination & Azolla Cultivation | — | 225 | 225 |
  | Small water harvesting tanks | — | — | 66 |
- **Key implementation detail (FY2022-23):** 544 households' homestead-garden-development schemes were geo-tagged (latitude/longitude + photos) for MGNREGS Annual Action Plan submission, in coordination with the Executive Assistant of Banarhat II GP and the Nirman Sahayak of Chamurchi GP.
- **Key setback (FY2024-25):** **MGNREGS implementation stalled statewide in West Bengal due to a Centre-State dispute** — 544 sanctioned schemes could not be implemented as a direct result. This is a significant, honestly-reported external/political risk factor affecting this specific project. Separately, 6 SHGs were in the process of negotiating bank loans to compensate via alternative financing.
- **Open Questions:** No update yet on how the Centre-State MGNREGS dispute in West Bengal was ultimately resolved or whether it affected the project's ability to hit its 2,500-family target by the end of the 4-year term (which would be ~2026).

## 6. Rainwater Harvesting for Agricultural Livelihoods via MGNREGS — Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh & Rajasthan
- **Status:** Active — ongoing since **2019** (Tikamgarh, MP), expanded to partner-NGO-supported states from **2022** onward
- **Description:** Water-resource-development project leveraging MGNREGS Individual Beneficiary Schemes (IBS) to create farm ponds/water-harvesting tanks for small farmers, alongside capacity-building for NGO partners and government officials on accessing MGNREGS funds. Distinctive as KABIL's only major initiative entirely **outside** the Northeast/Assam region and delivered mostly through **technical assistance to partner NGOs** rather than direct implementation.
- **Sector:** Natural Resource Management (NRM) / Water Security / MGNREGS Technical Assistance
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 2 (Zero Hunger), SDG 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation), SDG 13 (Climate Action — climate-resilient agriculture)
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied — Water Resource Management, Pisciculture
- **Geography (as stated):** **Tikamgarh district, Madhya Pradesh** (Tikamgarh & Ballabhgarh blocks, 5 panchayats each); also **Sonbhadra, Prayagraj, Varanasi, Hamirpur, Jhansi, Mahoba (Uttar Pradesh)**; **Karauli, Kotra/Udaipur (Rajasthan)**
- **Named Funders/Partners:** Initiated **"at the invitation of Tikamgarh District Administration, MP"**; delivered in partnership with **4 (unnamed) NGOs** and youth volunteers in MP/UP/Rajasthan from 2022 onward; works with district fishery and agriculture departments
- **Target groups:** Small and marginal farmers
- **Metric Trend Across Years:**
  | Metric | FY2022-23 | FY2023-24 | FY2024-25 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Waterbodies/tanks created or planned | 35 waterbodies (Tikamgarh) | 71 schemes developed jointly; 60 schemes estimated (~₹120 lakh); 34 schemes approved for execution (~₹68 lakh) | 60 tanks estimated/planned (via MGNREGS "SECURE" portal) |
  | Funds leveraged | ₹70 lakh (Tikamgarh) | ~₹68 lakh approved (of ~₹120 lakh estimated) | Not stated |
- **Notable outcome named specifically:** An "Amrit Sarovar" (a large MGNREGS-linked water body under the national Amrit Sarovar Mission) was excavated in Tikamgarh, MP, visited by the Janpad CEO.
- **Open Questions:** The 4 partner NGOs in MP/UP/Rajasthan are never named in any of the 3 reports reviewed; no cumulative multi-year total for waterbodies created (each year's figures appear to describe that year's specific activity/pipeline rather than a running cumulative total, making true trend analysis difficult).

## Master List of Open Questions / Data Gaps

1. Founding-year discrepancy (~2000 per original query vs. December 2012 per every official source) — unresolved (see Info.md).
2. Finance figures identical across FY2022-23 and FY2023-24 reports — likely a drafting error, casts some doubt on the reliability of either year's cited figure.
3. No FCRA/12A/80G registration numbers found publicly, despite strong indirect evidence (Foundation-grade funders) that some tax-exemption status must be in place.
4. Relationship between the "Promoting Livelihoods... Mazbat Block" (Programme 4) FY2024-25 line item and the previously-concluded APF Mazbat project (Programme 2) is unclear — possible renewal/rebrand, not explicitly stated.
5. The 4 partner NGOs supporting MP/UP/Rajasthan rainwater-harvesting work are never named across any of the 3 annual reports.
6. No cumulative, reconciled "total unique beneficiaries across all KABIL history" figure exists — the homepage's "26,000+ SHG Households" is the closest official aggregate figure, but it's not clear if this fully captures non-SHG beneficiaries counted separately in some projects (e.g., the ABF project's "SHG and Non-SHG families").
7. No independent/press coverage of KABIL was found during this research (unlike other organizations researched in this series) — all data derives from KABIL's own official website, its own Annual Reports, and LinkedIn/professional profiles of its founder and partner organizations. This is not necessarily a red flag (many credible, effective grassroots NGOs receive little press), but it means there is less independent triangulation available for KABIL's claims compared to some other profiles in this research series.
