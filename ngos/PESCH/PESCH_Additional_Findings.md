# PESCH — Additional Findings (Beyond the Organization Profile & Programs Files)

> This is a **new, separate file** capturing information found *after* `PESCH_Organization_Profile.md` and `PESCH_Programs.md` were first written — it does not repeat what's already in those two files, except where new evidence directly updates/corrects something stated there (clearly flagged below). See those two files for the core organizational and program-area picture.

## 1. MAJOR FINDING: Systematic Recycling of Boilerplate Text and Figures Across Annual Reports

This is the single most important new discovery in this pass, and it significantly strengthens (with direct proof) the "recycled boilerplate" concern already flagged in `PESCH_Programs.md`.

**The FY2020-21 Annual Report and the FY2023-24 Annual Report contain a virtually word-for-word identical narrative passage describing an "Indigenous Cotton" training session at Sibilong village — with only the year changed:**

- **FY2020-21 report:** *"Meeting with SHGs on Promotion of Indigenous cotton. On: 21st April 2020, Venue: Sibilong... The meeting was attended by 33 women where all of them are member of SHGs and 4 men. The meeting started with a prayer pronounced by Mr. Gaichiandin, VDC member which was followed by welcome address from Mr. Meithailung, VDC secretary. The Resource person Mr. Namkhinlung Pamei, Director of PESCH speaks on the topic revival of indigenous cotton farming... vote of thanks from Khiulingliu, president of Tidai Phuam SHG."*
- **FY2023-24 report:** The *exact same sentence structure, same names (Gaichiandin, Meithailung, Namkhinlung Pamei, Khiulingliu), same attendance count (33 women + 4 men), same venue (Sibilong)* — with only the date changed to **"21st April 2023."**

The same pattern repeats for the broader "Improved Quality and Dignity of Human Life" project narrative — the bullet-point list of achievements ("150 youth participated in sport day at Sibilong"; "75 people participated awareness at Oinamlong, Nungkao"; "25 people participated training on rights and entitlements"; "6 homesteads and 1 horticulture field... 7 women SHG land mapped"; "6 Land Tenure Certificates issued"; "12 people bee-keeping training"; "20 women mushroom training, 3 women of Kaimai practicing"; "15 women traditional crops," "5 women homestead," "4 women horticulture") appears **identically** in both the FY2020-21 and FY2023-24 reports.

**Assessment:** This is no longer just a "possible boilerplate reuse" hypothesis (as cautiously flagged in `PESCH_Programs.md`) — it is now **directly confirmed** that at least one entire narrative section, complete with specific named individuals and headcounts, was copy-pasted across two annual reports three years apart, with only the year changed. This means:
- Any single-year metric drawn from these specific recycled passages (the cotton-training attendance figures, the "150/75/25 people" entitlements bullets, the land-mapping figures) **should not be treated as a genuine, independently-measured FY2023-24 result** — it is very likely leftover FY2020-21 text that was never updated.
- This casts a shadow over the reliability of narrative (non-tabular) sections specifically. By contrast, the **Target & Achievement table** (found uniquely in the FY2020-21 report, not observed in FY2023-24) and the **audited financial statements** (see below) appear to be genuinely period-specific, since their numbers are internally consistent and tied to dated bank transactions.
- **Open question for direct follow-up with PESCH:** How much of the narrative content across all 13 annual reports is templated/reused? This finding suggests a need for real caution before treating any single narrative figure as reliable without independent corroboration (e.g., from the financial statements or the Target/Achievement tables).

## 2. A Third, Distinct "Villages/Households Served" Figure — Building a Real Growth Trend Line

`PESCH_Programs.md` already noted that FY2021-22 and FY2023-24 both state "62 villages / 7,800 households" (likely the same recycled text), while FY2022-23 states a different "57 villages / 7,113 households." This pass found a **fourth data point, from FY2020-21**, which — being chronologically earliest — actually helps establish a plausible real trend:

| Fiscal Year | Villages | Households | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2020-21 | **41** | **2,301** | Distinct figure, plausible organic baseline |
| FY2021-22 | 62 | 7,800 | |
| FY2022-23 | 57 | 7,113 | Distinct figure — lower than FY2021-22 |
| FY2023-24 | 62 | 7,800 | **Identical text to FY2021-22 — likely recycled, not a real FY2023-24 measurement** |

**Interpretation:** A genuine growth story from 41→62 villages and 2,301→7,800 households between FY2020-21 and FY2021-22 is plausible (COVID-era program expansion, new grants like the GGF cotton project and NABARD TDF scaling up). The FY2022-23 dip to 57 villages/7,113 households could reflect a real contraction (e.g., project-village rotation as one grant cycle ends and another begins) or a more conservative/accurate recount. The FY2023-24 figure should be treated as **unknown** (not genuinely 62/7,800) given the boilerplate-reuse finding above.

## 3. Target vs. Achievement Table (FY2020-21) — The Only Place PESCH Reports Shortfalls, Not Just Successes

The FY2020-21 report contains a detailed, ~37-row **"Target & Achievements"** table for the "Improved Quality and Dignity of Human Life" project — a level of self-critical, target-vs-actual transparency **not found in any other PESCH report section reviewed**, since narrative sections in other reports only describe what was accomplished, not what was planned/missed. Selected highlights (Target → Achievement):

| Activity | Target | Achievement | Gap? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formation of VDCs | 3 | 3 | Met |
| Formation of SHGs | 6 | 6 | Met |
| Sports meet | 1 | 1 | Met |
| Social audits in project villages | 8 | 6 | **Shortfall** |
| Participation in regional/national/international land conferences | 2 | 1 | **Shortfall** |
| Advocacy/lobbying on land tenancy policy | 2 | 1 | **Shortfall** |
| Village registry development with land tenure certificates | 3 | 2 | **Shortfall** |
| **Support for progressive pig farmers** | **40** | **10** | **Large shortfall (25% of target)** |
| Capacity building of SHG federations/FPO | 4 | 3 | **Shortfall** |
| **Convergence with NABARD/financial institutions for credit linkage, storage, market yard, etc.** | **4** | **1** | **Large shortfall (25% of target)** |
| **Market linkage & value addition of 1 key SHG product** | **1** | **0** | **Target completely missed** |
| Awareness/capacity building on sustainable NRM, community conservation, biodiversity register, etc. | 4 | 1 | **Large shortfall (25%)** |
| Fishery development (bio-flock) | 3 | 1 | **Shortfall** |
| Water Resources Development convergence | 3 | 1 | **Shortfall** |
| **Convergence with NABARD/MGNREGS/DRDA/ICAR/SFURTI for forest-food regeneration plan** | **10** | **2** | **Large shortfall (20% of target)** |
| Traditional crop farming (black rice, taro, beans) | 55 | 55 | Met |
| Homestead system for landless farmers (goat/poultry) | 15 | 15 | Met |
| Support for women beneficiaries in cotton farming | 100 | 100 | Met |
| Support for women beneficiaries in yarn-making/value addition | 50 | 50 | Met |
| ASER survey villages | 30 | 4 | **Large shortfall** |

**Assessment:** This table reveals a genuine, candid pattern: **direct beneficiary-facing activities tied to simple training/farming targets (SHG formation, crop distribution, cotton/yarn support) were consistently met at or near 100%**, while **institutional convergence targets requiring coordination with external government/financial bodies (NABARD credit linkage, multi-agency forest-regeneration convergence, market-linkage/value-addition, ASER survey scale) were consistently and substantially under-achieved** — often at only 20-25% of target. This is valuable, credible evidence of PESCH's genuine capacity constraints (as the org's own Director's Message repeatedly acknowledges: "shortage of fund, Lack of Knowledge, Lack of Human resources, Lack of cooperation") rather than a purely promotional self-report.

## 4. Real Audited Financial Statements — The First Genuine Financial Figures Obtained in This Entire Research Project

Unlike every other NGO researched previously in this project (SNEHPAD, Diya Foundation, MOSONiE, Projonmo — where financial figures were either entirely absent or embedded in non-extractable image/table sections), **PESCH's FY2020-21 Audited Statement (by S.L. Gangwal & Company, Chartered Accountants, Imphal) was successfully read in full**, giving genuine, itemized financial data for the first time in this project. (Note: the FY2021-22 Audited Statement was also downloaded but turned out to be a scanned/image-only PDF with no extractable text — only FY2020-21 could be read.)

**Key figures, FY2020-21 (year ended 31 March 2021), all amounts approximate due to some OCR ambiguity in the source PDF's column layout:**

- **Total Grant in Aid received (Annexure D):** ~₹18,15,100 combining:
  - **Foreign contribution, RNBA/EED-Brot Project:** ₹9,42,100 (routed through the **FCRA-designated SBI New Delhi Main Branch account** and a Central Bank of India FCRA account)
  - **Local funding, NABARD Programme (FPO):** ₹2,73,000
  - **Local funding, Gramya Vikas Nidhi (Kaimai village):** ₹6,00,000
- **Expenditure by programme:**
  - **RNBA/EED-Brot Programme:** ₹9,63,286.65 — including **Personnel Cost ₹5,98,824** (by far the single largest line item — i.e., the majority of this grant funds staff salaries), Administration Cost ₹36,172.65, Procurement ₹1,34,800, and a **COVID-19 Relief Programme line item of ₹88,500**.
  - **NABARD Programme:** ₹13,81,631 — dominated by **Rural Haat construction at Kaimai village** (materials/vehicle ₹8,13,196 + electrification ₹6,070 + inauguration ₹10,734 + labour/"Mistry" charges ₹1,70,000 ≈ ₹10,00,196 combined) plus FPO registration/administration (~₹1,50,631) and SHG promotion via "Sangai & Orange Festival" (₹44,000).
  - **Global Green Grants Fund (GGF) Programme (Indigenous Cotton):** ₹4,89,144.15 — covering cotton-farming promotion (₹2,37,720), processing/value-addition (₹1,27,645), a field coordinator's honorarium (₹1,00,000), plus travel and admin/audit costs.
  - **Beti Bachao Beti Padhao Programme:** ₹50,000 (fooding/lodging ₹8,300 + travel ₹41,700).
  - **Office building construction:** ₹5,00,000 (a capital expenditure, funded via "founder village contributions" per the Chairman's narrative in later reports).
- **Bottom line:** The organization recorded an **"Excess of Expenditure over Income" (deficit) of ₹9,19,838.16** for FY2020-21, transferred against the Capital Fund (closing Capital Fund balance: ₹13,26,775.52) — largely explained by the ₹5,00,000 office-building capital outlay plus the accounting treatment of programme advances.
- **Bank accounts on record:** Multiple Union Bank of India (UBI) accounts (general, OSAAT, NABARD/FPO, local contribution), a Central Bank of India (CBI) account explicitly labelled "Another FCRA account," and an **SBI New Delhi Main Branch account labelled "Main FCRA account"** — this is concrete, primary-source confirmation that **PESCH does hold active FCRA (Foreign Contribution Regulation Act) registration**, resolving that category of uncertainty that had been flagged for a different NGO (Projonmo) in this project's earlier research and is now positively confirmed for PESCH specifically.
- **Fixed assets on the books (net of depreciation, as of 31 March 2021):** ~₹11.6 lakh, including land, the office building under construction, a four-wheeler vehicle ("Bolero LX"), a two-wheeler, a solar power-backup system, a computer/printer, and an LCD projector.
- **Audit caveat (explicitly stated by the auditor):** Due to the **COVID-19 lockdown in Manipur**, "the scope of Audit was curtailed to a large extent and accordingly physical verification and third-party confirmation were not possible to a large extent" — the auditor relied substantially on management's oral/written representations. This is a meaningful, self-disclosed limitation on the reliability of this specific year's audit.

## 5. Newly-Discovered Document Archives (Confirmed to Exist, Not Yet Fully Read)

Beyond the 13 Annual Reports already noted in `PESCH_Organization_Profile.md`, the website hosts two additional, even deeper archives:

- **Audited Statements: 19 years available — FY2003-04 through FY2022-23** (a longer run than the Annual Reports archive itself, which starts at FY2006-07). Only FY2020-21 has been read in full so far (see above); FY2021-22 was attempted but is image-only/unreadable; the remaining 17 years are downloaded-but-unread or entirely unfetched.
- **FCRA documents: FY2006-07 through FY2022-23** (labelled "Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act — audited accounts" on the site), confirming a long, continuous history of foreign-funding compliance filings.

This means PESCH's true documentary depth is **13 Annual Reports + up to 19 Audited Statements + up to 17 years of FCRA filings** — a combined archive far exceeding anything else found in this research project. Systematically reading the full Audited Statement run in a future pass would likely allow reconstruction of a genuine, verifiable multi-decade income/expenditure trend line for the organization — something not yet available for any other NGO in this project.

## 6. Granular Village-Institution Data (FY2020-21) — Named SHG and VDC Membership Rolls

The FY2020-21 report includes **complete, named membership rosters** for several Self-Help Groups and Village Development Committees formed that year — a level of micro-detail not seen in the FY2022-23/23-24 reports reviewed (which describe SHG/VDC formation only in aggregate counts). Examples:
- **Rangkekiulong village:** "Teikhuang SHG" (9 named members, President: Sangjuanliu Rongmei) and "Raengdai SHG" (14 named members); a Village Development Committee (VDC) with 10 named members (Chairperson: Gaithaodimliu Kamei).
- **Nungkao-II village:** "Chunchamphuam SHG" and "Machumphuam SHG" (10 named members each); a VDC with 13 named members (Chairperson: Hanbiliu).
- **Oinamlong-II village:** "Gaiphuam SHG" and "Circle of Friends SHG" (10+ named members each); a VDC with 9 named members (Chairperson: Gailoliu).

**Formation process detail (dated, with named facilitators):** VDC/SHG formation meetings were held on **20, 25, and 27 May 2020** across the three villages, each hosted at a named individual's residence or the community hall, and facilitated either by PESCH's own coordinators (**D. Puanthanlung, G. Gaihiamliu**) or by **trained local volunteers** (**Ms. Pricilla Kamei** at Nungkao-II, **Ms. Sathuiliu Kamei** at Oinamlong-II) — indicating PESCH uses a "barefoot facilitator" model, training village residents to run formation meetings in their own communities rather than relying solely on outside staff. Baseline/PRA (Participatory Rural Appraisal) data collection is also documented as village-specific: 28 households surveyed at Rangkekiulong, 51 at Nungkao-II, and 170 at Oinamlong-II.

**Open question:** Why does Oinamlong-II's baseline cover 170 households when the whole "Improved Quality and Dignity of Human Life" project (across all its villages) is elsewhere described as reaching only a few hundred households in total? This may indicate Oinamlong-II's 170-household baseline count is a full village census (not all of whom become active project participants), rather than a project-beneficiary count — worth clarifying.

## 7. Confirmed Project Naming/Timeline Detail Not Previously Captured

- The flagship **"Improved Quality and Dignity of Human Life"** project is explicitly described in the FY2020-21 report as **"New phase-I," a sixth-year programme running April 2017 – March 2024** — i.e., an 7-year (not 6-year, despite the report's own "sixth year" phrasing, which appears to be an internal inconsistency) programme cycle. The FY2023-24 report describes the same project as covering **April 2017 – March 2026**, meaning **the project's own end-date was extended by two years at some point between the FY2020-21 and FY2023-24 reports** (from a stated 2024 end to a 2026 end) — a substantive, previously unrecorded change to the project's duration/funding cycle.
- In FY2020-21, the project covered **5 villages** in Tousem Block; by FY2023-24, this had grown to **6 villages** — a real, incremental expansion (consistent with, and lending credibility to, the overall organizational growth narrative, even where specific headline village/household totals are unreliable due to boilerplate reuse).
