# People's Endeavour for Social Changes (PESCH) — Program Areas

> This document is updated continuously as new information is found. See `PESCH_Organization_Profile.md` for organization identity, leadership, and governance history.
>
> **This is, by a wide margin, the richest primary-source archive found across all Northeast India NGOs researched in this project.** PESCH's website hosts **13 real, working Annual Reports (FY2006-07 through FY2023-24)**. Three of the most recent (FY2023-24 in full, FY2022-23 and FY2021-22 partially) have been read so far, yielding genuinely granular, dated, named, and quantified program data — individual training session dates, venues, named facilitators, and attendance headcounts are routinely recorded. This is a marked contrast to SNEHPAD, Diya Foundation, and Projonmo, where broken report links forced reliance on undated website marketing text.

---

## The Five Official Programme Areas (per pesch.org.in/our-work)

### 1. Institution Building
- **Status:** Active, foundational to all other work
- **Description:** Forming and strengthening Self-Help Groups (SHGs), village-level federations, Farmers Producer Organizations (FPOs), youth clubs, and Village Planning Committees (VPCs) — with capacity-building on governance, women's leadership, market linkages, and youth participation in village governance and farming.
- **Sector:** Institutional Capacity Building (enabling)
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 5, SDG 16, SDG 17
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None — institutional/enabling
- **Geography (as stated):** Across all PESCH project villages in Tousem Block (Tamenglong district) and Jiribam district, Manipur, and Cachar district, Assam
- **Named Funders/Partners:** NABARD (FPO formation); MSRLM (Manipur State Rural Livelihood Mission, SHG linkage); Brot für die Welt ("Bread for the World") via RNBA
- **Metric Trend Across Years (now with real figures):**
  - **FY2022-23:** 17 SHGs formed/strengthened and linked to MSRLM; 27 SHGs given training on account keeping/income generation; 1 FPO (Jiri-Barak Farmers Producer Company Ltd.) mobilized **522 shareholder-members**; 20 Udyan Vikas Samiti (Garden Development Committees) formed in TDF project villages; 1 Rural Haat under construction.
  - **FY2023-24:** 3 youth clubs revived/strengthened (Nungkao-II, Rangkekiulong, Oinamlong-II — each with dated, named meetings and attendance: e.g., Nungkao-II meeting on 14 Sept 2023 drew 20 male + 15 female attendees); Jiri-Barak Farmer Producer Company continued operations (registered under Companies Act 2013, CIN U01100MN2020PTC014130, originally registered 25 August 2020); 2 Rural Haats **completed and handed over** to Women SHGs via newly-formed Rural Haat Management Committees, at **Sibilong and Kaimai** villages (NABARD-funded).
- **The Jiri-Barak Farmers Producer Company Limited** (a flagship institutional outcome): registered under the Companies Act 2013; deals in hill broom, areca-leaf disposable plates, sugandhmantri (a medicinal/aromatic root), orange nursery saplings, dry turmeric, vegetables, smokeless king chilli, dalchini (cinnamon), and indigenous cotton. FY2023-24 activities: 5 mobilization meetings across 5 villages (120 farmers participated); 3 Board of Directors trainings (covering SALT farming/green business, basic FPO/business development via an online BIRD — Bankers Institute of Rural Development — session, and horticulture, delivered respectively by named resource persons **David Gandhi**, an online BIRD trainer, and **Dhiren**, Horticulture Inspector, Jiribam); 2 CEO trainings attended by **Ditung Kuame**, the FPO's CEO.
- **Open Questions:** What is the FPO's actual sales/revenue performance against its planned targets (e.g., 2,000 kg hill broom at ₹70/kg, 500 kg smokeless king chilli at ₹800/kg, etc., as listed in the FY2023-24 report's activity table)? No actual-vs-target reconciliation was found in the pages read.

### 2. Skill Development
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Entrepreneurship-focused training for rural youth and women in value addition of local produce (Tamenglong oranges, Naga king chilli, forest fruits) and traditional bamboo/cane crafts.
- **Sector:** Livelihoods / Vocational Training
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 4, SDG 8
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Retail, Commerce & Craft → Creative & Traditional Trades, 🔴 Informal (Rural Youth & Women)
- **Geography (as stated):** Tousem Block, Tamenglong district, and Jiribam district, Manipur
- **Named Funders/Partners:** Brot für die Welt (via RNBA); Azim Premji Foundation
- **Named projects listed under this programme (per website):** "Promoting Tribal Livelihood & Food Security through community based Land & Biodiversity Management"; "System of Rice Intensification (SRI) for Jiribam and Tamenglong"; "People Empowerment for Accessive Rights to Livelihood (PEARL) project"; "Food Security for Small and Marginal Landholders based on Diversion Based Irrigation (DBI)"; Rural Haat construction; Indigenous brown/white cotton project; "Ensuring dignified livelihood for 400 marginalized farmers"; "Improved ecosystem governance for resilient livelihood and sustainable development."
- **Metric Trend Across Years:**
  - **FY2023-24:** Bee-keeping training for **12 interested youth**; mushroom-cultivation training for **20 women** (3 of whom, from Kaimai, are practicing at home); youth/women in carpentry, handicraft, and mushroom cultivation self-employment reported to have increased annual income by **20% vs. baseline**.
  - **FY2022-23:** **15 youth and women** who underwent skill training moved into self-employment (carpentry, handicraft, orchard farming, mushroom cultivation); **6 youth trained as "Pashu Sakhi"** (livestock para-veterinary resource persons at village level).
- **Open Questions:** Is the "20% income increase vs. baseline" figure independently verified/measured, or a self-reported organizational estimate? The same 20% figure appears in both the FY2022-23 and FY2023-24 reports, which — as with several other NGOs researched in this project — raises the question of whether it's a genuinely re-measured, stable trend or a repeated/recycled claim.

### 3. Livelihood
- **Status:** Active — PESCH's largest and most-funded programme area
- **Description:** Sustainable agriculture through kitchen gardens, Sloping Agricultural Land Technology (SALT) farming, and seed banks, plus livestock banks (piggery, goatery, poultry/country chicken) for diversified rural income and food security.
- **Sector:** Livelihoods / Agri-Allied
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 2, SDG 8, SDG 13
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied Ecosystem → Cultivation & Sourcing / Livestock & Animal Husbandry, 🔴 Informal (Small/Marginal Tribal Farmers)
- **Geography (as stated):** Primarily **Aben Range, Tousem Block, Tamenglong district** (4 villages: **Longchai, Aben, Charinapang, and Impa Ningdi** — 247 households, 1,480 population per the 2011 census, in the Manipur Tribal Area Autonomous District Council); Jiribam district for SRI/DBI-related work.
- **Named Funders (extensively documented, with actual rupee figures):**
  - **NABARD** — via the **Tribal Development Fund (TDF)** project, Aben Range: **cumulative grant sanctioned/disbursed as of 31 March 2024: ₹63.368 lakh.**
  - **Azim Premji Foundation (APF)** — convergence funding: **₹3.90 lakh** (livestock bank, vegetable farming, Pashu Sakhi, forest-food regeneration, for 50 beneficiaries).
  - **MGNREGS** (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme) — shramdan/labour convergence: **₹17.674 lakh**.
  - **Community/family contribution:** families contributed **₹12.185 lakh** as shramdan (voluntary labour) toward the TDF project.
  - **MSRLM** (Manipur State Rural Livelihood Mission) — women's health camps, SHG linkage.
  - **Global Green Grants Fund (GGF)** — funds the indigenous cotton revival sub-project (see below).
- **Metric Trend Across Years — TDF Aben Range project (the flagship, most quantified initiative):**
  - **Status as of 31 March 2024:** 200 families covered, 200 acres under the project (out of 247 total households in the 4 villages).
  - **Plantings to date:** ~20,000 orange plants, 19,400 lemon plants, 4,000 parkia (tree bean) plants, 800 coconut plants, plus areca nut and other species — estimated to sequester **~10,000 tonnes of CO₂/year**.
  - **1 FPO promoted:** Jiri-Barak Farmer Producer Company Ltd. (covering all 200 TDF families across the 4 villages).
  - **20 SHGs and 4 Farmer Clubs formed**, all 20 SHGs linked to MSRLM.
  - **Water/other infrastructure:** 100 farmers received bee boxes (20 farmers had active bee hives as of FY23-24); 100 farmers received water ponds (HDPE-lined, 5x4x1.5m); 50 of 100 targeted farmers "reached water" with improved plant health (FY22-23 update).
  - **FY2023-24 vegetable sales from the project: ₹1,00,000** (₹1 lakh) in that financial year alone.
  - **FY2022-23 (broader, non-TDF-specific Sustainable NRM figures):** 200 farmers practicing SALT across 4 Tousem villages; 6 forest-food regeneration programmes (20 indigenous species, 6,000 plants, 60 acres); GI-tag certification conducted for 2 villages/39 farmers; 200 farmers given barbed-wire fencing support; 100 farmers given bee-keeping support; 250 MGNREGS man-days utilized.
  - **FY2023-24 (Livelihood promotion under APF, separately reported):** 13,552 plants (mosambi/sweet lime, lemon, areca nut, orange, coconut) planted in SALT farms by **56 beneficiaries**; **97 beneficiary families** reduced vegetable expenses and increased nutritional food consumption via kitchen gardens (14 species cultivated); 9,000 plants of Homalomena aromatica ("Sugandhmantri," a medicinal/aromatic plant), timber, and wild vegetables planted across 60 acres of forest regeneration; **164.5 acres** brought under sustainable agriculture (SALT + forest regeneration + kitchen gardens) that year.
- **Named case study (FY2023-24): Mr. Athui Gonmei (52), Longchai village** — studied to 8th standard; received PESCH support for SALT farming, livestock, and bee-keeping; planted 20,000 orange/lemon/parkia/jackfruit/jujube/areca plants; also took up goatery (20 goats), poultry (12 ducks, 200 chickens), and 3 bee boxes after training from **SUNBURD TRUST** (goatery) and under the APF "Pashu Sakhi" project (piggery); opened his own input/output shop, **"Panti Rural Sanitary Mart (PRSM),"** selling poultry and goatery products/services to the wider community — described as a genuine beneficiary-to-micro-entrepreneur graduation story.
- **Rural Haat (market) infrastructure:** 2 Rural Haats completed (Sibilong and Kaimai villages) under NABARD funding, handed over to Women SHG-run Rural Haat Management Committees; 1 additional Rural Haat was "under construction" as of FY2022-23. A separate **electric/smokeless king-chilli drier** was installed at Sibilong, funded by the **Manipur Organic Mission Agency (MOMA)**.
- **Open Questions:** The TDF project's own "Status as of 31 March 2024" section is headed as such but one line still refers to "31 March 2023" for the cumulative grant figure (₹63.368 lakh) — is this a typo carried over from the prior year's report, or does the ₹63.368 lakh genuinely represent the FY2022-23 cumulative total, not yet updated for FY2023-24 disbursements? This should be checked against the FY2022-23 report's own TDF figures.

### 4. Natural Resource Management & Climate Change
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Nature-based solutions — reviving forest fruit species, conserving agrobiodiversity through seed banks, building check dams for water recharge, distributing improved cookstoves (to reduce firewood dependency), and promoting community forest stewardship and agro-ecotourism.
- **Sector:** Environment / Climate Resilience
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 13, SDG 15, SDG 6
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None (environmental/public-goods program, though it overlaps with the Livelihood programme's SALT/forest-food work)
- **Geography (as stated):** Tousem Block villages (Tamenglong district) and wider Manipur/Assam project areas
- **Named Funders/Partners:** Not separately specified beyond the overlapping NABARD/APF funding already noted under Livelihood
- **Named projects listed under this programme (per website):** "Environment Development and Management programme"; "Repair of flood-affected houses in Assam and Manipur"; "Awareness Campaign on Natural Resource Management, Tamenglong"; "Flood relief distribution"; "Springshed Development"; "Agro-ecotourism."
- **Metric Trend Across Years:**
  - **FY2022-23:** Springshed water-based programme implemented at **Tamah village**.
  - Forest-food regeneration and SALT figures overlap substantially with the Livelihood programme (see above) — PESCH's own reporting structure does not cleanly separate NRM from Livelihood in practice, since SALT/forest-food work is counted under both.
- **Open Questions:** Given the significant overlap in reported activities/metrics between "Livelihood" and "Natural Resource Management & Climate Change" (SALT farming, forest-food regeneration, and seed banks are claimed under both programme headings across different report sections), are these genuinely two distinct programmes with separate budgets, or one integrated program area split into two headings for website/reporting presentation purposes?

### 5. Value Education
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Engagement with government primary schools, School Management and Development Committees (SMDCs), Anganwadi (ICDS) and ASHA workers to improve learning/health outcomes and reduce child migration from remote villages; establishment of local libraries preserving community knowledge (forest management, customary law, traditional practices, cultural values).
- **Sector:** Education / Child Welfare
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 4, SDG 3
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None
- **Geography (as stated):** Tamenglong and Jiribam districts, Manipur
- **Named Funders/Partners:** **ASER Centre** / **Pratham Education Foundation** (national education-assessment partner); **Manipur Voluntary Health Association (MVHA)** (health awareness component)
- **Named projects (per website):** "Smokeless biomass heat-generated drier Programme, Tamenglong — Repair of Aben Govt. Upper Primary School"; "Annual Status of Education Report (ASER), Tamenglong."
- **Metric Trend Across Years:**
  - **FY2022-23:** ASER survey conducted using **15 volunteers**, covering **30 villages in Tamenglong** and **10 villages in Jiribam** (40 villages total).
  - **FY2023-24:** Counselling/career guidance session in Jiribam for students from Tousem-area families living in rented accommodation to attend school — **~20 students** attended (covering moral education, discipline, and interest/career exploration); skill training (mushroom cultivation, SALT/integrated farming) for "returnee students"; a domestic-violence awareness session specifically for **Muslim women** in Kamranga (Jiribam); a survey of children aged 5-15 in Jiribam on COVID-19's impact on learning.
- **PESCH's own ASER volunteer team (per FY2023-24 staff roster):** 4 named ASER volunteers — **Gangdunang Gangmei** (MSc Physics), **Joel Rongmei** (B.Com), **Jesse Rongmei** (BSc), and **Duangamliu Gangmei** (GNM/nursing) — each with 2-3 years' association with the organization.
- **Open Questions:** What were the actual ASER learning-outcome results (e.g., % of children at grade-level reading/maths) for Tamenglong/Jiribam — the report confirms the survey was conducted but the pages read did not include the actual findings/statistics.

## Additional Cross-Cutting Programmes Found in the Annual Reports (not separately listed on the website's 5-program menu)

### 6. Revival of Indigenous Brown & White Cotton Farming and Culture
- **Status:** Active, FY2023-24 (a named, funded sub-project)
- **Description:** Promotion of indigenous brown and white cotton cultivation — brown cotton is highlighted as requiring no dyeing (naturally colored) and being chemical-free, positioned as a culturally significant and market-differentiated product.
- **Named Funder:** **Global Green Grant Fund (GGF)**
- **Geography:** Sibilong, New Kaiphundai, and Njukok villages (Tamenglong district)
- **Metric Trend Across Years (FY2023-24, individually dated/attended training sessions):**
  - Sibilong, 21 April 2023: 33 women (all SHG members) + 4 men attended; resource person **Namkhinlung Pamei** (PESCH Director).
  - New Kaiphundai, 19 April 2023: 27 women (all SHG members) + 2 men attended; same resource person.
  - Njukok, 19 May 2023: 16 women (all SHG members) attended; same resource person.
  - **Combined reach across the 3 documented sessions: ~76 women and 6 men.**
- **Open Questions:** Beyond awareness/training sessions, what is the actual area under cotton cultivation, and has any cotton crop been harvested/sold yet as of FY2023-24? The report documents the training rollout in detail but not yet a harvest/production outcome.

### 7. COVID-19 / Disaster Relief (Brot Relief Programme)
- **Status:** Time-bound (COVID-19 response), documented FY2023-24 (referring to a June 2023 distribution — i.e., relief activity continuing well after the initial 2020 pandemic onset)
- **Description:** Distribution of essential relief commodities (rice, dal, potato, masks, hand sanitizer, soap) to lockdown/pandemic-affected households and quarantine centres.
- **Named Funder:** **Bread for the World ("Brot")**, via RNBA
- **Geography:** 6 villages — **Nungkao, Oinamlong, Kaimai, Sibilong, Chingkao, and Kaiphundai** (5 current + 1 former PESCH project village)
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** **3 June 2023** distribution: **175 households** and **10 Quarantine Centres** reached. Quantities: Rice 1,250 kg, Dal 50 kg, Soap 200 pcs, Masks 300 pcs, Hand Sanitizer 200 pcs, White Phenyl 200 pcs, Dettol liquid 100 pcs.
- **Open Questions:** Why was a "COVID-19 lockdown relief" distribution still being carried out in June 2023, well after most pandemic restrictions had eased nationally — was this a delayed disbursement of previously-committed Brot funds, or a response to a later, localized outbreak/lockdown?

## Cross-Cutting Observations

1. **Recycled boilerplate text confirmed across years, similar to patterns found in other NGOs researched in this project:** The Director's Message in the **FY2021-22** report states *"We have served across 62 villages with 7800 households thus far"* — and the **FY2023-24** report's Director's Message uses the **exact same sentence, word for word** ("We have served across 62 villages with 7800 households thus far"). In between, the **FY2022-23** report gives a *different, lower* figure: *"we have served for 26 years in across 57 villages with 7113 households."* This strongly suggests the FY2023-24 report's opening section reused FY2021-22's boilerplate text without updating the headline village/household count, rather than genuinely reporting a real reach figure for FY2023-24 — a clear "same text, different year" red flag, and a caution against treating that specific "62 villages / 7,800 households" figure as a reliable year-specific data point for FY2023-24.
2. **Extraordinarily well-documented governance history.** PESCH's own annual reports contain a complete, named leadership succession from 1995 to present — including the organization's two earlier names before "PESCH" (SWDA → CDO → CDS → PAFCORD → PESCH) and every Chairman/Director/Board member with their village and tenure dates. This level of institutional-memory documentation is unmatched among the other NGOs researched in this project.
3. **A dense web of government and institutional convergence** is a defining feature of PESCH's model — nearly every project explicitly stacks multiple funding/technical sources together (e.g., the TDF project alone converges NABARD + Azim Premji Foundation + MGNREGS + community shramdan + MSRLM + ASHA workers in a single village cluster).
4. **SeSTA** appears again (as in MOSONiE, Diya Foundation, and SNEHPAD's research) as a capacity-building partner — specifically credited with facilitating a "NODE-SeSTA project" that helped PESCH redevelop its governance manual, organisational strategy, fundraising approach, data management, HR development, and communications during FY2023-24 — reinforcing the finding that SeSTA operates as a shared regional intermediary across many of Northeast India's grassroots NGOs.
5. **PESCH's overlapping "Livelihood" and "Natural Resource Management & Climate Change" programme boundaries** (see Program 4's Open Questions) suggest the organization's own 5-programme website taxonomy is a simplified external presentation layer over a more integrated, harder-to-cleanly-separate set of field activities — a pattern also seen (to varying degrees) in other Northeast Indian NGOs in this project (e.g., MOSONiE's overlapping "Environmental Conservation" and "Soh Marit" programmes).
