# PRDA — Programs & Reports Deep-Dive

Primary source for detailed programme data: **PRDA's own official Annual Report (April 2020 – March 2021)**, downloaded directly from indiadonates.org (an NGO-verification/donation platform) and saved locally as `PRDA_Annual_Report_2021.pdf`. This is PRDA's own self-published report, structured around 5 core project activities plus an audit report (Contents page confirms: I. CML, II. ActionAid LRP, III. CHILDLINE, IV. One Stop Centre, V. Nasha Mukt Bharat Campaign, plus Audit Report) — **note that the copy of this PDF obtained during this research runs only 15 pages and stops after the LRP section**, meaning the CHILDLINE, One Stop Centre, Nasha Mukt Bharat Campaign, and Audit Report sections listed in its own Table of Contents were **not actually present in the retrieved file** (see Open Questions). Supplementary detail on current (post-2021) programming comes from PRDA's official website (prda.org.in) and its official Instagram.

## 1. Centre for Micro-Finance & Livelihood (CML) — Solar Energy Financing Programme
- **Status:** Active as of FY2020-21 (the specific solar-financing project reported appears time-bound but is described as building a permanent "revolving fund" mechanism intended to continue beyond the project period)
- **Description:** A loan-based, revolving-finance model to provide affordable, decentralized solar energy solutions (for lighting and livelihood uses) to households and small businesses lacking reliable grid electricity, primarily in backward tribal areas of Manipur. Explicitly designed as a **financial-inclusion/asset-financing pilot** — introducing an "asset-based financing mindset" to both clients and local banks — rather than a one-time equipment giveaway.
- **Sector:** Financial Inclusion / Renewable Energy / Livelihood
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1 (No Poverty), SDG 7 (Affordable and Clean Energy), SDG 8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth)
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Financial Services — Microfinance/Revolving Credit; Agritech & Allied / Home-based livelihoods (weaving, small business) enabled by lighting
- **Geography (as stated):** Implemented across **4 districts of Manipur: Bishnupur, Noney, Chandel, and Thoubal** — notably PRDA's broadest multi-district reach of any programme documented
- **Named Funders:** Not explicitly named in the retrieved report section (referred to only as "the Grant amount" and a "Revolving Fund (RF) at Bank" — funder identity not stated)
- **Target groups:** Poor and very poor households and small businesses without reliable electricity access, "mainly backward tribal areas of Manipur"
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** Single-year (FY2020-21) data only:
  - **Target: 125+ households** (main objective)
  - **113 households** installed via grant funds (against a stated target of 110 for the year — exceeded)
  - **15 households** installed via the Revolving Fund at Bank (against a target of 15 — met)
  - **12 households** installed from RF recovery specifically
  - **Revolving Fund recovery: ₹2,79,780** (against a target of the same amount — fully met per the report's own table, though the table's "target" and "achievement" columns appear identical, which may indicate the figure is a plan rather than a verified actual — flagged)
- **Named beneficiary case studies:**
  - **Tongbram Ajitkumar Singh** (Ishok village, Nambol Block, Bishnupur District) — 150WP system for lighting, weaving, fan, and TV
  - **Thangasem Sekho** (Dolang village, Noney District) — 40WP system for lighting
  - **Rangneingir Jatung** (age 60, graduate, farmer, Dolang village, Noney District) — 150WP system; detailed first-person testimonial describing his family's prior total reliance on solar lamps/lanterns and unreliable state electricity, and how the PRDA-CML solar installation eliminated power outages, allowed his children to study at night, and let him watch television during leisure time after farm work
- **COVID-19-specific challenges (explicitly documented):** Lockdowns prevented in-person village awareness programmes, leading some villagers to initially refuse solar installation; a state government moratorium on loan collection during lockdown directly hurt monthly repayment collection; borrowers' own income loss during lockdown affected repayment ability; general financial shortages delayed installations for some beneficiaries.
- **Open Questions:** Funder for this specific programme is never named in the retrieved report text; the RF recovery table's identical target/achievement figures raise a question about whether this reflects a real achieved outcome or a projected one; no data found for years after FY2020-21 on this specific solar programme (whether it continued, expanded, or concluded).

## 2. ActionAid Local Rights Programme (LRP) — including Child Sponsorship
- **Status:** Active as of FY2020-21 (ActionAid child-sponsorship programmes are typically long-running, multi-year commitments, so likely still active, though not independently confirmed post-2021)
- **Description:** A rights-based community empowerment programme under ActionAid India's "Local Rights Programme" model, titled in PRDA's report as **"Empowering Vulnerable Local Communities for Sustainable Development."** The specific activity documented in detail is the **Child Message (CM) collection process** — a core mechanic of ActionAid's international child-sponsorship model, where sponsored children's messages/drawings are collected and presumably relayed to sponsors.
- **Sector:** Women & Child Rights / Community Empowerment
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 4 (Quality Education — implied via child sponsorship), SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities)
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: Not directly applicable (this is a child-welfare/sponsorship programme, not a livelihood programme)
- **Geography (as stated):** 5 village clusters in Bishnupur district — **Kwasiphai & Upokpi Khunou; Thanga Karang & Chingkha; Waroiching & Heinoubok; Ishok I, II & Naorem; Thanga Ngaram, Oinam & Ithing**
- **Named Funders:** **ActionAid India** (implicit — this is ActionAid's own LRP/child-sponsorship model; individual child sponsors are the ultimate funding source, channeled through ActionAid)
- **Target groups:** Sponsored children across the 5 clusters
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** Single-year (FY2020-21 "CM1 2020 collection") data:
  - **Total sponsored children across all 5 clusters: 875** (160 + 167 + 110 + 231 + 207)
  - **860 child messages actually collected** in the CM1 2020 round (a ~98% completion rate despite pandemic disruption)
  - **6 new sponsored children added** from Heinoubok cluster, replacing children who had migrated away
- **Named field staff per cluster:** Gunamani (Kwasiphai/Upokpi Khunou, 160 children), N. Bidyalaxmi Devi (Thanga Karang/Chingkha, 167 children), Sanahal (Waroiching/Heinoubok, 110 children), T. Ajitkumar Singh (Ishok I/II/Naorem, 231 children), O. Raju Singh (Thanga Ngaram/Oinam/Ithing, 207 children) — all supervised by Sponsorship Coordinator Pounga Newme
- **COVID-19-specific challenges (explicitly documented):** Villages blocked outside entry (including PRDA's own field staff) during pandemic waves; large in-person gatherings of children for collection were prohibited, forcing a shift to door-to-door collection; collection schedules originally planned for Feb-March 2020 stretched out to as late as June 2020 in some clusters due to these disruptions; some sponsored children were temporarily unreachable (staying in hostels, visiting relatives, or in one rare case, affected by parental divorce).
- **Open Questions:** No financial/grant-size figures given for this programme; no information found on how many total sponsors are involved or the sponsorship model's specific terms; status/continuation after 2021 not independently confirmed.

## 3. People Credit and Thrift Project (PCTP) — Financial Inclusion / SHG Credit Linkage
- **Status:** Per the original user-supplied data, active "since 1999-2000" — PRDA's official website confirms this remains a **current, named flagship activity** under its "Financial Inclusion" service area ("Our People Credit and Thrift Project (PCTP) supports low-income households by encouraging savings habits and providing small loans for productive purposes")
- **Description:** A savings-and-credit programme for underserved/low-income households, built around encouraging structured savings habits and providing small loans for productive (income-generating) purposes — the classic SHG (Self-Help Group) thrift-and-credit model.
- **Sector:** Financial Inclusion / SHG & Microfinance
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 5 (Gender Equality — per the user-supplied data's emphasis on women SHG members), SDG 8
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Financial Services — SHG-linked microcredit/thrift
- **Geography (as stated):** Bishnupur District, Manipur
- **Named Funders:** Not identified in official sources reviewed (the current website describes PCTP only in general terms, without naming a specific funder)
- **Target groups:** Low-income households, particularly women (per user-supplied data: **160 SHGs with 1,500 women members**)
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** No official-source year-by-year data could be independently found beyond the user-supplied topline figures (160 SHGs, 1,500 women members) — this is one of PRDA's longest-running programmes (25+ years, since 1999-2000) but, unusually, is the *least* independently documented in terms of granular official-source metrics among PRDA's activities, likely because it predates the organization's current website and the retrieved 2021 Annual Report focused on other, more recently-funded activities (CML, LRP) instead.
- **Open Questions:** This is the single largest gap in this research pass — PCTP is named as a flagship, decades-long financial inclusion programme by both the user's original data and PRDA's own current website, but no detailed, dated, official-source activity report (annual figures, loan sizes, repayment rates, SHG-federation structures) could be located. A direct request to PRDA or a dedicated PCTP report (if one exists) would be needed to fill this gap.

## 4. CHILDLINE
- **Status:** Uncertain/disputed — see Verification Notes in `PRDA_Info.md`
- **Description:** Per PRDA's own 2021 Annual Report, this was listed as one of 5 core project activities, with a dedicated 4-person "Childline" team. CHILDLINE is a national child-protection helpline/response service (1098) under the Ministry of Women & Child Development.
- **Sector:** Child Protection / Women & Child Rights
- **Geography (as stated):** Bishnupur District
- **Named Funders/Implementing structure:** Per the **independent, official Bishnupur District government website**, CHILDLINE Bishnupur (operational since 1 March 2015) is implemented under CHILDLINE India Foundation (Mumbai) with **New Life Foundation, Thamnapokpi**, named as the "sole custodian" — **PRDA is not mentioned on this government page at all.**
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** None available — the relevant section of PRDA's own Annual Report (which would have covered this) was not present in the retrieved 15-page PDF, despite being listed in its Table of Contents.
- **Open Questions:** This entire programme's actual relationship to PRDA (past implementing partner? parallel/complementary informal activity? a reporting inconsistency?) is unresolved and should be treated with caution in any funder-facing material — see full discussion in `PRDA_Info.md` Verification Notes.

## 5. One Stop Centre (OSC) for Women in Distress
- **Status:** Referenced in the user-supplied data and in PRDA's own 2021 Annual Report's Table of Contents, but **no descriptive content could be retrieved** — the relevant report section was not present in the retrieved PDF, and the current official website does not mention "One Stop Centre" by name anywhere in the pages reviewed.
- **Description:** One Stop Centres (OSCs, also known as "Sakhi Centres") are a standard Government of India (Ministry of Women & Child Development) scheme providing integrated support (medical, legal, psychological, police, shelter) to women affected by violence, typically run by or in partnership with an implementing NGO under state government oversight.
- **Sector:** Women's Rights / Gender-Based Violence Response
- **Open Questions:** Essentially all specifics (location, capacity, case numbers, funding source, current operational status) are unknown from official sources reviewed — this is a significant, clearly-flagged data gap, not a fabricated placeholder. As with PCTP, direct outreach to PRDA would be needed to properly document this activity.

## 6. Nasha Mukt Bharat Campaign (Drug-Free India Campaign)
- **Status:** Referenced in the user-supplied data and PRDA's Annual Report Table of Contents, but **no descriptive content could be retrieved** (same limitation as CHILDLINE and OSC above — this section was not present in the retrieved 15-page PDF).
- **Description:** Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyan is a national Government of India (Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment) anti-drug-abuse awareness and de-addiction campaign, typically implemented locally through partner NGOs and de-addiction centres.
- **Sector:** Health / Substance Abuse Prevention
- **Geography (as stated, per relevance):** Bishnupur District — plausible given Manipur's well-documented, serious substance-abuse public health challenges, but not independently confirmed for PRDA specifically beyond the user-supplied data and the Annual Report's table of contents listing.
- **Open Questions:** Same as OSC above — no operational specifics found in official sources reviewed.

## 7. Disaster Response & Humanitarian Support (current thematic area, incl. HAI/Start Fund partnership)
- **Status:** Active — this is one of PRDA's 8 current official thematic service areas, and is corroborated by recent (2025) activity
- **Description:** Per the official website: "PRDA supports communities during disasters and humanitarian emergencies through relief, healthcare, and rehabilitation efforts... organizes free medical camps... provides emergency relief such as food, essential supplies, and medical assistance during crises."
- **Sector:** Disaster/Emergency Relief, Health
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 3, SDG 11
- **Geography (as stated):** Manipur (general; specific crisis locations not named in the generic website description)
- **Named Funders/Partners:** **Humanitarian Aid International (HAI)** and the **Start Fund** — confirmed via PRDA's own official Instagram (July 2025 post): "With #StartFund support, HAI and LOCAL platform member PRDA are putting people first. Customised aid for a dignified recovery." This confirms PRDA's membership in the **LOCAL platform**, a localization-focused humanitarian coordination network, and its ability to receive rapid-response emergency funding via the Start Fund mechanism (a well-known international crisis-response pooled fund) channeled through HAI.
- **Institutional network context:** PRDA's Secretary, Longjam Suranjoy Singh, is a **Board Member of Sphere India** and **Convenor of IAG Manipur** — both credible, independently-verifiable humanitarian-sector network affiliations that support this thematic area's credibility.
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** No specific beneficiary counts, dates, or crisis-response details found for the HAI/Start Fund-supported activity beyond the single Instagram post referenced.
- **Open Questions:** What specific crisis/emergency in Manipur (given the state's significant ethnic conflict and displacement context in recent years) this July 2025 Start Fund support responded to is not stated in the source found; no quantified relief-delivery figures (households reached, aid value, etc.) were located.

## Master List of Open Questions / Data Gaps

1. **PCTP (People Credit and Thrift Project)** — PRDA's own longest-running (~26-year) programme has essentially no independently-documented granular metrics; the 160 SHGs/1,500 women figures come only from the user's original data table, not independently verified against an official PRDA source.
2. **CHILDLINE custodianship** — genuine, unresolved conflict between PRDA's own report and an official government website (see `PRDA_Info.md`).
3. **One Stop Centre and Nasha Mukt Bharat Campaign** — essentially undocumented beyond their names; the Annual Report sections that would cover them were not present in the retrieved PDF.
4. **Form 10B financials (2021-2024) inaccessible** due to a server-side SSL configuration issue on PRDA's own hosting — this is a technical/access limitation, not a transparency failure on PRDA's part (the documents are clearly intended to be public).
5. **CML solar programme's funder** is never named; unclear if/how the programme continued past FY2020-21.
6. **HAI/Start Fund disaster response activity (2025)** — no details on the specific triggering crisis or scale of response.
7. **No comprehensive, current (post-2021) staff count or full board roster** — the 48-staff/7-board-member figures are specifically dated to FY2020-21; only the top 4 board positions were confirmed as still current via the homepage.
