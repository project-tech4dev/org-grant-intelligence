## 3. Geographic Fit

- **Core geography: Bishnupur District, Manipur** — specifically Ithai Barrage-affected villages (Ishok and surrounding areas)
- **Bishnupur is NOT a NITI Aayog Aspirational District** (Manipur's only Aspirational District is **Chandel**, confirmed via NITI Aayog's official list) — unlike Better Life Foundation (Kiphire) and KABIL (Udalguri/Baksa) researched earlier in this series, **PRDA cannot claim this specific government-priority geographic tag**. This is a neutral-to-negative finding worth being upfront about rather than omitting.
- **PRDA's distinctive geographic narrative instead is infrastructure-displacement/environmental-justice-based**: the Ithai Barrage's ongoing, decades-long flood impact on Ishok and neighboring villages is a specific, well-documented, place-based crisis narrative — arguably a stronger and more unique "origin story" for funder pitches than a generic Aspirational District tag, particularly for **environmental-justice, climate-resilience, or infrastructure-mitigation-focused funders**.
- **Manipur's broader context** (a state that has experienced significant ethnic conflict and displacement in recent years) may itself be a relevant framing for **humanitarian/conflict-response funders**, though this research did not find PRDA directly attributing any specific programme to that broader conflict context (its Start Fund/HAI-supported 2025 activity's specific trigger was not identified).

## 6. Distinctive "Selling Points" for a Funder Pitch

1. **The strongest, most fully-documented compliance profile in this research series** — active FCRA (renewed through 2027), confirmed 12A/80G/PAN/TAN/Darpan, and 4 years of published audited financials. This alone should materially de-risk PRDA in any funder's due diligence process, domestic or international.
2. **A specific, compelling, place-based origin story** (Ithai Barrage displacement, 1983) — a concrete, single-incident environmental-justice narrative that is easy to communicate and emotionally resonant, arguably more distinctive than a generic "underserved district" framing.
3. **Proven, multi-year INGO partnership (ActionAid India)** — de-risks PRDA for other INGOs/Category 10 funders considering a first-time partnership, since PRDA has demonstrable experience meeting an international NGO's reporting/programmatic standards.
4. **Genuine humanitarian-sector network integration** (Sphere India Board membership, IAG Manipur Convenorship, LOCAL platform membership, Start Fund access via HAI) — positions PRDA well above a typical single-district grassroots NGO in terms of sector credibility and rapid-response funding access.
5. **26-year-old flagship financial-inclusion programme (PCTP)** — while under-documented in this research, its sheer longevity (since 1999-2000) is itself a credibility signal worth highlighting, pending better internal data capture.
6. **Founder/Secretary's personal, verifiable story** (fisherman-turned-humanitarian-leader) — a strong, human-interest anchor for donor communications and proposal narratives.

## 7. Gaps a Funder Would Likely Flag

1. **PCTP, One Stop Centre, and Nasha Mukt Bharat Campaign are essentially undocumented** at the granular level despite being named flagship/ongoing activities — a funder doing real due diligence would ask for specific data PRDA needs to have ready (loan books, case counts, outcomes) that could not be found publicly.
2. **CHILDLINE custodianship discrepancy** — this specific, findable conflict between PRDA's own report and an official government website is exactly the kind of thing a careful funder's due-diligence team might discover and question; best proactively clarified by PRDA before it's raised externally.
3. **No large/diversified institutional Foundation or CSR funder relationships documented** (unlike KABIL's Axis Bank Foundation/Azim Premji Foundation/Tata Trusts portfolio) — despite superior compliance credentials, PRDA appears to have a smaller, more concentrated funder base to date.
4. **Financial statements (Form 10B) technically inaccessible during this research** — while their existence is a strong positive signal, a funder would still need PRDA to directly share these (e.g., as PDF attachments) rather than relying on the currently-broken public download links.
5. **Governance transparency drops off below the top 4 officers** — only President, Secretary, Assistant Secretary, and Treasurer were confirmed current; the remaining 3 of 7 board seats (per the 2021 report) have unconfirmed current status.
6. **No Aspirational District or other official geographic-priority tag** — Bishnupur does not carry this particular credibility marker, unlike two other organizations researched in this series; PRDA's pitch should lean on its distinctive place-based (Ithai Barrage) narrative instead.
