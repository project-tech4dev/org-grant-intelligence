## History & Origin

PRDA's origin is directly tied to a specific infrastructure/environmental crisis, distinct from most other organizations researched in this series:

- In **1983**, construction of the **Ithai Barrage** (at the confluence of the Manipur River, Khuga River, and Ungamel channel, for hydropower generation) caused **widespread, recurring flooding and submergence of approximately 10,000 hectares of cultivable land** (out of a total ~80,000 hectares across Bishnupur, Imphal West, and Thoubal districts), particularly devastating **Ishok village**. Floodwater combined with inflows from the Merakhong, Nambol, and Nambul rivers and backwater from Loktak Lake, along with invasive floating biomass ("phumdi") carried from upstream lakes (Sanapat and Naorem Utrapat), destroyed crops annually and caused recurring outbreaks of malaria and waterborne disease.
- In response, local youth from Ishok village formed the **Ishok Makha Young Development Club (IMYDC) in 1988** to address flood impacts and raise awareness of community issues.
- In **1995**, IMYDC's Governing Body renamed the organization **People's Resource Development Association (PRDA)** and formally registered it as a non-profit, non-sectarian, secular organization under the Societies Registration Act, subsequently obtaining 12A and FCRA status.

### Founder/Secretary biographical note (Longjam Suranjoy Singh)
Per Sphere India's own official Executive Committee page (an independent, credible secondary source): Longjam Suranjoy Singh, from Ishok Makha Leikai, Bishnupur District, **came from a humble fisherman family** and worked in fishing himself (including supplying fish feed locally) before the Ithai Barrage's construction left thousands homeless — this personally motivated his community organizing work. He remains PRDA's Secretary today and has since become a **Board Member of Sphere India** (a national humanitarian coordination network) and **Convenor of IAG Manipur** (Inter-Agency Group Manipur, a state-level humanitarian coordination body), reflecting PRDA's integration into wider national/state disaster-response networks beyond its own direct programming.

## Verification / Discrepancy Notes

1. **Founding year discrepancy (minor):** The user-supplied data table states PRDA "evolved from IMYDC youth group (1990)... Registered 1995." **Official sources consistently state IMYDC was formed in 1988, not 1990** — a 2-year discrepancy. The "1995" formal registration/renaming date matches exactly across the user's data and all official sources, so this appears to be a minor, isolated error in the origin year specifically, not a broader mischaracterization.
2. **Website domain:** The user's data table cites `www.prda.in`; this domain did not resolve (DNS failure) during this research. The organization's current live site is at `prda.org.in`. It's possible `prda.in` was a formerly-used domain that has since lapsed — the 2020-21 Annual Report itself lists "Website: www.prda.in" as of 2021, confirming this was indeed once PRDA's domain, but it appears to no longer be active.
3. **CHILDLINE custodianship discrepancy:** PRDA's own 2020-21 Annual Report lists "CHILDLINE" as one of its 5 core project activities, with 4 named team members. However, the **official Bishnupur District government website** (bishnupur.nic.in) states unambiguously that CHILDLINE Bishnupur (operational since March 2015) is "under the direct supervision of CHILDLINE INDIA FOUNDATION, Mumbai" and that "**NEW LIFE FOUNDATION, Thamnapokpi monitors, guides and controls the Childline Bishnupur operation... as it is the sole custodian**." This is a real, unresolved discrepancy — possible explanations (none confirmed) include: PRDA may have been an earlier or parallel implementing partner before New Life Foundation became sole custodian; PRDA's report may describe a related but distinct child-protection activity also informally called "Childline" internally; or the government page may be incomplete/outdated. Flagged rather than resolved.
4. **80G certificate number:** confirmed to exist (listed on the official "Official Documents" page, with a viewable/downloadable certificate) but the specific certificate number could not be extracted during this research (see technical note below).
5. **Technical limitation — Form 10B and FCRA renewal certificate downloads:** PRDA's Official Documents page links to Form 10B certificates for **2021, 2022, 2023, and 2024**, plus FCRA renewal certificates for **2016–2021** and **2022–2027**, all of which would provide excellent audited-financial and compliance-currency detail. However, all of these files are hosted on a subdomain (`wordpress.prda.org.in`) that returned a **TLS/SSL handshake error** across multiple tool attempts (fetch, download, and browser navigation) during this research session — this is a server-side technical/configuration issue on PRDA's own hosting, not a case of the documents not existing or being hidden. Their existence and filenames (e.g., "PRDA-10B-2024.pdf," "FCRA-Renewal-2022-2027.pdf") were directly confirmed from the Official Documents page itself.
6. **Ethnic community claims (from user's data table):** The user's table states PRDA works with Naga, Meitei, Chiru, and Kuki communities in Bishnupur District. This could not be independently verified from official PRDA sources reviewed (the website and Annual Report describe beneficiaries generically as "tribal"/"marginalized" without naming specific ethnic communities) — presented as unconfirmed, not contradicted.
