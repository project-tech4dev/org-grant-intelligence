## Programme Portfolio (Structured Deep-Dive)

### 1. Livelihood (Horticulture, Animal Husbandry & Mushroom Cultivation)
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Multi-component livelihood programme combining (a) horticulture — 10 compact orchard farms with low-chilling fruit varieties, and (b) animal husbandry & mushroom cultivation — goat farming, poultry, piggery, mushroom cultivation, and garlic farming, with credit linkages and subsidies integrated from various government schemes.
- **Sector:** Livelihood / Agriculture & Allied (Horticulture, Livestock)
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1 (No Poverty), SDG 2 (Zero Hunger), SDG 8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth)
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied — Horticulture, Animal Husbandry, Mushroom Cultivation, 🔴 Informal transitioning toward credit-linked formal micro-enterprise
- **Geography (as stated):** Tuensang and Kiphire districts, Nagaland
- **Named Funders:** BLF's own website does not attribute a single funder in this section; but **cross-referenced against BLF's official Facebook page and the newly-found "JIVA Project" launch (see Programme 12 below), NABARD is confirmed as a/the funder of the horticulture (low-chill fruit orchard) sub-component specifically** — a Facebook post states: "Thanks to NABARD-funded horticulture projects focused on low-chill fruit orchards, communities are building resilient incomes, restoring land..." The animal husbandry/mushroom cultivation sub-component's funder(s) remain unattributed.
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** Only a single, undated cumulative figure is given (no year-by-year breakdown found):
  - **2,000+ marginal families** benefited (repeated across both the horticulture and animal husbandry sub-components — unclear if this is the same 2,000 families counted twice or two distinct cohorts totaling more)
  - **250% annual rise in family incomes** reported since project intervention (horticulture component specifically)
- **Open Questions:** No start date given for this specific programme (distinct from the organization's 2009 founding); unclear whether the "2,000+ families" cited under horticulture and the "2,000+ families" cited under animal husbandry/mushroom cultivation are the same families (i.e., total unique beneficiaries could be 2,000 or up to 4,000); no year-by-year trend data available — would need annual reports (not currently published) to establish one; unclear whether this NABARD-funded horticulture sub-component is the same initiative as the JIVA Project (Programme 12) or a separate, parallel NABARD-funded effort.

- **Sector:** Livelihood / Tourism & Cultural Preservation

- **Metric Trend Across Years:** No numeric visitor counts given — only a qualitative claim: "Hosted thousands of Domestic, national, and international tourists over the last 5-10 years." This vague range (5-10 years) itself suggests the site content may not be regularly updated with precise current figures.
- **Recognition (verified via icrt.global and independent travel press, expanding on what BLF's own site states):**

No count of how many former insurgents have been trained/employed as guides, nor any named individual account of this specific reconciliation work (unlike the named farmer testimonials elsewhere on the site).

### 9. Shodh Yatra Tuensang & Kiphire
- **Status:** Periodic/recurring (participates in the nationwide Shodh Yatra initiative; two specific editions documented)
- **Description:** A "learning journey" campaign — part of a nationwide Shodh Yatra initiative (associated with SRISTI/Honey Bee Network innovation-scouting tradition) exploring local biodiversity, indigenous knowledge, and community challenges through immersive travel with a multi-state team of "yatri" (journey participants).
- **Sector:** Cultural Preservation / Grassroots Innovation Scouting
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 4 (Quality Education), SDG 15 (Life on Land)
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None
- **Geography (as stated):** Tuensang district (2016 edition) and Kiphire district (2024 edition), Nagaland
- **Named Funders/Partners:** Organized with SRISTI and National Innovation Foundation (NIF) doing follow-up work on local innovations identified
- **Metric Trend Across Years:**

| Edition | Year | Location | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 38th Shodhyatra | 2016 | Tuensang district | Part of a nationwide initiative covering 5,000+ km across India |
| 50th Shodhyatra | 2024 | Kiphire district | 56 yatri from 14 states |

- **2016 edition findings (as documented):** explored biodiversity, indigenous knowledge, and challenges including limited livelihood options leading to wildlife hunting during lean months; high school dropout rates due to remote terrain (some villages had makeshift hostels for students); noted strengths — community cleanliness, rooftop water harvesting, artistic traditions; centenarians shared wisdom on compassion/resilience; local innovations noted included multipurpose food processors and bamboo tools, prompting SRISTI/NIF follow-up.
- **2024 edition:** Framed as a "mutual learning and respect" exchange between 56 yatri (from 14 Indian states) and local Kiphire communities.
- **Open Questions:** Gap between the 38th (2016) and 50th (2024) editions suggests roughly 12 Shodhyatras occurred nationally in the 8-year interval, but none of those intervening editions are documented as occurring in BLF's operating area specifically — unclear if BLF was involved in any editions between 2016 and 2024.

## Reconciling BLF's Beneficiary Figures (Website vs. Independent Press)

Two different beneficiary totals exist for BLF's core farmer/livelihood work, from two different source types:

| Source | Figure | Date | Context |
|---|---|---|---|
| BLF official website (`betterlifefoundation.net`) | **2,000+ marginal families** | Undated (current site content) | Cited separately under both the Livelihood programme and the Marketing & Value Chains (FPO) programme |
| Nagaland Tribune (independent press, reporting on the Rohini Nayyar Prize) | **1,200 marginal farmers and their families** | 21 December 2022 | Describing Sangtam's overall body of work at the time of the award |
| BLF Facebook/LinkedIn (Dr. KRS Foundation project) | **500 marginalized families** | Undated (recent post, post-2022 given Dr. KRS Foundation's typical multi-state programme timing) | Specific to the "Strengthening Livelihood Security" baseline study, Longkhim Block only |

**Interpretation:** The most likely explanation is genuine organizational growth over time — 1,200 families (as of the Dec 2022 press report) growing to 2,000+ families (as of the current website) is a plausible ~67% increase over roughly 2-3 years for a scaling grassroots NGO. The 500-family Dr. KRS Foundation cohort in Longkhim Block is most plausibly a **subset** of the larger 2,000+ figure (i.e., already counted within it), rather than an additive, entirely separate population — but this is **not confirmed** by any source and is presented as the most parsimonious interpretation rather than a verified fact.

### 12. JIVA Project (NABARD-Funded Natural Farming Pilot, Kiphire District)
- **Status:** Active — launched 22 May (year not specified in source, but article dated "May 23" alongside 2025-dated site content elsewhere, so most likely 2025)
- **Source:** Morung Express (credible Northeast India news outlet), reporting directly from the launch event — not on BLF's own website's "Our Works" page, similar to the Dr. KRS Foundation project (Programme 11). This further supports the finding that BLF's official website under-represents current live programme activity.
- **Description:** A NABARD-funded natural/organic farming pilot project, with BLF acting as the **Project Implementing Agency (PIA)**, aiming to shift farmers from chemical-based farming to organic, eco-friendly, natural farming practices. Officially described (by NABARD's General Manager at the launch) as being "not just about farming... about empowering villages, conserving natural resources, and ensuring long-term food security."
- **Sector:** Livelihood / Sustainable & Natural Agriculture
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1 (No Poverty), SDG 2 (Zero Hunger), SDG 13 (Climate Action)
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied — Natural/Organic Farming, Fruit Orchards (apple, kiwi, peach, tamarillo nurseries), 🔴 Informal
- **Geography (as stated):** Amahator Village, Kiphire district, Nagaland (first-ever JIVA project in the entire state of Nagaland); a second Farming Society named — Pungro Farming Society — also received support at the same launch event
- **Named Funders/Partners:** **NABARD** (funder); District Administration, Kiphire (host); Agriculture & Allied Departments (technical support); local Self-Help Groups (implementation partners)
- **Launch event details:** Launched 22 May by Deputy Commissioner Kiphire, Temsuwati Longkumer (NCS), with P. Bulte (General Manager, NABARD Nagaland RO) as guest of honour; Sethrichem Sangtam (title given as **"MD, Better Life Foundation (PIA)"**) gave the project overview.
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** Single, precisely-dated pilot-phase target (no multi-year trend yet, consistent with a just-launched project):
  - **Pilot phase target: 30 farmers, including 16 lead farmers**
  - Design principle: **"365 days of crop cover"** for food security, income security, and health security
  - Activities: capacity building, foundational systems, training sessions for lead farmers, exposure visits, and nursery creation for apple, kiwi, peach, and tamarillo
  - **1 mobile van each** handed over to Amahator Farming Society and Pungro Farming Society (NABARD-supported, BLF-implemented) at the same launch event
- **Official statement on future scaling (P. Bulte, NABARD GM):** more projects will be sanctioned "basing on the success and outcome of JIVA project" if farmers carry it forward with dedication — i.e., pilot success is an explicit precondition for expansion, not a guaranteed rollout.
- **Cross-reference:** This project's nursery crops (apple, kiwi, peach, tamarillo) and NABARD funding closely match the "10 compact orchard farms with low-chilling fruit varieties" described under the main **Livelihood** programme (Programme 1) on BLF's official website — which previously had **no funder attributed** in that section. **This NABARD-JIVA finding likely resolves that open question**: NABARD appears to be the funder (at least in part) of BLF's low-chill fruit orchard/horticulture work, consistent with a separate BLF Facebook post stating "Thanks to NABARD-funded horticulture projects focused on low-chill fruit orchards, communities are building resilient incomes, restoring land..."
- **Open Questions:** Exact launch year not stated in the article itself (inferred as 2025 from context); no information on JIVA project's funding amount, duration, or exit criteria for the pilot phase; not clear whether "JIVA" is a NABARD national programme name (applied here for the first time in Nagaland) or a Nagaland/BLF-specific project title — the article's phrasing ("first ever JIVA Project... in Nagaland") suggests JIVA is a pre-existing NABARD scheme being extended to the state for the first time, not a BLF-invented name, but this is not fully confirmed.

## Named Success Stories / Testimonials (from Home Page)

1. **Thirise Sangtam**, Progressive Farmer, Lirise Village, Tuensang — through BLF's **Tribal Development Fund (TDF) project**: previously relied on unstable jhum (slash-and-burn) cultivation and ran a small photocopying shop to make ends meet; transitioned to kiwi plantation; now generates annual revenue of ₹3-4 lakh from his orchard, providing financial stability.

2. **Hojung**, 62-year-old Progressive Farmer, Lirise village, Longkhim block — awards: Best Farmer Tuensang District (2018), Best Farmer Nagaland (2019); serves as a trainer for the Agricultural Technology Management Agency (ATMA). Background: 9 years with Assam Rifles (posted in Aizawl, Mizoram), voluntarily resigned in 1988, initially combined small-scale farming with hunting; in 2009 (same year BLF was founded) dedicated himself fully to farming. **Turning point:** a 2012 exposure visit to Dr. Y.S. Parmar University of Horticulture and Forestry, Solan, Himachal Pradesh — organized by BLF with NABARD support — introduced him to multi-fruit grafting, nursery propagation, and vermicomposting. Today manages a diversified 15-acre farm (tea, pine, rose, tamarillo, stone fruits, apple, pear, plus beekeeping, poultry, fisheries); income grew from ₹60,000/year to **₹4.7 lakh/year over 5 years**.

*(Note: the "Tribal Development Fund (TDF) project" mentioned in Thirise Sangtam's story is not otherwise described anywhere else on the site — it does not appear as a named programme under "Our Works." This is flagged as an open question below.)*

## Master List of Open Questions / Data Gaps

1. **No genuine Annual Reports, Financial Statements, or Newsletters exist on the official website** — the "Reports & Newsletters" page is entirely placeholder content. This is the single biggest data gap: it means there is no year-by-year trend data available for ANY of BLF's programmes, unlike other NGOs researched previously in this session.
2. **FCRA status could not be confirmed** — no registration number or explicit status statement found anywhere on the site (see also `Better_Life_Foundation_Info.md`).
3. **Founding year and HQ discrepancy** — the original query described "established in 1991" and "HQ: Senapati District, Manipur," but BLF's own site consistently states 2009 and Dimapur, Nagaland. Not resolved (see `Better_Life_Foundation_Info.md` verification note).
4. **"Tribal Development Fund (TDF) project"** — named only in one testimonial (Thirise Sangtam) but not documented as a distinct programme anywhere else on the site; unclear if this is a sub-component of the main Livelihood programme or a separate initiative entirely.
5. **Beneficiary double-counting risk** — the "2,000+ marginal families" figure appears (a) under Livelihood/horticulture, (b) under Livelihood/animal husbandry, and (c) under Marketing & Value Chains/FPOs — it is not clear whether these all refer to the same 2,000 families viewed through 3 different programme lenses, or represent additive/overlapping-but-distinct populations. Total unique beneficiaries could range from ~2,000 to ~6,000 depending on interpretation.
6. **"100% job placements"** claim (Women & Youth Empowerment programme) has no supporting detail (job types, wages, retention, time period) and should be treated as an unverified organizational claim.
7. **No numeric data at all for Health Camps & Pandemic Response** — the least quantified of BLF's 7 core "Our Works" programmes.
8. **No visitor/revenue figures for Eco-tourism Initiative** — only a vague "thousands over 5-10 years" claim.
9. **No date given for the Blue Planet/T.M. Krishna concert** in Angangba.
10. **Gap in Shodh Yatra participation between 2016 (38th) and 2024 (50th) editions** — unclear if BLF participated in any of the ~12 intervening editions.
11. **"Publication" and "Workshop & Events" tabs on the Media Centre page — re-checked and confirmed to be a site rendering limitation**, not an unexplored gap: clicking either tab does not change the displayed content (it continues to show the "Videos" tabpanel regardless of which tab is marked active). This was verified by directly clicking both tabs and inspecting the resulting page state. No additional content is retrievable from these tabs as currently implemented.
12. **"Our Partners & Awards" footer link is non-functional** (`href="#"`) — a dedicated partners/awards list may exist in the site's intended design but is not currently accessible, meaning the partner list compiled in `Better_Life_Foundation_Info.md` is assembled from partners named incidentally across other pages, not a single authoritative list.
13. **The "Oranogram" (organogram) page contains only an image file** (`Better_Life_Foundation_Oranogram.png`), not text — its organizational-structure content could not be extracted as text in this research.
14. **"Strengthening Livelihood Security" (Dr. KRS Foundation) project is not mentioned anywhere on the official website** — it was found only via BLF's official Facebook/LinkedIn presence. This raises a broader question: **BLF's official website may not be fully up to date with all current programme activity**, since a funded, named project with a completed baseline study doesn't appear in the "Our Works" page at all. Future research should treat BLF's social media channels as a necessary supplementary source, not just the website.
15. **No genuine confirmation of overlap/non-overlap between the 500-family Dr. KRS Foundation cohort and the 2,000+/1,200 figures cited elsewhere** — see the dedicated "Reconciling BLF's Beneficiary Figures" section above.
