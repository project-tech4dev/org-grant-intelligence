### 1. SuATI — Support to Agroecological Transformation Processes in India
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** A natural-farming/agroecology programme reducing chemical input dependency and restoring ecological balance through bio-fertilizers, bio-pesticides, composting, and Participatory Rural Appraisal (PRA)-based community engagement, run across the Hatigaon and Chutki clusters.
- **Sector:** Livelihood / Sustainable Agriculture
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1 (No Poverty), SDG 2 (Zero Hunger), SDG 13 (Climate Action)
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied — Natural/Organic Farming, 🔴 Informal
- **Geography (as stated):** Hatigaon and Chutki clusters (11 hamlets: 8 in Hatigaon, 3 in Chutki), Assam
- **Named Funders:** GIZ (Deutsche Gesellschaft für Internationale Zusammenarbeit)
- **Target groups:** Smallholder/subsistence farmers; FY2024-25 data shows 100% ST (Scheduled Tribe) farmer profile; 60% of surveyed households headed by women
- **Metric Trend Across Years:**

| Metric | FY2023-24 | FY2024-25 |
|---|---|---|
| Villages/hamlets covered | 11 villages | 11 hamlets (8 Hatigaon + 3 Chutki) |
| Beneficiaries/farmers trained | 500+ beneficiaries | 343 farmers trained (30+ sessions) |
| Demo plots | Established (no count given) | 20 (12 Chutki, 8 Hatigaon) |
| Area covered | Not quantified | 2,817 acres (agriculture & forest land) |
| Bio-input adoption | Not quantified | 40% using bio-inputs (compost, FYM, jeevamrit) |
| Bio-pest repellent use | Not quantified | 53% (incl. wood ash, neem extract) |
| Soil health practices | Not quantified | 46% practicing |
| Input-cost reduction | Not quantified | 53% achieved |
| Sales impact | Not quantified | ₹15+ lakh in vegetable sales reported |

- **Q2 2025 (Apr-Jun newsletter) activity detail:** Plant Clinic inaugurated at Chaygaon (10 June 2025) for crop pest/disease diagnosis; 5 INM & IPM training sessions (Apr-May) on biochar/vermicompost/biopesticides; 2 seed preservation training sessions; Environment Day observed (5 June) — tree planting, campus clean-up, reduced-chemical-use dialogues; exposure visit to Umswai Valley natural-farming models (30-31 May) — 23 farmers; certified paddy seed distribution (16 June) — 20 farmers, Ranjit & Bahadur varieties for Kharif season; Plant Clinic orientation (12 June) on case filing/documentation/expert linkages; PoP training on Sali Paddy (25 June) — nursery raising, organic inputs, pest control; DFO field visit (20 June) reviewing progress and exploring forestry convergence; DLSC meeting (5 May) — district-level Natural & Mixed Natural Farming (NMNF) planning at the DC office
- **Testimonial:** *"Before SuATI, we farmed the way our fathers did — unaware of the soil health we were losing. Now, I use compost from my own livestock, and my field feels alive again."* — **Rupali Das**, Farmer, Chutki Village
- **Open Questions:** No FY2021-22/2022-23 narrative data exists to establish a longer trend (only 1-2 years of programme metrics are documented); unclear whether the "500+ beneficiaries" figure in FY2023-24 and "343 farmers trained" in FY2024-25 represent the same cohort measured differently (cumulative vs. annual) or a genuine change in scale — flagged rather than assumed.

### 4. Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM)
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** A rural drinking-water access programme building community-level governance (Gram Panchayat/Village Water & Sanitation Committee/Water User Committee structures) for the operation, tariff collection, and long-term sustainability of piped water supply schemes.
- **Sector:** Relief & Rehabilitation / WASH (Water, Sanitation & Hygiene)
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation), SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities)
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None (community infrastructure/governance programme, not livelihood/occupation-based)
- **Geography (as stated):** 305 villages across 4 districts of Lower Assam; specific village-level activity named in Q1 2025 newsletter: Barkhata Nalbari, Barkhata PT3 cluster, Nagaon
- **Named Funders:** P.H.E.D. (Public Health Engineering Department), Government of Assam
- **Target groups:** Rural households in Lower Assam lacking reliable piped water access
- **Metric Trend Across Years:**

| Metric | FY2023-24 | FY2024-25 |
|---|---|---|
| Villages reached | 305 | 305 |
| Beneficiaries | 100,000+ | 100,000+ |
| Gram Panchayats | Not stated | 83 |
| Community participation activities | Awareness campaigns, need assessments | 12 (as a discrete count on the impact dashboard) |

*(The village/beneficiary count appears unchanged year-on-year — likely reflects a stable/mature-phase implementation rather than continued expansion; not explicitly confirmed as "no further growth" in the source text.)*

- **Q1 2025 (Apr-Jun newsletter) activity detail — "Grassroots Democracy in Action" feature:**
  - **Barkhata Nalbari:** community-level meeting under a tented setup; PHED officials explained technical details of the piped water scheme; residents' Q&A framed the scheme as "their water system rather than just a government initiative"
  - **Across Goalpara:** meetings emphasizing community ownership — protecting infrastructure, reporting leaks, ensuring fair use
  - **Barkhata PT3 cluster-level meetings:** multiple villages' committees shared successes, discussed common issues (e.g., water pressure), with official reassurance of continued support
  - **Community contribution:** households agreeing to contribute funds/labour for scheme maintenance, often documented via receipts, described as creating both a financial base for repairs and a "psychological investment" in protecting infrastructure
  - **Nagaon:** households now contributing via **monthly tariff collection**, managed by Village Water & Sanitation Committees, to fund routine maintenance and operator support
- **Testimonials:** None named/quoted in the source materials reviewed for this specific programme (all JJM narrative is in aggregate/village-level terms, no individual beneficiary quotes found).
- **Open Questions:** No data found on the specific number of Water User Committees (WUCs) formed/trained, nor on the total value of community tariff/contribution funds collected to date — these were described qualitatively but not quantified in either annual report or the Q1 2025 newsletter.
