# SNEHPAD — Detailed Program Area Table (with Metric Trends & Open Questions)

**Organization:** Society for North East Handmade Paper Development (SNEHPAD), Jorhat, Assam, India
**Sources used:** SNEHPAD's own Annual Reports for FY2013-14, 2014-15, 2017-18, 2018-19, 2019-20, 2020-21, 2021-22 (downloaded as PDFs from snehpad.org/annual_report.php), plus current website narrative content (snehpad.org, snehpad.linker.store) for the most recent activities. **Important limitation:** the site's links for Annual Reports 2022-23, 2023-24, and 2025-26 are broken (404 — files not actually hosted), and the "Activity Report 2024-25.pdf" that does exist is a scanned/image-only PDF with no extractable text layer. FY2015-16 and FY2016-17 reports are not published on the site at all. As a result, **the last ~4 years of activity are documented only through unreferenced/undated website snippets, not verifiable annual reports** — this is flagged per-row below and is the single biggest data gap.

A second global caveat: SNEHPAD does not publish an official SDG-alignment matrix or occupation taxonomy. The **SDG Tags** and **Occupation Taxonomy** columns below are analyst-assigned (by this report's author) based on program content, for consistency with the requested format — they should be treated as inferred, not as SNEHPAD's own classification.

| **Sector** | Livelihoods / Handloom & Textiles |
| **SDG Tags** *(inferred)* | SDG 1, SDG 5, SDG 8 |
| **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)* | Retail, Commerce & Craft → Creative & Traditional Trades, 🔴 Informal (Handloom Weavers, majority women) |
| **Geography (as stated)** | New Sonowal Missing Gaon/Moriani (2014); Silikhabari Grant, Panichokuwa, Kakajan Bon Gaon (2017-18); Kachukhat & Kamalpur Gaon, Titabor (2018-19); Sukanjan & Bekajan, Titabor (2019-20); Borhola GP (2020-21... report text says "Ajay Nagar, Titabor" for the technology training and "Borhola GP" in the secretary's note — both appear); Ajay Nagar, Titabor (2020-21 MSDP); FY2024-25 (per website): Baroghoriya Village, Sarupathar, Golaghat District |
| **Named Funders** | CSIR-NEIST (technology partner, "10-year tie-up" per 2020-21 report); NABARD–IDBI Bank (credit linkage, 2017-18); ONGC (CSR funding for newest CFC, stated on current website, year unclear) |
| **Metric Trend Across Years** | 52 weavers, 3 batches (2014-15) → 120 weavers, 3×40 (2017-18) → 80 weavers, 2×40 (2018-19) → 80 weavers, 68 shifted to Jacquard (2019-20) → 40 weavers, 32 set up Jacquard units (2020-21 technology training) + 51 trained/39 units (2020-21 MSDP) → 51 weavers, 38 acquired looms (2021-22) → cumulative claim of "more than 1,000 weavers trained" over "last 10 years" (stated in 2020-21 report) → new CFC for 200 weavers announced (undated, FY2024-25-era website/social post) → **by June 2025 (per Rang De field-visit blog): the programme has consolidated into 4 named weaving clusters across Assam, each with 200-600 women, i.e. roughly 800-2,400 women total** — by far the largest scale figure found for this program anywhere in the record |
| **Additional Details Found (beyond the standard fields)** | A June 2025 field-visit blog post by microfinance NGO **Rang De** (rangde.in), published after a partnership struck at the "Gram Samriddhi Conference," adds substantial independent, dated confirmation and detail not present in SNEHPAD's own reports: (1) **ONGC has been supporting Snehpad's handloom projects continuously since 2016**, backing "at least 100 women in each project" — this resolves the earlier open question about when ONGC funding began; (2) many of the women weavers hold **Khadi cards** (formal KVIC artisan verification) and have been "actively engaged with Snehpad since 2019," with dedicated **cluster coordinators operating since 2019**; (3) a **SFURTI grant of ₹3.5 crore** under the Ministry of MSME's cluster development programme was, as of mid-2025, "in final approval stages" — a specific, sizeable rupee figure not found in any annual report; (4) weavers are being connected to both **corporate-gifting and online-retail channels**, with **WeAct** confirmed as the ongoing online-marketing partner (WeAct previously appeared only once before, as a one-off exhibition sponsor in the FY2020-21 annual report); (5) Rang De's own site-visit assessment: "seamless operations with well-laid systems" and experienced field teams, but noted "the central office needs more managerial support" — a rare instance of external, even mildly critical, third-party due-diligence commentary on SNEHPAD's operations. |
| **Open Questions** | The stated cumulative "1,000+ weavers trained" (as of FY2020-21) does not obviously reconcile with the sum of year-by-year batch figures found in the surviving reports (~350-400 across FY2014-15 to FY2020-21) — does the gap come from untracked activity in 2005-2013 or missing 2015-16/2016-17 reports? Also unclear whether the 51 trained in 2021-22 are additive to, or overlapping with, the 80 trained in 2019-20 (same broad Titabor-area weaver pool). Separately, does the "4 clusters, 200-600 women each" figure from the 2025 Rang De blog represent genuinely new growth since the 2021-22 annual report, or is it simply the first time SNEHPAD's *total* active weaver base (as opposed to a single year's new trainees) has been reported in one place? |

## 3. Common Facility Centres (CFC) & Yarn Bank Infrastructure
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Active — newest CFC reported under construction on the current website (year of the announcement is ambiguous, see Open Questions) |
| **Description** | Decentralized, equipped production/training centres (Jacquard looms, Eri-silk spinning/reeling machines, warping drums, potter wheels, etc.) plus co-located Yarn Banks giving weavers affordable raw-material access |
| **Sector** | Livelihoods Infrastructure / Handloom & Craft |

| **Named Funders** | CSIR-NEIST (2014 CFC); Ministry of MSME / SFURTI scheme, PPDC-Agra (nodal agency), EmpowerMSME Trust-Hyderabad (technical agency) for the 2020-21 Pottery CFC; ONGC CSR (newest Handloom CFC) |
| **Metric Trend Across Years** | 1 CFC opened 2014 (covering ~150+150 weavers/artisans in Phase 1) → 1 CFC built 2020-21 (covering 128 potter families) → 1 new CFC under construction, for 200 weavers (undated recent announcement) → per the June 2025 Rang De field visit, Snehpad now runs **4 weaving clusters, each built around a Common Facility Centre equipped with Jacquard looms** |
| **Additional Details Found** | The newest Handloom CFC (Baroghoriya Village, Sarupathar, Golaghat District) is explicitly described as "the first initiative of this kind in this locality," equipped with **Jacquard Looms, Warping Drums, Eri Spinning Machines, and a Yarn Bank** — i.e., the CFC and Yarn Bank are being built as one combined facility, not two separate pieces of infrastructure, at least in this newest instance. This CFC is tied to the ONGC CSR project and to a specific sub-activity: "Eri silk yarn spinning, reeling, and weaving of designer Eri products using Jacquard handloom technology" — the first explicit mention of **Eri silk** (as opposed to cotton/generic yarn) in any SNEHPAD source reviewed. |
| **Open Questions** | No report gives a running total of how many CFCs/Yarn Banks are simultaneously operational today (the Rang De blog's "4 clusters" figure is the closest thing to a total, but doesn't confirm each cluster has its own distinct physical CFC). The organization's own social-media posts about the newest CFC ("National Handloom Day" inauguration) carry a **2026** date stamp consistently across many independent posts in the scraped content (not just one) — this looks like a systematic clock/timestamp quirk on SNEHPAD's Instagram or in how content is being served/cached, rather than a one-off typo, but the *actual* calendar date of the Baroghoriya CFC inauguration still cannot be pinned down precisely from available sources. |

This strongly suggests the "About" page's identical boilerplate paragraph is indeed a reused website template rather than six distinct, verified VDP funding relationships, and the other five funder attributions on that page should be treated with similar caution unless independently confirmed.** |

## Cross-Cutting Observations

1. **Documentation gap for recent years.** SNEHPAD's own website advertises Annual Reports for 2022-23, 2023-24, and 2025-26, but none of these files actually resolve (HTTP 404) on the server — meaning the organization's most recent, detailed self-reporting is effectively unavailable to the public. The one recent document that does exist (Activity Report 2024-25) is a scanned image PDF with no machine-readable text.
2. **Naming instability.** Several program families (entrepreneurship trainings: MEDP/REDP/MSDP/ESDP/EAP/MDP; pottery clusters: KVIC-funded vs. SFURTI-funded) are renamed or re-scoped almost every year, making true apples-to-apples, multi-year trend analysis difficult without raw underlying beneficiary lists.
3. **No official SDG/occupation taxonomy exists.** All SDG and occupation-taxonomy tags in this table are this analyst's inference from program descriptions, not SNEHPAD's own classification — flagged throughout as "(inferred)."
4. **Consistent core funders across the full 13-year span reviewed:** NABARD and SIRD/SIPRD (institutional/government-scheme side), and CSIR-NEIST and KVIC (technology/craft side), appear as partners in nearly every year's report. EDII becomes a consistent entrepreneurship partner from 2017-18 onward. Ministry of MSME (via the SFURTI scheme) becomes central from 2020-21. **A large, specific pending grant — ₹3.5 crore under a Ministry of MSME/SFURTI handloom cluster application — was in final approval as of mid-2025 (per Rang De), which if confirmed would be by far the single largest funding figure found anywhere in this research (roughly 8-25x the annual marketing-outlet sales revenue figures seen in the annual reports).**
5. **Geographic core vs. expansion claims.** Nearly all verifiable (annual-report-sourced) activity is concentrated in Jorhat district, with expansion into Golaghat and Majuli districts from around 2017-18. The only claims of activity outside this Upper Assam core — Bongaigaon (Lower Assam) and Mairang, Meghalaya (2018-19, a single MEDP batch) — are thinly documented and would benefit from direct confirmation.
6. **Website vs. third-party reporting diverge materially.** The single richest, most specific, and most independently credible dataset found on SNEHPAD's current (2025) activities did not come from SNEHPAD's own website or reports at all, but from a partner NGO's blog (Rang De). This suggests that a fuller picture of SNEHPAD's current program portfolio may require checking the websites/reports of its *partners* (ONGC CSR disclosures, KVIC/SFURTI public cluster lists, WeAct, Dhwani Foundation/RNBA) rather than relying on SNEHPAD's own (partially broken) self-reporting.

## Additional Cross-Cutting Facts Found (not tied to a single program row, but relevant background)

These items surfaced repeatedly across the annual reports and don't map neatly onto one "program," but add useful context:

- **Sales/marketing outlet revenue trend (a rough proxy for overall producer income passing through SNEHPAD):** Rs. 1,97,790 (2013-14, one-off Bhogdoi outlet figure) → Rs. 6,14,635 (2017-18) → Rs. 6,58,437 (2018-19) → **Rs. 55,67,635 (2019-20 — an ~8.5x jump, explicitly attributed to a new Amazon e-commerce tie-up)** → Rs. 9,23,129 (2020-21, own outlet only) → Rs. 7,95,776 (2021-22, own outlet only) + a separate one-day WeAct-sponsored Exhibition cum Sales Event that sold Rs. 2,00,000 (2020-21). *Open question: the FY2019-20 spike is far larger than any surrounding year — was the Amazon channel a one-time bulk/institutional order, or did it become a sustained new revenue channel? No later report mentions Amazon again, which itself is notable.*
- **Exhibitions & trade fairs attended:** Mahalaxmi SARAS Fair, Mumbai (2013-14, 15 days); International Trade Fair, Guwahati (2013-14, 12 days); Surajkund Mela, Haryana and 11th International Trade Festival, Guwahati (2014-15); WeAct-sponsored Exhibition cum Sales Event, Assam Women University (2020-21, inaugurated by the Vice Chancellor, Dr. Ashok Bhattacharjya).
- **Institutional MOUs signed (with dates, where given):** CSIR-NEIST handloom cluster MOU (25 Nov 2013); Centre for Micro Finance & Livelihood (CML) capacity-building MOU (24 Sep 2014); DRDA Jorhat MOU for the Bhogdoi Sales Outlet (4 Feb 2015); a second CML MOU (25 Mar 2015); NEDFi Representative Office established at SNEHPAD's own Jorhat premises (2018-19) to improve local credit access.
- **Staff capacity-building / international exposure:** Over the years, SNEHPAD staff attended external trainings at NIRD (Guwahati & Hyderabad), NIPCCD, IIE Guwahati, SIRD Guwahati, the Indian Institute of Foreign Trade (Delhi), and a Tata Trust programme in Bhubaneswar (2014-15); GPDP Trainers' Training at SIRD and Micro-Entrepreneurship Trainers' Training at EDII Bangalore (2017-18). Foreign delegations from **Denmark and Japan** visited SNEHPAD's project sites in 2017-18 to study rural Assamese culture and economic traditions — the only documented international visibility event found.
- **Governance/management continuity:** The Managing Committee's composition is strikingly stable across every report reviewed (2013-14 through the current site): Sri Prakash Thakur has been Secretary throughout the org's history (from founding Vice-turned-Secretary role in 2005 to present); by the 20th-anniversary (2024-25) period, Dr. M.P. Borthakur (Retired Professor, Assam Agricultural University) is Chairman, with Dr. Gakul Sarmah (Retired Veterinary Surgeon) and Sri Jibon Gohain (Retired Banker) as Vice-Chairmen — Dr. Gakul Sarmah had himself been Chairman as recently as the FY2020-21 report, suggesting a Chairman transition sometime between FY2021-22 and the 20th-anniversary materials.
- **Internship programme:** A recurring, small-scale activity — MSW (Master of Social Work) students from Kaziranga University placed for ~1 month each: 6 students (2018-19), 5 students (2019-20), 2 students (2020-21); more recently, an internship programme is also mentioned for students of Cinnamara College (undated, current website).
- **Awards & recognition:** National Award, 2010, for Best Rural Industries Service Centre (RISC) — the organization's only formal national award identified; ISO certification (undated); NNE-1 OCM Certificate of Completion with an 87.5% score (undated, recent).
