# MOSONiE Socio-Economic Foundation — Program Areas

> Part of a multi-file research set. See `MOSONiE_Organization_Profile.md` for org/governance info and `MOSONiE_Additional_Notes_and_Sources.md` for open questions and sources. The full text of 4 of the 6 downloaded annual reports (FY2021-22, FY2022-23, FY2023-24, FY2024-25) has been read and merged in below, giving genuine year-by-year metric trends for almost every program. **FY2019-20 and FY2020-21 remain unread** — those two report PDFs are scanned/image-only with no extractable text layer (OCR not available in this setup), so there is a two-year gap in the trend lines below between "programme launch" and FY2021-22. This document is updated continuously as new information is found.

The current website groups MOSONiE's work under two broad tabs — **"Health and Hygiene"** and **"Livelihood"** — but effectively presents nine distinct, named programs. Full detail below.

---

### 1. Community Health Camps
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Free health camps bringing basic medical check-ups, consultations, health screenings, and awareness sessions on preventive healthcare, hygiene, and nutrition to remote villages in Ri-Bhoi District with limited medical facilities.
- **Sector:** Health
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 3
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None
- **Geography (as stated):** Remote villages, Ri-Bhoi District, Meghalaya
- **Named Funders:** Not specified in the source text reviewed
- **Target groups:** Women, children, the elderly, and economically vulnerable households
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** No dedicated "Community Health Camps" section with its own headcount was found in any of the 4 fully-read annual reports (FY2021-22 through FY2024-25) — this suggests the website's "Community Health Camps" program label may be a broader/rebranded umbrella for what the annual reports actually itemize as several distinct health activities: **COVID-19 vaccination drives, mental health awareness sessions, and the "Awareness on Mental Health" event** (see below). No report contains a generic, year-by-year "X health camps held / Y patients seen" figure.
- **Related, more specific health activity found (FY2022-23):** *Awareness on Mental Health* — organized in collaboration with **District Mental Health Programme (DMHP), Nongpoh**, held at Umtrynga Village on **30 January 2023**; resource person **Ms. Daphilarisa**, a Psychiatrist from DMHP Nongpoh, who covered mental illness signs/symptoms, common types, and prevention/treatment; open to participants from neighbouring villages (no headcount given).
- **Open Questions:** Is "Community Health Camps" (website) simply a rebrand/umbrella term covering the mental-health-awareness and vaccination-drive activities documented in the annual reports (see Programs 10 and 11 below), or is it a genuinely separate, general-medical-checkup activity not yet captured in any report read so far (possible it's covered in the two unreadable FY2019-20/FY2020-21 reports)?

### 2. Energy for Health: Operations & Maintenance (O&M)
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Supports the smooth functioning and long-term sustainability of solar energy systems installed in **all government healthcare facilities across Ri-Bhoi District** — regular maintenance, monitoring, and repair to ensure reliable electricity for lighting, vaccine refrigeration, medical equipment, and emergency care. Also promotes local capacity building through technical support and training.
- **Sector:** Health / Clean Energy Infrastructure
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 3, SDG 7
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None (infrastructure/institutional program)
- **Geography (as stated):** All government healthcare facilities, Ri-Bhoi District, Meghalaya
- **Named Funders / Partners:** Implemented **"in partnership with the State and District Health Departments and SELCO Foundation."**
- **Metric Trend Across Years (now confirmed with real figures):** Program described in the FY2024-25 report as running **"since 2022."** **FY2023-24: 49 health centres** monitored/maintained across Ri-Bhoi District. **FY2024-25: 47 health centres** monitored across **4 blocks** of Ri-Bhoi District — a slight decrease from 49 to 47 (likely normal fluctuation in active-facility count rather than a real program contraction). FY2024-25 specifics: MOSONiE, in partnership with SELCO Foundation, conducted **"Community Sensitization and Asset Ownership Meetings"** at **Mawlyndep HWC (Umsning Block)** and **Mawdem CHC (Jirang Block)** — introducing communities to the installed solar systems, promoting 24x7 awareness, and encouraging local ownership. Participants included village heads, teachers, healthcare workers, and residents. Forward-looking action points identified: strengthening security for solar assets, training local technicians for routine maintenance, and establishing a structured community-contribution model for upkeep.
- **Open Questions:** The "since 2022" start date for this specific O&M program is now confirmed, but it's unclear whether this is when MOSONiE first began working with SELCO on health-centre solar systems generally, or specifically when the formal "monitoring" role began (the broader Clean Energy/DRE partnership with SELCO started in 2017 — see Program 4).

### 3. Menstrual Health Management & Promotion of Eco-Friendly Sanitary Cloth Pads
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Community awareness programmes, school-based education initiatives, and promotion/distribution of affordable, eco-friendly (reusable cloth) menstrual hygiene products, targeting the "border villages" of Ri-Bhoi District. Includes building capacity of teachers and community members.
- **Sector:** Health / Gender & Rights / Education
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 3, SDG 5
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None
- **Geography (as stated):** Border villages, Ri-Bhoi District, Meghalaya
- **Named Funders / Partners (now confirmed, evolving over the years):** **SHUDDHI organization** (FY2021-22 partner); **Azim Premji Foundation (APF)** (funder for the FY2023-24 menstrual-hygiene component, delivered as part of the broader "Enhancing Income in 10 Tribal Villages" 3-year program — see Program 12); **BAALA** (reusable sanitary pad brand/project, explicitly named as a co-partner in FY2024-25 alongside MOSONiE, per a named beneficiary's testimonial — see below).
- **Metric Trend Across Years (now confirmed with real figures):**
  - **FY2021-22: 140 reusable sanitary pads** distributed; awareness sessions held in collaboration with SHUDDHI at **Roman Catholic Upper Primary School (RCUP), Umtrynga Presbyterian School, and Jymgkyrem School**. Separately, on **International Women's Day (FY2021-22)**, in collaboration with **Young Indians**, an event at Nongspung C Community Hall (theme: "Gender equality for sustainable tomorrow") reached **50 women and adolescent girls**, with **Mrs. Suman Jain, Rural Chairman of YI**, demonstrating eco-friendly pad use.
  - **FY2022-23: 800+ free reusable sanitary pads** distributed among rural women and adolescent girls (IEC materials also developed/printed/distributed).
  - **FY2023-24: 30 menstrual hygiene awareness sessions** conducted, **2,000 women and adolescents** reached in total across Ri-Bhoi District (funded by Azim Premji Foundation); one specific session reached **150 women and adolescent girls**.
  - **FY2024-25: 36 awareness sessions** conducted across **29 villages**; **2,000 menstrual hygiene kits** distributed to women/adolescent girls; **6 in-house MOSONiE staff trained** as Menstrual Health Management (MHM) trainers.
  - **This is one of the clearest, most consistent year-over-year growth trends found across all of MOSONiE's programs:** 140 (FY21-22) → 800+ (FY22-23) → 2,000 (FY23-24) → 2,000 kits + expanded to 29 villages / 36 sessions (FY24-25).
- **Testimonials (named individuals):**
  - **Ritalish Syngkli**, a Teacher, quoted on the website: *"MOSONiE's menstrual hygiene program changed lives in our village. Girls now understand their health, have access to reusable sanitary pads, and can manage their periods with confidence and dignity."*
  - **Vishakha Shadap**, 39, a teacher at Umtyrnga Presbyterian School with 20+ years' experience (FY2024-25 report success story): previously used cotton cloth, then switched to disposable pads due to leakage (despite feeling guilty about environmental impact); now uses **BAALA reusable sanitary pads** introduced by "MOSONiE SEF & Project BAALA," praising their softness and ease of washing, and is now promoting them to students and friends.
- **Open Questions:** With the FY2022-25 trend now well-documented, the main remaining gap is FY2019-20/FY2020-21 (unreadable reports) — was the program active pre-2021-22, and if so at what scale?

### 4. Building Sustainable Energy Eco-system through Clean Energy Solutions
- **Status:** Active — this is MOSONiE's flagship, most externally-documented program.
- **Description:** Solar-powered livelihood solutions for farmers, women entrepreneurs, and small business owners, addressing erratic/unreliable grid electricity. Combines technology, capacity building, and linkages to financial and government support systems.
- **Sector:** Livelihoods / Clean Energy (Decentralized Renewable Energy, DRE)
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 7, SDG 8
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Retail, Commerce & Craft / Agritech & Allied — Energy-enabled Micro-enterprise, 🔴 Informal
- **Geography (as stated):** Rural communities across Ri-Bhoi District, Meghalaya; specific villages named in third-party coverage include **Nartap** and **Warmasaw** (both Ri-Bhoi), and **Liarkhla village, Bhoirymbong administrative block**
- **Named Funders / Partners:** **SELCO Foundation** (co-implementer since program inception); banks and village organizations (for beneficiary loan linkages); a 2022 **Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE)** national framework is cited as encouraging this class of solar livelihood tools

**Sub-activities documented in detail via independent journalism:**
- **Solar-powered Eri silk spinning machines** — introduced by MOSONiE in collaboration with SELCO Foundation starting in **2017** (per VillageSquare/CEEW) or **2018** (per an Instagram/CSP post) — sources give slightly different start years.
- **Solar-powered rice hullers** — e.g., the Nartap village unit installed by farmer-entrepreneur couple **Ioanis/Ioanis Kurbah (37)** and **Jenita Jyrwa (35)**, with 12 solar panels (10kW capacity) in their backyard, MOSONiE + SELCO covering ~85% of costs (~₹3.8 lakh) via subsidy and the couple taking a ₹68,000 zero-interest SHG loan for the remaining 15%; the machine processes rice for 85+ of Nartap's 92 households at ₹20/5kg tin (+₹10 if husk is kept), generating ₹18,000-40,000/month for the operators, and has already fully repaid its loan.

- **Metric Trend Across Years — NOW FULLY CONFIRMED with a genuine multi-year trend line from the annual reports (this resolves the Pass-1 open question about program scale):**
  - **Program launch confirmed: 2017**, in **Umling Block**, starting with **just 3 end-users** as a demonstration — 3 Basic Home Lighting Systems (each: 3 lights of 3W & 5W, 1 DC table fan, mobile-charging point, 60AH battery, 75 WP panel) installed in **3 villages: Jolsyiem, Umlathu, and Jorbil**. *(This confirms 2017, not 2018, resolving the Pass-1 discrepancy between VillageSquare and the Instagram/CSP post.)*
  - **As of FY2022-23 (cumulative since 2017): 138 home lighting systems + 56 livelihood solutions** installed across **3 blocks** (Jirang, Umling, Bhoiryngbong). New FY2022-23 livelihood-solution types introduced that year: integrated solar-powered water pump (piggery), Eri spinning machines, LSK (photocopy machine), solar-powered bakery/eatery + Pitha-making equipment, and poultry-farm street lighting. Installation contractors: **Envo Renewable Energy Services Pvt. Ltd. (ERES)** and **Soltech Solar (OPC) Pvt. Ltd.**, in collaboration with SELCO Foundation; end-users linked to **Meghalaya Rural Bank (MRB)** for loans. Ecosystem-building partners that year: MSRLS, District Sericulture Department, Central Silk Board.
  - **FY2023-24: 170 new livelihood solutions + 30 new home lighting systems** installed that year alone, now across **4 blocks** (adding Umsning). Three dedicated **eri-spinning centres plus a MOSONiE Resource Centre** were inaugurated (August-September 2023) across Jirang, Umling, and Bhoirymbong, benefiting **118 individuals** in eri-silk processing and egg incubation. Two capacity-building trainings on solar-powered eri-silk spinning reached **60 women** (in collaboration with Central Silk Board and the Sericulture Department). Two exposure visits for **65 end-users** were organized to the Eri Weave Kdonghulu village cluster and Kineho Eri Enterprise Centre. A financial-literacy camp in Warmawsaw village reached **40 participants** (with Meghalaya Rural Bank and MSRLS). A solar-powered egg-incubator training (14 & 23 February 2024) reached **37 participants**.
  - **FY2024-25 (cumulative total, per the dedicated "MOSONiE Impact Metrics" page): 534 total livelihood-based DRE solutions installed** — broken down as **388 livelihood solutions + 146 home lighting systems**. This is the clearest headline cumulative figure found in any MOSONiE report.
  - **Reconciling the cumulative math:** 138 home-lighting + 56 livelihood (end of FY22-23) → +30 home-lighting + 170 livelihood (FY23-24) = 168 home-lighting + 226 livelihood by end of FY23-24 → 146 home-lighting + 388 livelihood by FY24-25 (per the cumulative figure). The home-lighting count (168 → 146) appears to **decrease**, which is odd for a cumulative figure — possibly some systems were decommissioned/replaced, or the FY2024-25 figure uses a different counting methodology (e.g., "currently active" vs. "ever installed"). Flagged as an open question below.
  - **Independent-journalism figures (VillageSquare/CEEW, Instagram/CSP), not clearly dated to a specific fiscal year:** 250 households / 117 eri-spinning micro-enterprises; 3 training centres for eri-spinning (this may correspond to the FY2023-24 "three eri-spinning centres" figure above — likely the same 3 centres, described slightly differently by the two sources). Per-spinner productivity: yarn output increased from **1 kg/month (traditional *takli*) to up to 4 kg/month** with the solar machine; **Ritngen Lamare** (Liarkhla village) went from 1 kg per two months to 2-4 kg/month. **Average income for women spinners rose from ₹1,200/month to ₹7,000/month** (VillageSquare figure) — a separate, even higher FY2024-25 named-beneficiary case (see "Linda's Journey" below) reports income rising from ₹2,500 to ₹10,000/month, suggesting continued productivity gains after the original VillageSquare article was published. Eri-spinning machine unit cost: **₹25,000-35,000**.
  - **Eri-Silk Spinning training specifics (FY2021-22):** Since 2017, MOSONiE trained eri-silk spinners on the solar machine in collaboration with **Central Silk Board** and **Resham Sutra Pvt. Ltd.**, across three locations — **Warmawsaw, Diwon, and Bhoiringbom villages**. Resource persons: **Kunal Das (Resham Sutra Pvt. Ltd., Delhi)** and **Dipali (Central Silk Board, Khanapara)**. **43 women** received training input that year.
  - **New named success story (FY2024-25 report): Linda's Journey towards Eri Silk** — before MOSONiE's intervention, Linda sold raw cocoons at ₹700/kg; spinning manually via *takli* took ~1.5 months to produce 1 kg of yarn. After the solar-powered machine, she now spins **5-6 kg/month**, raising her income from **₹2,500 to ₹10,000/month**. She has since become a **master trainer**, training **over 100 women** in eri-silk spinning — one of the strongest individual "graduation" stories found in any of the research files to date (beneficiary → trainer).

- **Named beneficiaries/success stories:**
  - **Jacinta Maslai** (Warmasaw village) — farmer's wife who took up solar eri-spinning to supplement farm income and now pays for children's education from spinning income.
  - **Ritngen Lamare** (Liarkhla village) — whose injured son took up eri-spinning as his livelihood after being unable to do outdoor work.
  - **Theilyn Syngkli (60)**, Nartap — benefits from the solar rice huller, no longer needs the manual bamboo-tray (*pdung* and *prah*) winnowing method that caused her chronic back/shoulder pain.
  - **Thomas Syngkli (80)** and his son **Nicholas Jyrwa (49)** — earlier operators of a diesel rice huller in Nartap, both suffered respiratory/eye irritation from diesel fumes and husk dust, now made redundant by the solar unit (diesel-huller owners are described as "running into losses because no one uses them anymore").

- **Technical detail:** Diesel huller comparison — a diesel machine cost ~₹17,500 (year 2000 purchase price), used up to 25 litres of diesel/month (≈64 kg carbon emissions), cost ~₹3,000/month in fuel, and required 70-80 litres of water/hour for cooling (piped from a natural cave uphill in Nartap). The solar alternative runs cleanly, quietly, needs no water for cooling, but has lower output in winter (Dec-Feb, shorter daylight) and requires spare parts (blades/screens) sourced from Jorhat, Assam (~350 km away, ~₹1,000 per order including courier).

- **Named experts quoted:** **Kakali Kalita** (SELCO Foundation) — on flexible working hours and post-installation O&M being "our biggest hurdles" in remote areas (often 2x costlier than accessible locations); **Dr. Rajshri Shukla** (Just Transition Research Centre, IIT-Kanpur) — on solar vs. diesel sustainability and "just transformation."

- **Related sector data point:** A Council on Energy, Environment and Water (CEEW) report estimates **63,000+ livelihoods in Meghalaya** could be improved through decentralized renewable energy (DRE) — cited as the sectoral opportunity context for MOSONiE's work, not a MOSONiE-specific claim.

- **Open Questions (updated):** The 2017 vs. 2018 start-year discrepancy is now resolved in favor of **2017** (confirmed three separate times: FY2021-22 report, FY2022-23 report, and FY2024-25 report all independently state 2017). The remaining open question is the **home-lighting-system count appearing to decrease** between the implied FY2023-24 cumulative total (168) and the FY2024-25 stated cumulative total (146) — is this a real net decommissioning of ~22 systems, or a change in what counts as a "home lighting system" vs. "livelihood solution" between report years (e.g., some systems reclassified from one bucket to the other)? Also, the independent-journalism "250 households / 117 enterprises" figure still cannot be pinned to a specific fiscal year — it sits somewhere between the FY2022-23 (194 total: 138+56) and FY2023-24 (394 total: 168+226) cumulative figures, suggesting it was likely published based on FY2022-23 or early-FY2023-24 data.

### 5. Community Development and School Engagement Initiatives
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Two named sub-initiatives:
  - **Cloth for Work (CFW):** Community members contribute time/effort to village-benefiting activities (improving public spaces, maintaining community assets); participating households receive "essential family support packages" in recognition.
  - **School-to-School (S2S):** Creates a positive learning environment via student-led activities (classroom cleanliness drives, tree plantation, drawing competitions, other educational programmes); also establishes **community libraries** for children, youth, and adults.
- **Sector:** Community Development / Education
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 4, SDG 11, SDG 17
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None
- **Geography (as stated):** Ri-Bhoi District, Meghalaya — specifically Jirang, Umling, and Bhoirymbong Blocks (confirmed across years; Umling first, then expanded)
- **Named Funders (confirmed): GOONJ Organization** — funds both CFW and S2S, plus the related "Community/School Library" and "Distribution of Community Health Kit to ASHAs" activities, across every year reviewed (FY2021-22 through FY2024-25).
- **Metric Trend Across Years (now fully confirmed):**
  - **CFW start date confirmed: August 2022**, launched in **3 villages, Umling Block**.
  - **FY2022-23: 850 family-package bags** distributed across **15 far-flung remote villages** in Jirang and Umling Blocks. Activities carried out under CFW that year: repairing roads, cleaning unused wells/ponds, digging water-harvesting wells, building bamboo toilets/bathrooms/dustbins, fencing (public areas, ICDS Centres), drainage/sanitation work, kitchen gardens. Village leaders (Headman, Secretary), Youth, ASHA/AWW workers actively participated; 1-2 members per household participated for 2 days per activity cycle.
  - **FY2023-24: 1,400 family-package bags** distributed across **24 far-flung remote villages**, now spanning **3 blocks** (Jirang, Umling, **and Bhoirymbong** — newly added). S2S that year included pottery-making with mugs and drawing competitions on "village maps," with school bags/essential items given as gifts.
  - **FY2024-25: 800 CFW kits distributed across 39 villages**; separately, **985 School-to-School (S2S) kits distributed across 14 schools**. (Note: the FY2024-25 report text has a slight internal redundancy, repeating the "800 CFW kits...39 villages" figure twice on the same page — likely a copy-paste artifact in the report rather than two different figures.)
  - **Community/School Library (FY2022-23 specific):** Free books (story books, dictionaries, encyclopedias, handwriting/picture books) donated by GOONJ to **2 schools**: St. Peter School and Patharkhmah Higher Secondary School, Jirang.
  - **Distribution of Community Health Kits to ASHAs (FY2022-23, also GOONJ-funded):** Kits containing ORS, Paracetamol, and digital thermometers distributed to ASHA workers.
  - **Trend summary:** CFW kits/packages grew from 850 (FY22-23, 15 villages) → 1,400 (FY23-24, 24 villages) → 800 (FY24-25, but now spread across a much larger 39 villages, implying a lower per-village intensity that year) — the FY24-25 dip in total kits despite village-count growth is worth flagging (see Open Questions).
- **Open Questions:** Why did total CFW kit numbers fall from 1,400 (FY23-24) to 800 (FY24-25) even as the village count nearly doubled (24 → 39)? Did the "kit" itself change in composition/cost, or did GOONJ's funding allocation shrink that year? Also, how many schools/libraries exist cumulatively under S2S — the reports give per-year school counts (2 in FY22-23, 14 in FY24-25) but never a running cumulative total.

### 6. Environmental Conservation and Agroforestry
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Tree plantation initiatives promoting fruit-bearing and other useful tree species, integrating tree-based farming with existing agricultural practices to diversify household food/income sources while restoring green cover and biodiversity.
- **Sector:** Environment / Agroforestry / Livelihoods
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 13, SDG 15, SDG 1
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied Ecosystem → Agroforestry, 🔴 Informal
- **Geography (as stated):** Tribal households in **Meghalaya and Manipur** — this is the one program explicitly stated to extend beyond Ri-Bhoi/Meghalaya into Manipur
- **Named Funders / Partners (confirmed, multiple over the years):** **Young Indians (Guwahati Chapter)** and **Round Table** organization (FY2021-22 fruit-sapling distribution, as a COVID-19 response); **GOONJ** (FY2022-23 fruit-sapling distribution); a separate, undated **Tree Plantation Drive at Nongthymmai Village VO Community Land** was also run with Young Indians Guwahati Chapter, distributing Banana and Black Pepper saplings to SHG members.
- **Metric Trend Across Years (now confirmed with real figures):**
  - **FY2021-22: 700 fruit-tree saplings** (Banana, Pears, Litchi, Orange, Lemon, Coconut, Jamun) distributed to **4 villages, Umling Block** — explicitly framed as a **COVID-19 response** ("to build a Green and Clean planet and bring sustainable livelihood"), in collaboration with Young Indian and Round Table. First plantation drive held at **Umtrynga Village**, attended by Block Development Officer **Mr. Roderick Nongrum** (Umling Block), Young Indian team, Village Headman/Secretary, ASHA workers, and the Umtynga School Principal.
  - **FY2022-23:** Fruit saplings (Guava, Lemon, Gooseberry, Orange, Banana, Plum, Peach, Mango, Jackfruit) provided to **4 villages in Jirang Block**, in collaboration with **GOONJ**. World Environment Day tree-plantation drive held **6 June 2022**, jointly with teachers/students from **Patharkhmah Government Higher Secondary School** and **Umkynshier Government UP School**. Saplings distributed to **48 farmers**.
  - **Homepage cumulative claim:** **50,000+ trees planted** (undated, likely aggregating this program plus the Soh Marit black pepper/banana saplings — see cross-reference note below).
- **Open Questions:** The year-by-year sapling counts found in the annual reports (700 in FY21-22, plus an unspecified number to 48 farmers in FY22-23) are far too small to add up to "50,000+ trees" on their own — this strongly suggests the homepage's 50,000+ figure **is** substantially driven by the separately-reported Soh Marit black pepper/banana plantings (1,300 black pepper + 430-420 banana saplings/year in FY23-24 and FY24-25 — see Program 9) plus possibly other tree-planting activity in the two unread (FY2019-20/FY2020-21) reports. The two programs (this one and Soh Marit) should probably be considered a **combined agroforestry effort** for impact-counting purposes, even though they are presented as separate line items in the annual reports.

### 7. Livestock Activities
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Support for marginalized households, particularly women and small farmers, in piggery, poultry, and other traditional livestock-rearing practices — training, technical guidance, and inputs to improve animal husbandry, animal health, and farm management. Promotes integrated farming by linking livestock rearing with kitchen gardening and agroforestry.
- **Sector:** Livelihoods / Agri-Allied
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 2, SDG 8
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied Ecosystem → Livestock & Animal Husbandry, 🔴 Informal
- **Geography (as stated):** **10 tribal villages, Umling Block, Ri-Bhoi District** — this program is now confirmed to be run under a formal, named umbrella: **"Enhancing Income and Improving Menstrual Hygiene in 10 Tribal Villages"** — a **3-year program (2023-2026)** targeting **1,100 households**, funded by **Azim Premji Foundation (APF)**. (Its menstrual-hygiene component is covered under Program 3 above; this entry covers the livestock/piggery livelihood component.)
- **Named Funders / Partners (confirmed):** **Azim Premji Foundation (APF)** (program funder); **Office of the Assistant Director, Vocational Training Centre, Kyrdemkulai, Animal Husbandry & Veterinary Department, Government of Meghalaya** (training delivery partner); Village Organizations (VOs) as the loan-disbursement channel.
- **Metric Trend Across Years (now confirmed with detailed figures):**
  - **FY2022-23 (earlier, separate poultry activity, likely a precursor):** One-day capacity-building training on **backyard poultry rearing and management**, delivered by **Retd. Professor Krishna Sharma** (College of Veterinary Science, Khanapara — also a MOSONiE Board Member/Director), held 12 December 2022 at the MOSONiE-SEF Office, Latakata. Farmers from **4 villages** (Behenanodi, Nongspung-A, Nongthymmai, Chibra) participated; **250 free chicks** distributed.
  - **FY2023-24: 140 farmers trained** in scientific pig rearing (piggery focus, in collaboration with the Vocational Training Centre, Kyrdemkulai). **103 of the 140** went on to adopt piggery by taking a loan from their Village Organization (VO); MOSONiE provided a **Revolving Fund of ₹4,500 per trained member**. Separately, a **4-batch sericulture/eri-silk processing training reached 37 women** (targeting daily-wage earners/marginal farmers already producing 15-20 kg of cocoons/year but lacking yarn-processing skills), delivered with the **Department of Textiles, Nongpoh** and **Resham Sutra**, supported by APF and SELCO Foundation.
  - **FY2024-25 (per the dedicated "MOSONiE Impact Metrics" page — the most detailed livestock figures found in any report):**
    - **119 farmers trained** in scientific pig rearing.
    - **104 of the 119 (87%) adopted piggery** as a livelihood.
    - **₹4,98,000 (₹4.98 lakh) disbursed as a revolving fund**, across **9 Village Organizations**, in **3 batches**, at ₹4,500/farmer.
    - **96 farmers linked to piggery insurance** (a slightly different figure — 95 or 96 — appears in two places in the same report; treated as the same figure, likely a minor internal rounding/typo).
    - **16 community volunteers trained as community vaccinators** (administering medicines, vaccines, ear-tagging for insurance).
    - Veterinary support: program participants linked to the veterinary department for ongoing medication/vaccination.
  - **Named success story (FY2024-25): Neina's Journey towards Pig Rearing** — Neina Sangma (34), Mawiong village, a housewife with no prior pig-rearing experience, attended MOSONiE training, received ₹4,500 to purchase a piglet, reared it for 9 months, and sold it for **₹18,000** — repaying her loan, covering household expenses, and buying a new piglet with the profit. Notably had the "biggest pig in her batch." Continues to receive MOSONiE veterinary support on call.
  - **Trend summary:** Poultry-focused pilot (250 chicks, 4 villages, FY22-23) → formalized into the APF-funded, 10-village, 1,100-household, 3-year piggery program: 140 trained/103 adopted (FY23-24) → 119 trained/104 adopted, ₹4.98 lakh revolving fund, 96 insured, 16 vaccinators (FY24-25).
- **Open Questions:** Why did the trained-farmer count drop slightly from 140 (FY23-24) to 119 (FY24-25) while the adopter count rose slightly (103 → 104)? This produces a notably higher adoption *rate* in FY24-25 (87% vs. 74%) — is this a sign of improving program targeting/quality, or simply a smaller, more pre-qualified cohort in year 2 of the 3-year program? Since this is a 2023-2026 program, one more annual report (FY2025-26, not yet published) should show whether the 1,100-household target is reached.

### 8. Relief Supplies
- **Status:** Active (appears to be an ongoing welfare line, not a single time-bound disaster response — distinct from a COVID-specific program, though COVID relief may fall under this umbrella; to be confirmed from annual reports)
- **Description:** Distribution of essential food and household supplies to vulnerable individuals/families facing economic hardship, specifically **widows, single-parent households, Persons with Disabilities (PwDs), elderly individuals**, and other economically disadvantaged families, particularly those dependent on irregular daily-wage income.
- **Sector:** Humanitarian / Welfare
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 10
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None
- **Geography (as stated):** **Jirang and Umling Blocks, Ri-Bhoi District** (COVID-19 relief, FY2021-22); **Timbokgre Village, Umling Block** (walking-stick distribution, FY2022-23)
- **Named Funders / Partners (now confirmed):** **SELCO Foundation, Young Indians, Round Table, Sustain Plus Energy Foundation** (COVID-19 ration/hygiene-kit relief, FY2021-22); **Young Indians Guwahati Chapter** (walking-stick distribution for the aged, FY2022-23).
- **Metric Trend Across Years (now confirmed — this program is indeed the umbrella for MOSONiE's COVID-19 relief work, resolving the Pass-1 open question):**
  - **FY2021-22 (COVID-19 relief, in Jirang and Umling Blocks):** **Dry ration distributed to 638 families**; **hygiene kits distributed to 1,100 individuals**. Dry ration contents: rice, dal, potato, soya beans, salt, onion, turmeric powder, tea leaf, sugar, noodles, oil. Hygiene kit contents: detergent powder, bathing soap bar, mask, hand sanitizer. Explicitly targeted at families without ration cards, the old-aged, orphans, widows, and disabled persons.
  - **FY2021-22 (related, separate activity): Vegetable seed distribution** — **150 farmers** benefited with **10 varieties of vegetable seeds**, covering **12 villages** in Jirang and Umling Blocks — funded/supported by **SELCO Foundation** specifically as a way to help farmers maintain food/income access despite COVID-19 movement restrictions cutting off market access.
  - **FY2022-23:** A **walking stick** was provided to an aged resident of **Timbokgre Village, Umling Block**, supported by **Young Indians Guwahati Chapter** — a much smaller-scale, non-food relief activity, suggesting the program's scope broadened from pure emergency (COVID) relief into general "home-based services to the aged."
- **Open Questions:** This program's scale dropped enormously from FY2021-22 (638 families + 1,100 individuals, a genuine mass emergency response) to a single walking stick in FY2022-23 — this is best explained by the end of the acute COVID-19 emergency (the 2021-22 relief was explicitly pandemic-driven), rather than a real program failure, but the annual reports don't explicitly say the program "ended" — it's simply not mentioned at comparable scale in FY2023-24 or FY2024-25. Is there an ongoing, smaller "welfare/relief" line for the elderly/disabled that just isn't prominently reported anymore?

### 9. Soh Marit: Black Pepper for Sustainable Livelihoods
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Supports farmers in reviving homestead gardens and cultivating high-value crops — **areca nut, black pepper, and banana** — as a way to improve productivity, diversify income, and strengthen long-term livelihood security in the face of small landholdings and daily-wage dependence. Farmers receive training and technical support on improved cultivation practices. The programme also encourages **farmer collectivization** to strengthen market linkages and bargaining power. "Soh Marit" is Khasi for "black pepper."
- **Sector:** Livelihoods / Agri-Allied
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 2, SDG 8
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied Ecosystem → Cultivation & Sourcing, 🔴 Informal (Small/Marginal Farmers)
- **Geography (as stated):** **Umling Block, Ri-Bhoi District, Meghalaya** — specific villages named across years: **Nongspung-A and Timbokgre** (FY2022-23); **Behenanodi, Chibra, and Umtrynga** (newly added, FY2023-24)
- **Named Funders (now confirmed — this resolves the Pass-1 open question, and independently confirms a key finding from the separate Diya Foundation research):**
  - **FY2022-23: "IDeA-the ant Organization"** provided an **Incubation Grant** for the project.
  - **FY2023-24 onward: "Roots to Branches Foundation"** is named as the supporting funder for the same Soh Marit project.
  - **This is the same transition independently documented in this research project's Diya Foundation files:** Diya Foundation's internal "IDeA" institutional-capacity-building program was discontinued and spun off as the independent "Roots to Branches Foundation" — and here, from a completely different NGO's (MOSONiE's) own annual reports, we can see that exact transition happening in real time as a funding-relationship rename between FY2022-23 and FY2023-24.
- **Program launch year confirmed: 2022** (per the FY2024-25 report: "In 2022, MOSONiE set out to revive homestead gardens in Umling Block...").
- **Metric Trend Across Years (now confirmed with detailed figures):**
  - **FY2022-23 (program launch year): 2 villages** (Nongspung-A, Timbokgre) selected; **80 households** covered in a baseline survey; **30 farmers/households identified**; **40 farmers/households** ultimately covered under the initiative. Activities: PALMS (Participatory Learning Methods) exercises, farmer consultation meetings, farmer exposure visit to a Resource Institute.
  - **FY2023-24: 21 new project participants enlisted in 3 new villages** (Behenanodi, Chibra, Umtrynga), with a new baseline survey conducted. **1,300 black pepper saplings + 430 banana saplings distributed to 29 farmers.** A one-day exposure visit to the **District Horticulture Farm, Byrnihat** (28 April 2023) involved **17 participants** from Nongspung-A and Timbokgre villages, plus MSW interns from **Assam Don Bosco University (ADBU)**. A household-level holistic-planning consultation meeting was held at Timbokgre Village (20 March 2024) with **19 farmers**, facilitated by **Romen Rabha of Diya Foundation** as the resource person (covering income/expenditure planning, alternative income sources like areca nut harvesting, and poultry-based entrepreneurship ideas).
  - **FY2024-25: 50 farmers across 5 villages** received capacity-building training and input supplies; **1,300 black pepper saplings + 420 banana saplings** distributed (near-identical to the FY2023-24 sapling count, suggesting a standardized annual input package per cohort rather than genuinely new/expanding volumes); **720 kg of organic manure** distributed to **41 farmers**.
  - **Named success story (FY2024-25): Mr. Beldish Sangma's Journey** — a 46-year-old farmer previously reliant on inconsistent daily-wage labour and mixed cash crops (brinjal, bitter gourd, pumpkin, bottle gourd, betel nut/leaves); joined Soh Marit in 2022, trained in black pepper cultivation (site selection, planting, irrigation, mulching, vermicompost-making, pest management); within one year his banana plants began bearing fruit, generating income and improving his family's living conditions; now planning to expand both banana and black pepper plantation.
  - **Trend summary:** 2 villages/40 households (FY22-23) → 3 new villages/21 new participants + 1,300 black pepper & 430 banana saplings to 29 farmers (FY23-24) → 5 villages total/50 farmers + 1,300 black pepper & 420 banana saplings + 720kg manure to 41 farmers (FY24-25) — a clear, credible, steadily-scaling program.
- **Open Questions:** The near-identical 1,300 black pepper sapling figure in both FY2023-24 and FY2024-25 (to different-sized farmer cohorts — 29 vs. 41-50) suggests this may be a fixed annual procurement/distribution batch size rather than a per-farmer-scaled figure — worth clarifying directly with MOSONiE. Also see the cross-reference in Program 6 (Environmental Conservation) about whether Soh Marit's saplings are being double-counted within the website's "50,000+ trees planted" headline claim.

### 10. MOSONiE NCPE School (Non-Conventional Practical Education)
- **Status:** Active as of FY2021-22 (the only year this program is documented in the reports read); not mentioned on the current website at all — a significant gap, since this appears to be a genuine, long-running school.
- **Description:** An alternative, English-medium school providing practical education from the primary level, in "advance subjects which have high scope of employment and earning" — namely **Agriculture, Environment, and Health** — delivered in a "hobby form of learning environment." Framed as giving out-of-school children a form of education they could not otherwise access.
- **Sector:** Education
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 4
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None
- **Geography (as stated):** **Timbokgre Village**, Ri-Bhoi District, Meghalaya — a village of **50 households, population 233** (113 male, 120 female), with **no schools** and a literacy rate so low that "there is not even a single Graduate" in the village, as of the report's writing.
- **Named Funders / Partners:** **SELCO Foundation** — funds the "Digital Education Program (DEP)" sub-component (see below).
- **Program start date confirmed: October 2016** — making this one of MOSONiE's longest-running programs (predating even the 2017 Clean Energy/DRE program by about a year), yet it is completely absent from the current website's program list.
- **Sub-component — Digital Education Program (DEP):** A solar-powered digital learning system (supported by SELCO Foundation) providing educational videos (sourced from YouTube channels) for grades Nursery through Class IV, aimed at making classroom teaching "more meaningful" and improving student enrollment.
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** No specific current student enrollment figure is given in the FY2021-22 report (only the village's total population/household figures, describing the target community rather than actual program enrollment).
- **Open Questions:** Why does this program not appear anywhere on the current website, despite being described as an ongoing, named school with an 8-year history (2016-2024+) by the time of the FY2021-22 report? Is the NCPE School still operating as of FY2023-24/FY2024-25 (the reports read for those years do not mention it), or did it quietly wind down? This is a strong candidate for direct follow-up with MOSONiE, since a physical school is a much larger institutional commitment than most of MOSONiE's other, more awareness/training-oriented activities.

### 11. COVID-19 Vaccination Drive & Pandemic Awareness (distinct from the Program 8 ration/hygiene-kit relief)
- **Status:** Time-bound, pandemic-specific; active FY2021-22 and FY2022-23 (the "second wave" response); not applicable/mentioned in later reports as the pandemic emergency phase ended.
- **Description:** Awareness campaigns and vaccination-drive support targeting the most remote, "zero-vaccination" village clusters — hard-to-reach areas with poor monsoon-season road access, where public transport is unavailable/unaffordable, making it difficult for government Community Health Centres (CHCs) to reach residents.
- **Sector:** Health (Public Health / Pandemic Response)
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 3
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None
- **Geography (as stated):** **Jirang Block, Ri-Bhoi District** — **37 villages covering 4 sub-centres** (both FY2021-22 and FY2022-23 reports cite the same "37 villages" figure, working with **Patharkhmah CHC**).
- **Named Funders / Partners: Azim Premji Foundation** (funder, both years); **Office of the District Medical and Health Officer, ANM Killing and Pillangkata Sub-Centre** (FY2021-22 general COVID-19 awareness); **Patharkhmah CHC** (vaccination-drive-specific partner, both years); volunteer nurses from **GNRC, Guwahati** and **NEMCARE Hospital** served as resource persons (FY2021-22); the whole FY2021-22 general-awareness program was also supported by **SELCO Foundation**.
- **Metric Trend Across Years:**
  - **FY2021-22 (general COVID-19 awareness cum sensitization, separate from the vaccination drive specifically):** **16 villages** covered in Umling Block — awareness on COVID-19 seriousness, social behaviour, hygiene, care of positive patients, helpline numbers, vaccination importance, myths/facts. Delivered via video screenings; face masks and hand sanitizer distributed; gatherings limited to <20 people per COVID protocol, targeting village leadership (Headman, Secretary, Youth President/Secretary, SHG leaders, Church Pastors/Catechists, ASHA workers, teachers) rather than open public gatherings.
  - **FY2021-22 (vaccination drive specifically):** **37 villages, 4 sub-centres**, Jirang Block — targeting "zero vaccination" village clusters.
  - **FY2022-23 (repeat/continuation of the vaccination drive):** Again **37 villages under Jirang Block** — including home-to-home awareness and household data collection, in collaboration with Patharkhmah CHC, supported by Azim Premji Foundation. Framed in this report as a response to the pandemic's "second wave."
- **Open Questions:** The identical "37 villages" figure appearing in both FY2021-22 and FY2022-23 — is this because the same 37 villages needed repeat visits/follow-up (plausible, given the "zero vaccination cluster" framing), or is one of the two reports simply repeating the previous year's figure without a genuine second round of activity? The near-identical language between the two years' descriptions (both mention "zero vaccination village clusters," poor road conditions, high transportation costs) makes this hard to distinguish from the text alone.

## Cross-Cutting Observations

1. **The "Chartered Village" strategy** (introduced in the FY2021-22 report, not mentioned in FY2022-23 through FY2024-25 reports) is described as one of MOSONiE's "mission strategies to bring about Socio-Economic Re-Architecturing," involving inclusive village development planning done jointly with villagers, linking them to government/non-government schemes, and providing skill-development/livelihood support (agriculture, entrepreneurship, livestock). This sounds like it may be the conceptual umbrella originally intended to tie together several of the individual programs above (Soh Marit, Livestock, Clean Energy, etc.) — but the term itself disappears from later reports, suggesting either a rebranding (perhaps folded into the FY2024-25 "ViMiVASTRA" strategic-planning language) or a discontinued framing concept.
2. **Program launch years, now mostly confirmed:** MOSONiE NCPE School (Oct 2016) → Clean Energy/DRE (2017) → CFW (Aug 2022) → Soh Marit (2022) → "Enhancing Income in 10 Tribal Villages" / formal piggery program (2023, as a 2023-2026 three-year program) → Energy for Health O&M (2022, per FY24-25 report). This gives a much clearer institutional timeline than was available after Pass 1.
3. **Two clean, credible growth trends stand out** as MOSONiE's best-evidenced success stories: the **Menstrual Hygiene program** (140 → 800+ → 2,000 → 2,000 kits/29 villages) and the **Decentralized Renewable Energy program** (3 end-users in 2017 → 194 total systems by FY22-23 → 394 by FY23-24 → 534 by FY24-25).
4. **Several named individual "graduation" stories** (beneficiary → trainer/entrepreneur) recur across the reports, most strikingly **Linda** (eri-silk spinning, now a master trainer of 100+ women) and **Neina Sangma** (pig rearing, repaid her loan and reinvested in a new piglet within 9 months) — both from the FY2024-25 report's "Stories from the Field" section.
