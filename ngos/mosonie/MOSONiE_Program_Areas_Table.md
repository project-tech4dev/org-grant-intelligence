## Organization Overview

- **Full name:** MOSONiE Socio-Economic Foundation (MOSONiE SEF)
- **Founded:** 2015; **registered** in 2016 as a **Section 8 Company** under the Companies Act, 2013
- **Leadership:** A **women-led** organization with **five founding members**: **Kaikha Sapriina** (Founder), **Liinai Margaret** (Founding Member, Executive Director / Director), **Ringbila Pungding** (Founding Member, Finance & Admin / Director), **Rinlan Phungther** (Founding Member, Networking & Fundraiser — also holds a Master's in Social Work and is described in one source as MOSONiE's HR Manager), **Savitri Salome** (Founding Member, Program Manager – Clean Energy), and **Vungngaihlian** (Founding Member, Program Manager – Livelihood).
- **Registered office:** House of Hiren Roy Suting, Umtyrnga Village, Killing Centre, Ri Bhoi, Byrnihat, Meghalaya, India, 793101 (an earlier/alternate listing gives the address as "Pillangkata, Ri Bhoi, Meghalaya, 793001" — the MNRE and CSR Universe profiles use this Pillangkata address, while the current website uses the Umtyrnga Village address; both are in Ri-Bhoi District, so this is likely just an office relocation between when those third-party profiles were last updated and now).
- **Name origin:** "MOSONiE" is a word from the **Mao tribe** of Manipur meaning **"Creation of Commonwealth"** — reflecting a philosophy of "less of me, more of us." The organization describes itself as "a small drop in the world's need for shared, responsible human initiative," seeking to "Create, Recreate, and Rejuvenate the resources around us." A related description (via RCRC partner page) elaborates that the word denotes wealth created through communal nurturing of the earth, shared "according to each person's needs in an egalitarian context," and reflects "a utopian quality of togetherness" and "reverential approach to nature."
- **Mission:** "Socio-Economic Re-Architecturing for North East India" (per LinkedIn/RCRC) / "Empower underserved communities through collective action towards equitable access and resource regeneration" (per current website).
- **Vision:** "Regeneration of common wealth towards the well-being of people and the planet."
- **Geography:** Primarily **Ri-Bhoi District, Meghalaya** (core operating area for almost all programs); environmental/agroforestry work also extends into **Manipur** ("tribal households in Meghalaya and Manipur"); the "Soh Marit" black pepper livelihoods program is specifically implemented in **Umling Block**, Ri-Bhoi District.
- **Headline impact claims (current website homepage, undated/cumulative):** **15,000+ households**, **50,000+ trees planted**, **20,000+ people reached**.
- **Recent recognition:**
  - Selected as part of **Dasra's Rebuild India Fund — Cohort 11** (a pooled, flexible, long-term funding vehicle for grassroots Indian NGOs; per Dasra's own materials, Rebuild has supported 300+ NGOs since November 2022, providing multi-year flexible grants and capacity-building support).
  - Partner organization of **RCRC (Responsible Coalition for Resilient Communities)**.
  - Featured by **India Development Review (IDR)** and **Centre for Systems Practice** for its Eri silk/solar spinning work (a dedicated **IDR Ground Up video**: "Women weavers find a solar way to Eri silk in Meghalaya").
  - Featured in independent journalism: **101Reporters** ("In a remote Meghalayan village, one solar husker is changing fortunes, freeing up time for women") and **VillageSquare.in / CEEW** ("Solar power a money-spinner for women spinning eri silk yarn").
  - Registered/listed with the **Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE)** as an implementing organization for solar livelihood interventions.

## Program Areas

The current website groups MOSONiE's work under two broad tabs — **"Health and Hygiene"** and **"Livelihood"** — but effectively presents nine distinct, named programs. Full detail below.

### 1. Community Health Camps
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Free health camps bringing basic medical check-ups, consultations, health screenings, and awareness sessions on preventive healthcare, hygiene, and nutrition to remote villages in Ri-Bhoi District with limited medical facilities.
- **Sector:** Health
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 3
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None
- **Geography (as stated):** Remote villages, Ri-Bhoi District, Meghalaya
- **Named Funders:** Not specified in the source text reviewed
- **Target groups:** Women, children, the elderly, and economically vulnerable households
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** No specific beneficiary counts found yet in the website text — pending review of the downloaded annual reports for camp counts/patient numbers per year.
- **Open Questions:** How many camps per year, and how many patients seen? This is exactly the kind of metric likely to be in the (not-yet-read) annual report PDFs.

### 2. Energy for Health: Operations & Maintenance (O&M)
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Supports the smooth functioning and long-term sustainability of solar energy systems installed in **all government healthcare facilities across Ri-Bhoi District** — regular maintenance, monitoring, and repair to ensure reliable electricity for lighting, vaccine refrigeration, medical equipment, and emergency care. Also promotes local capacity building through technical support and training.
- **Sector:** Health / Clean Energy Infrastructure
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 3, SDG 7
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None (infrastructure/institutional program)
- **Geography (as stated):** All government healthcare facilities, Ri-Bhoi District, Meghalaya
- **Named Funders / Partners:** Implemented **"in partnership with the State and District Health Departments and SELCO Foundation."**
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** No specific facility count or O&M-visit count found yet in the website text.
- **Open Questions:** How many healthcare facilities are covered under this O&M program in total, and since what year did MOSONiE take over/begin this maintenance role?

### 3. Menstrual Health Management & Promotion of Eco-Friendly Sanitary Cloth Pads
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Community awareness programmes, school-based education initiatives, and promotion/distribution of affordable, eco-friendly (reusable cloth) menstrual hygiene products, targeting the "border villages" of Ri-Bhoi District. Includes building capacity of teachers and community members.
- **Sector:** Health / Gender & Rights / Education
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 3, SDG 5
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None
- **Geography (as stated):** Border villages, Ri-Bhoi District, Meghalaya
- **Named Funders:** Not specified in the source text reviewed
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** No specific beneficiary count found in the website text.
- **Testimonial (named individual):** **Ritalish Syngkli**, a Teacher, is quoted: *"MOSONiE's menstrual hygiene program changed lives in our village. Girls now understand their health, have access to reusable sanitary pads, and can manage their periods with confidence and dignity."*
- **Open Questions:** How many villages/schools and how many girls/women reached to date?

### 4. Building Sustainable Energy Eco-system through Clean Energy Solutions
- **Status:** Active (this is MOSONiE's flagship, most externally-documented program — see below for rich independent-journalism detail)
- **Description:** Solar-powered livelihood solutions for farmers, women entrepreneurs, and small business owners, addressing erratic/unreliable grid electricity. Combines technology, capacity building, and linkages to financial and government support systems.
- **Sector:** Livelihoods / Clean Energy (Decentralized Renewable Energy, DRE)
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 7, SDG 8
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Retail, Commerce & Craft / Agritech & Allied — Energy-enabled Micro-enterprise, 🔴 Informal
- **Geography (as stated):** Rural communities across Ri-Bhoi District, Meghalaya; specific villages named in third-party coverage include **Nartap** and **Warmasaw** (both Ri-Bhoi), and **Liarkhla village, Bhoirymbong administrative block**
- **Named Funders / Partners:** **SELCO Foundation** (co-implementer since program inception); banks and village organizations (for beneficiary loan linkages); a 2022 **Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE)** national framework is cited as encouraging this class of solar livelihood tools
- **Sub-activities documented in detail via independent journalism:**
  - **Solar-powered Eri silk spinning machines** — introduced by MOSONiE in collaboration with SELCO Foundation starting in **2017** (per VillageSquare/CEEW) or **2018** (per an Instagram/CSP post) — sources give slightly different start years.
  - **Solar-powered rice hullers** — e.g., the Nartap village unit installed by farmer-entrepreneur couple **Ioanis/Ioanis Kurbah (37)** and **Jenita Jyrwa (35)**, with 12 solar panels (10kW capacity) in their backyard, MOSONiE + SELCO covering ~85% of costs (~₹3.8 lakh) via subsidy and the couple taking a ₹68,000 zero-interest SHG loan for the remaining 15%; the machine processes rice for 85+ of Nartap's 92 households at ₹20/5kg tin (+₹10 if husk is kept), generating ₹18,000-40,000/month for the operators, and has already fully repaid its loan.
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** **250 households** have received solar solutions through the MOSONiE-SELCO joint DRE intervention overall, **of which 117 are eri-spinning micro-enterprises** (VillageSquare/CEEW article, undated but referencing "so far"); **3 training centres** established across Ri-Bhoi District for eri-spinning training. Per-spinner productivity: yarn output increased from **1 kg/month (via traditional hand-held *takli* tool) to up to 4 kg/month** with the solar machine; one named spinner, **Ritngen Lamare** of Liarkhla village, went from 1 kg per **two months** (via takli) to **2-4 kg/month** with the solar machine. **Average income for women spinners rose from ₹1,200/month to ₹7,000/month.** Eri spinning machine unit cost: **₹25,000-35,000**.
- **Named beneficiaries/success stories:** **Jacinta Maslai** (Warmasaw village) — farmer's wife who took up solar eri-spinning to supplement farm income and now pays for children's education from spinning income; **Ritngen Lamare** (Liarkhla village) — whose injured son took up eri-spinning as his livelihood after being unable to do outdoor work; **Theilyn Syngkli (60)**, Nartap — benefits from the solar rice huller, no longer needs the manual bamboo-tray (*pdung* and *prah*) winnowing method that caused her chronic back/shoulder pain; **Thomas Syngkli (80)** and his son **Nicholas Jyrwa (49)** — earlier operators of a diesel rice huller in Nartap, both suffered respiratory/eye irritation from diesel fumes and husk dust, now made redundant by the solar unit (diesel-huller owners are described as "running into losses because no one uses them anymore").
- **Technical detail:** Diesel huller comparison — a diesel machine cost ~₹17,500 (year 2000 purchase price), used up to 25 litres of diesel/month (≈64 kg carbon emissions), cost ~₹3,000/month in fuel, and required 70-80 litres of water/hour for cooling (piped from a natural cave uphill in Nartap). The solar alternative runs cleanly, quietly, needs no water for cooling, but has lower output in winter (Dec-Feb, shorter daylight) and requires spare parts (blades/screens) sourced from Jorhat, Assam (~350 km away, ~₹1,000 per order including courier).
- **Named experts quoted:** **Kakali Kalita** (SELCO Foundation) — on flexible working hours and post-installation O&M being "our biggest hurdles" in remote areas (often 2x costlier than accessible locations); **Dr. Rajshri Shukla** (Just Transition Research Centre, IIT-Kanpur) — on solar vs. diesel sustainability and "just transformation."
- **Related sector data point:** A Council on Energy, Environment and Water (CEEW) report estimates **63,000+ livelihoods in Meghalaya** could be improved through decentralized renewable energy (DRE) — cited as the sectoral opportunity context for MOSONiE's work, not a MOSONiE-specific claim.
- **Open Questions:** Two different start years are given for the Eri-spinning/DRE partnership with SELCO (2017 per VillageSquare, 2018 per an Instagram/CSP post) — which is correct? Also, are the "250 households / 117 eri-spinning enterprises" figures current as of a specific year, or a cumulative running total never updated since original publication — the annual reports should clarify a proper year-by-year trend line here, since this is clearly MOSONiE's single most significant, externally-verified program.

### 5. Community Development and School Engagement Initiatives
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Two named sub-initiatives:
  - **Cloth for Work (CFW):** Community members contribute time/effort to village-benefiting activities (improving public spaces, maintaining community assets); participating households receive "essential family support packages" in recognition.
  - **School-to-School (S2S):** Creates a positive learning environment via student-led activities (classroom cleanliness drives, tree plantation, drawing competitions, other educational programmes); also establishes **community libraries** for children, youth, and adults.
- **Sector:** Community Development / Education
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 4, SDG 11, SDG 17
- **Occupation Taxonomy**: None
- **Geography (as stated):** Not specified beyond the general Ri-Bhoi operating area
- **Named Funders:** Not specified in the source text reviewed
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** No beneficiary/village counts found in the website text yet.
- **Open Questions:** How many "family support packages" have been distributed under CFW, and how many schools/libraries exist under S2S? Likely answered in the annual reports.

### 6. Environmental Conservation and Agroforestry
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Tree plantation initiatives promoting fruit-bearing and other useful tree species, integrating tree-based farming with existing agricultural practices to diversify household food/income sources while restoring green cover and biodiversity.
- **Sector:** Environment / Agroforestry / Livelihoods
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 13, SDG 15, SDG 1
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied Ecosystem → Agroforestry, 🔴 Informal
- **Geography (as stated):** Tribal households in **Meghalaya and Manipur** — this is the one program explicitly stated to extend beyond Ri-Bhoi/Meghalaya into Manipur
- **Named Funders / Partners:** A **Tree Plantation Drive at Nongthymmai Village VO Community Land** was run "in collaboration with **Young Indians Guwahati Chapter**" (per a LinkedIn post), distributing Banana and Black Pepper saplings among Self-Help Group (SHG) members.
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** Homepage claims (cumulative, undated): **50,000+ trees planted**. No year-by-year breakdown found yet.
- **Open Questions:** What fraction of the 50,000+ trees were planted through this program specifically vs. through the separate "Soh Marit" black pepper/banana program (there is likely overlap, since Nongthymmai's Banana/Black Pepper sapling drive could belong to either)?

### 7. Livestock Activities
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Support for marginalized households, particularly women and small farmers, in piggery, poultry, and other traditional livestock-rearing practices — training, technical guidance, and inputs to improve animal husbandry, animal health, and farm management. Promotes integrated farming by linking livestock rearing with kitchen gardening and agroforestry.
- **Sector:** Livelihoods / Agri-Allied
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 2, SDG 8
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied Ecosystem → Livestock & Animal Husbandry, 🔴 Informal

- **Sector:** Humanitarian / Welfare

### 9. Soh Marit: Black Pepper for Sustainable Livelihoods
- **Status:** Active
- **Description:** Supports farmers in reviving homestead gardens and cultivating high-value crops — **black pepper and banana** — as a way to improve productivity, diversify income, and strengthen long-term livelihood security in the face of small landholdings and daily-wage dependence. Farmers receive training and technical support on improved cultivation practices. The programme also encourages **farmer collectivization** to strengthen market linkages and bargaining power.
- **Sector:** Livelihoods / Agri-Allied
- **SDG Tags** *(inferred)*: SDG 1, SDG 2, SDG 8
- **Occupation Taxonomy** *(inferred)*: Agritech & Allied Ecosystem → Cultivation & Sourcing, 🔴 Informal (Small/Marginal Farmers)
- **Geography (as stated):** "Implemented across several villages in **Umling Block**," Ri-Bhoi District, Meghalaya
- **Named Funders:** Not specified in the source text reviewed
- **Metric Trend Across Years:** No specific household/farmer counts found in the website text yet.
- **Open Questions:** "Soh Marit" is Khasi for "black pepper" — is this program linked to, or the same initiative as, the Nongthymmai tree-plantation drive that also distributed Black Pepper saplings (see Program 6)? Needs the annual reports to clarify whether these are one program or two separately-run, overlapping initiatives.
