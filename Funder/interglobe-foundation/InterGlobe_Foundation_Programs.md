## PART A — Culture & Heritage Preservation: Individual Project Deep-Dives
Source: https://www.interglobe.com/foundation/culture-heritage and linked `/csr/` project pages

### 1. Abdur Rahim Khan-I-Khanan's Mausoleum ("Rahim's Tomb"), Delhi — Heritage Conservation
- **Partner:** Aga Khan Trust for Culture (AKTC); the monument is owned/protected by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)
- **Started:** 2014 — one of very few conservation initiatives undertaken with a corporate partner at a nationally protected Indian monument
- **Scope:** Structural repairs and restoration of the mausoleum's dalans (halls), canopies (chattris), dome, façade, landscape, wall and ceiling surfaces; landscape restored to original slopes/height; dome conservation completed with symbolic marble cladding addition
- **Techniques:** Detailed structural analysis followed by sequential repair/stabilisation/reconstruction using traditional tools, techniques, and materials; careful cleaning revealed intricate incised plaster patterns, which were restored; original marble patterns on the dome were hand-carved on-site by stone craftsmen, with missing portions recreated by master craftsmen
- **Context:** Part of the World Heritage Site encompassing Humayun's Tomb and other monuments
- **Completion & reopening:** Opened to the public on **17 December 2020** (Rahim's birth anniversary), after **6 years** of restoration work. Event presided over by Union Minister of State for Tourism & Culture Shri Prahlad Singh Patel, alongside **Rohini Bhatia** (Chairperson, InterGlobe Foundation) and **Ratish Nanda** (CEO, AKTC)
- **Quote (Rohini Bhatia):** "India is known for its cultural heritage, hence the preservation of our art and culture is essential for our identity... At InterGlobe Foundation, we strive to conserve the cultural heritage of our nation. Through restoration projects like Rahim's tomb in Delhi and Indra Kund stepwell in Rajasthan, InterGlobe Foundation has impacted thousands of lives while successfully preserving the historical and cultural values that bind us as a nation."
- **Quote (Ratish Nanda, AKTC):** "The tomb's condition was precarious, to say the least, when the project began. There were deep cracks in the crypt, the first floor and the dome... the support from InterGlobe Foundation came in the nick of time to restore the monument."

### 2. Abdur Rahim Khan-I-Khanan's Mausoleum — Cultural Revival component
- Alongside physical restoration, a **cultural revival programme** researched, documented, and disseminated Rahim's legacy via publications, films, music, and a permanent on-site exhibit.
- **March 2017:** Three-day festival **"Celebrating Rahim"** — conferences, concerts, exhibitions, and a site visit; accompanied by a month-long thematic exhibition on Rahim's life/works. **~80,000 people** visited the exhibition.
- **3 October 2017:** Launch of the book **"Celebrating Rahim"** — a collection of essays by national/international scholars covering Rahim as statesman, patron, and poet; includes 100+ archival Mughal-era images/paintings, plus an accompanying music DVD of Rahim's verses set to ragas and vernacular symphonies, performed by folk and classical artists for the first time.
- AKTC also wove the conservation project together with **environmental and socio-economic development programmes** in the neighbouring basti (slum) — ecological restoration, urban improvement, education, vocational training, community health, water/sanitation, housing improvement, and early childhood care/development for nearby craftsman communities.
- The project used **more than 2,50,000 man-days** of employment for master craftsmen across the complete conservation and landscaping work.

### 3. Restoration of the Tilework on the Dome of Muhammad Qutb Shah's Mausoleum, Hyderabad
- **Location:** Qutb Shahi Heritage Park, Hyderabad
- **Partner:** Aga Khan Trust for Culture (AKTC)
- **Scope:** Restoration of the dome's glazed tilework — vibrant colours and intricate designs
- **Key figures:**
  - **80,000+** glazed green tiles manufactured to match the original composition/colour
  - **850 square metres (~9,000 sq. ft.)** of dome surface covered
  - **70,000** stainless-steel screws used to replace rusted iron nails (replicating historical technique while preventing corrosion)
  - **92,000** multi-coloured tiles crafted/installed on the facades
  - Restoration also extended to minaret bulbs, dome neck, cornice bands, and corner minarets, using hand-cut tiles and traditional lime mortar, with custom formwork for precision
  - Only **3%** of the original glazed tile bases were found intact — these fragments were meticulously preserved on the dome
  - **5,500 man-days** of skilled craftsmanship involved in this specific tilework restoration

### 4. Restoration of Indra Kund — A Heritage Stepwell in Delwara, Rajasthan
- **Location:** Delwara, a historic town near Udaipur, Rajasthan — one of the 19 princely states of erstwhile Mewar
- **Partner:** Seva Mandir (supporting the local citizens and panchayat of Delwara)
- **Context:** The 19th-century Indra Kund stepwell was once a public space and community water source, but had fallen into disrepair over decades of neglect
- **Started:** December 2018
- **Scope:** Cleaning the water and restoring stonework; fencing to prevent garbage dumping; recreation of Indra Kund's rare historic **Persian Wheel**; restoring the heritage look while creating entrepreneurial opportunities for local youth

### 5. My City My Heritage — Cultural Mapping & Heritage Walks Campaign
- **Partner:** Sahapedia
- **Aim:** Rediscover the cultural/heritage potential of Indian cities via exploration, documentation, and dissemination — including booklet publication
- **Format:** Heritage walks, museum tours, baithaks (community gatherings), and educational activities for school students and the public
- **Phase 1 cities:** Ahmedabad, Indore, Prayagraj, Goa, Shillong, Bhubaneswar, Chandigarh, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Nashik (10 cities total)
- **2024 expansion:** Srinagar, Kozhikode, Lucknow
- **2025 expansion:** Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, Ahmedabad (repeat), Puducherry, Gwalior, Patiala
- **Output:** 200+ cultural points of interest identified across cities (cultural mapping articles, self-guided trails, listicles, museum-mapping pieces, cultural calendars, photography) — compiled into city booklets (print + digital)
- **50+ cultural activities** conducted as part of the "cultural interface" component, including curated events for children with special needs, covering built spaces, knowledge traditions, literature/language, practices/rituals, histories/institutions
- **COVID-19 adaptation:** Post-March 2020, adopted a hybrid on-ground + virtual walk model based on feasibility
- **Digital engagement:** "Did You Know" posts, informational photo/video content, city info slides, and campaigns tied to International Tourism Day and International Museums Week
- **Target audience:** Children with special needs and from disadvantaged backgrounds, culture enthusiasts, scholars, heritage professionals, tourists, local residents/administration/businesses — aiming to build young local scholars' capacity through collaborative research/documentation/mapping
- **Official city booklets confirmed live on the site** (verified via the project page's own download links; 18 cities to date — listed here for reference, not bulk-downloaded): Ahmedabad, Indore, Prayagraj, Goa, Shillong, Bhubaneswar, Chandigarh, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Nashik, Kozhikode, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, Lucknow, Srinagar, Gwalior, Patiala, Gangtok, Puducherry. Each is hosted directly under `interglobe.com/wp-content/uploads/` as an official PDF. (Gangtok had not previously appeared in press releases or the main campaign narrative text — its booklet's existence indicates the campaign has covered at least one additional city beyond what's stated in the page's own body copy.)

### 6. Rediscovering Cultural Heritage through Living Dance Tradition
- **Partner:** Sudhaaya Dance Foundation
- **Aim:** Document and celebrate historical dance figurines — feminine sculptural motifs (**Yakshis, Shaalabhanjikas, Alasakanyas**) found across Buddhist, Jain, and Hindu religious architecture (stupas and temples), representing liberated, accomplished, confident womanhood
- **Format:** Photo documentation of selected sculptures linked to dance movements from **Odissi**, connecting visual and performing arts
- **Theme:** "Interpreting the Feminine in Sculpture through Odissi, a Tradition in Dance" — highlighting "Paradigms of the Feminine" and celebrating womanhood in India
- **Event:** First event held on **5 March 2020** (International Women's Day-adjacent) at Sunder Nursery Amphitheatre, Delhi — featuring renowned Odissi dancer **Ms. Shagun Butani**, visualising the imagery of "Shaalbhanjika" (women's relationship with trees in sculptural motifs)

### 7. The InterGlobe HERITAGE Fellowships
- **Since inception (2014):** Foundation has undertaken the above heritage projects plus cultural mapping/documentation of ten cities and the "My City My Heritage" campaign
- **Purpose:** Bring together a cross-section of the public to document and research India's tangible and intangible cultural heritage — which is being lost faster than it can be documented — empowering individual fellows to explore previously undocumented aspects and raise awareness of lesser-known heritage
- **Editions:** **Four editions completed successfully** (2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 — the 2025 edition announced via a 24 July 2025 press release)

## PART B — Livelihood Promotion: Individual Project Deep-Dives
Source: https://www.interglobe.com/foundation/livelihood and linked `/csr/` project pages

### 1. Skill Development Programme for 200+ Underprivileged Youth (Delhi-NCR & Chennai)
- **Partners:** Empower Foundation and CAP Foundation (affiliated with the National Skill Development Corporation)
- **Target group:** Underprivileged youth, ages 18–35, minimum qualification Matriculation (10th pass)
- **Training tracks:** Retail, IT, and Hospitality profiles
- **Format:** 3-month vocational training programme, 30–35 people per batch, including on-the-job training and placement support
- **Placement partners:** Big Bazaar, PVR, Burger King, HDFC Bank, Café Coffee Day, Shoppers Stop, Spice Food Centre, SPAR Hypermarket, Empower Pragati, ALM Electronics, and others

### 2. "Pragati" — Areca Nut Leaf Cutlery Production, Kamrup, Assam
- **Partner:** Dhritii (Delhi-based NGO)
- **Aim:** Improve employability of rural women in Kamrup, Assam, by training them to manufacture biodegradable tableware from areca nut sheaths (an abundant, otherwise-wasted local resource)
- **Model:** Hub-and-spoke; home-based production allows women to balance work with other responsibilities; also creates secondary employment (sheath collection, cleaning) in the value chain
- **Market linkage:** Products sold via intermediary **Tamul Plates Marketing Private Limited**
- **Results:** 34 women trained; 30 now run home-based Areca-nut cutlery production units, 4 engaged in dryer enterprises; average earnings **₹5,000–6,000/month** (up to ₹15,000–18,000/month for top performers); all trained women organised into an entrepreneurs' collective (self-help groups)
- **Featured beneficiary:** Alpana Das (video testimonial referenced on the project page)

### 3. "GramJyothi" — Women's Livelihood Project, Kurnool, Andhra Pradesh
- **Partner:** OMPLIS (a federation of women's groups)
- **Scale:** 4,000 women assisted to enhance incomes
- **Multi-step strategy:**
  1. Organising women into Self-Help Groups (SHGs) of 10–15 members
  2. Training in small-savings skills so some credit needs are met from members' own funds
  3. Basic management/governance skills training, building an aspirational plan per member
  4. Connecting groups with banks and government schemes to leverage credit
  5. Direct grants provisioned for the most deprived families in extreme cases
- **Featured beneficiary quote:** Nirmala Devi (Tadkanpalle) — "I am very thankful to the GramJyothi project... I make Rs. 10,000-12,000 per month by preparing khova at home and selling it in the local market."

## PART C — Environment Protection: Individual Project Deep-Dives
Source: https://www.interglobe.com/foundation/environment and linked `/csr/` project pages

### 1. Waste Management Project, Kumbhalgarh (Kelwara), Rajasthan — "No Open Waste (NOW)" Zone
- **Context:** Kumbhalgarh Fort is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and major tourist draw; the surrounding village of Kelwara lacked a proper waste-management system, compounded by waste from numerous nearby hotels
- **Partners:** Seva Mandir and SAAHAS
- **Started:** December 2018
- **Approach:** Holistic village-development initiative — waste segregation, collection, and disposal mechanisms — implemented via multi-stakeholder platforms involving the Gram Panchayat, local citizens, hotels, and other stakeholders
- **Outcome:** The NOW Kelwara Solid Waste Management (SWM) system significantly improved living conditions in Kelwara and contributed to environmental preservation near the World Heritage site

### 2. "No Open Waste (NOW)" Project, Gurugram
- **Partner:** Feedback Foundation, in collaboration with the Municipal Corporation of Gurgaon (MCG)
- **Duration:** September 2015 – March 2018 (2.5 years), across **7 wards** of Gurugram
- **Reach:** Almost **4,00,000 people** educated on waste-segregation methods and benefits, encouraged to adopt wet-waste composting
- **Stakeholders engaged:** Resident Welfare Associations (RWAs), waste pickers, shopkeepers' associations, hawkers' associations, hotels' associations, and students

### 3. Community-Owned Forestry Project, Thuamul Rampur, Odisha
- **Partner:** Gram Vikas, Bhubaneswar
- **Scope:** Community-owned afforestation across **60 acres** of land in 2 villages of Thuamul Rampur block
- **Impact targets:** Biodiversity regeneration; improved land/water situation; tree cover increase from **10 acres to 70 acres**; sequestration of **19,200 tonnes of CO2** over the plantations' lifetime
- **COVID-19 response tie-in:** During the pandemic, Gram Vikas (via this IGF-supported project) provided local income-generation opportunities through land-development works and plantation activities — wages generated locally, ecology rehabilitated long-term; activities were opened up to residents of nearby villages as well, to support more vulnerable families

### 4. Water Conservation Study, Gurugram
- **Partner:** Centre for Ecology Development and Research (CEDAR)
- **Duration:** 8-month research project
- **Purpose:** Inform urban environment planning by the Gurugram Metropolitan Development Authority (GMDA); Gurugram (fastest-growing city in Haryana, in a low-rainfall arid zone) over-extracts groundwater by ~300% and depends on Yamuna canal flows facing climatic threats/competing demands
- **Progress (as reported):** CEDAR helped GMDA map **395 water bodies** in Gurugram and developed a methodology for measuring groundwater levels in sample water bodies
- **Planned next steps:** Groundwater-level measurement vs. historical data; full water-balance calculation for two localities (tentatively Sikandarpur area and Sector 55); citizen/state sensitisation; rainwater-harvesting planning; preventing waste run-off; promoting judicious water use
