# Sir Horace Kadoorie Charitable Foundation ("Kadoorie Charitable Foundation" / "Kadoorie Foundation")

> **IMPORTANT SOURCING NOTE:** Unlike the other organisations researched in this session, the Kadoorie Charitable Foundation **does not appear to operate its own public website**. It is a private, low-profile Hong Kong-based grant-making trust that funds other organisations rather than running public-facing programmes itself. All information below is therefore compiled from **secondary/official-adjacent sources** — Wikipedia, university/institutional pages (Oxford, HKU), grantee organisations' own websites (KFBG, PCD, Chatteris, Gurkha Welfare Trust, China Kadoorie Biobank), the Hongkong and Shanghai Hotels (HSH) corporate press materials, and philanthropic directories. **No content here comes from an official Kadoorie Charitable Foundation website, because none could be found.** Where sources conflict or information is unverified/dated, this is noted.

**Sources used:** en.wikipedia.org (Horace Kadoorie, Kadoorie family, Lawrence Kadoorie, The Gurkha Welfare Trust, China Kadoorie Biobank), kfbg.org, hongkongheritage.org / hshgroup.com press release, pcd.org.hk, chatteris.org.hk, gwt.org.uk, ckbiobank.org, ouh.nhs.uk, ctsu.ox.ac.uk, Foundation Directory Online (Candid) listing.

## 1. Identity & Structure

- **Full/alternate names used across sources:** "Kadoorie Charitable Foundation," "Kadoorie Foundation," "Sir Horace Kadoorie Charitable Foundation." These appear to refer to the same underlying Hong Kong trust.
- **Founded:** 1970, as a Hong Kong-based trust, by the **late Sir Horace Kadoorie** (per Partnerships for Community Development / PCD, an org it funds).
- **Legal form:** Private charitable trust (not a public operating charity); classified in one directory (Candid/Foundation Directory Online) as a "public charity" grantmaker.
- **Listed location (per Candid/Foundation Directory Online, profile last updated 2018):** Room 102, St. George's Building, Central, Hong Kong.
- **Current family trustee/leadership associated with the Foundation:** **Sir Michael Kadoorie** (son of Lawrence Kadoorie, Baron Kadoorie) — described in his HKU honorary-degree citation (2004) as "trustee of the Kadoorie Charitable Foundation."
- **Guiding philosophy/motto:** *"Help people to help themselves."*
- A LinkedIn profile for a "Director at Kadoorie Charitable Foundation" (Peter Paul de Groote) confirms the organisation has professional staff and operates internationally, and separately warned the public about a scam impersonating the Foundation (fake conference invitations sent by someone falsely claiming KCF affiliation) — indicating KCF deliberately keeps a low public profile and does not organize public conferences.

## 2. The Founder — Sir Horace Kadoorie (1902–1995)

- **Full title:** Sir Horace Kadoorie, CBE
- **Born:** 28 September 1902; **Died:** 22 April 1995, in Hong Kong (buried in the Jewish Cemetery, Happy Valley, Hong Kong)
- **Occupation:** Industrialist, hotelier, and philanthropist
- **Family:** Son of Sir Elly Kadoorie; brother of Lawrence Kadoorie, Baron Kadoorie; member of the Kadoorie family, originally Iraqi (Baghdadi) Jews who settled in Bombay (Mumbai), India, in the mid-18th century before the family's business centre moved to Shanghai (mid-19th century) and then Hong Kong (20th century).
- **Education:** Spent a year (1913–14) at Clifton College, Bristol, England, in Polack's House (a boarding house exclusively for Jewish boys).
- **Early career:** Worked with his brother Lawrence for Victor Sassoon in the 1920s–30s, managing Sassoon's Shanghai hotel, and also worked for their father Sir Elly Kadoorie's businesses.
- **Shanghai philanthropy:** In 1937, founded the **Shanghai Jewish Youth Association**; helped establish a committee that looked after some 20,000 Jewish refugees who had fled to Shanghai from Nazi-occupied Europe. A school he supported became a haven for Jewish refugee children in the Shanghai ghetto.
- **Key insight that shaped his philosophy:** In Shanghai, he concluded that one of the best ways to help people in need was to **empower them to help themselves** — a principle he later applied to Hong Kong's post-war refugees and rural poor.
- **1951 — Kadoorie Agricultural Aid Association (KAAA):** Co-founded with his brother Lawrence (together with Norman Wright and Woo Ting Sang) to help destitute refugees in Hong Kong's New Territories become self-supporting farmers. KAAA donated village houses, farm animals, farming materials, cash grants for feed, medicine, clothes, and blankets. In the 1960s, this helped hundreds of thousands of peasants in rural Hong Kong become independent farmers.
- **New Territories Benevolent Society:** Founded together with Lawrence, establishing small hospitals and clinics in Hong Kong's rural districts.
- **Nepal / Gurkha welfare:** Helped Gurkha soldiers readjust to rural civilian life after leaving the British military; opened training farms in Nepal (later expanded to two more Nepal farms). For this work, the **Nepal Government** awarded him the **Gorkha Dakshin Bahu (First Class)** — one of Nepal's highest honours.
- **1962:** Jointly awarded the **Ramon Magsaysay Award** for public service (with brother Lawrence).
- **Also:** Appointed **Chevalier of the Légion d'Honneur** by the French government (jointly with Lawrence).
- **1989:** Knighted (becoming "Sir Horace") specifically in recognition of his work helping Nepalese Gurkha soldiers in Hong Kong adjust to civilian life.
- **KAAA → KFBG:** His founding of KAAA in 1951 "paved the way" for the later creation of the **Kadoorie Farm and Botanic Garden (KFBG)**.
- **Memorial:** A statue of Sir Horace Kadoorie was unveiled at KFBG in May 2011 to honour his contribution; it sits on a bench in KFBG's plaza. Inscribed tribute: *"…(Sir Horace) was that rare, extraordinary & human, one who devotes his every moment of life, seeking to help others. There are certainly thousands, if not millions, who are indebted to this man..."* — sculpted by Lawrence Holofcener (also known for sculptures of John Lennon, Albert Einstein, and Nelson Mandela).

## 3. Sir Horace's Brother & Co-Founder — Lawrence Kadoorie, Baron Kadoorie (1899–1993)

- Born 2 June 1899; died 25 August 1993, Hong Kong. Industrialist, hotelier, photographer, and philanthropist.
- Married Muriel Gubbay (1938); children: Michael Kadoorie and Rita Kadoorie (later Rita Laura McAulay).
- Made CBE (1970), knighted (1974), and created **Baron Kadoorie of Kowloon in Hong Kong and of the City of Westminster** (1981) — the first Hong Kong-born person elevated to the peerage as a life peer / member of the House of Lords.
- Jointly received the Magsaysay Award (1962) and Légion d'Honneur (Chevalier) with Horace.
- An enthusiastic amateur photographer; his 1950s street-life photographs of Hong Kong are preserved by the Hong Kong Heritage Project and were exhibited in "Eye of Hong Kong" (2017).
- Co-founded KAAA (1951) and the New Territories Benevolent Society with Horace.

## 4. Wider Kadoorie Family Context

- **Sir Elly Kadoorie (1867–1944):** Father of Lawrence and Horace; businessman/philanthropist known as the "Prince of Philanthropists." Believed "wealth is a sacred trust to be administered for the good of society." Divided his fortune in his will: one-third to the British Government for schools in "Palestine or Mesopotamia," one-third to the Anglo-Jewish Association, one-third to his brother Ellis.
- **Sir Ellis Kadoorie (1865–1922):** Horace/Lawrence's uncle; founded the **Ellis Kadoorie Chinese Schools Society**, establishing six non-denominational free schools for Chinese students in China and supporting the University of Hong Kong; helped establish the Helena May Institute (for women living/working away from home); funded a school for Hong Kong's Indian children and endowed scholarships for underprivileged Indian children.
- **Sir Michael Kadoorie (b. 1941):** Lawrence's son; current head of the family's business and philanthropic activities; Chairman of The Hongkong and Shanghai Hotels, Limited (HSH) and CLP Holdings Limited; trustee of the Kadoorie Charitable Foundation; also involved with the Kadoorie Agricultural Aid Association in Nepal. Established the **Sir Horace Kadoorie Youth Development Centre for Hospitality** in Shanghai (with the Peninsula Hotel Shanghai), targeting migrant/disadvantaged youth with a two-year hospitality training course. Founded the **Hong Kong Heritage Project** in 2007.
- The family has been described as the **wealthiest non-ethnic-Chinese family in Hong Kong**.
- **1990s:** The Kadoorie family donated US$500,000 toward construction of the new **Shanghai Museum**.
- **1961 Hong Kong drought:** The family donated cement and pipes to Yim Tin Tsai villagers and contributed money/effort to lay water mains delivering water from Mud Bay to Yim Tin Tsai island.

## 5. Major Programmes / Institutions Funded or Founded by the Kadoorie (Charitable) Foundation

### 5.1 Kadoorie Agricultural Aid Association (KAAA) — Hong Kong & Nepal
- Founded 1951 by Horace & Lawrence Kadoorie (with Norman Wright and Woo Ting Sang).
- Helped hundreds of thousands of rural Hong Kong peasants become independent farmers (1950s–60s).
- **Nepal field team:** KAAA also operates as the Kadoorie Charitable Foundation's Nepal field presence, working closely with **British Gurkhas Nepal** and in partnership with **The Gurkha Welfare Trust (GWT)** — a UK charity (est. 1969, Reg. Charity No. 1103669) supporting Gurkha ex-servicemen and dependants.
  - Per GWT's own "Partners & Affiliations" page: *"The Trust works in partnership with the Kadoorie Charitable Foundation's Nepal field team – the Kadoorie Agricultural Aid Association (KAAA) – which also has a close relationship with British Gurkhas Nepal."*
  - Per Wikipedia's Gurkha Welfare Trust article: *"With funding from the Kadoorie Agricultural Aid Association, the Trust organises eight annual open medical camps, performing thousands of dental extractions and cataract operations while offering free GP and gynaecology checks, performing minor surgery and distributing free medication. Around 14,500 Nepali people benefit from these camps each year."*

### 5.2 Kadoorie Farm and Botanic Garden (KFBG)
- A public–private partnership, incorporated and designated as a conservation and education centre by Hong Kong Legislative Council Ordinance (Chapter 1156) on 20 January 1995.
- **Publicly designated but privately funded by the Kadoorie Foundation.**
- Focus since 1995: promoting conservation and sustainable living in Hong Kong and South China — flora/fauna conservation, organic agriculture promotion.
- Mission statement: *"We exist to increase the awareness of our relationship with the environment and bring about positive change in the world through conservation and education."*
- Address: Lam Kam Road, Tai Po, New Territories, Hong Kong. Website: www.kfbg.org
- Houses the Sir Horace Kadoorie memorial statue (see Section 2).

### 5.3 Partnerships for Community Development (PCD)
- Established in Hong Kong in **May 2001**; works in mainland China and Hong Kong.
- **"Established and continues to be funded by the Kadoorie Foundation (via a stream of funds allocated by the Hon. Mrs McAulay)"** — i.e., funded through Rita Laura McAulay (Lawrence Kadoorie's daughter, Sir Michael Kadoorie's sister).
- Guided by the same motto: "Help people to help themselves."
- Registered in Hong Kong as a tax-exempt charity under Section 88 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance; opened a Beijing Representative Office in August 2017 (registered with the Beijing Public Security Bureau; Ministry of Ecology and Environment as Professional Supervisory Unit).
- No religious/political affiliation; works via cultural reflection, nurturing community facilitators, and building networks/platforms.
- **Mainland China work:** biodiversity conservation, ecological agriculture, nature education, environmental protection, pollution prevention/control.
- **Hong Kong work:** four themes — Transformative Learning, Community Economy, Community-Based Actions on Climate Change, and Culture and Community Building.
- Website: www.pcd.org.hk

### 5.4 China Kadoorie Biobank (CKB) — University of Oxford
- One of the world's **largest prospective cohort studies**, a long-term collaboration between the University of Oxford (Clinical Trial Service Unit & Epidemiological Studies Unit / Nuffield Department of Population Health) and China (originally China CDC, later Peking University / Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences).
- **Over 512,000 adult participants** recruited from 10 regions of China (5 rural, 5 urban), aged 30–79, baseline survey 2004–2008; resurveys in 2008, 2013, and 2020; a 25,000-person subgroup retested periodically.
- Aims to generate evidence on lifestyle, environmental, and genetic determinants of common diseases (heart attack, stroke, diabetes, cancer) to inform global disease prevention, risk prediction, and treatment.
- **Funding (per CKB's own site):** *"The CKB baseline survey and the first re-survey were supported by the Kadoorie Charitable Foundation in Hong Kong."* Long-term follow-up has since been additionally supported by Wellcome, UKRI Medical Research Council, Cancer Research UK, British Heart Foundation (UK), and Chinese national science/technology funding bodies.
- Principal Investigators: Professor Zhengming Chen (Oxford) and Professor Liming Li (Beijing).
- Celebrated its 20th anniversary (per CKB news page).

### 5.5 Kadoorie Institute for Trauma, Emergency and Critical Care — John Radcliffe Hospital, Oxford (OUH)
- Purpose-built research/education facility at the John Radcliffe Hospital, Oxford; set up in **2003**; occupies Level 3 at the front of the main building.
- **Sir Michael Kadoorie generously supported its initial establishment**, and — due to its success — supported a further **expansion in 2012**.
- Aim: develop knowledge to improve care for patients suffering trauma, emergency, or critical-care events through targeted research and development.

### 5.6 Kadoorie Professorships — University of Oxford
- The **Kadoorie Foundation** endows **two professorial chairs** at Oxford:
  - **Kadoorie Professorship of Trauma Rehabilitation**
  - **Kadoorie Associate Professorship of Emergency Medicine**
- Both aimed at improving the quality of trauma, emergency, and critical care globally, in conjunction with the Kadoorie Centre/Institute for Critical Care Research and Education based at the John Radcliffe Hospital.

### 5.7 Chatteris Educational Foundation — "One for One, Adopt a School" (Hong Kong)
- **2016:** The Kadoorie Charitable Foundation became the **first external sponsor** of Chatteris's "One for One, Adopt a School" project, supporting two partner secondary schools.
- **2020:** KCF **renewed** its sponsorship with a new 3-year grant to expand the project to **four new secondary schools**, this time targeting a new beneficiary group — schools with a high proportion of newly-arrived children from mainland China and cross-boundary students commuting daily from Shenzhen.
- Over its history with KCF support (as of the 2020 renewal), "One for One" had grown to impact **12 different schools and over 10,000 disadvantaged students**, providing English-language tuition support (via Chatteris Tutors delivering an English Development Programme), summer camps, guest talks, and corporate office programmes (e.g., at LinkedIn's Hong Kong office).
- Quote (Chatteris, 2020): *"...to continue our partnership with the Kadoorie Charitable Foundation over the next three years."*

### 5.8 Hong Kong Heritage Project (HKHP) — related but organisationally distinct
- Founded by **Sir Michael Kadoorie in 2007** to preserve history and promote heritage awareness in Hong Kong.
- Operates a public-access archive of records from the Kadoorie Family's businesses; organises cultural programmes.
- **Sponsored by:** Sir Elly Kadoorie and Sons Limited, CLP Holdings Limited, and The Hongkong and Shanghai Hotels, Limited — **not** explicitly listed as funded by the Kadoorie Charitable Foundation itself, but part of the same family philanthropic ecosystem.
- Houses Lord Lawrence Kadoorie's 1950s Hong Kong photography collection (exhibited as "Eye of Hong Kong," 2017).
- Contact (per its own site, subject to change): (852) 2678 8087, enquiry@hongkongheritage.org, 147 Argyle Street, Kowloon, Hong Kong.

## 6. Corporate/Family Philanthropic Context (via The Hongkong and Shanghai Hotels, Limited)

From HSH's 2016 "150 Years of Giving Back to the Community" press release (majority shareholder: the Kadoorie family):
- Frames the Kadoorie family's philanthropy as predating the modern concept of "CSR" by over a century.
- Describes the founding of KAAA (1951) by "the Kadoorie brothers, together with Norman Wright and Woo Ting Sang," distributing village houses, farm animals, farming materials, and cash grants for feed, medicine, clothes, and blankets to Hong Kong's rural poor.
- Notes Sir Michael Kadoorie's establishment of the Hong Kong Heritage Project, with HSH providing financial support.
- HSH's own "Sustainable Luxury Vision 2020" (under CEO Clement Kwok) is presented as a continuation of the same family philanthropic ethos, though it is a distinct corporate CSR programme, not the Kadoorie Charitable Foundation itself.

## 8. The Foundation's Own Grant-Making Model & Programme Areas (via a 2020 job posting on Devex)

This is the most direct first-hand description of **how the Foundation itself operates** found in this research — from a "Grants & Programme Manager" job listing posted **by the Kadoorie Charitable Foundation** on the Devex international-development jobs platform (posted 24 November 2020, closed 31 December 2020):

> **"About the Organization:** The Kadoorie Charitable Foundation has been supporting partners for over 22 years mainly in **Mainland China, Hong Kong SAR and South & South East Asia**. Most projects focus on **livelihood, health, education or environment** and these are carried out by **local, often grassroots partners**. Identification of new partners, selection of projects and subsequent monitoring & evaluation follow a thorough standardised process. As part of this process partners and projects are visited on a regular basis as the Foundation values a strong connection with partners, beneficiaries and relevant authorities."

**Key operational facts from the posting:**
- **Geographic focus:** Mainland China, Hong Kong SAR, South & South East Asia.
- **4 core programme/funding areas:** Livelihood, Health, Education, Environment.
- **Delivery model:** Funds **local, grassroots partner organisations** (not direct service delivery) — consistent with everything found elsewhere in this research (KFBG, PCD, Chatteris, GWT/KAAA, CKB are all grantees/partners, not internal KCF programmes).
- **Process:** Standardised partner/project identification → selection → ongoing monitoring & evaluation, including **regular in-person visits** to partners, beneficiaries, and relevant government authorities.
- **Role advertised:** Senior-level, full-time "Grants & Programme Manager," with responsibilities split ~30% grants administration / 70% partner-portfolio management — confirming KCF runs a professionalised, structured grant-making operation (not just informal family giving).
- **Application/contact address given in the posting:** *"The Kadoorie Charitable Foundation, Room 102, 1st Floor, St. George's Building, 2 Ice House Street, Central, Hong Kong"*
- **Email address given in the posting:** **kadcf@kadoorie.com**
- This matches and refines the address found in the Candid/Foundation Directory Online listing (Room 102, St. George's Building, Central, Hong Kong).

**Staff (LinkedIn-confirmed, names only, no bios verified beyond titles):**
- Peter Paul de Groote — Director, Kadoorie Charitable Foundation
- Roy Tsang — Programme Officer, Kadoorie Charitable Foundation (self-described "experienced grant administrator with all-rounded project management skills")

**Estimated organisation size (per DevelopmentAid.org directory listing):** 11–50 employees; classified under "Community Development & NGOs."

## 9. Additional Kadoorie-Named Institutions & Facilities (HKU)

Per the University of Hong Kong's own "Giving" / named-buildings pages:

- **Kadoorie Biological Sciences Building (嘉道理生物科學大樓), HKU:** Opened **17 January 2000**; dedicated by **Lady (Muriel) Kadoorie** in memory of her late husband, **Lord Lawrence Kadoorie**. Designed by the same architectural firm (Leigh & Orange) as HKU's iconic 1912 Main Building. Houses Zoology, Botany, Ecology, and Biodiversity departments plus facilities for the Institute of Molecular Biology; also houses the Hong Kong Biodiversity Museum's specimen collection.
- **Kadoorie Centre (formerly the Kadoorie Agricultural Research Centre), Shek Kong, New Territories:** Established **June 1986** with support from **both Kadoorie brothers** (Lawrence and Horace); serves as a research site for environmental science, agriculture, horticulture, and nature conservation — run under HKU.
- **HKU Honorary Doctorates awarded to Kadoorie family members** (in recognition of philanthropy): Lord Lawrence Kadoorie (1961), **Dr Horace Kadoorie (1981)**, Lady (Muriel) Kadoorie (1999), Sir Michael Kadoorie (2004).
- **Sir Michael Kadoorie** has also served roughly a decade on **HKU's Council**.
- Other places/landmarks bearing the family name in Hong Kong (per HKU Giving page): Kadoorie Avenue, Kadoorie Hill, Kadoorie Beach — reflecting the family's broader civic footprint (these are place names honouring the family, not necessarily Foundation-funded programmes per se).

## 11. Gaps / What Could NOT Be Found (Honesty Note)

Despite thorough searching — including an attempted direct query of Hong Kong's Inland Revenue Department (IRD) public register of Section 88 tax-exempt charities, which is CAPTCHA-protected and could not be automated — the following could **still not** be located for the Kadoorie Charitable Foundation itself:
- **No official website, annual report, or financial disclosure** (e.g., total assets, annual grant-making budget, or a complete public grants list). Hong Kong charitable trusts are not required to publish the same level of public disclosure as, e.g., US 501(c)(3) Form 990-PF filings or UK Charity Commission filings, and KCF does not appear to voluntarily publish one.
- **No confirmed, complete list of current trustees/board** — only Sir Michael Kadoorie is confirmed as a trustee (per his 2004 HKU honorary-degree citation).
- **No official IRD/Section 88 registration number** was confirmed (the search tool requires solving a CAPTCHA not accessible to this research method; the Candid/Foundation Directory Online listing also does not expose a registration number without a paid subscription).
- **No total/annual grant-making budget figure** was found anywhere (e.g., HK$ amount disbursed per year).
