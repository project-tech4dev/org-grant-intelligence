## 1. Overview & Relationship Between Entities

1. **WAPR (Indian Chapter) — WAPR (IC):** The Indian national chapter of the international NGO **World Association for Psychosocial Rehabilitation (WAPR)**. Founded **1989**. The professional association/chapter itself.
2. **WAPR India Foundation:** A **registered Charitable Trust**, created in **2010** as the funding/legal "adjunct" arm to WAPR (IC), enabling it to receive donations, hold 12AA tax-exempt registration, and fund advocacy/research/conference activities.

Both share the same registered office; WAPR IC Board members serve as WAPR India Foundation Trustees.

## 2. WAPR (Indian Chapter) — Founding & History

- **Started:** 1989, by the **Late Dr. M. Sarada Menon**, with 12 other mental health professionals and interested individuals.
- **Hon. Life President:** Late Dr. M. Sarada Menon
- **1st President:** Late Dr. P. S. Gopinath
- **Status:** Not-for-profit; Organisational Member of the WAPR World Body.
- **Primary aim:** "Promotion of Education, Science, and Social-reform, public health and social service targeted at improving the status of persons with chronic mental illness on a National level." Pioneered psychosocial rehabilitation in India.
- **Newsletters:** publishing since 2011 (5 News Bulletins to date).

## 3. Objects of the Association (WAPR IC) — Full List

1. Establish a representative, authoritative non-governmental national consortium to strengthen medical, social, and vocational rehabilitation services for the mentally ill.
2. Serve as a voluntary national coordinating, consultative, and informational centre for agencies concerned with rehabilitation of the mentally ill.
3. Assist in organising national/regional/sub-national bodies where none exist; provide technical consultation.
4. Provide a national forum reviewing national plans, trends, programmes, and goals in rehabilitation.
5. Organise/assist national or regional conferences, congresses, or special commissions.
6. Arrange exchange of scholars, researchers, practitioners, and administrators.
7. Strengthen collaborative efforts among national bodies (public/private) concerned with rehabilitation goals.
8. Exchange views/information on innovative experiences serving as models for replication.
9. Foster research projects and training activities for rehabilitation professionals.
10. Issue publications/journals as a professional communication link.
11. Encourage educational programmes/public media use to inform on mental illness and counter stigma.
12. Publish articles, reports, and papers on rehabilitation.
13. Provide technical assistance to government/private agencies.
14. Provide speakers at symposia/congresses.
15. Maintain contact/collaborate with WHO, ILO, and other inter-governmental bodies.
16. Collaborate with Indian Psychiatric Society, Indian Association for Social Psychiatry, and international bodies (Rehabilitation International, World Federation for Mental Health, etc.).
17. Foster advocacy for physical/psychological/vocational adjustment of the mentally ill; stimulate financial support, housing, employment opportunities.
18. Assist implementation of government/inter-governmental resolutions on rights of the handicapped.
19. Encourage non-professional involvement; facilitate self-help groups.
20. Implement WAPR World Body policies/formulations.
21. Create a rehabilitation-personnel network; organise workshops/seminars/lectures.

## 11. WAPR Worldwide (International Parent Body)

- **WAPR** = World Association for Psychosocial Rehabilitation — international NGO of interdisciplinary mental health professionals and people with lived/service-user/family experience, from all world regions (www.wapr.org).
- **Structure:** 48-member International Board of Directors, incl. 6 past Presidents, Regional VPs (+deputies) for 6 WHO-aligned regions, consumer/family/voluntary-org representatives, permanent reps in Geneva and New York. 40+ national secretaries representing national chapters worldwide.
