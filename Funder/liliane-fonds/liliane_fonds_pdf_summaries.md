## 1. `LilianeFonds_Annual_Report_2025.pdf` — "Every Voice Counts: Building an Inclusive World Together" (35 pages)

### Structure
1. What drives Liliane Fonds — 2. Our international approach — 3. Collaborations — 4. Lobbying and networking — 5. Our international results — 6. Our approach in the Netherlands — 7. Our organisation — 8. Outlook — 9. Report of the Board of Directors & the Supervisory Board — 10. Financial Information.

### Foreword (Executive Director Erik Ackerman)
- Frames 2025 as Liliane Fonds's **45th year**, still closely tied to founders Liliane and Ignaas Brekelmans's original vision of close personal connection — both with children/local helpers on the ground and with Dutch supporters.
- Notes a "divided world" backdrop — conflicts in Sudan, Congo, Ukraine, Gaza — and describes Liliane Fonds as part of a movement making "systemic change from the bottom up."
- Highlights individual stories: Lebohang (Zimbabwe, had surgery, now walks/attends school independently); Dorcas (a new-generation caregiver being trained in Cameroon); a special-needs teacher in the Philippines now paid by the local authority (evidence of successful government hand-over).

### What Drives Liliane Fonds
- Restates mission/vision/strategy (see `liliane_fonds_organisation.md`).
- Frames policy around 4 conventions/frameworks: UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (Article 23), UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, WHO's ICF framework, and the CBR strategy.
- Ties thematic work explicitly to SDGs: **SDG 1** (extreme poverty), **SDG 10** (inequality), **SDG 17** (partnerships) as cross-cutting; **SDG 3** (family-oriented rehabilitation), **SDG 4** (inclusive education), **SDG 11** (transport/communication, with MIVA), **SDGs 4 & 8** (employment/income), **SDGs 3, 4 & 5** (SRHR).
- Case study: **Annabel** (Sierra Leone) — a girl who fell into a coma after a high fever as an infant, was hidden by her family (stigma, a traditional healer blaming "something supernatural"), and was found years later by CBR coordinator **Babra Samura** of partner organisation **Dorothy Springer Trust**, who created a treatment/support plan.

### Report of the Board of Directors & Supervisory Board (Governance)
- Confirms the **two-tier governance model** (Executive Director vs. Supervisory Board), the MIVA "personele unie" partnership since 2018, and CBF charity recognition with annual external audit (2025: **Dubois & Co Chartered Accountants**).
- **Management/admin costs**: €780,672 (2.1% of total expenditure).
- **Investment policy**: defensive/sustainable since July 2021 (mostly bonds, ~25% shares), 10–15 year horizon, aligned with UN Global Compact.
- **Executive Director**: Erik Ackerman (also ED of MIVA; Vice-Chair of Partos; Chair of the Board of the Dutch Coalition on Disability and Development).
- **Supervisory Board 2025**: Dr. Thos Gieskes (Chair), Dr. Piet Kuijper, Dr. Annika Galle, Dr. Emine Kaya, Wouter Reuvers MSc RA, Hilda Alberda MSc — met 5 times in 2025; undertook a working visit to **Zimbabwe** partner organisations; held its annual Works Council consultation.
- **Committees**: Financial Audit Committee (Reuvers – Chair, Galle) met 5x; Remuneration and Appointments Committee (Kaya – Chair, Gieskes).
- **Remuneration**: no Supervisory Board pay beyond fixed expense compensation (€2,100 chair / €1,050 members; 6% cross-charged to MIVA).

### Finance (FY2025)
- **Total revenue**: 26% above budget, 8% higher than 2024 — driven mainly by **legacy/bequest income**; donations and active-donor numbers also grew; institutional donations significantly exceeded budget (partially offsetting a large expected foundation/equity-fund donation that was deferred to 2026).
- **Total spend on objectives**: €30.2 million (12% above budget).
- **82.3%** of total expenditure went to programme objectives (CBF minimum: 70%).
- **Fundraising costs**: 15.2% of total income (lower than budgeted, due to higher revenue).
- **Balance sheet (31 Dec 2025)**: Total assets €36,521,545 (up from €32,179,105 in 2024); Reserves €26,209,544; Earmarked funds €348,000; Cash & cash equivalents €14,593,812.

## 4. `LilianeFonds_Annual_Report_2022.pdf` — "Think Big, Act Pragmatic" (60 pages, full edition)

### Structure
1. What drives us — 2. Our international approach — 3. Our approach in the Netherlands — 4. Our organisation — 5. Outlook — 6. Report of the Board of Directors and Supervisory Board — 7. Financial Information 2022.

### Foreword (Erik Ackerman, Executive Director since 1 October 2022)
- Describes 2022 as a year of emerging from COVID-19: restrictions relaxed across the **25 countries** where Liliane Fonds worked at the time (a notably higher count than the ~16–20 target range in the later 2024–2027 strategy).
- COVID-19 had disproportionately affected children with disabilities: interrupted education, halted physiotherapy, harder access to vital care.
- **New developments in 2022**: a special treatment centre for children with Down's syndrome set up in the **Philippines**; first students in Cameroon completing new courses in physiotherapy, occupational therapy, multi-skilled rehabilitation, and CBR.
- **Early detection projects launched in Burundi and India** (2022) — aiming to ensure children get the right treatment within the **first 1,000 days of life**, via healthcare-worker training and improved referral procedures. *(Note: India does not appear on Liliane Fonds's current, more recent "where we work" country list — indicating this may have been a shorter-term/pilot initiative or a country later phased out, consistent with the 2024–2027 strategy's move toward a smaller, more focused set of countries.)*
- Notes all **19 local partners** (in the countries covered by this report) had formal agreements with local authorities — described as key to sustainable, replicable impact.

### Five WHO Priority Areas (early childhood, per infographic)
Early identification and intervention; Health; Early childhood development; Enabling environment; Responsive parenting and nutrition.

### Thematic/SDG Framing
Same SDG structure as later reports (SDG 1/10/17 cross-cutting; SDG 3 family-based rehabilitation; SDG 4 inclusive education — noting **9 out of 10 children with a disability in developing countries do not attend school**; SDG 11 transport/communication with MIVA; SDGs 4&8 employment/income; SDGs 3,4&5 SRHR).

### Case Study Example
**Elsa Gasper**, a teacher at partner organisation **KAISAKA** in Manila, Philippines, specialising in early diagnosis/treatment — illustrated via the story of **Nicole (3)**, whose developmental delays were identified through KAISAKA's home-visit outreach model.

## 5. `LilianeFonds_Multiannual_Strategy_2024-2027.pdf` — Summary of Multiannual Strategy 2024–2027 (9 pages)

### Context & Rationale
- Published to mark **44 years** of Liliane Fonds's work (as of drafting).
- Frames growing urgency: vulnerability of children with disabilities is **increasing** due to climate change, conflict, food crises, weakening public services, and shrinking civil-society operating space.

### Mission, Vision, Core Values
- **Mission**: "Children with disabilities in the poorest parts of the world participate fully."
- **Vision**: "An inclusive society in which children and young people with disabilities in developing countries enjoy equal rights and opportunities."
- **Core Values**: **Trust** (basis of successful partnerships), **Connection** (starts with listening), **Openness** (requires vulnerability/courage; essential for organisational learning).

### Strategic Targets for 2024–2027
- Reach **~60,000 children with disabilities every year by 2027**.
- Work towards inclusive care, education, and participation across **16 to 20 focus countries** (down from the 25 countries active as of the 2022 report — a deliberate consolidation for deeper impact).
- **Deepen thematic focus** — deploy expertise more specifically within focus countries to raise programme quality.
- Take a **leading expert role** on "disability-inclusive development" and "leave no one behind."
- Achieve **ambitious revenue growth**, focusing on institutional fundraising while expanding private fundraising.
- Be a **contemporary employer**, maximising diverse talent internally.

### "More Impact in Fewer Countries" — Geographic Consolidation
- Support currently (as of the strategy's writing) spread across **25 countries**; ambition to narrow to **16–20 countries** for deeper, more structural impact.
- Priority: the **poorest and most fragile countries (Least Developed Countries)** — target of **70% of aid** going to these areas.
- Practically: increased focus on **West and East Africa and Asia**; **careful wind-down and hand-over of the Latin America programme** in the coming years.

### Intersecting Themes (cross-cutting priorities for 2024–2027)
1. **Shifting the power** — developing a vision/action plan for more equal relations with Africa/Asia/Latin America partners; starts with internal awareness and challenging existing relationship dynamics.
2. **Data & digitisation** — a roadmap (from early 2024) toward becoming a fully data-driven organisation by 2027.
3. **Learning and innovation** — responding to external trends; dedicated budget/manpower for innovation.

### The Four Roles of Liliane Fonds
1. **Fundraiser** — mobilising resources for local partners/programmes.
2. **Connector** — linking funding from supporters/partners to child-benefiting programmes.
3. **Advocate** — championing "disability inclusion" and "leave no one behind."
4. **Expert** — building a research/learning base (CBR and disability-inclusive development) so interventions are proven effective.

### Thematic Focus Details
- **Community-Based Rehabilitation** remains central, involving the child's whole environment.
- **Child safeguarding** — "disability-inclusive child safeguarding guidelines" applied across all programmes, given target-group vulnerability.
- **Health & rehabilitation** — earlier health/developmental screening as a spearhead.
- **Education** — reaching children as early as possible; quality inclusive pre-school/early education.
- **SRHR** — rollout of **Body Talk** and the **Make Way** strategic partnership.
- **Work and income** — expanding the approach to economic independence for young people with disabilities.

### Partnership with MIVA ("Two Missions, One Destination")
- MIVA and Liliane Fonds "complement and strengthen each other": Liliane Fonds works toward inclusive participation (healthcare/education access); MIVA supplies the **transport and communication means** (e.g., ambulances) that make that access physically possible, especially in remote/poor areas.
