## Vision & Mission
- **Mission:** "We want children with disabilities in the poorest parts of the world to participate fully in society."
- **Vision:** "We strive for a society in which children with a disability have equal opportunities and belong."
- **Tagline:** "See the strength in every child."
- **Strategy:** Together with local partners, empower children with a disability in Africa, Asia and Latin America by providing personalised care, supporting their development, making their environment more accessible, and standing up for their rights.
- **Grounding:** Policy is based on the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (Article 23), the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, the WHO's International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF), and the Community-Based Rehabilitation (CBR) strategy — aligned with UN Sustainable Development Goals (notably SDG 1, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10, 11, 17).

## Our Founders — Liliane & Ignaas Brekelmans
(Source: lilianefonds.org/about-liliane-fonds; Annual Report 2025 Foreword)
- **Liliane Brekelmans (1929–2009)** contracted **polio** as a child, leaving her permanently paralysed in both legs. Known in her youth as "the girl with the handicap," she refused to let her disability define her and fought for independence and a meaningful life.
- Her life changed when she met **Agnes**, a girl from Indonesia — this personal connection is described as the spark that led to the Foundation's creation.
- Liliane and her husband **Ignaas Brekelmans** together laid the foundation for what Liliane Fonds is today, built on close personal connection: with the children and local helpers on the ground, and with supporters back in the Netherlands.
- The organisation describes itself (as of the 2025 Annual Report) as being in its **45th year**, remaining "as intimately linked as ever" to the founders' original vision.

## History Timeline
- **1980** — Liliane Fonds established as a Dutch NGO / special fund for children and young people with disabilities in developing countries.
- **2018** — Liliane Fonds and **MIVA** formalise a partnership ("personele unie"): shared Executive Director, shared department managers, and shared Supervisory Board composition (same chair and members for both organisations).
- **2021 (July)** — Adopts a defensive, sustainable investment policy for part of its assets (bonds + ~25% shares), given the negative-interest-rate environment; aligned with the UN Global Compact and excludes banks/investors that violate children's rights.
- **2024** — Publishes its current **Multi-Annual Strategy 2024–2027**.
- **2025** — 45th anniversary year; ~47,000 children with disabilities supported; €30.2 million spent on objectives (26% above budget, 8% higher than 2024, driven substantially by legacy/bequest income).

## Legal / Financial Structure & Governance
(Source: lilianefonds.org/about-liliane-fonds/governance-and-accountability; Annual Report 2025)
- Governed under a **two-tier (supervisory board) model**: management/policy/execution (Executive Director) is separated from supervision (Supervisory Board), per the Articles of Association, Supervisory Board Regulations, and Board Regulations.
- **Executive Director**: appointed by the Supervisory Board, responsible for day-to-day management; assisted by a management team. Erik Ackerman also serves as Executive Director of MIVA, Vice-Chair of Partos (the Dutch sector association for development cooperation), and Chair of the Board of the Dutch Coalition on Disability and Development.
- **Supervisory Board**: minimum 5 members, 4-year terms (one renewal possible). 2025 composition: Dr. Thos Gieskes (Chair), Dr. Piet Kuijper, Dr. Annika Galle, Dr. Emine Kaya, Wouter Reuvers MSc RA, Hilda Alberda MSc.
  - Sub-committees: **Financial Audit Committee** (Wouter Reuvers – Chair, Annika Galle; met 5x in 2025) and **Remuneration and Appointments Committee** (Emine Kaya – Chair, Thos Gieskes).
  - No remuneration paid to Supervisory Board members beyond fixed annual expense compensation (€2,100 for chair, €1,050 for other members; 6% cross-charged to MIVA).
- **External recognition/oversight**: Recognised as a charity by the **Central Bureau of Fundraising (CBF)** in the Netherlands (periodically assessed); audited annually — 2025 audit by **Dubois & Co Chartered Accountants**.
- **Sector memberships**: **Goede Doelen Nederland** (Dutch charities sector body — sets governance/financial-management/remuneration guidelines Liliane Fonds follows); **Foundation for the Internal Supervision of Charities (ITDG)**; **Partos** (Dutch international-cooperation sector association).
- **Governance codes followed**: SBF Code for Good Governance, Erkenningsregeling Goede Doelen (Charity Recognition Regulations), Goede Doelen Nederland financial-management and director-remuneration guidelines, GDPR.
- **Investment policy**: 10–15 year investment horizon; assets mostly in bonds (~75%) with the remainder (~25%) in shares; managed with a Dutch asset manager under UN Global Compact principles; excludes banks/investors violating children's rights.
- **Recognition marks**: NGO Source (US equivalency determination), CBF recognition, Nationale Postcode Loterij (Dutch Postcode Lottery) partner, ANBI (Dutch tax-deductible-donation status).

## Media & Storytelling
- **Podcast — "The Child in the Pond"**: a 3-episode English-language podcast (~30 min/episode) presented by journalist Max Boogaard, who travels with Liliane Fonds to **Sierra Leone** to follow local aid workers protecting children with disabilities (featuring children Annabel, Fatima, Joshua, Alhaji, and Sara). Inspired by philosopher **Peter Singer's 1972 essay** on the ethics of helping a drowning child nearby vs. a child suffering far away — used to explore moral-distance questions in global aid/funding priorities. Designed for use in team discussions, classrooms, or policy workshops. Contact: Timo Vorstenbosch (Content Manager) — tvorstenbosch@lilianefonds.nl.
