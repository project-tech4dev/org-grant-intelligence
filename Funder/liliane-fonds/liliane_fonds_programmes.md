# Liliane Fonds — Approach, Programmes, Themes & Where We Work

Source pages: lilianefonds.org/what-we-do, /what-we-do/our-approach (+ CBR, effective-networks, lobby-and-advocacy subpages), /what-we-do/where-we-work, /what-we-do/impact, /what-we-do/projects, /work-with-us, /contact/funding, /contact/frequently-asked-questions, /resources

## The Problem Liliane Fonds Addresses
- **240 million children and youth worldwide** live with a disability; **95%** do not receive the support they need (85% of all children with disabilities live in low- and middle-income countries).
- Children with disabilities face compounding barriers: physical (lack of healthy food, poor hygiene, limited medical access, violence, climate-change effects) and invisible (lack of rights, poor safety, little recognition/stigma).
- Consequence cited in the 2025 Annual Report: children and people with disabilities die **up to 20 years earlier** — not because of the disability itself, but because of lack of access to care.

## Our Approach — Three Pillars
(Source: lilianefonds.org/what-we-do/our-approach)

### 1. Community-Based Rehabilitation (CBR/CBID)
- Liliane Fonds's core strategy: a WHO-recognised approach promoting equal opportunity and independence for people with disabilities by involving the community in their care and support. Covers **five domains (the "CBR Matrix")**: Health, Education, Livelihood, Social, and Empowerment.
- **Underlying principles:** children/families/communities at the centre; holistic, multi-sectoral approach; country context is key; rights-based programming.
- **Five CBR "change pathways"** — identified through the collaborative **"Breaking Down Barriers"** research (led by Liliane Fonds and Radboud University, with partner universities in Cameroon, Sierra Leone, and Zambia) — are central to enhancing rights and wellbeing for children/youth with disabilities and their families. The pathways work in synergy; progress in one area catalyses improvements in others.
- **What makes a high-quality CBR programme:** covers all five CBR Matrix domains; implements multiple interconnected interventions across the five change pathways; aligns with the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD), national legislation, and regional/international standards.
- **Recommendations to partners/development actors:** promote CBR as a strategy for Community-Based (Disability) Inclusive Development (CBID); strengthen community-level disability-specific services alongside mainstreaming; empower families/caregivers to exercise their rights; foster social inclusion by reducing stigma via community leaders; embed CBR in public systems for sustainability; share lessons learned to achieve structural change.
- Terminology note (explicit disclaimer on the site): Liliane Fonds is aware of contested views on "CBR" vs. "CBID" terminology, and prioritises the substance (access to community/family-level rehabilitation services and functional referral systems) over the label — partners are encouraged to align terminology with their own country's policy framework.
- Example CBR networks highlighted: **Rwanda** and **Zambia** (including the "ZECREP" CBR documentary).

### 2. Effective Networks
- Liliane Fonds supports strong **partner networks** that stay close to communities, amplify local voices, and influence policy/systems — proven to be more effective than any single organisation acting alone.
- Support provided to networks: shared vision-setting, multi-year programmes, coaching and practical tools, strong governance and fundraising-capacity building, and structures fitted to local realities.
- **Observed outcomes from effective networks:** better access to CBR services; less stigma/discrimination; stronger and more equal partnerships; greater policy influence; improved fundraising capacity; local organisations increasingly leading change.
- This networked model is **scaling across all 16 focus countries**.

### 3. Lobby & Advocacy
- Grounded in the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities and the SDG principle of **"leave no one behind."**
- **Activities:** building evidence-based advocacy agendas; strengthening youth voices so children/youth with disabilities can speak for themselves; promoting inclusion in national/international networks and forums; sharing perspectives in public/policy discussions.
- Notable advocacy moments: participation in the **Global Disability Summit** (Berlin, 2025); the **UN Commission on the Status of Women (CSW)** in New York; **"We Ring the Bell"** campaign (e.g., in Burkina Faso, on inclusive education); International Conference on Family Planning (ICFP) 2025; advocacy for a **National Action Plan for people with albinism in Zambia** (ratified 2026).

## Thematic Focus Areas
(Source: lilianefonds.org/what-we-do, /contact/frequently-asked-questions)
1. **Health & Rehabilitation** ("Family-Oriented Rehabilitation" per 2025 Annual Report / SDG 3) — mobility, independence, daily life skills; early identification/intervention and access to assistive devices; training parents, caregivers, and local authorities; funding operations, therapy, equipment, and home adaptations where local support is insufficient.
2. **Inclusive Education** (SDG 4) — learning environments for all; helping governments make schools accessible; training teachers in optimised methods; adapted teaching/learning resources; early childhood and lifelong learning opportunities.
3. **Livelihoods** ("Employment and Income," SDGs 4 & 8) — skills, jobs, and income opportunities; supporting training courses and employers to involve young people with disabilities so they can learn a trade and become financially independent.
4. **SRHR (Sexual and Reproductive Health and Rights)** (SDGs 3, 4 & 5) — accessible, rights-based information and services; sex education, self-determination, access to contraception and medical care; breaking taboos among parents, teachers, and healthcare workers; empowering children/youth to protect themselves against abuse.
5. **Child Protection** — safe environments free from harm; protecting children from abuse, neglect, and harm.
6. **Meaningful and Inclusive Youth Participation** — "Nothing about us without us"; includes a dedicated **Liliane Fonds Youth Panel** giving young people with disabilities direct influence over programming and decisions.
7. **Transport and Communication** (SDG 11, delivered with **MIVA**) — transport and equipment so children can reach school/medical care; tablets and computers for remote education and care.

## Key Projects (comprehensive list from lilianefonds.org/what-we-do/projects)

### Research & Learning Initiatives
- **Breaking Down Barriers (BdB)** — Liliane Fonds's flagship multi-annual research initiative with **Radboud University**, launched **2014**. Uses **"pracademics"** (people operating at the intersection of academia and practice) and Master's students to co-produce research.
  - **Phase 1 (2014–2020)** — focus: disability advocacy. Produced 11 studies → policy briefs, 2 disability-advocacy papers, 2 peer-reviewed academic papers; won the **Impact Challenge Award (2019)**; key output: *"Pushing Boundaries in Disability Advocacy"* (lessons from Sierra Leone, Zambia, Cameroon — movement building, intersectionality, personal transformation).
  - **Phase 2 (2020–2024)** — focus: inclusive development, with global emphasis on Education, SRHR, and CBR. Produced 20 studies → 23 policy briefs, shared at an international conference (2024). Key outputs: brief on inclusive school leadership (Cameroon, Zambia, Sierra Leone); synthesis brief on barriers to family-planning access for women/girls with disabilities (Cameroon, Zambia, Sierra Leone, Uganda — 8 policy recommendations); *"Unlocking Synergies: The Power of Community-Based Inclusion"* policy brief (Sierra Leone, Cameroon, Zambia).
  - **Phase 3 (2025–2028)** — focus: CBR/CBID sustainability. Produced (so far) a literature review, a 25+ country partner survey, and in-depth country studies in Cameroon, Rwanda, and Zambia; discussed at a 2026 learning/exchange event. Key finding: sustaining CBR/CBID is primarily an **institutional design issue** (funding, governance, integration into public systems), not just a technical challenge.
  - **Contacts:** Jan Apperloo (Learning & Knowledge Development Adviser, BdB Coordinator) — japperloo@lilianefonds.nl; Dr. Willem Elbers (Principal Investigator, Radboud University) — willem.elbers@ru.nl.

- **The Nexus Project** — explores disability-inclusive programming in **fragile contexts** (conflict + climate change); "Nexus" = the intersection of humanitarian aid and long-term development. **19 of Liliane Fonds's 25 countries are considered fragile** in at least one dimension (per the OECD 2022 fragility index).
  - Mixed-methods research conducted in **4 countries**: Bangladesh (partner: Centre for Disability in Development/CDD; coastal hazards — floods, cyclones, salinity; 300 CYWD interviewed, ages 6–25); Indonesia (partner: NLR Indonesia; droughts, flash floods, landslides; 180 CYWD, ages 10–24); Burkina Faso (partner: Caritas Koudougou; armed conflict/displacement/food insecurity; 388 total participants incl. 84 CYWD); Cameroon (partner: Cameroon Baptist Convention Health Services/CBCHS; Anglophone crisis, displacement; 81 CYWD + 17 key informants + 7 focus groups).
  - Follow-up pilot projects in all 4 countries planned from **end of 2025** onward, based on a "learning agenda" derived from the research.
  - Published learning briefs on: climate/conflict impacts on CYWD; six rights-based approaches for fragile contexts; cross-sector collaboration success factors; and priorities for future pilots.
  - **Contacts:** Zoë Dings (Programme Officer) — zdings@lilianefonds.nl; Jan Apperloo — japperloo@lilianefonds.nl.

### Livelihoods / Economic Empowerment
- **"The Wheel of Livelihoods"** — Liliane Fonds's own comprehensive, phased model for disability-inclusive livelihoods programming, supporting youth with disabilities (YWD) from unemployment/exclusion toward employment, entrepreneurship, and inclusion.
- **Work and Better Life ("Kazi na Maisha Bora")** — a 3-year pilot (in Kenya and Tanzania) of the WHEEL of Livelihoods model; implemented via Cheshire Disability Services (CDSK, Kenya) and Beyond Inclusion (BI, Tanzania). Aims to directly empower **380 youth with disabilities** and engage **150+ stakeholders**. Innovations piloted: **Personal Professional Development Plans (PPDP)**; **Social-Emotional Learning (SEL)** peer groups; **vlogging** (youth document their own transformation journeys, building digital skills/visibility). Funded in part by **Dioraphte**.
- **KUBIK — Inclusive Business & Entrepreneurs in Indonesia** — improves access to work for youth with disabilities via entrepreneurship support.
- **"Driving Success: Youth with Disabilities at the Steering Wheel of Their Lives"** — project to unlock the potential of youth with disabilities (further WHEEL-model-related livelihoods work).

### Education
- **Inclusive Play-Based Early Childhood Education (IPECE)** — described as a "fundamental step for life-long learning for all"; provides play-based learning/educational activities for young children including those with disabilities.
- **We Ring the Bell** — Liliane Fonds's flagship global advocacy campaign, running in **20+ countries**, for the right to education for children with disabilities. 2026 theme: financing IPECE, timed to align with the **first-ever International Finance Summit for Early Childhood (2027)** and SDG 4.2.
- **"Early Support for Children with Disabilities"** — targets the fact that children with severe disabilities are often the last reached by education systems, especially in low/middle-income countries.

### Health & Rehabilitation
- **Testing RehApp in the DRC** — a mobile app to enhance the rehabilitation knowledge, skills, and planning of Community-Based Rehabilitation (CBR) workers caring for children.
- **ZECREP / ZECREP II (Zambia Enhanced Community-Based Rehabilitation Program)** — improving quality of life for people with disabilities in Zambia via enhanced CBR (documented in the "ZECREP CBR documentary").
- **Rehabilitation Compass** — ensures good rehabilitation care is accessible for children with disabilities in **Cameroon**.
- **Teleconsultation and Telerehabilitation (India and the Philippines)** — uses ICTs (internet, tablets, phones) for remote diagnosis/rehabilitation therapy; a related "Telerehabilitation" project is also referenced on the Philippines country page. *(Note: this is one of the only current references to India-related activity found on the live site.)*
- **Feeding and eating-difficulty support** — addresses the fact that children with disabilities often struggle with chewing/swallowing and related nutrition challenges.
- **Improved home-based rehabilitation for children with neurological disorders** — addressing limited educational background among CBR field workers to improve home-based care quality.
- **"These Shoes" (Training for Orthopedic Shoe Makers)** — an older project (ended 2018) with **Netherlands Leprosy Relief (NLR)**, funded by the Dutch National Postcode Lottery; the training programme it established in **Hanoi** continues to run today.

### Sexual & Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR)
- **Body Talk / Body Talk 1.0 / Body Talk 2.0** — healthy sexual development programme for youth with disabilities; Body Talk 1.0 ran in the Philippines; **Body Talk 2.0** runs in the Philippines, Indonesia, and Bangladesh.
- **My Body is Mine** — sexual education programme for children with disabilities, helping reduce abuse risk by increasing SRHR knowledge; active in Indonesia.
- **Make Way** — a five-year SRHR-focused programme (2020–2025) in Ethiopia, Kenya, Rwanda, Uganda, and Zambia; includes **"Make Way Youth-Led Research (YLR)"** on the barriers/opportunities youth with disabilities face in accessing SRHR services.
- **Award:** In February 2023, **The Zero Project** awarded **both Body Talk and My Body Is Mine** for their work on Sexual Health and Reproductive Rights.
- Related Dutch coalition: Liliane Fonds is a founding member (Aug 2026) of a new **11-organisation Dutch SRHR alliance** ("SRHR Next Alliance"), launched with the Dutch Minister of Foreign Trade and Development Cooperation, to improve sexual and reproductive health and rights worldwide.

### Protection / Rights of Specific Groups
- **Albinism Programme — "Out of the Shadows for Good" (Zambia, 2024–2027)** — full title: *"An end to the murder, mutilation, and exclusion of children and adults with albinism in Zambia."* Delivered with the **Cheshire Homes Society of Zambia (CHSZ)** and the **Africa Albinism Network (AAN)**, with additional funding from the **Nationale Postcode Loterij** (Dutch Postcode Lottery).
  - **Need:** people with albinism in Zambia face myth-driven violence (including mutilation/killing for body parts sold to traditional healers), plus exclusion from healthcare, education, and legal protection.
  - **Geographic focus:** Luapula, Northern, Eastern, and Muchinga provinces.
  - **Five-pillar approach** (previously proven successful in **Tanzania**): (1) strategic advocacy for protection/health/education; (2) capacity development of local albinism groups; (3) awareness-raising campaigns in hotspots; (4) investigation & legal-system access (prosecuting offenders, supporting victims); (5) direct support (skin-cancer screening/prevention, medical appliances).
  - **Targeted results:** a national action plan (NAP) with budget for people with albinism; equal community participation; universal access to skin-cancer screening/sunscreen in the 4 provinces; improved education access; a significant increase in reporting/follow-up of attacks.
  - **Milestone:** Zambia officially ratified its National Action Plan for people with albinism in April 2026.
- **DS Hub (Developmental Care and Support Services Hub)** — first hub officially handed over in the **Philippines** (14 July 2026).

- **"Participation" theme page** — built around the principle **"Nothing about us without us"** (grounded in UNCRPD Article 29 and UN CRC Article 12).
  - Cites a 2022 statistic: only **66% of town halls, 63% of ministries, and 48% of NGOs** were accessible to wheelchair users.
  - References the **"Flower of Participation"** tool (developed by CHOICE for Youth and Sexuality and YouAct) for explaining Meaningful Youth Participation (MYP).
  - Features youth ambassador **Eric Ngabonziza** (Rwanda), who has a disability himself and represented Liliane Fonds at the 2025 Global Disability Summit in Berlin and other international forums (e.g., ICFP 2025).
  - Liliane Fonds runs a dedicated **Youth Panel**, giving young people with disabilities direct influence/voice over programme decisions (see `liliane_fonds_organisation.md`/news references).
  - **Contact:** Daphne Visser (Advocacy & SRHR Adviser).

## Country Profiles (examples — individual country pages exist at lilianefonds.org/countries/[region]/[country])

### DRC (East — Uvira, South Kivu)
- **Facts:** 71% of youth with disabilities needing medical care last year didn't receive it; 52% needing mobility aids lack access; 39% have never completed a year of school.
- **Partner network:** "Muungano Tegemeza" — six local organisations working across health, education, livelihoods, social inclusion, and empowerment via CBR (inclusive education, psychosocial support, vocational training, SRHR).

### Indonesia
- **Facts:** 2.78 million children (ages 5–17) live with a disability; only 3% of disabled people are registered; 36% of children with disabilities don't attend school; only 20% of districts have disability action plans.
- **Partner:** Yayasan NLR Indonesia and other local partners, active in East Java, Central Sulawesi, and Nusa Tenggara Timur; work spans early identification/home-based rehabilitation, inclusive education/health/livelihoods, local care-network/policy strengthening, and SRHR access (including support for people affected by leprosy, given the NLR — Netherlands Leprosy Relief — partnership lineage).

### Who Can Partner
Liliane Fonds welcomes collaboration with:
- Non-governmental organisations (NGOs)
- Government agencies and policymakers
- Research institutions
- Donors and funding organisations
- Community-based groups and local service providers

### Ways to Get/Stay Involved
- **Co-creating multi-year programmes** with governments, foundations, and financial partners to tackle root causes of exclusion.

- **Advocacy/awareness-raising** — mobilising communities, shaping public opinion toward inclusion.
