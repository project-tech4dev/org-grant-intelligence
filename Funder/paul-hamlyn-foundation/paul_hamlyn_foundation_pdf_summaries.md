## 1. `PHF_Annual_Report_2025-26.pdf` — Trustees' Report and Financial Statements 2025/26 (59 pages, published 31 July 2026)

### Structure
Chair's Statement → Chief Executive's Statement → Trustees' and Directors' Report → The Year in Numbers → Grants by Region → Strategic Report (three themes: *Investing for the Long Term*, *Fit for the Future*, *Togetherness*) → Managing Risk → Plans for the Future → S172 Statement → Financial Review → Independent Auditor's Report → Statement of Financial Activities → Balance Sheet → Statement of Cash Flows → Notes to the Financial Statements.

### Chair's & CEO's Statements
- Chair **James Lingwood** (new, since Jan 2026) describes a "challenging year" of volatility and turbulence affecting grantee organisations, and pays tribute to outgoing Chair **Jane Hamlyn** (Paul Hamlyn's daughter, Chair for 23 years/over two decades, stepped down end of 2025, remains a trustee) and outgoing Vice Chair **Tom Wylie** (4 years), welcoming new Vice Chair **Akeela Ahmed MBE**.
- CEO **Halima Khan** frames the year around three themes: *investing for the long-term*, *being fit for the future*, and *togetherness* — balancing long-term systemic change work with urgent/immediate needs (e.g., a new "**Kinship**" programme in a "test and learn" phase on young people's hopeful futures; **59 grants** made to migration organisations facing urgent needs from rising hostility/unrest).
- **£7.7 million** (72 grants) went specifically to work addressing racial inequity, continuing PHF's anti-racism commitment.
- The report notes the death of a PHF colleague, **Louisa Robinson**, and a resulting institutional donation to Future Dreams (a breast cancer support charity).

### The Year in Numbers (FY 2025/26)
- **1,955 total applications** (1,725 UK funds requesting £120.7m; 230 India Fund requesting £2.5m) — total requested £123.2m.
- **UK open-fund approval rate: 9.4%.**
- **419 grants approved** from **2,150 decisions**.
- **£36.1 million** total grant value awarded (up slightly from £35.9m in 2024/25).
- **Endowment value: £997 million at 31 March 2026** (up sharply from ~£918m in 2022/23).
- Racial-equity breakdown (of 293 grants analysed): 27% to organisations led by people from racially-inequitable communities; 26% for work specifically addressing racial inequity; 15% for work addressing racial inequity AND led by such communities.
- Grant size distribution: most grants fall in the **£20k–£50k** band (177 grants) and **£100k–£250k** band (127 grants); only 4 grants exceeded £500k.
- Grant duration: 193 grants were "over 3 years"; 115 were "1–2 years"; 38 were "up to 1 year" (89 of these short grants specifically supported organisations responding to immediate/urgent challenges).
- Spend by programme area (£k): Arts £6,579k; Investing in Young People £6,364k; Migration £5,570k; Education and Learning through the Arts £5,558k; Evidence and Learning £2,969k (this appears to be India, see note below); Nurturing Ideas and People £1,593k; India £1,440k; Voice, Influence and Partnership £360k; Kinship £302k. *(Note: chart labels in the source PDF are visually paired and slightly ambiguous in text-extraction; treat individual figures as approximate.)*

### Strategic Report — "Investing for the Long Term"
- Four organisations completed **10 years** of PHF funding this year: Children's Law Centre Northern Ireland, Empire Fighting Chance, Football Beyond Borders, and Gloucestershire Young Carers.
- **Youth Strategic Investment Fund** marked its **10th anniversary** (started 2016).
- Place-based/"Neighbourhood Fund" grantees: Camden Giving, Islington Giving, Young Camden Foundation.
- Grant via Strategic Interventions Fund to **Community Foundation for Northern Ireland** for a community-resilience programme responding to rising racist violence in NI.
- £400,000 grant to **Civic Power Fund** (grassroots community organising; also runs the Movement Solidarity Fund with Migration Exchange to protect frontline social-justice groups).
- Case studies: **Muskaan** (£61,332/2 yrs, supporting Pardhi community youth); **We Belong** (£550,000/5 yrs, supporting young migrants navigating the UK immigration system); **My Life My Say** (£550,000/5 yrs, youth democratic participation).

### Strategic Report — "Fit for the Future"
- Funded **Lumos Transforms** (with Trust for London) to build a resilience/wellbeing programme for migration-sector organisations, based on Lumos's Resilience Toolkit.
- **Arts Fund suspended to new applications**: since its 2024 refresh, it received **£166 million in applications against a £6.5 million annual budget** — only **7% success rate**. PHF chose to pause new applications rather than continue an overly competitive process, while maintaining funding to the existing portfolio.
- Internal anti-racism work: all-staff away day on anti-racism; reflective spaces facilitated by the Social Justice Collective.
- Case studies: **TACO!** (£144,000/3 yrs, community arts practice); **Motionhouse** (£251,000/3 yrs, dance in primary schools); **Development Education Centre South Yorkshire** (£162,000/2 yrs, climate change education through drama).

### Strategic Report — "Togetherness"
- First-ever **Ideas and Pioneers Fund "Celebrate and Connect" event** (40 people, mostly first-time grantees).
- Annual **"TeachMeet"** for Teacher Development Fund participants.
- Annual **Migration Residential** (130+ attendees) on "what makes a movement for migrant justice?"
- New funder collaboration: **"Lead the Change"** — with 8 other funders including BBC Children in Need and The National Lottery Community Fund, focused on youth spaces, facilitation, and participation.
- All PHF staff conducted **20 site visits** to funded organisations in June.
- Case studies: **360Giving** (£200,000/5 yrs, unrestricted grant — funds cited AI-bot-scraping cybersecurity costs); **Disabled Students UK** (£19,200/1 yr, Representative Empowerment Project); **Institute for Voluntary Action Research/IVAR** (£150,000/3 yrs).

### Financial Review
- Endowment Fund = original Paul Hamlyn gifts + net investment gains; trustees aim to preserve its real purchasing power while retaining discretion over disbursement levels.
- **Investment portfolio return: +12.6%** for the 12 months to March 2026, vs. a benchmark of +6.9% (UK RPI +4%) — a "barbell" strategy (significant equities/venture exposure balanced by ~1/3 in cash and physical gold) was credited for capturing upside while limiting downside risk amid market volatility (tariffs, a "third Gulf war" in Feb 2026, and an ongoing AI infrastructure investment boom).
- **Net assets at 31 March 2026: £950.84 million**; after removing tangible fixed assets (£5.87m) and illiquid fixed-asset investments (£527.10m), **total available reserves: £417.87 million**, against a required minimum of ~£40.67 million (6 months' working capital).
- Grant and charitable expenditure rose 3% (£1.1m) year-on-year, reflecting inflation; investment management costs rose 9% to £10.2 million.
- Signatory to the Funder Commitment on Climate Change; operates "Our Neighbourhood Fund" (est. 2018) allocating 1% of annual grant funding to the local King's Cross community; participant in the Knowledge Quarter local network.

## 2. `PHF_Annual_Report_2024-25.pdf` — Trustees' Report and Financial Statements 2024/25 (59 pages, published 16 July 2025)

### Key Themes
"A period of renewal, strengthening relationships, and meeting the challenge."

### Leadership Transition
- **Moira Sinclair** departed as CEO after **10 years**; **Halima Khan** joined as new CEO in late 2024.
- Trustees departing: Tim Bunting, Tony Hall, Andy Headley, Charles Leadbeater.
- New trustees welcomed: Bernard Dallé, Marcus Davey, Jennifer Forster, Martha Mackenzie.
- Chair (at the time): **Jane Hamlyn CBE**.
- **30th anniversary of Awards for Artists** celebrated (Nov 2024); conclusion of the "Major Grants" programme, with its final grant going to the **Royal Liverpool Philharmonic Society** for music education in the Liverpool City Region.

### Fund "Renewal"
- **Arts Fund** and **Migration Fund** relaunched in 2024 after a strategic review (renamed from "Arts Access and Participation" → "Arts", and "Migration and Integration" → "Migration").
- **Ideas and Pioneers Fund** reopened after being closed for **2.5 years**.

### The Year in Numbers (FY 2024/25)
- **291 grants approved** from **1,485 application decisions** — **20% approval rate**.
- **£35.9 million** total grant value.
- **Applications up 75%** year-on-year in number, and **almost 90%** in value requested — attributed partly to the reopened/relaunched funds and heightened sector need.
- UK grant applications: 1,818 (note: slightly different framing from the "year in numbers" infobox vs. body text — both figures ~1,800).

### Context & Collaboration
- The **racist riots of summer 2024** across the UK directly affected many PHF grantees, reinforcing the need to tackle root causes of division alongside immediate crisis needs.
- Active co-funding collaborations: **Justice Together**, **Power of Pop Fund**, **Civic Power Fund**, **LocalMotion**, **UK Democracy Fund**.
- Increased **Access Support Bursary** usage (covers costs like British Sign Language interpreters for applicants).
- Arts Fund team began visiting all shortlisted applicants "on their home turf."

## 3. `PHF_India_Programme_Strategic_Plan_2023-33.pdf` — India Programme Strategic Plan 2023–33 (42 pages, published 14 November 2023)

### Purpose
Sets out PHF's India programme strategy and guiding principles for the **10-year period 2023–2033**, updating the previous strategy (in place since 2007). PHF has funded development work in India since **1992**.

### Executive Summary
Built on the belief that, with suitable guidance, communities can think through their own development challenges and build equitable relationships with the rest of society. PHF supports NGOs designing and implementing community-co-created social development initiatives grounded in empathy, mindfulness, equity, inclusion, and diversity. The programme continues to operate via **open grants** (projects led by an organisation's own convictions, not by PHF-set themes) in existing **priority geographical areas**, and commits to upholding the dignity and values of the **Constitution of India**.

### Mission & Values
- **Mission:** to be an effective and independent funder, partnering with organisations to place disadvantaged people at the heart of leading change.
- **Values:** Trust; Collaboration and Connection; Openness; Thoughtfulness; Best Practice and Innovation; Equality and Diversity (explicitly tied to constitutional values of justice, liberty, equality, fraternity).

### Strategic Aims (4 main + 2 secondary)
1. Encourage NGOs to take on community-centric development programmes
2. Enable communities to take charge of their own development
3. Encourage development funding to address systemic change
4. Enhance the capacity of organisations and people who facilitate the above
5. *(secondary)* Enhance knowledge and learning about development action
6. *(secondary)* Develop PHF itself as an "exemplar foundation"

### Key Shifts / Emerging Context Since the Previous Strategy
- **Urbanisation** — new urban challenges affecting young people, especially men, driven by work/housing/entitlement/lifestyle factors.
- **Employment** — rising unemployment despite gig-economy growth; declining profitability of natural-resource-based livelihoods (agriculture, forest access); emerging social entrepreneurship.
- **Growing economic/social inequality** — compounded by the rise of Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) funding, which tends to prioritise quick impact over long-term "process-led" approaches favoured by traditional development organisations.
- **Changing funding ecosystem** — new philanthropy types entering alongside traditional funders, creating collaboration opportunities.
- **Public perception** of the social development sector shifting, an emerging concern.
- **COVID-19** — significant disruption to social development processes.

### Structure of the Rest of the Document (per Table of Contents)
Priority geographical areas (p.24) · Levels of change (p.25) · Overarching criteria (p.27) · Reviewing and supporting organisations (p.29) · Reviewing the strategy (p.32) · Post-Covid initiatives (p.32) · Emergency support (p.33) · Collaborating with other donors (p.34) · Programme budgets (p.35) · The India programme team (p.36) · Glossary (p.37).

## 4. `PHF_Racial_Justice_Audit_2024-25.pdf` — Racial Justice Audit Analysis of PHF's UK Grant-making 2024/25 (27 pages, published 9 December 2025)

### Purpose
The **fifth annual racial justice audit** of PHF's UK grant-making, using the **Funders for Race Equality Alliance (FREA)** toolkit. Tracks the percentage of PHF funding on grants "by and for" communities experiencing racial inequity, year over year since 2020/21.

### Methodology
- **Three criteria** assessed per grant: (1) intended to benefit communities experiencing racial inequity; (2) funded organisation's mission/purpose is to benefit people experiencing racial inequity; (3) funded organisation is "led by" communities experiencing racial inequity.
- Grants meeting criterion 1 = "benefit" grants; grants meeting all three = "by and for" grants.
- **Methodological changes this year:** the "led by" threshold reverted to the original **majority definition** (≥50% of Board AND ≥50% of senior staff from racialised communities), having previously been aligned to a higher **DEI Data Standard** threshold (≥75% Board, ≥50% senior staff) in 2022/23–2023/24. Also, the definition of "experiencing racial inequity" was **narrowed** — no longer automatically includes all people with migration experience; grantholders are now separately asked if their mission is primarily to benefit migrants.

### Which Grants Are Covered
Of **£35,756,273** total grants (296 grants) made in 2024/25, the audit covers **80% of value (£28,579,344) / 71% of number (209 grants)**. Excluded: regranting (e.g., Helen Hamlyn Trust, £2.0m), grants to individuals (Nurturing Ideas and People, £1.34m), and grants abroad (India programme, £3.07m), plus Major Grants (£761k).

**Breakdown of audited funding by portfolio:** Arts £6.5m (29 grants); Investing in Young People £6.98m (52 grants); Migration £5.51m (41 grants); Arts Education £5.32m (35 grants); Other £4.27m (52 grants).

**Open vs. invitation-only:** 72% of audited funding was through open/unsolicited funds (Arts Fund, Arts-based Learning Fund, Teacher Development Fund, Youth Fund, Migration Fund); 28% invitation-only (Our Neighbourhood Fund, Development Fund, Backbone Fund, Evidence and Learning Fund, Youth Strategic Investment Fund, Youth Led Change grants).

### Headline Findings (2024/25)
- **32%** of audited funding went to grants benefiting communities experiencing racial inequity — a **decrease** vs. previous years (44% in 2020/21 and 2023/24; 35% in 2021/22; 38% in 2022/23), largely attributed to the narrower "racial inequity" definition excluding migrants by default.
- **17%** of funding went to grants "led by and for" racialised communities (majority definition) — comparable to prior years (11%, 16%, 20%*, 16%* in 2020/21–2023/24 respectively, *= DEI Data Standard threshold years); under the higher DEI Data Standard threshold, 2024/25 would be **13%**.
- **Applications:** total open-fund applications rose **14%** year-on-year; applications from "led by" organisations rose **65%**, now **15% of all applications** (up from 10% the prior year).
- **Approval rates:** "led by" organisations had a **19%** approval rate vs. **13%** for others — a consistent pattern across audit years.
- **Grant size/length parity:** average grant size for "by and for" grants (£154,339 in 2024/25) was within ~1% (adjusted for inflation) of "non-by and for" grants (£143,161) — closer parity than in previous years (2020/21: £137,373 vs £128,108).

### What This Means for PHF's Grant-Making
The report frames these results within PHF's continuing, explicit organisational commitment to **anti-racism and DEI**, positioning the narrowing of definitions as methodological refinement rather than a substantive retreat from its racial-justice commitments, while noting opportunities for further improvement in reaching "led by" organisations, especially in funds like Arts-based Learning and Teacher Development where such organisations are underrepresented among applicants.
