# Paul Hamlyn Foundation — India Fund

Source: phf.org.uk/funding/india-fund; India Programme Strategic Plan 2023–33 (PDF, downloaded); phf.org.uk/about/history

India is the **only country PHF funds outside the UK**. PHF has worked in India since **1990/1992** (accounts vary slightly — the relationship began circa 1990–1992 with D.R. Mehta of BMVSS; PHF's own India Programme materials state "we have been working in India since 1992").

## Aim of the Fund
Funding small NGOs working with the most vulnerable communities in priority geographical areas. Strategic aims:
- Encourage NGOs to take on community-centric development programmes.
- Enable communities to take charge of their own development and improve their lives.
- Encourage development funding to address systemic change.
- Enhance the capacity of organisations and people who facilitate the above aims.

## Amount & Duration
- **Amount:** ₹10,00,000 to ₹40,00,000 (₹10 lakh–₹40 lakh, roughly £9,500–£38,000 GBP equivalent)
- **Duration:** 1 to 2 years
- **Deadline:** rolling application cycle; **applications assessed biannually in March and October**

## Strategic Plan (2023–2033)
PHF's India Programme Strategic Plan covers a **10-year period (2023–2033)**. Full document downloaded: `PHF_India_Programme_Strategic_Plan_2023-33.pdf`.

## Who PHF Wants to Support
Organisations must be **local Indian NGOs with FCRA (Foreign Contribution Regulation Act) registration**. PHF looks for organisations that are:
- Working with communities to develop their own ideas.
- Improving overall community wellbeing long-term (not just short-term fixes).
- Developing links with panchayats (rural) and municipalities (urban).
- Aware of and mitigating their environmental impact, seeking to improve local ecology.
- Working to promote gender equality and support people of all genders to make change.

## Vulnerable Communities Targeted
Including but not limited to:
- Communities with little/no access to education
- Communities with little/no access to health services
- Dalit communities, particularly those in dangerous/harmful work
- Disabled people, particularly children
- Families living in extreme poverty
- Tribal communities fighting for/dispossessed of land and resources
- Women

## Priority Geographical Areas
PHF **only** funds work in these priority areas — central parts of India among the poorest, stretching from eastern Gujarat to West Bengal/Assam:

**Core priority states:**
- Madhya Pradesh
- Chhattisgarh
- Odisha
- Jharkhand
- Bihar
- West Bengal
- Assam

**Additional culturally identifiable sub-regions (facing extreme poverty within otherwise broader states):**
- Mewar and Hadoti (southern/south-eastern Rajasthan)
- Bundelkhand (northern Madhya Pradesh + southern Uttar Pradesh districts bordering MP)
- The Dangs (south-eastern Gujarat)
- Telangana (northern Andhra Pradesh — note: as written on PHF's site, listed as a sub-region of Andhra Pradesh)
- Vidharbha (eastern/northern Maharashtra)

> **Overlap note for funder-matching:** several of these priority states (Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand) directly overlap with Azim Premji Foundation's own Field Institute states — useful for cross-referencing NGOs that might be eligible for both funders.

## What PHF Will NOT Fund (India Fund specific)
- Non-Indian organisations, or locally registered branches of non-Indian organisations
- Organisations **without FCRA registration** (note: PHF does **not** work on "prior permission" as an alternative to FCRA)
- Political or religious institutions
- Applications received through consultants or fundraisers — organisations must apply to PHF directly

## Application Process — Two Tiers
Applications are **open year-round**; PHF does not issue formal calls for proposals.

### Micro-level
- Small-to-medium NGOs with a **direct implementation role** with communities.
- Local to their area of work, with field presence; already implementing (or have implemented) initiatives there.
- Typically an **annual outlay of less than ₹1.5 crore** (≈ £150,000 GBP).
- All micro grants restricted to the **priority geography areas** listed above.
- **~70–80% of India Fund's total funding** goes to this level.

### Meso/Macro-level
- Larger-scale organisations able to view change from a broader perspective; compile/consolidate learning from multiple micro-level initiatives.
- Function as "organisations of learning" — advocating policy change, improving implementation of existing policy, and enhancing the capacity of other NGOs.
- **Can be located outside the priority geography.**
- **~10–15%** of total funding goes to this level.
- Since most funding is at micro-level, most applicants should use the micro application form; a separate meso/macro application form exists for larger-scale applicants.

## How to Apply
- Online application system; eligibility questions asked at login to confirm fit.
- Eligibility questions can be previewed in advance in **English and Hindi**.
- Contact: India Fund team via the "Get in touch" / email us option on the India Fund page.

## Context: Historical Roots
- Early relationship (from ~1990) with **D.R. Mehta**, founder of **Bhagwan Mahaveer Viklang Sahayata Samiti (BMVSS)**, a Jaipur-based non-profit providing free mobility aids/prosthetics, developer of the low-cost "**Jaipur Foot**" prosthetic leg.
- In 2012 (PHF's 25th anniversary), PHF made a **£1 million endowment gift** to BMVSS to help it provide prosthetic limbs in India and other countries.
- PHF's India-focused spending has been reported at roughly **£2.2 million/year** (per third-party InfluenceWatch summary), covering health, education, shelter, disability support, and other social development activities.
