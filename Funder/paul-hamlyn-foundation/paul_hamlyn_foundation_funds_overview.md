# Paul Hamlyn Foundation — Funds & Programmes

Source pages: phf.org.uk/funding, /where-does-our-funding-go, /advancing-social-justice and each fund's own page.

## Overview
PHF's grant-making is organised around **six strategic funding priorities**, each mapped to specific funds. In 2024/25, PHF approved **291 grants totalling £35.9 million**; 80% of the value went to the six priority areas below. Most funds use a **rolling, open application process** (no fixed annual deadline) — exceptions are noted per fund.

| Strategic Priority | Fund(s) |
|---|---|
| Investing in Young People | Youth Fund (+ invitation-only Youth Strategic Investment Fund) |
| Migration and Integration | Migration Fund |
| Arts Access and Participation | Arts Fund |
| Education and Learning through the Arts | Arts-based Learning Fund + Teacher Development Fund |
| Nurturing Ideas and People | Ideas and Pioneers Fund + Awards for Artists |
| Creating Opportunities for People and Communities in India | India Fund |
| *(cross-cutting)* | Backbone Fund (invitation-only, strengthens civil-society infrastructure) |

## 1. Arts Fund
- **Focus:** organisations working at the intersection of art and social change; strengthening the cultural sector.
- **Status:** currently **closed to new applications** (paused following a strategic review; PHF maintains funding to the existing portfolio).
- **Definition of "arts":** crafts, creative writing (incl. poetry), dance, design, film, music, opera, photography, digital arts/media, theatre and drama, visual arts, cross-arts practices.
- **Eligibility:** UK-wide; turnover of no less than **£60,000/year** (last audited accounts or 3-year average, whichever higher); funds up to **50% of an organisation's annual turnover over three years**.
- **What's funded:** core costs and programmes central to the organisation's mission — building capacity/resources for historically underfunded communities, exploring artists' role in social justice, creating infrastructure for a more equitable cultural sector.
- **Excludes:** newly registered orgs without audited accounts; individuals; capital projects/equipment purchase (plus general exclusions).
- **Priority:** applications that are actively anti-racist and intersectional; consideration of impact from racial inequity, socio-economic disadvantage, disability, rural isolation, immigration-system experience.

## 2. Youth Fund
- **Amount:** up to **£150,000** total (max £50,000/year for 3 years; no grants under £30,000/year)
- **Duration:** 3 years
- **Deadline:** rolling
- **Focus:** organisations working with young people aged **14–25** to drive systemic change so future generations can thrive; funds strategic, long-term investment (not one-off projects).
- **Approach required — "asset-based working":** starts from young people's strengths/potential; supports youth-led work where power is shared and youth voice influences decision-making.
- **Particularly welcomes** applications from organisations led by people most impacted by racism, ableism, classism, sexism, xenophobia, homophobia, and/or transphobia.
- **For core costs:** salaries, organisational and delivery costs — to grow the impact of existing work, not new/untested approaches.
- **Excludes (fund-specific):** newly registered orgs w/o audited accounts; statutory-responsibility work; project-based activity with no scope for impact beyond immediate beneficiaries; service delivery without a focus on wider change; research without a direct link to action; work focused on hospice/end-of-life care for young people.
- **Scale:** ~20 grants/year; average success rate 10–12%.
- **Application:** two-step process via online portal; access-support bursary of up to £750 available.
- **Related invitation-only fund:** **Youth Strategic Investment Fund** — recently made grants of £550,000 (plus £175,000+ of expert support) to Gendered Intelligence, Peer Power Youth, and Reaching Higher (announced June 2026, total £2m+).

## 3. Migration Fund
- **Amount:** up to **£60,000/year (3–4 years)**; up to **£50,000/year (5 years)**
- **Duration:** 3 to 5 years
- **Deadline:** rolling
- **Focus:** "a world in which everyone is free to move, and no one is forced to move." Went through in-depth consultation with the migration field; new criteria in place since **April 2024**.
- **Eligibility:** not-for-profits of any size, UK-wide; **newly established and unincorporated groups are explicitly welcome** (one of only two funds accepting unincorporated applicants, along with Ideas and Pioneers).
- **Priority applicants:** organisations led by migrants and diaspora communities; those working with historically underfunded groups/regions; annual turnover under £500,000; those with less access to funding elsewhere.
- **What's funded:** core funding (salaries, organisational costs, safeguarding practice costs), programme-specific funding, or partnership funding.
- **Special exclusion carve-out:** this is the **only fund that supports work benefiting people outside the UK**, as long as directly related to benefiting people in the UK.
- **Migration Advisory Group:** includes PHF staff and representatives from funded organisations across the migration sector, feeding into strategic decisions.

## 4. Ideas and Pioneers Fund
- **Amount:** up to **£20,000** (plus a flexible support package)
- **Duration:** up to 18 months
- **Deadline:** annual call — the 2025 round has closed; reopens periodically (2025: 319 applications received, 30 grants awarded)
- **Focus:** individuals and groups exploring bold/brave ideas for social change — new approaches or adaptations of existing approaches.
- **Eligibility:** as of the latest round, organisations with **turnover over £150,000 in the last financial year are not eligible** (changed from a staff-headcount threshold, to prioritise smaller/less-funded organisations); accepts **unincorporated groups**.
- **Note:** ~10% of applications in the first round were ineligible (fund-specific or general exclusions).

## 5. Arts-based Learning Fund
- **Amount:** £30,000–£300,000 total; up to £100,000/year
- **Duration:** 1 to 4 years
- **Deadline:** rolling
- **Focus:** arts/cultural organisations partnering with **formal education settings** (state-funded primary/secondary schools, SEND specialist schools, FE colleges, alternative provision e.g. Pupil Referral Units, some state-funded Early Years settings) to embed arts-based learning into curricula.
- **Work must:** take place during school hours (no after-school/holiday clubs); centre equity and care; embed into the curriculum (any subject); take an in-depth, sustained approach (not one-off).
- **What's funded:** core funding (salaries/org costs), delivery costs (practitioner fees, teacher cover, materials), training/CPD, evidence-gathering costs.
- **Excludes:** non-state-funded settings (with some SEND/AP exceptions); recently registered orgs w/o accounts; statutory-entitlement work; therapeutic-practice work; non-arts-based creativity work. Rarely funds >50% of an applicant's annual turnover.
- **Optional step:** book an "enquiry call" with a grants manager before applying (response within 4 weeks).
- **Process:** rolling; ~4 months from application to decision; successful applications ~6 months from application to first payment.

## 6. Teacher Development Fund
- **Focus:** supports **teachers** (not just arts organisations) to develop arts-based approaches for equitable classrooms; companion fund to Arts-based Learning.
- **Eligibility:** lead applicant can be a state-sector primary school OR an arts/cultural organisation; must include **1+ arts/cultural organisation partner**; **6–10 schools** per project; each school commits ≥2 teachers + 1 senior leader; mainstream, SEND, or Alternative Provision primary-age settings; state sector only.
- **Deadline:** two-stage application; next Stage 1 deadline **11 November 2026, 12 noon**.
- **Access support:** bursary up to £750 for accessibility needs during application.

## 7. Awards for Artists
- **Type:** invitation/nomination-based (not an open application) — a **£75,000, no-strings award over 3 years** per artist.
- **Established:** 1994; celebrated 30th anniversary in 2024.
- **Track record:** 367 artists supported, totalling **over £12 million** since inception.
- **Focus rotation:** composers (1994), choreographers (1995), visual artists/sculpture (1996), poets (1997); since 1998 consistently **visual artists**, and since 2007 also **composers** — now the two recognised art forms.
- **Selection basis:** talent, promise, tenacity, need, and achievement to date — explicitly "an award, not a prize" (not for commercial success).
- **COVID-era special rounds:** 2020 — every eligible nominee (100+ artists) received £10,000 (£1m+ total); 2021 — 40 artists (20 visual artists + 20 composers) received £25,000 each (£1m total).
- **Confidentiality:** nominee identities remain confidential throughout the selection process.
- Notable past recipients include Anthea Hamilton, Yinka Shonibare, Ryan Gander, Phyllida Barlow, Gustav Metzger, Sonia Boyce, Rose Wylie, Ed Atkins, and many others (full list on PHF site).

## 8. Backbone Fund (invitation-only)
- **Amount:** most grants for **5 years, max £250,000**; PHF allocates **£2 million/year**, making ~6–8 new awards annually.
- **Focus:** organisations strengthening **civil society infrastructure** — capacity building, policy/advocacy, influencing, networking, research; "backbone" organisations that provide shared services/networks.
- **Goals:** equitable opportunities in the voluntary sector (especially for historically underrepresented groups); engagement with marginalised communities; pursuit of deep structural change.
- **Selection:** invitation-only; a cross-department working group (migration, arts, youth, strategic teams) deliberates over ~6 months; shortlist goes to the Board of Trustees for final approval.
- **How to express interest:** organisations can email the "Backbone Inbox" to log interest, though the fund itself is not open-application.

## 9. Other Invitation-Only & Ad-Hoc Funds
(Source: phf.org.uk/funding/invitation-only-funding)

These sit outside the six main open funds and are awarded at the Foundation's/trustees' discretion rather than through unsolicited applications.

### Annual invitation-only funds
- **Backbone Fund** — see section 8 above.
- **Youth Strategic Investment Fund** — up to **£550,000**, 5-year duration, rolling; PHF's long-term offer to organisations with a strong track record of working with young people to achieve positive change. Marked its **10th anniversary** in 2025/26 (started 2016). Recent awards (2025/26): £550,000 each to **Gendered Intelligence**, **Peer Power Youth**, and **Reaching Higher** (£2m+ total, plus £175,000+ of expert support).

### Ad-hoc funds (smaller, more responsive to context)
- **Our Neighbourhood Fund** — allocates **1% of PHF's annual grant funding** to organisations within **one mile of PHF's King's Cross offices**; organisations identified/visited directly by PHF staff and trustees to build genuine local relationships. Established 2018 in response to rising local hardship. Recent grantees include Camden Giving, Islington Giving, and Young Camden Foundation.
- **Evidence and Learning Fund** — a **closed** fund; grants made only by invitation or through opportunities identified via other funding streams/collaborative work.
- **Voice, Influence and Partnerships Fund** — exists to amplify the voice/experience of organisations and individuals working towards social justice. Provides small grants to help fund holders: support/develop advocacy work; bring together sector allies and improve partnership working; commission work specifically focused on giving people a voice. Example grantees: Alliance Publishing Trust, Association of Charitable Foundations, British Refugee Council, Migrants Organise, NAVCA, The Refugee Asylum and Migration Policy (RAMP) Project, Voice4Change England.
- **Development Fund** — supports development of **new areas of work and partnerships** aligned with PHF's overall strategic direction (e.g., a £5,700/6-month grant to Actors Touring Company to evaluate their "Tambo & Bones" performances).
- **Emergency Funding** — PHF's flexible capacity to respond quickly to emergencies/humanitarian appeals, awarded at trustees' discretion. Notable examples: a **£20 million emergency COVID Response Fund**; a response to the UK cost-of-living crisis; contributions to **Disasters Emergency Committee (DEC)** appeals for overseas disasters.
- **Major Grants** — large, discretionary grants/endowments to organisations making a "significant contribution" to PHF's fields of work, reflecting PHF's freedom as an independent endowed foundation to plan for the long term. This programme has now **concluded**; its final grant went to the **Royal Liverpool Philharmonic Society** (music education support for children/young people in the Liverpool City Region). No new Major Grants are planned while PHF's "Kinship Discovery" process (see below) is underway.

## 10. Kinship / Kinship Discovery (emerging initiative)
(Source: PHF Annual Report 2023/24; PHF Annual Report 2025/26)
- **"Kinship Discovery"** was launched as a year-long programme (from 2023/24) of exploration, research, and insight-generation to inform a **major new funding initiative focused on young people and hope for the future**.
- It explores the possible implications of releasing a significant portion of PHF's endowment specifically to support children and young people's futures — this is part of why PHF paused new Major Grants during this process.
- By 2025/26, "**Kinship**" appears as its own line item in PHF's grant-spend-by-programme-area chart (£302k in 2025/26), suggesting the discovery phase has moved into an early "test and learn" implementation phase, per the CEO's 2025/26 statement: "our Kinship programme...has entered a 'test and learn' phase exploring how young people can build more hopeful futures."

## 11. Notable Funder Collaborations
PHF frequently co-funds with other trusts, foundations, and statutory bodies to extend reach:
- **Act for Change Fund** — with Esmée Fairbairn Foundation and the National Lottery Community Fund's #iwill Fund (£3.6m partnership for youth-led social-justice action; now concluded)
- **Justice Together (Initiative)** — 14-funder collaboration (incl. the Greater London Authority) increasing access to justice for people in the immigration system (PHF committed £2m)
- **Lead the Change** — new (2025/26) £3.4m, 3-year collaboration with 8 other funders including BBC Children in Need and The National Lottery Community Fund, to grow young people's confidence/leadership through workshops, mentoring, and youth-led community projects (includes a youth panel with real influence over funding decisions)
- **Power of Pop Fund**, **Civic Power Fund**, **LocalMotion**, **UK Democracy Fund** — ongoing collaborations supporting a "just society" agenda
- **Movement Solidarity Fund** (via Civic Power Fund + Migration Exchange) — protects frontline social-justice groups

## Grant Spend & Application Stats (2024/25)
- 291 grants approved, totalling £35.9 million.
- 885 decisions made on applications to UK open funds; 128 grants resulted — an average **14% success rate** across UK open funding.
- "Led by" organisations (per FREA racial-justice definition) had a **19%** approval rate vs **14%** overall average.
- Arts Education portfolio = Arts-based Learning Fund + Teacher Development Fund.
- Nurturing Ideas and People portfolio ≈ half Ideas and Pioneers Fund, half Awards for Artists.
- Investing in Young People portfolio ≈ half Youth Fund, half invitation-only funding.

## Who Is Eligible — General Rules Across Funds
- **UK-registered not-for-profit organisations** (charities, community organisations, social enterprises, not-for-profit companies) — the default requirement for most funds.
- **Unincorporated groups** accepted only by the **Migration Fund** and **Ideas and Pioneers Fund**.
- **Individuals** can be supported only via **Awards for Artists** (invitation/nomination-based) and the **Ideas and Pioneers Fund** (individuals and groups).
- For **India**: only **Indian-registered NGOs with FCRA registration**, working in PHF's priority geographic areas (or meso/macro-level organisations, which can be based elsewhere in India). See `paul_hamlyn_foundation_india_fund.md`.
- Turnover thresholds vary by fund (e.g., Arts Fund requires ≥£60,000/yr turnover; Ideas and Pioneers Fund excludes orgs with turnover over £150,000/yr, to prioritise smaller/earlier-stage groups).

## Geographic Priorities
- **UK:** all funds except the India Fund are UK-wide; PHF "actively seeks to fund work outside of London and across the UK."
- **India:** the only country PHF funds outside the UK — see `paul_hamlyn_foundation_india_fund.md` for priority states.
- **Migration Fund exception:** can support work benefiting people **outside the UK**, provided it's directly related to benefiting people in the UK.

## General Exclusions (apply across most funds)
PHF will **not** fund:
- Work already delivered, or due to start within 4 months of application (grants typically take ~4 months from application to payment)
- Non-charitable activity, or activity breaking UK law
- Proposals benefiting only one individual (except Awards for Artists / Ideas and Pioneers)
- General fundraising appeals / non-specific donation requests
- Advancement of religion
- Loan/debt repayments or deficit reduction
- **Multiple simultaneous grants** to the same organisation/individual
- Organisations without a Safeguarding Policy (where working with children/young people/adults at risk)
- Property, capital items, building/habitat restoration, or equipment purchase (as the main focus)
- Academic research, scholarships, bursaries, student fees
- Standalone websites, publications, events, or seminars (unless part of a wider proposal)
- Statutory-duty work (e.g., core local-authority education, social care, waste, planning, housing, roads, libraries services)

**Conditional exclusions (discuss with PHF first):**
- Grants where the annual amount requested exceeds the applicant's annual turnover
- For-profit organisations
- Endowment-building proposals (unless invited by the Board)
- Reapplications within 12 months of a decline (except by invitation, or in some Arts Fund cases)
- Unincorporated groups (except Migration Fund & Ideas and Pioneers Fund)
- Work benefiting people outside the UK (except India Fund priority geographies, and the Migration Fund's UK-linked exception)

## Application Process (general, across funds)
1. Use the **Fund Finder tool** (phf.org.uk/funding/find-the-right-fund-for-you/fund-finder) if new to PHF, to identify the right fund.
2. Most funds: **online application system**, rolling deadlines — exceptions: Ideas and Pioneers Fund (annual call), Teacher Development Fund (two-stage, fixed Stage 1 deadline), India Fund (assessed biannually in March/October).
3. Some funds (e.g., Arts-based Learning) offer an **optional "enquiry call"** with a grants manager before applying (response within 4 weeks).
4. **Sample application forms** (DOCX, incl. large-print versions) are published per fund for advance review.
5. **Access support bursary of up to £750** available if the application process is inaccessible at any stage.
6. Typical timeline: ~4 months from application to decision; successful applicants may wait ~6 months to first payment (Arts-based Learning Fund example).
7. A **Diversity, Equity and Inclusion monitoring form** is used across funds.

## Anti-Racism / DEI Priority (relevant to matching)
- Explicit, long-standing **anti-racist funder commitment since 2020**; applications from organisations "led by and for" communities experiencing racial inequity are actively prioritised and see higher approval rates across most funds (19% vs 14% overall in 2024/25; 19% vs 13% in the 2024/25 Racial Justice Audit's applicant-journey analysis).
