## Vision & Mission
- **Mission:** "to be an effective and independent funder, using all our resources to create opportunities and support social change." PHF partners with organisations and individuals to put them at the heart of leading change and designing solutions to overcome inequality.
- **Vision:** "a just society in which everyone, especially young people, can realise their full potential and enjoy fulfilling and creative lives."
- **Values:** Working together (power of collaboration), Trust, Openness, Kindness (every decision affects someone).

## Our Founder — Paul Hamlyn (1926–2001)
(Source: phf.org.uk/about/history/our-founder; phf.org.uk/news-and-publications/remembering-paul-hamlyn-100-years-on)
- **Born Paul Hamburger in Berlin, 1926**, to a Jewish family. His family fled Nazi persecution in **1933**, when Paul was 7, eventually settling in London — a formative experience as a child refugee that shaped his lifelong commitment to social justice.
- Anglicised his name to **Paul Hamlyn**. Served during WWII as a "**Bevin Boy**" (the name given to young men conscripted to work in coal mines instead of the armed forces).
- **1949** — started his first business selling books from a stall/wheelbarrow in **Camden Market**, London.
- Built this into **Hamlyn Books**, then **Octopus** and other publishing imprints, becoming the most successful popular publisher of his generation. Pioneered mass-market, high-quality, affordable publishing formats: the first illustrated cookbooks, the "Music for Pleasure" classical recordings series, and high-quality arts books.
- Described by publishing historian Iain Stevenson as "a natural entrepreneur with a flair for design and an instinctive sense of what the public wanted."
- Favourite motto: **"There has to be a better way"** — reflecting both his publishing innovation and his philanthropic risk-taking.
- His daughter (and PHF's Chair for 23 years until Dec 2025), **Jane Hamlyn**, recalled: "He loved to take risks, to do the unexpected by backing a person or an idea that no one else would support. He was always determined to look forward rather than back."
- **1987** — established the **Paul Hamlyn Foundation** for general charitable purposes, with an initial gift of £50 million, driven by his interest in social justice, challenging prejudice, and opening up the arts and education to everyone (particularly young people).
- Early flagship initiative: **"Hamlyn Nights" at the Royal Opera House** — heavily subsidised/free tickets so people who had never attended before could experience opera; this model influenced later PHF audience-development funding across the UK arts sector.
- **Died August 2001**, leaving the bulk of his estate (~£308 million) to the Foundation, transforming it into one of the UK's largest independent grant-making organisations.
- In **2012**, PHF published a free **audio oral history documentary** about Paul Hamlyn's life, drawing on interviews with family, friends, and colleagues (available on phf.org.uk).
- PHF's **Ideas and Pioneers Fund** is explicitly described as carrying forward his entrepreneurial, risk-backing spirit — often giving individuals/organisations their first-ever grant.
- Over 25+ years since his death, PHF has given **more than £500 million** to "many thousands" of individuals and organisations.

## History Timeline
(Source: phf.org.uk/about/history)
- **1987** — Paul Hamlyn Foundation established by publisher/entrepreneur Paul Hamlyn, guided by a commitment to social justice and opening up arts and education to everyone, particularly young people.
- **1987 (early years)** — "Hamlyn Nights" at the Royal Opera House: heavily subsidised opera tickets to widen access; this evolved into broader audience-development funding programmes across the UK.
- **1990/1992** — Grant-making in India begins. Paul Hamlyn met D.R. Mehta, founder of Bhagwan Mahaveer Viklang Sahayata Samiti (BMVSS), a Jaipur-based non-profit developing the low-cost "Jaipur Foot" prosthetic. Regular PHF funding helped BMVSS grow; in 2012 (25th anniversary), PHF made a £1m endowment gift to support BMVSS.
- **1991** — First large-scale initiative: the **National Commission on Education**, funded with PHF's largest single grant to that point (£1m). Its report, "Learning to Succeed," was published in 1993.
- **1994** — **Awards for Artists** launched — no-strings financial awards to artists (see `paul_hamlyn_foundation_funds_overview.md`).
- **2001** — Paul Hamlyn dies, leaving the bulk of his estate (~£308 million) to the Foundation, transforming its scale.
- **2002–2006** — PHF backs early-stage/new organisations as first funder, including The Reading Agency (founded 2002), Teach First, and the Prison Radio Association (funded from its 2006 inception).
- **2007** — Explicit **Social Justice programme** launched, including the "Right Here" programme (young people's mental health) and the Supported Options Initiative (undocumented young people).
- **2020–present** — Current strategy launched September 2020, built around six funding priorities: Investing in Young People; Migration and Integration; Arts Access and Participation; Education and Learning through the Arts; Nurturing Ideas and People; Creating Opportunities for People and Communities in India. Also in 2020, PHF made a formal **commitment to be an anti-racist funder**.
- **2025** — Leadership transition: Moira Sinclair (CEO for 10 years) succeeded by **Halima Khan**.

## Legal / Financial Structure
- Registered charity (Charity Commission for England & Wales, no. 1102927) and a company limited by guarantee (Companies House no. 05042279), incorporated 29 March 2004, succeeding the original 1987 foundation.
- PHF does **not fundraise**; all funding comes from its own **endowment and investment returns**, giving it full financial independence.
- Governed by its Memorandum and Articles of Association (constitution).
- Investment portfolio composition (2022/23): publicly listed equities 33%, venture capital 26%, cash 13.5%, hedge funds 12.1%, gold 11.4%, real estate 4%.
- Has an **Investment Committee** (reporting to the Board of Trustees) and an Investment Director on the senior leadership team; assesses fund managers on ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance) criteria; signatory to the Charities Responsible Investment Network and the UN Principles of Responsible Investment.
- Occasionally co-funds with other funders/statutory bodies, e.g. "Act for Change" (with the National Lottery Community Fund's #iwill Fund) and "Justice Together" (a 14-funder collaboration including the Greater London Authority).

## Anti-Racism & DEI Commitment
- In 2020, PHF made a formal commitment to be an **anti-racist funder**, informing both its grant-making and its work as an employer.
- Conducts an **annual Racial Justice Audit** of its UK grant-making since 2020/21, using the toolkit provided by the **Funders for Race Equality Alliance (FREA)**.
- 2024/25 audit findings: of £28.5 million funding audited, **32% went to projects benefiting communities experiencing racial inequity**; **17% went to grants for organisations "led by and for" communities experiencing racial inequity** (defined as ≥50% of Board and ≥50% of senior staff from such communities).
- Approval rates are higher for "led by" organisations (19%) than the overall average (14%).
- Has a staff **DEI Staff Forum** to explore anti-racism/DEI issues internally and in PHF's funded work.

## Working at PHF (Careers)
(Source: phf.org.uk/about/working-for-us)
- **Location/hybrid working:** offices in King's Cross, London; hybrid approach balancing in-person work ~40% of time (most full-time staff in-office 2 days/week).
- **Perks carried from Paul Hamlyn's own traditions:** a discretionary free lunch provided daily to all employees in the office, to encourage staff to know each other and take a proper lunch break.
- **Learning & development:** described as a "learning organisation"; annual personal professional-development budget of **£1,000** per staff member; staff encouraged to attend conferences/learning events and visit funded organisations directly (e.g., 20 staff site visits made in 2025/26).
- **Benefits:** 25 days annual leave + bank holidays + discretionary Christmas–New Year closure; generous employer pension contribution; private medical insurance; life insurance; employee assistance programme; free eye tests; free sanitary products.
- **Internal DEI structure:** a staff-created and staff-led **DEI Staff Forum** (also referred to as the "Justice, Equity, Diversity and Inclusion Collective") to embed anti-racism/DEI culture change internally.
- **Visa sponsorship:** explicitly **not offered** to job applicants.
- Current vacancies listed at phf.org.uk/about/working-for-us/current-jobs-and-opportunities.

## Learning, Transparency & Evidence Practices
(Source: phf.org.uk/advancing-social-justice/learning)
- PHF describes itself as a **"learning organisation"** — prioritising learning over pure outcomes, both in its own practice and in the work of grant holders.
- **Public grants transparency:** publishes a historical archive of every organisation funded **since April 2006** (who was funded, what work, how much) — searchable via its **grants database**, and published in the open **360Giving** data standard (see grantnav.threesixtygiving.org/org/GB-CHC-1102927).
- **Grant Holder / Applicant Perception Reports:** commissioned every ~4 years (methodology also now annual per recent reports) from the **Center for Effective Philanthropy (CEP)**, surveying grant holders and declined applicants on PHF's impact, application process, engagement, and DEI. The 2025 survey found **74% of grant holders received assistance from PHF beyond funding** (higher than CEP's typical funder benchmark), and trust in PHF was reported higher than in the 2022 survey.
- **Racial Justice Audit:** annual, since 2020/21, using the FREA toolkit (see `paul_hamlyn_foundation_pdf_summaries.md`).
- **Evidence and Learning support:** part of PHF's "Grant Plus" offer to grant holders (non-financial support alongside the grant itself).
