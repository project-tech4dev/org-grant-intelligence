## 1. Overview / About

Arpan is a globally recognised, award-winning non-profit dedicated to **eliminating Child Sexual Abuse (CSA) in India**.

- Based in **Mumbai**, with a team of **over 140 dedicated professionals**.
- India's **largest non-profit** providing CSA Prevention and Intervention services for both children and adults.
- A thought leader in CSA Prevention and Intervention — drives policy and systemic change at local, state, and national levels through advocacy and research.
- Working since **2007**; has impacted **over 20.3 million children and adults** to date.
- Received **10 National and 2 International Awards** for its work.
- Services are provided irrespective of socio-economic background, gender, sexuality, caste, religion, and political affiliation.
- **Operational at:** Mumbai, Thane and Palghar (Maharashtra), and across India through training programmes.
- **For whom:** Children, Parents, Teachers, Schools, Adult Survivors, NGOs, Social Workers, Counsellors, and Government Duty Bearers.

Arpan's flagship **Personal Safety Education (PSE) programme** equips children and adolescents (ages 4–18) with age-appropriate knowledge and skills to recognise and respond to unsafe situations. Arpan also trains and empowers teachers and parents, and uses advocacy and research to push for policy and systemic change.

## 4. The Issue: Child Sexual Abuse (CSA)

**Definition (WHO, 1999):** CSA is the involvement of a child in sexual activity that they do not fully comprehend, cannot give informed consent to, or that violates the laws or social taboos of society. It is committed by someone in a position of power/authority/trust and violates both the child's body and trust.

**Contact Sexual Abuse includes:** penetration (anus/vagina/oral), fondling of private parts, forcing child to fondle abuser, forcible kissing, sexual touching.

**Non-Contact Sexual Abuse includes:** exhibiting private parts (to/by child), photographing a child nude, making a child view sexual content, online abuse (grooming, live-streaming, CSAM), sexually explicit talk.

**Global Context:**
- WHO estimate: ~20% of girls and 5–10% of boys are victims of sexual abuse (Finkelhor, 2012).
- Systematic review (2018, 55 studies/24 countries): CSA rates range 8–31% (females), 3–17% (males).
- ~1 in 10 children abused in USA; ~1 in 20 in UK.

**Indian Context:**
- 2007 Ministry of Women and Child Development (MoWCD) national study: 53% of 12,447 children interviewed reported experiencing sexual abuse (52% boys, 47% girls).
- **NCRB 2023 data:**
  - Total crimes against children: 1,77,335 (≈9% increase over 2022's 1,62,449).
  - Sexual crimes against children (IPC + SLL): 71,641 (40% of all crimes against children); 5% increase over 2022.
  - Child rape cases: 42,106 (59% of sexual crimes against children).
  - Highest reported states: Uttar Pradesh (9,852 / 14%), Maharashtra (8,673 / 12%), Madhya Pradesh (6,559 / 9%).
  - Cases under POCSO Act: 67,694; 99% of victims are girls.
  - 89% of cyber crime cases against children involved publishing/transmitting sexually explicit material of children.
  - 52% of POCSO victims are aged 16–18.
  - 97% of offenders were known to the victim.

**Impact of CSA:** Recognised as 1 of 24 risk factors contributing to global disease burden (and one of the few preventable ones). Physical impacts (pregnancy, STDs, injuries), psychological impacts (anxiety, PTSD, depression, dissociation, regressive behaviours, suicide risk), and social impacts (withdrawal, aggression, academic decline). Framework referenced: Traumagenic Dynamics Model (Finkelhor & Browne, 1985) — traumatic sexualization, betrayal, powerlessness, stigmatization.

## 5. Programmes / Our Work

### 5.1 Personal Safety Education (PSE) — Flagship Programme
A comprehensive model empowering children (age 4–18) to:
1. Gain knowledge/skills to prevent CSA and seek support if it occurs.
2. Sensitize adult caregivers (school management, teachers, parents, staff) to create safe environments.
3. Provide children a safe space to disclose abuse/misbehavior.
4. Heal children who disclose abuse.

Built on life skills: decision-making, problem-solving, critical thinking, interpersonal relationships, self-awareness, gender awareness, resilience, empathy, de-stigmatization. Delivered through the school network in **3 districts of Maharashtra**, with a "step-up" refresher programme after 2–3 years.

**Impact:**
- Trained **176,374 children** and **124,013 adults** (parents/teachers/staff) across **270+ schools/colleges, 24 communities, 4 shelter homes/institutes** in 3 Maharashtra districts.
- **12,129** completions via Arpan eLearn; **58,392** via LMS partners.
- Psychotherapy for **12,462 cases** (6,806 CSA cases, 3,257 inappropriate behaviour, 2,399 other emotional concerns); psychoeducation to **7,875 children** and **4,399 family members/caregivers**.

**Evaluation findings (short-term):**
- Immediate increase in knowledge/skills (pre- vs post-session scores).
- 3–8% of children disclose abuse (often for first time); 100% of those who disclose receive therapeutic support.
- Increased positive communication with parents, confidence, alertness, self-esteem.

**Evaluation findings (medium/long-term):**
- External evaluation: 60/754 children (8%) reported past incidents; 90% of these sought help from a trusted adult.
- Case-study evaluation: 9/31 children (29%) disclosed unsafe experiences; 100% showed help-seeking behaviour.
- Knowledge retention: 92%→69% depending on age and time elapsed since programme.

Contact for PSE bookings: **communications@arpan.org.in** / **1800 267 2444**

## 11. Child Protection Policy

Arpan holds that child protection is "the duty of all adults and the right of every child." Its work is child-centric — children are treated as integral stakeholders in decisions concerning them. This philosophy is embedded in all of Arpan's programmes, policies, and practices, backed by a detailed formal Child Protection Policy document.

## 12. Child Safety Week (CSW)

- Launched by Arpan in **2019**; observed annually **14–20 November**.
- A nationwide, people-led movement for collective action against CSA.
- Coincides with: Children's Day–India (Nov 14), World Day for Prevention of & Healing from Child Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Violence (Nov 18), World Day for Prevention of Child Abuse (Nov 19), World Children's Day (Nov 20).
- **2024 campaign:** #ProtectedByPOCSO — first campaign to directly address offenders ("Child Sexual Abuse is a punishable offence – Stop Right Now or Get Caught").
- **2025 campaign (7th edition):** **#POCSOPakadLega** — theme focused on deterrence/enforcement, with message "If you sexually abuse a child, the law will catch you."
  - Faces of the campaign: Vidya Balan (Goodwill Ambassador), Shweta Kawaatra, Manav Gohil, Anup Soni.
  - Multimedia reach target: **100 million+**, via OTT (JioCinema, Zee5, MX Player), YouTube, social media/influencers, 10 billboards + 45 digital signage (Mumbai), 50 branded delivery bikes (Thane), 1,350 digital screens across 260 residential societies, banners at 51 public gardens, Mumbai Metro Lines 2A & 7 branding, bus branding (BEST, TMC/TMT, NMMT), 130 digital screens across Western Railway stations, 30 banners across 8 Central Railway stations.
  - School/NGO outreach: 100 schools activated; 33 districts & 218 block officers across Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, Goa, Madhya Pradesh reaching 2,000+ government schools; 40+ NGO collaborations (Mumbai, Kanpur, Delhi, Lucknow, Siliguri); 80,000+ trained teachers equipped with toolkits.
  - Mall activations (Infiniti Malad, Nexus Seawoods, Lakeshore Thane) and Metro station selfie booths.
  - Partnership with Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (ongoing, per site).

## 17. Research Publications (Selected, per official Research page)

- *Evidence and Implications for Conducting a Step-up Programme among Children Receiving a One-Time Intervention* — supports the case for periodic "step-up" refresher sessions since message retention declines over time (step-up PSE started 2016).
- *Integration of Personal Safety Education Programme in Schools: A Case Study* (published 2017) — evaluates PSE after curriculum integration; shows children can identify, refuse, and report unsafe situations, and aware caregivers help prevent/intervene.
- *Rescue and Remedy: A Process Documentation of Psychotherapy with Children in Institutions* (published in English, 2015) — documents long-term psychotherapy with rescued minors in institutions.
- *Recounting Abuse, Reporting Abusers: Reflections from Survivors on Mandatory Reporting* — interviews with adult CSA survivors on the POCSO Act's mandatory reporting clause.
- *Between the Lines – An Analysis of Media Reportage on Child Sexual Abuse* — analysis of CSA reporting trends in Mumbai newspapers (2007–2011); synopsis also published in the ISPCAN international newsletter.
- *Evaluation of Personal Safety Education Programme* (first evaluation conducted by International Market Research Bureau, 2014) — studied 620 PSE-attendee children (7 schools) vs. 150 non-attendee children (2 schools); found 8% of children reported an unsafe situation, 90% of them sought help; 100% of children discussed the programme afterward (75% with their mothers); 100% wanted to attend again.

Full page: https://www.arpan.org.in/research/
