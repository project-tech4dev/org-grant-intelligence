## 2. Arpan's Strategic Framework (as defined in the Annual Report 2024–25)

Arpan's work is delivered through **3 strategies**, implemented via **5 programmes**:

**Strategies:**
1. **Prevention and Healing Services** — for children and adults; includes building curriculum/content for new demographics and expanding reach via digital content and multi-language translation.
2. **Training of Stakeholders** — enabling replication/scaling of CSA prevention & healing services, including via digital content.
3. **Public and Systems Change** — via research and representation, to create mass awareness and advocate for systemic/policy change (curriculum integration, support services) at local, state, and national levels.

**Programmes:**
1. Personal Safety Education (PSE) Programme
2. Training and Capacity Building (Training & Advocacy) Programme
3. Digital Learning Programme
4. Mental Health Services
5. Public Awareness

**FY2024–25 organising theme:** "The Power of the 3Ms" — **M**odels for Change, **M**otivation, **M**omentum.

## 4. Programme 1: Personal Safety Education (PSE) — FY2024–25 Report Detail

### Content & Curriculum
- Arpan's **PSE curriculum spans Grades 1 to 12**.
- **New in FY2024–25:** Introduced **online safety content for Grades 1–4** (previously only older students had this) — action-oriented, illustrated modules using daily-life examples to teach identifying safe/unsafe online situations, self-protection steps, and help-seeking. Piloted in schools; field-observation insights documented for refinement.

### Psychotherapeutic Intervention (part of PSE)
- **2,074 children (16%)** sought help after the PSE programme this year.
  - 1,048 needed no therapeutic intervention (supported directly by PSE facilitators).
  - **1,026 children (8%)** needed therapeutic intervention → 1,025 referred to Arpan's in-house counsellors; 1 to a school counsellor.
- Counselling services provided to **1,277 children** via **6,393 individual sessions** and **587 group sessions** (252 of these cases carried over from 2023–24).
  - Ongoing: 275 cases; Closed: 1,002 cases.
  - Of the 1,002 closed cases: **38% CSA, 45% Inappropriate Behaviour, 17% Other Emotional Concerns.**
- Psychoeducation provided to **399 helpful adults** via 416 individual + 142 group sessions.
- Piloted **classroom-based interventions** in two schools for adolescents around teasing/misbehaviour/bullying, covering: safe behaviours, boundaries & empathy, healthy vs. harmful behaviours.

### Research: "Assertive Refusal" Study (FY2024–25)
- Qualitative study with **66 stakeholders** (experts, implementers, child & adult beneficiaries) via in-depth interviews + literature review, using the **Social-Ecological Model** (individual, interpersonal, community, societal levels).
- **Key findings:**
  - Assertiveness training is effective for CSA prevention, especially combined with rights-based education, strong family-school-community support, and child-friendly communication.
  - Children often prefer *avoiding* the abuser over saying "No," due to fear of harm/disbelief; refusal is easier with known abusers.
  - Barriers identified by parents/teachers: threats, blackmail, community exclusion, manipulation, lack of family support.
  - Enablers: confidence-building, supportive family communication, school-based training, reinforcing "it's never the child's fault."
- **Recommendations:** age/gender-responsive PSE integrated across homes & schools (roleplay-based), reinforcement of key messages, consistent adult support to counter fear/stigma/power imbalances.

### Stories of Change (FY2024–25, identities withheld/changed)
1. **Grandmother's realisation:** A grandmother attending a PSE parent session (in place of daily-wage-worker parents) recalled how her niece had been blamed and punished after disclosing an abusive relationship years earlier. Realising the family's response had been wrong, she got school permission to start conducting her own child-safety activities.
2. **9th-grader's disclosure:** During an individual step-up PSE session, a boy recalled inappropriate touching by a neighbour when he was in 3rd grade. After learning to recognise unsafe touch via PSE, he pushed the man away, told his father (who believed him immediately, having attended a parent session), and later protected a friend from the same neighbour — leading to the man being denied contact with children.
3. **Identical twins' disclosure:** After a step-up PSE session, a 14-year-old girl disclosed ongoing abuse by an uncle to a counsellor and then her mother, who ensured the uncle had no further contact. Through counselling (activities like "Feelings in a Box" and a personified-anger character called "Vulture"), it emerged she had a twin sister who—after the counsellor built trust over time—also disclosed abuse by the same uncle. Each twin healed on her own timeline (16 vs. 13 individual sessions, plus 3 joint circles).

## 5. Programme 2: Training and Advocacy Programme — FY2024–25 Report Detail

### Scale
- **51,456 professionals trained** in FY2024–25 (target was 45,000).
  - **41,138** received **advanced training** (to replicate Arpan's prevention/intervention model); **93%** were from government sectors (Education, Health, Women & Child Development, Home Affairs/Police, Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation).
    - These reached **779,256** children/adults via offline replication + **5,552** via digital modules.
  - **10,318** trained via the **basic training module** (foundational knowledge/intervention skills).
- Named training tracks: **"Personal Safety Education Training of Trainers (PSE ToT)"** and **"Counselling Skills"** training (for social workers/mental health professionals).
- Ripple effect: each trained stakeholder now reaches an average of **15 others** (up from 11 in prior years).

### Training Support Unit (TSU) — New in FY2024–25
- A dedicated structure providing **ongoing handholding/follow-up** to trained teachers/stakeholders post-training — observation, feedback, and regular engagement (shift from one-off training to sustained support).
- Field associates provide local, real-time guidance; introduced a calling/messaging application for communication & data collection with trainees.

### Proactive Mental-Health-Professional Training
- Arpan began proactively (not just reactively) engaging colleges and government stakeholders to build trauma-informed care/counselling capacity, beyond responding to inbound training requests.

### Systems/Policy Advocacy — FY2024–25 specifics
- **National:** PSE integrated (since 2020) into Ayushman Bharat's School Health & Wellness Programme (SHWP) under **Module 10 (Safety and Security against Violence and Injuries)** and **Module 11 (Promotion of Safe Use of Internet, Gadgets and Media)**. In 2024–25: ~**1 million children** and **75,000 teachers** covered under SHWP; Arpan's PSE reach via SHWP now **15.4 million children**, with potential reach of **260 million**.
  - Newly integrated into the **NCERT Grade 4 EVS (Environmental Studies) textbook** this year.
- **Maharashtra:** Participated in MSCERT's 2-day workshop to revise the Training Manual on Child Rights and Prevention (aligned to NEP learning stages); reviewed the Online Safety Module for teacher training (MSCPCR + Child Fund India).
- **Andhra Pradesh:** Trained 250+ State-Level Master Trainers (SIEMAT) who cascaded training further.
- Advisory/expert engagement with: Chhattisgarh State Technical Working Group, Delhi & Bihar State Curriculum Framework (SCF) Committees, Himachal Pradesh (as National Resource Person for SHWP refresher training), and ongoing SCERT engagement in Assam, Delhi, Sikkim, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Telangana.

### Content & Curriculum (Training)
- Developed a new **flipchart-based, illustration-rich training module** for two grade bands — **Primary (Grades 1–5)** and **Secondary (Grades 6–10)** — to help less-confident teachers deliver PSE more quickly and engagingly.

### Research/M&E — Training Programme
- Uses the **Kirkpatrick Model** (Kirkpatrick & Kirkpatrick, 2016) — 4 levels: reaction, learning, behaviour change, results.
  - **N=30,762** participants rated training; 97–98% rated "Good"–"Excellent" across 5 parameters. Highest: relevance of session content (74% "Excellent"). Lowest: confidence to talk to adults about CSA/PSE (65% "Excellent"). Only 2–3% rated "Scope for Improvement."
  - **Pre–post knowledge assessment, N=26,413:** Pre-score 52% → Post-score 71% (**19-point absolute shift; 37% relative shift** in knowledge).

### "Rural Baseline Study" (Rajasthan, FY2024–25)
- Full title: *Rural Baseline Study to Assess the Knowledge, Attitudes, and Practices on Child Sexual Abuse Prevention.*
- Conducted with external agency **Basic Paradigm** in **Ajmer, Dausa, and Sirohi districts, Rajasthan**.
- Sample: **822 children** (age 6–16, quantitative survey) + **220 adult stakeholders** in-depth interviews (53 teachers, 39 non-teaching staff, 53 parents/caregivers, 24 Panchayati Raj Institution/PRI members, 24 Frontline Health Workers/FLWs).
- **Key child findings:**
  - Only 53% aware of "Private Parts" concept (lower among younger children); only **17%** could correctly identify all Private Parts.
  - 85% showed limited understanding that a *known* person could sexually abuse them.
  - 40% knew unsafe situations could happen "anywhere," but only **7.6%** identified online spaces as unsafe.
  - **70%** didn't know how to keep themselves safe (worse in younger children); 13.5% overall (25.2% ages 6–10) didn't know how to respond to unsafe situations.
  - 74.1% could identify a trusted adult to turn to; 15.4% could not.
  - Online unsafe situations: 50% would delete their profile, 33.3% would simply ignore it (passive responses dominant).
- **Key adult findings:**
  - 35.8–40.7% of adults wrongly believe only girls are vulnerable; 17.9–32.1% believe only older children are vulnerable.
  - FLWs (40.7%) and PRIs (54.2%) wrongly believe CSA always involves physical force/visible injury (vs. teachers 73.6% and school staff 76.9% who understand better).
  - 20.6% believe only strangers abuse children; 27.7% believe family members "never" abuse children; 38.3% believe no one in their community could.
  - 84.4% know CSA can happen "anywhere," but online-risk awareness is much lower.
  - 44.4–50% (parents/FLWs/PRIs) believe children must obey adults even in unsafe situations.
  - 48–70% (across PRIs/FLWs/parents/teachers/staff) wrongly believe it's "sometimes the child's fault."
  - 23.6% underestimate long-term CSA impact ("children will get over it").
  - 19.6% don't think disclosure is hard for children; 19.8% don't think parental support matters after disclosure.
- **Recommendations:** PSE for ages 6–16 via teachers; train all teachers on CSA/PSE + parent-facing content; equip parents/community on CSA awareness & handling disclosures; myth-busting campaigns targeting teachers/parents/community.

### Story of Change (Training)
- A **Zilla Parishad Primary School** principal (Maharashtra), after Arpan's Training of Trainers, personally ran PSE for Grades 9–10 (covering safe/unsafe touch, safe/unsafe relationships, decision-making consequences, and the POCSO Act) after noticing a Grade 9 student planning to elope with an 18-year-old. The student changed her mind, returned to regular attendance, and improved academically. The principal called Arpan's ToT "the most meaningful professional development experience of his career."

## 6. Programme 3: Digital Learning Programme — FY2024–25 Report Detail

### Course Revisions & Expansion
- **Personal Safety Course (8–10 yrs):** revised version launched in **Hindi and Marathi**.
- **Personal Safety Course (11–13 yrs):** now live in **Punjabi, Gujarati, and Bengali** (in addition to English/Hindi).
- **Pilot for 14–18-year-olds:** qualitative pilot conducted to test delivery formats for older learners (safety, relationships, online safety) — second pilot round this year (see M&E below).
- **Digital Training Course on PSE for Trainers (new, in development):** audio-video recordings, animations, and illustrations produced; facilitator surveys conducted; **8 topics/proofs-of-concept** completed in English and Hindi (near-final version).
- **AI chatbot (new, in development):** built in collaboration with the **Centre for Digital Public Infrastructure (CDPI)** — offers real-time introductory guidance to website visitors on personal safety and can redirect users to a human counsellor.

### Platform & Partner Integrations
- Content (age bands 4–7, 8–10, 11–13) integrated into: **AAS Vidyalaya, EdIndia's Teachable, Adhyayan Foundation's Abhyaas**; new collaborations with **Avanti Fellows** and **HubbleHox Technologies Pvt. Ltd.**
- Educational videos broadcast via **PM eVidya** platform in **Maharashtra and Chhattisgarh**.
- **International reach:** **11,000+ users across ~93 countries**; top countries: **USA, Philippines, Canada, China, UK**.
  - Ongoing collaboration with **Asociación Pop No'j** (Guatemala), which uses Arpan's 8–10-yr Personal Safety Course for children in remote areas.

### FY2024–25 Digital Learning Numbers
- **4,357 completions** via Arpan eLearn + key partners (DIKSHA, TFI-Firki, TCSion, Periwinkle, Inner Wheel, Pop Noj, training partners).
- **2,178 individuals** accessed Arpan's e-books on CSA/personal safety.

### Research/M&E: 14–18-yr Digital Course, Second Pilot
- Evaluation with **324 students** across 2 schools + 2 junior colleges (quizzes/tests + focus groups/interviews).
- **Findings:** Strong recall of core messages (recognising unsafe situations, saying "No," "Get Away," seeking help — "Get Away" concept especially well understood); more students recognised abuse "is never the child's fault"; preference for self-paced, mobile-friendly, in-school learning.
- **Challenges:** long modules, complex language, technical issues, discomfort with certain video scenes (notably Grades 9–10).
- **Recommendations:** simplify language, refine assessments, improve navigation, shorten modules, add interactivity; consider animation instead of live-action for sensitive scenes.

## 7. Mental Health Services — FY2024–25 Report Detail

### New Therapeutic Model at a Child Care Institution (CCI)
- For **Children in Conflict with Law (CICL)** under **POCSO (2012, amended 2019)**.
- Piloted a new model to address earlier challenges (short stays, unplanned exits, inaccessibility post-release causing therapy discontinuation): combines **individual therapy** (deep/personalised) with **group sessions** (peer learning/accountability).
  - Group sessions cover: boundaries, empathy, legal awareness, developmental changes, rights & responsibilities.
  - Individual sessions cover: accountability, emotional regulation, managing sexual urges — using **regulation checklists** as practical tools.

### Restorative Justice (RJ) Work — Deepened in FY2024–25
- RJ = facilitated dialogue/activities between children who caused harm and those affected, to repair harm and restore relationships; also involves parents/teachers.
- **27 children** engaged through RJ practices this year; for the first time, RJ was applied **within a CCI** (previously only in schools via PSE).
- Extended to address **sexual bullying** and sexual misbehaviour among children; included circle-based sessions with teachers/parents to spot early signs and favour restorative (not punitive) responses.

### FY2024–25 Mental Health Services Numbers
- **103 children** received therapy (751 individual sessions) for CSA impact; 95 helpful adults psychoeducated in parallel.
  - 77 of the 103 completed their therapeutic journey (631 sessions); 65 helpful adults psychoeducated (123 sessions + 10 joint sessions).
- **69 adults** with lived experience of CSA received therapy (752 individual sessions); 44 completed therapy. 13 of their support-system members psychoeducated (18 individual + 4 joint sessions).
- **258 boys in conflict with the law** (residing in the CCI) received therapeutic support via **1,213 individual sessions** — of these, **115 cases were sexual misbehaviour** and **143 cases involved consensual sexual relationships** (i.e., POCSO cases arising from consensual adolescent relationships).

### Research/M&E: Therapeutic Outcome Scale & CICL Study
- Piloted a **standardised therapeutic outcome scale** (replacing an informally developed one) to track a child's healing journey/behavioural-emotional progress in a simple, efficient, interpretable way.
- **Qualitative study with the Juvenile Justice Board (JJB), Mumbai:** in-depth interviews with **21 children** (CICL, sexual misbehaviour/consensual-relationship cases) and their counsellors.
  - **Findings:** children valued counsellors' non-judgmental approach and humour; improved emotional regulation/coping; better understanding of boundaries; greater responsibility/impulse management; mixed family engagement (some families noticed and appreciated change, others remained unaware); many children struggled to envision a hopeful future; counsellors found fulfilment but struggled with uncertain institutional stay durations and post-release inaccessibility.
  - **Recommendations:** strengthen goal-setting for a hopeful future outlook; increase family involvement; ensure continuity of counselling by optimising timing/duration.

### Story of Change (Mental Health / CCI)
- A 15-year-old boy in a CCI, charged under POCSO for sexual misbehaviour with a younger child (initially claiming false accusation), gradually engaged through rapport-building, then group sessions on "boundaries" (metaphor: "your body is like a house, you decide who enters"), consent exploration ("if someone freezes and says nothing, is that a yes?" → "No, that means they're not okay"), a "Consequence Chain" mapping activity, and emotional-regulation tools (5-4-3-2-1 grounding technique; five-finger breathing). Over 8 individual + 2 group sessions, he moved from resistance to reflection and began helping peers speak up.

## 12. Resource Materials for Children, Adolescents, Parents & Teachers (from official Resource pages)

*(To request any of these materials: communications@arpan.org.in)*

### For Children & Adolescents
- **Personal Safety Course (8–10 yrs):** Digital course on arpanelearn.com; teaches the **Personal Safety Rule** — "It is never alright for someone to touch, look at, and talk about their Private Body Parts except to keep them clean and healthy." Comprises **12 original videos** + a dozen+ quizzes. Main characters: **ChiSa** (an exercise-loving dog host/guide), **Neha** and **Aamir** (two 9-year-olds who ask relatable questions). Link: https://arpanelearn.com/coursedetails.php?id=162
- **Personal Safety Course (11–13 yrs):** Opens with a story of "Rohan & Ayesha" (Class 7 & 8 students) navigating an unsafe situation online/offline. English: id=18; Hindi: id=32 (on arpanelearn.com) — now also in Punjabi, Gujarati, Bengali (per FY24-25 AR).
- **"FAQ for Adolescents" (eBook):** Answers embarrassing/hard-to-ask questions about adolescent development; helps adolescents find safe solutions to unsafe situations; available in English and Hindi.
- **"My Safety Book"** (picture book, ages 5+): Teaches children their body belongs to them; encourages refusing unsafe touch and seeking help; available in English, Hindi, and Marathi.
- **"My Personal Safety Workbook"** (ages 8–10): Builds self-esteem/self-confidence as the foundation for assertiveness, decision-making, critical thinking. Available in Coloured and Black & White versions, each in English, Hindi, Marathi.
- **PSE Posters for Children:** "Touching Rules to keep me safe" and "Parenting Tips to keep Children Safe" — for schools/homes/institutions, aiding message recall.

### For Parents & Teachers
- **"Facilitator's Course"** (5-minute course): Prepares any parent/teacher/adult to facilitate the children's Personal Safety course. English: id=12; Hindi: id=13 (arpanelearn.com).
- **"Caregivers Handbook":** Parenting-skills booklet on identifying CSA, initiating open communication, and setting boundaries with children.
- **"Understanding Child Sexual Abuse – FAQ's" (booklet):** Compiles common parent/teacher/caregiver questions from Arpan's own awareness/training sessions — covers legal aspects, PSE, handling disclosures, abuse impact, and the healing process.
- **"Personal Safety Education Training Kit":** Full instructional kit/tools for teachers, NGO professionals, counsellors to teach children Personal Safety; available in English, Hindi, Marathi.
- **"Personal Safety Education Abridged Kit":** A shorter, story-based version of the full curriculum — the original comprehensive curriculum required **~6 days for Grades 1–4** and **~4 days for Grades 5–10**; the abridged version condenses this for stakeholders needing a quicker/simpler delivery format. Available in English, Hindi, Marathi.
- **"Poster for Parents":** Visual/text posters for schools, residential societies, homes, and corporate offices to spread Personal Safety messages to parents/caregivers.
