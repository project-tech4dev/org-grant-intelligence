# Azim Premji Foundation — Education Program

Source pages: azimpremjifoundation.org/what-we-do/education, /what-we-do/education/azim-premji-schools, /who-we-are/how-we-are-organised/field-institutes, /who-we-are/history-and-timeline

## Mission
Education has been the Foundation's core work since inception. The stated goal is to **improve the quality of and equity in India's government (public) school system** — not to build a parallel private-school network, but to strengthen the existing public system, with focus on the most disadvantaged areas of the country.

> "School education is perhaps the most important organised process for developing the individual and society. Education must develop knowledge, capacities and values in individuals who then form the society."

## Evolution of Approach
1. **2001–2011 (Programmatic phase):** Direct interventions in government schools — teacher training programmes, initiatives to reduce dropout rates and make schools safer/more inclusive.
2. **Post-2011 (Institutional phase):** Shift to permanent **Field Institutes**, embedding sustained local engagement rather than short-term projects. Teams work closely and consistently with teachers, head teachers, education-department functionaries, and other stakeholders who directly influence classroom teaching and learning.

## Key Components

### 1. Field Institutes (Education)
- Present in **59–61 districts across 6–7 states** and 1 Union Territory (Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Telangana, Uttarakhand, Puducherry).
- Staffed by 10–60 members per institute; 1,700–2,000+ field staff living in 300+ towns.
- Run **296 "Teacher Learning Centres" (TLCs)** — workshops, Voluntary Teacher Forums, melas and sharing sessions to build teachers' subject knowledge, pedagogy and perspective.
- Beyond direct-presence states, the Foundation partners with **~17 additional states'** DIETs (District Institutes of Education and Training), SCERTs (State Councils of Education Research and Training) and Boards of Examination — extending policy/curriculum influence to school systems the Foundation doesn't directly operate in.
- Reach cited as touching **350,000+ schools** nationally through this combination of direct and collaborative/policy work.
- Direct work with ~2.8 lakh teachers and ~63 lakh students (per Foundation's FY2025-26 report).

### 2. Working With Teachers
- Central focus: building teacher capacity in **content knowledge, pedagogy, and perspective**.
- Runs an **Early Childhood Education (ECE)** initiative (started 2012 in Sangareddy, Telangana) to train Anganwadi workers under the government's ICDS scheme, for children aged 3–6.

### 3. Azim Premji Schools
- **9–11 "demonstration and training" schools** (with plans to expand to 66) across states where the Foundation works.
- Serve as models to "practise what we advocate" — testing pedagogical approaches, inclusive practices, constitutional-values-based education — while training government schoolteachers.
- Provide supplementary breakfast/lunch and regular health check-ups for children; organise events to raise parental awareness of children's diet/nutrition.
- Treat parents as equal partners in children's educational journeys via home visits and community meetings; emphasise strong teacher–student and school–community relationships.

### 4. Policy Engagement
- Significant contribution to the **National Education Policy (NEP) 2020** and the **National Curriculum Framework (NCF) 2023**.
- Works with state and central governments on curriculum development, textbook design, examination reform, and teacher-education policy.
- Conducts field research on real classroom issues, e.g., a widely cited **2021 report on "Loss of Learning during the Pandemic"** (a key reference alongside ASER/UNICEF data on COVID-19 learning loss in India).

### 5. Azim Premji Scholarship
- Supports **female students from disadvantaged backgrounds** to pursue college education through financial aid.
- Provides **₹30,000/year** (up to ₹1.5 lakh over the course of a degree).
- Merit-, caste-, income-, and religion-blind eligibility; only first-year students eligible; free application, applied online.
- Piloted 2024–25 across 100+ districts in Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh and Jharkhand; Cohort 2026–27 applications are open.
- Contact/address for the scholarship: Azim Premji Foundation, #134 Doddakannelli, Next to Wipro Corporate Office, Sarjapur Road, Bengaluru 560035.

### 6. Connection to Azim Premji University
- The University (Bengaluru & Bhopal campuses) offers a **Master's in Education**, **Master's in Early Childhood Care & Education (ECCE)**, diplomas (Inclusive Education, Teaching Children with Learning Disabilities, ECE, Educational Assessment), and a **Bachelor's in Science and Education / Integrated Teacher Education Programme (ITEP)**.
- Runs continuing-education/capacity-development programmes reaching **over 1 lakh (100,000) practitioners annually** (teachers, government officials, civil-society members).
- Publishes free teaching-learning materials: subject magazines, development case studies, online textbooks, and translations of higher-education material (see Anuvada Sampada, in the university file).
- Over **5,300 students across 13 cohorts** have graduated from the University's masters-level programmes; ~90% of graduates work in the social sector, many in grassroots field locations.

### 7. Adjacent Nutrition Initiative
- A large-scale mid-day-meal nutrition programme (egg/alternative provision for children in government schools and Anganwadis) — a ~₹1,500 crore commitment over 3 years, first rolled out in Karnataka covering 55+ lakh children.

## Notable Facts
- By ~2010 (before the institutional shift), the Foundation's programmatic work had already engaged an estimated **~2.5 million children across ~20,000 schools in 13 states**.
- The Foundation's education contribution has been widely covered internationally (e.g., BBC coverage of the University's founding endowment), aiming at the time for ~2,000 education graduates per year.

## Character of the Program
Distinctive for its **long-term, government-partnership model** rather than parallel private schooling — sustained, localised engagement via Field Institutes combined with high-level policy influence via the University and government collaboration.
