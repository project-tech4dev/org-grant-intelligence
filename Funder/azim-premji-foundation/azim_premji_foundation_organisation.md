## 1. Origins

- Grew out of discussions (1999–2000) between **Azim Premji** (Chairman, Wipro) and **Dileep Ranjekar**, who became the Foundation's founding CEO. These conversations produced a white paper that laid the groundwork for the Foundation's mission.
- Of several candidate focus areas (education, nutrition, healthcare, governance), **primary/elementary education** was chosen as the founding focus — seen as the most powerful lever to address India's social and economic inequities.
- Premji has cited two personal influences: his mother Gulbanoo Premji's decades running a children's charitable orthopedic hospital in Bombay, and Gandhi's idea of "trusteeship of wealth."

## 2. Formal Founding & Endowment

- **Formally established 9 March 2001**, registered under Section 25 of the Indian Companies Act, 1956 (a not-for-profit company), in Bangalore.
- Azim Premji has since contributed a substantial portion of his wealth to build the Foundation's philanthropic endowment.
- Endowment valued at approximately **₹2,40,000 crore (≈ USD 29 billion) as of January 2023**; more recent disclosures put it near **₹3,00,000 crore (~USD 36 billion) as of 30 June 2026**.
- **66% of the economic ownership of Wipro Ltd.** is held as part of this endowment (via the Azim Premji Trust / Azim Premji Philanthropic Initiatives Pvt. Ltd., which hold economic/dividend rights without corresponding voting control).
  (Source: azimpremjifoundation.org homepage; azimpremjiuniversity.edu.in/azim-premji-foundation)

## 3. Timeline of Key Milestones

(Compiled from the Foundation's "History and Timeline" page: azimpremjifoundation.org/who-we-are/history-and-timeline)

- **2000–2001** — Foundation set up; initial focus on government/primary school education.
- **2001–2011** — "Programmatic phase": direct interventions in government schools across many states (by ~2010, engaged an estimated ~2.5 million children across ~20,000 schools in 13 states).
- **2010** — Azim Premji University founded in Bengaluru, in response to a shortage of trained professionals for India's social sector.
- **2010–2019** — Landmark Wipro share transfers fund the endowment: 8.7% (2010, ~$2B), 12% (2013), additional 18% (2015), additional 34% (2019) — making Premji one of the world's most generous philanthropists (~$21B in commitments by 2019).
- **~2011–2012** — Shift to an **institutional approach**: permanent **Field Institutes / District Institutes (DIs)** established to work with the school system on the ground (replacing short-term "project mode").
- **Azim Premji Schools** launched — demonstration/training schools.
- **Grants programme** launched — financial support to partner NGOs/CSOs.
- **2020–2021** — **COVID-19 Response**: large-scale relief work; the Foundation, Wipro and Wipro Enterprises committed ₹1,125 crore for COVID relief and later ₹1,000 crore for vaccination support. This crisis exposed severe urban vulnerability and catalysed the Foundation's move into new domains.
- **2022–2023** — **Expansion into Health and Livelihoods**, leveraging the existing Field Institute network (starting in Bengaluru slums, then Chhattisgarh/Jharkhand).
- **2023** — **Azim Premji University, Bhopal** campus opens (50-acre campus); partnership with the **Karnataka government** (Health); **launch of the Azim Premji Scholarship**; partnership with **CMC Vellore** (Health).
- **2024–2026** — Ranchi (Jharkhand) campus construction begins (150-acre site; operational from 2026); first Foundation hospital targeted for 2027; further campus planned in the Northeast.

## 4. Legal / Financial Structure

The "Azim Premji Foundation" is an ecosystem of interlinked entities, not a single organisation:

- **Azim Premji Foundation** — core Section 8 not-for-profit company running programmatic/operational work.
- **Azim Premji Foundation for Development** — related non-profit entity.
- **Azim Premji Trust** and **Azim Premji Philanthropic Initiatives Pvt. Ltd. (APPIPL)** — hold economic/dividend rights to donated Wipro shares.
- **Azim Premji Trustee Company Pvt. Ltd.** / **Azim Premji Trust Services Pvt. Ltd.** — manage trusteeship functions.
- An in-house **Endowment Office** — a professional investment team managing the endowment (Wipro shares + diversified public equities, private equity, venture capital, real estate); investment returns fund annual spending (direct spend + grants totalled roughly ₹3,206 crore in FY2025-26 per the Foundation's own reporting).

## 5. How the Foundation Is Organised (self-description)

Three **operating units** (azimpremjifoundation.org/who-we-are/how-we-are-organised):

1. **Field Institutes** — "the strength behind our extensive on-the-ground work in School Education, Health and Livelihoods."
   - Spread across **59–61 districts of 6–7 states and 1 Union Territory** (Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Telangana, Uttarakhand, Puducherry).
   - Over **1,700–2,000 team members**, living in 300+ towns within these districts.
   - Run **296 "Teacher Learning Centres" (TLCs)**.
   - Run **9–11 "Azim Premji Schools"** (demonstration/training schools; expanding toward 66).
   - Also work with the school systems of **~17 other states** (via DIETs, SCERTs, Boards of Examination) without a full field presence there.
   - Run ~1,784 creches serving 30,000+ children (Health/nutrition work embedded in Field Institutes).
2. **Grants** — financial support for not-for-profits and civil society organisations that work across various areas of development and human well-being (see `azim_premji_foundation_other_areas.md`).
3. **University** — quality higher education for knowledge and capacity building for the social sector (see `azim_premji_foundation_university.md`).

Cutting across these are **expertise verticals/groups**: Education and Health are full "verticals"; Livelihoods is a vertical "in formation"; Disability, Gender, Art & Culture are smaller cross-cutting "groups." **Enabling functions** (Communications, Finance, IT, Infrastructure/Engineering, People/HR) plus the Endowment Office support the whole organisation.

## 6. How We Work

(azimpremjifoundation.org/who-we-are/how-we-work)

- **Directly, on the ground** — via Field Institutes, integrating Education, Health and Livelihoods work at district/block level.
- **Directly, "upstream"** — via the University's curriculum, research and campuses.
- **Through partners** — multi-year grants to NGOs/CSOs (currently **~1,150–1,640** supported) working in some of India's remotest geographies, on themes such as gender justice, constitutional values, disability, and governance.

## 7. Where We Work

(azimpremjifoundation.org/who-we-are/where-we-work)

- Field Institutes present in: **Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Puducherry, Rajasthan, Telangana, Uttarakhand** (states/UT with direct on-ground institutional presence).
- Deep engagement additionally across **7 Northeast states**.
- Selective work in a further **~5 states**.
- Grant-supported partner work spans a much wider set of states, including Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Chandigarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Delhi, Goa, Gujarat, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, and more (per the website's state directory).
- Example state profile — **Jharkhand**: work began 2022; active in Gumla, Ranchi, Simdega, Khunti districts; spans Education, Health, Livelihoods and Other Areas; runs 367 creches enrolling 7,500+ children; Livelihoods work (since 2023) covers 10 Gram Panchayats, 4,900 households, 12,000 people.
- Example state profile — **Chhattisgarh**: work began 2007; active in Baloda Bazar, Balrampur, Bemetara, Dhamtari, Janjgir-Champa, Jashpur, Raigarh, Raipur, Surguja districts; runs 390 creches (6 districts) enrolling 5,500 children; Livelihoods work (since 2022) covers 42 villages, 11,400 households, 38,000+ people; overall ~3,76,510 people served.

## 8. Careers / "Work With Us"

(azimpremjifoundation.org/work-with-us)

- The Foundation actively hires across roles: teachers (e.g., Azim Premji Schools), field "Resource Persons," subject specialists (Livelihoods: entitlements, horticulture, livestock, natural resource management), health workers, researchers, and enabling-function staff.
- Culture described as: "a culture of learning, reading, reflecting, and engaging with ideas in education, development and allied areas," combining fieldwork, research, operations and collaboration.
- Emphasis on personal development, wellness, and an extensive benefits programme.
- Typical Resource Person requirement: postgraduate/graduate degree in a relevant field, ~3 years' social-sector experience, Hindi fluency (for Hindi-belt postings).
