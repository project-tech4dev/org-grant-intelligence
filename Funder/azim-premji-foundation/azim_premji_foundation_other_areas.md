# Azim Premji Foundation — Other Areas & Grants Program

Source pages: azimpremjifoundation.org/what-we-do/other-areas, /who-we-are/how-we-are-organised, /who-we-are/how-we-are-organised (Grants section)

## Where It Fits
The Foundation organises its work through **three operating units**: Field Institutes (direct work in Education, Health, Livelihoods), Grants (financial support to external NGOs/CSOs), and University. The **"Other Areas"** section of the website is the public face of the **Grants** programme — showcasing thematic areas, beyond core Education/Health/Livelihoods, where the Foundation funds partner organisations rather than working directly.

> "Our growing canvas of work covers several other areas... The Foundation works on diverse areas through our partners. We also work to strengthen civil society in India in these areas, through capacity building, enabling networks of advocacy and supporting government initiatives."

## Scale of the Grants Programme
- Supports roughly **1,150–1,640 not-for-profit/civil-society organisations** across India (figure varies by year/source) with multi-year grants, and selectively with corpus grants.
- **Grant size:** a few lakh rupees up to a few tens of crores (roughly ₹0.02 crore to ₹10+ crore).
- **Duration:** 1–10 years, with most grants lasting **3–4 years**.
- Only Indian-registered not-for-profits are eligible (no support for organisations registered outside India).
- Geographic focus includes existing field-presence states plus expansion into **Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Eastern Uttar Pradesh, Northeast India, Odisha, and Telangana**.

## How Organisations Apply for a Grant
1. Complete the **NGO Registration Form** on the Foundation's website with organisational and contact details.
2. If details/email are valid, the NGO receives login credentials to the **Grant Management Portal** within **7 working days**.
3. Applications are reviewed on a **rolling basis** — no fixed deadline for the general grants pipeline (special calls, like the Early-Stage CSO call, have specific windows).
4. The Foundation warns applicants to be alert to **fraudulent communications** requesting money or personal information in its name.

## The "Other Areas" Sub-Programmes (as listed on the website)

### Art and Culture
Uses storytelling to take the Foundation's purpose to the people of India — through radio and podcasts (e.g., *Radio Azim Premji University*), art, music, and film. Articulates the Foundation's vision of a just, equitable, humane and sustainable society in conversational, accessible ways, enhancing public understanding of the Foundation's work, milestones, learnings and aspirations.

### Children
Works on vulnerable children's needs and rights — survival, development, protection, and participation. Grant-making covers children in need of care and protection: street children, child labourers, runaways, children in conflict with the law, and survivors of trafficking/abuse, as well as children of migrant workers. Supports 100+ partner organisations.

### Constitutional Values
Fellowships and youth programmes that promote the values enshrined in the Indian Constitution — supporting 40+ organisations working with marginalised rural youth to build democratic values and social-justice orientation.

### Disability
Addresses the vulnerabilities of persons with physical or intellectual disabilities and mental health issues. Supports 70+ organisations, including a subset (~17) focused specifically on mental health, plus work on avoidable disabilities (e.g., clubfoot, blindness).

### Gender Justice
Focuses on gender-based violence, issues around adolescent girls, and gender and sexual minorities. Supports 170+ organisations working on violence prevention, crisis response, counselling, shelter, and related health services for women, at-risk adolescent girls, and transgender/other gender minorities.

### Governance
Improving last-mile access to welfare services, access to justice for the vulnerable, and strengthening local democracy. Split into two strands:
- **Local Democracy & Welfare Rights** — Panchayati Raj institutions, entitlements, NREGA, grievance redressal.
- **Access to Justice** — legal aid for children, Dalits, Adivasis, and women.
A multi-state programme strengthens local governance and Panchayati Raj institutions; in Tamil Nadu, work supports access to the judicial system for the vulnerable.

### Land and Forest
Issues of small and marginal farmers; landless and water-deficient communities; Adivasis, pastoralists, and "denotified" groups. Split into:
- **Adivasi** strand — forest rights, pastoral rights, healthcare access (50+ organisations supported).
- **Rural Livelihoods** strand — farmers, artisans, and weavers; improved agricultural practices, reduced cultivation costs, market access, farmer collectives, and cash-crop/livestock diversification (50+ organisations supported).

### Strengthening Civil Society
Building institutional capacity and programmatic understanding/delivery for community-based organisations (CBOs) and early-stage civil-society organisations (CSOs) — covering leadership, governance, and basic systems/processes. Includes a dedicated **"Early-Stage CSO"** grants call for organisations registered after March 2019, targeting Northeast India, Jharkhand, Odisha, Uttarakhand, and other regions.

### Urban Initiatives
Issues affecting vulnerable people in urban spaces — the homeless, the aged, and migrant workers, as well as slum dwellers, informal-sector workers, manual scavengers and waste pickers. Supports 150+ organisations, notably active in Bengaluru and Chennai. Related "Stories of Change" content: *"Dignity and Hope for Bengaluru's Homeless."*

### Water
Addresses gaps in drinking water and irrigation in water-scarce areas, for water-deficient communities.

### Other Cross-Cutting / Geography-based Grant-Making
- **Odisha Nutrition** — reducing childhood stunting via health, nutrition, and hygiene interventions (systemic collaboration with the Odisha state government).
- **Geography (place-based) portfolio** — concentrated support in select regions, e.g., North Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Jharkhand.
- **Technology for the Social Sector** and **Strengthening Nutrition in Midday Meals** — emerging/adjacent initiatives mentioned alongside the core themes.
- **National Youth Initiative** — youth-focused programming alongside the Constitutional Values theme.

## Notable Systemic Collaborations (cutting across themes)
- **Odisha** — improving nutrition and hygiene, government collaboration.
- **Andhra Pradesh** — improving the livelihoods of small and marginal farmers.
- Multi-state programme to strengthen **local governance and Panchayati Raj institutions**.
- **Tamil Nadu** — centres set up in district hospitals to rescue, treat, and rehabilitate mentally ill homeless individuals.
- Support for **access to the judicial system** for the vulnerable.

## Publications Connected to Other Areas
The Foundation's Publications section (a digital archive for articles and titles from the Foundation and affiliates) includes:
- **Anuvada Sampada** — a collection of higher-educational resources translated into Indian languages.
- **Research Reports** — a curation of high-impact academic records on development, education, public policy, governance, sustainability and equity.
- **The Health Worker** — frontline stories and perspectives from across India.

## Related but Distinct Funding Line
Separate from the Grants/Other Areas portfolio, **Azim Premji University** also runs its own **Research Grants** (e.g., a Health Research Funding Programme, ₹5–40 lakh per study, 12–24 months) to generate empirical research informing policy/practice — administered by the University rather than the Foundation's Grants unit, though both fall under the broader Azim Premji Foundation umbrella.
