## 1. Goonj Centers of Circularity (how the model works)
Site: https://goonj.org/knowing-goonj/

All material collected from cities passes through **Goonj Centers of Circularity** — sorting, segregating, repairing, and packing before dispatch to remote villages. Goonj processes material rigorously because "the dignity of the receiver and giving with dignity is at the centre of all we do" — when people spend days building a bridge, digging a well, or building a community hall, they deserve a **reward, not charity**.

## 2. Cloth for Work (CFW) / Dignity for Work
Site: https://goonj.org/our-initiatives/ ; https://goonj.org/dignity-for-work/

Goonj's flagship model: instead of cash, **old material becomes the currency for rural infrastructure work**. Communities identify and undertake infrastructure improvements — building bamboo bridges, digging/cleaning wells and ponds, bunding land, building small irrigation canals and drainage systems, constructing village schools, and repairing roads — and are compensated with material resources (clothing, utensils, furniture, foodgrains). Recently renamed **"Dignity For Work"** to emphasise that villagers identify their own issues and are rewarded with necessities (food, clothes, furniture, utensils, toys, foodgrains) for solving them.

*Example (2025 field story):* In Nangda Pada, Banswara (Rajasthan), 57 villagers cleaned their only well under this initiative, restoring safe drinking water access.

## 3. Cloth Based Employment (CBE)
Source: Annual Report 2022-23

A value chain built on circularity that promotes transparency/traceability for all surplus textiles Goonj receives: collection of pre- and post-consumer textile waste (diverted from urban landfills) → employment generation to sort/process the waste → bartering the bulk of recycled/upcycled material for community labour on local infrastructure/environmental work (cleaning/reviving water bodies, digging micro-ponds, planting saplings) → selling finished recycled products under the **Green By Goonj** brand. In FY2022-23, CBE facilitated **400,000+ person-days of employment** across the value chain.

## 4. Not Just a Piece of Cloth (NJPC) / MyPad
Site: https://goonj.org/our-initiatives/

Launched in 2004 after Goonj learned of a woman who died of tetanus from a rusted hook in a menstrual cloth, this initiative addresses the "non-issue" of **menstrual health**: in the absence of clean cloth, millions of women in villages/city slums use rags, sand, or ash during menstruation. Goonj created an affordable, easy-to-use cloth sanitary pad from recycled/washed/disinfected cloth (initially priced at ₹500 for a pack of five). The product — now branded **"MyPad"** — is 100% biodegradable and reusable or disposable. By 2014, **4 million pads** had been distributed at a nominal ₹2 each. As of the Impact dashboard (Apr 2014–Mar 2025), **8 million units of Goonj MyPad** have been distributed cumulatively. This project won the **World Bank's Global Development Marketplace Award** (May 2007) and Goonj's third **Changemakers' Innovation Award** (June 2009). In 2025, NJPC won the **Don Norman Design Award – Humanity-Centred Design (HCD+) Organization Laureate**.

## 5. School to School (S2S)
Site: https://goonj.org/our-initiatives/

An award-winning initiative (Changemakers Innovation Award, 2004) addressing educational needs of remote, resource-starved village/slum schools by channelling under-utilised material from affluent city schools — not as charity, but as a tool for behaviour change in both givers and receivers. Urban children contribute old school supplies (bags, pencils, uniforms, etc.) to build **"School Kits"**; Goonj also creates activity centres in village schools. Many children in rural/slum schools drop out for want of a notebook, pencil, water bottle, or ability to pay recurring stationery costs. Partnered historically with the **SNR and Sons Charitable Trust**. Per the FY2022-23 Annual Report: S2S now encompasses **1,900+ school/educational set-ups**, with 150,000+ School Kits distributed.

## 6. Rahat (Disaster Relief & Rehabilitation)
Site: https://goonj.org/our-initiatives/ ; https://goonj.org/climate-emergencies/

For nearly two decades, Goonj has run **Rahat**, its disaster relief and rehabilitation initiative, responding to earthquakes, tsunamis, cyclones, and floods across India. Rahat has evolved into "an active, reliable and time-tested network of stakeholders in both rural and urban India" for need-based disaster relief. Goonj also runs public education on "sensitivities of giving" (mindful giving) and highlights under-recognised, preventable seasonal disasters like winter cold (clothing-shortage-related deaths). Rahat received the **iF Social Impact Prize** (Dec 2017).

## 7. SARRD — Societal Alliance for Resilience and Response to Disasters (formerly GARRD)
Site: https://goonj.org/sarrd/

SARRD (previously the **Goonj Alliance for Rapid Response to Disasters, GARRD**) is a cross-sectoral alliance initiated by Goonj — a first-of-its-kind global model uniting diverse actors with pre-committed resources to transform the disaster-response ecosystem. Its goals: proactive resource commitment (vs. reactive aid); recognising disaster-affected communities as active stakeholders, not passive recipients; inclusive, participatory governance; and embedding resilience/environmental stewardship into long-term institutional agendas rather than one-off relief.

**SARRD's 3-stage framework:**
1. **Laying the Groundwork** — mapping local realities/risks/capacities, identifying pre-commitments (material, skills, time), building trust before crises hit.
2. **Strengthening Systems** — coordinating decentralised preparedness, linking urban surplus with rural needs pre-disaster, designing rapid-activation tools.
3. **Sustaining Everyday Participation** — building a culture of readiness (not just response), amplifying recovery stories, turning seasonal challenges into year-round action plans.

**SARRD ecosystem participants:** communities at the centre; CSR/philanthropic partners; knowledge partners & media; grassroots organisations (CSOs/SHGs/FPOs); institutions & local governments; material contributors & volunteers.

In FY2022-23, Goonj/GARRD-related disaster response provided essential relief materials to **over 316,000 individuals** affected by disasters.

## 8. Green By Goonj
Site: https://goonj.org/our-initiatives/

A brand built around reusing/up-cycling even the last shreds of received material — **100+ product types** (purses, bags, file folders, mats, etc.) made from torn jeans, obsolete audio tapes, one-side-used paper, etc., crafted by women from nearby slums. Purchases fund Goonj's rural work regardless of external funding fluctuations, while also creating livelihoods for the women who make the products.

## 9. Goonj Fellowships
Site: https://goonj.org/our-initiatives/ ; https://goonj.org/goonj-fellowship/

- **Urban Fellowship** — first cohort launched 2018
- **Grassroots Fellowship** — launched 2019
- **Sanjha Fellowship** and **Next Generation Fellowship** — both launched 2020
Each is a one-year fellowship designed to fill specific gaps Goonj identified in the development-sector ecosystem. (Goonj's rural partnership network built through these fellowships, "Sanjha," was recognised by the World Economic Forum among the Top 50 "Covid Last Mile Responders" in 2020.)

## 10. Impact Areas (per goonj.org/impact)
- **Water** — reviving ponds, lakes, check dams; rainwater harvesting; community-led, locally-resourced water solutions in water-stressed rural areas.
- **Disaster Relief & Rehabilitation (Rahat)** — see §6 above.
- **Access & Infrastructure** — Cloth for Work-driven repair/rebuild of bandstands, community halls, roads, bridges, learning centres, libraries, drainage, irrigation canals.
- **Education** — School to School (see §5).
- **Menstrual Health** — Not Just a Piece of Cloth / MyPad (see §4).
- **Livelihood** — Green By Goonj and related processes directly employ large numbers of people (mostly women) in production and logistics.
- **Sanitation** — toilets/bathrooms work with women's involvement in decision-making, improving health/nutrition/dignity.
- **Environment** — conservation, revival, and prevention of natural-resource degradation via repurposing/reuse/recycling (circular economy).
