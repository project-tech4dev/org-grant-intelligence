## 1. About Rajiv Gandhi
Source: https://rgfindia.org/about-rajiv-gandhi-rgf/

Shri Rajiv Gandhi was born on August 20, 1944, in Bombay (now Mumbai). He spent much of his childhood with Jawaharlal Nehru at Teen Murti House, was educated at The Doon School, then studied at Trinity College, Cambridge and Imperial College London (mechanical engineering). He obtained a commercial pilot's licence and joined Indian Airlines before entering politics.

He became India's youngest Prime Minister on October 31, 1984, following the assassination of his mother, Indira Gandhi. Key initiatives during his tenure:
- **Education:** Established IGNOU and Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalayas; introduced the New Education Policy (1986).
- **Environment:** Steered the Environment (Protection) Bill, 1986 (Presidential assent 23 May 1986); revised National Forest Policy (1988); India's first Water Policy adopted (1987, with Gandhi as Chairman of the National Water Resources Council).
- **Economic liberalisation:** New industrial policy (2 June 1988) delicensed several industries, laying groundwork for 1991 reforms.
- **Decentralisation:** Championed Panchayati Raj; introduced the Constitution (64th Amendment) Bill for Panchayats (15 May 1989).
- **Peace accords:** Punjab Accord (25 Jul 1985), Assam Accord (15 Aug 1985), Mizoram Peace Accord (30 Jun 1986; Mizoram became a state 20 Feb 1987), Indo–Sri Lanka Agreement (29 Jul 1987).
- **International relations:** Nuclear disarmament advocacy — Delhi Declaration with Mikhail Gorbachev (27 Nov 1986); expanded US and China relations.

He was assassinated in 1991; the Foundation was created that year in his memory.

## 2. About the Foundation
Source: https://rgfindia.org/about-the-foundation-rgf/ ; https://rgfindia.org/our-story-rajiv-gandhi-foundation/

The **Rajiv Gandhi Foundation (RGF)** was established on **21 June 1991** by Sonia Gandhi to realize Rajiv Gandhi's vision of a modern, secular, progressive India that blends development with cultural tradition and empowers all citizens, especially the underprivileged.

- **1991–2009:** Worked across health, literacy, science & technology, women's/children's development, disability support, Panchayati Raj, natural resource management, and libraries.
- **2010 onward:** Refocused primarily on **education**, while continuing flagship programmes: INTERACT (conflict-affected children), Rajiv Gandhi Access to Opportunities (mobility aid for disabled youth), Rajiv Gandhi Cambridge Scholarship, Natural Resource Management/Gram Gaurav, and Wonderoom (children's library).
- Legal status: Charitable institution with 80G tax-exemption; donations are 50% tax-deductible under the Income Tax Act.

### Selected History Timeline ("Our Story")
- **1991:** RGF founded; office set up at Jawahar Bhawan, New Delhi. RGICS (think tank) founded August 1991. Seminars & aid distributed to disabled children in 4 metros. Literacy cell formed. Relief for Uttarkashi earthquake victims. Lifeline Express ("hospital on a train") concept launched with Impact India Foundation.
- **1992–93:** Rajiv Gandhi Pustakmala neo-literate book series launched. Prototype earthquake-proof housing developed (with University of Roorkee). First Rajiv Gandhi Memorial Lecture (23 May 1992, speaker: Robert McNamara). Riot relief (Bombay, Calcutta, Bhopal, Hyderabad, Munger, Kanpur). Seed funding (Rs 25 lakh) for what became INTERACT. Rajiv Gandhi (UK) Foundation founded in London (Feb 1993).
- **1993–94:** INTERACT formally promoted, reaching 150+ children across Assam, Punjab, Andhra Pradesh, J&K; "Sponsor a Child" scheme launched. 43 rural libraries set up. Health projects: Lifeline Express, HRIDAY (with AIIMS), TB mobile van, AIDS awareness.
- **1994–95:** Panchayati Raj task force work; "Charter for the Panchayats" released on Rajiv Gandhi's 50th birth anniversary.
- **1995–96:** Programmes for women/children (INTERACT, Project REACH, Bhopal gas tragedy aid); health (mobile TB clinic, AIDS crusade, Lifeline Express); disability welfare (tri-wheelers, National Centre for Employment of Disabled); S&T for development (barefoot livestock technicians, Ham Radio club, Biomass Energy Project); literacy (mobile libraries, Girl Child education initiative in J&K).
- **1996–97:** AIDS-awareness workshops; "Health for All" community healthcare (32 villages, Amethi); 100+ cycle rickshaws provided to replace hand-pulled rickshaws in Mussoorie, with a linked TB health-coverage scheme.
- **2001 onward (per Wikipedia):** Natural Resource Management work begins in Jaipur, Pali, Karauli districts (Rajasthan); scaled up via **Gram Gaurav** (est. 19 Dec 2011), working in 74 villages of Karauli/Dholpur.
- **2007–08:** Vidyagyan scholarship programme (with the Sri Sivasubramaniya Nadar Educational and Charitable Trust) for poor Muslim/lower-caste girls, classes 6–10, in select UP districts (~1,500 supported).
- **2010:** Decentralization of Education Governance initiative launched; education becomes the core strategic focus.
- **2013:** Library partnership with the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation (Barabanki & Rai Bareilly, UP); RGF has cumulatively set up 1,648 libraries across 22 states since 1993.
- **2019:** Sadbhavana Tree Plantation Drive launched; Rajiv Sadbhavana Media Lab established.
- **2021 (20 Aug, Rajiv Gandhi's 77th birth anniversary):** RISE & ACT launched (social empowerment/active citizenship training).
- **2021–22:** RASA (Rajiv Ajeevika Samvardhan Abhiyaan) micro-entrepreneurship initiative developed.
- **2022 (Oct/Nov):** MHA cancels RGF's FCRA (foreign contribution) licence (see Controversies, §6).
- **2024–25:** INTERACT supports 270 children across 13 states.

*(Full, continuously updated timeline: https://rgfindia.org/our-story-rajiv-gandhi-foundation/)*

## 3. Our Chairperson
Source: https://rgfindia.org/our-chairperson-rajiv-gandhi-foundation/

**Smt. Sonia Gandhi** (b. 9 December 1946) is Chairperson of the Rajiv Gandhi Foundation. Educated in English, French, and Russian at a foreign-language school, she met Rajiv Gandhi at Cambridge and married him in 1968. After his assassination, she founded RGF on 21 June 1991 and has led it for over 30 years. She has authored *Rajiv* and *Rajiv's World*, and edited *Freedom's Daughter* and *Two Alone, Two Together* (letters between Nehru and Indira Gandhi). Her personal interests include empowerment of the disadvantaged (especially women), children's welfare, handlooms/handicrafts, and the environment.

## 6. Wikipedia Background & Controversies
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi_Foundation

RGICS (est. Aug 1991) has hosted speakers including Robert McNamara, Nelson Mandela, Hillary Clinton, and Edward Said. Per Wikipedia, other notable initiatives include: enrolling 400 Muslim girls in Hyderabad's Yakutpura slum (with local agency Mahita); the Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Uran Akademi scholarship for women pilot trainees; a Teachers Empowerment Programme in Assam/Mizoram/Meghalaya/Manipur/Nagaland; an HIV/AIDS awareness campaign since 1993 (Sonia Gandhi flagged off the Red Ribbon Express on World AIDS Day 2009); ~800 heart surgeries conducted in 2012 with AGS charity; and the Rajiv Gandhi Cambridge Scholarship (with Cambridge Commonwealth Trust) for MPhil/LLM study.

**Controversies:**
- **1991 Ministry of Finance allocation:** FM Manmohan Singh's 1991-92 Budget proposed Rs 100 crore to RGF (Rs 20 crore/yr over 5 years) "as homage" to Rajiv Gandhi. After political backlash, the decision was reversed; RGF itself suggested the government invest the funds directly instead.
- **PMNRF fund diversion allegations:** The Congress-led UPA government was accused of channelling Prime Minister's National Relief Fund money to RGF while Gandhi family members/senior Congress leaders sat on both boards (funds reported received from several ministries, PSU banks, and PSUs in 2005-06 and 2007-08).
- **2022 FCRA licence cancellation:** In October/November 2022, the Union Ministry of Home Affairs (under Amit Shah) cancelled RGF's FCRA registration (and that of the related Rajiv Gandhi Charitable Trust), reportedly after RGF received ~Rs 1.35 crore from the Chinese Embassy — a major political flashpoint between the BJP government and Congress. Sources: [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/fcra-registration-of-rajiv-gandhi-foundation-cancelled-after-it-received-funds-from-chinese-embassy-amit-shah/article66257966.ece), [NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/rajiv-gandhi-foundations-foreign-funding-licence-fcra-cancelled-after-it-received-funds-from-chinese-embassy-amit-shah-3602798), [The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/politics/home-ministry-fcra-license-rajiv-gandhi-foundation-cancelled).
