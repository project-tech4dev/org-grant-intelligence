### Named Projects
- **Games24x7 | Accelerating Child-Led Advocacy: Karnataka** — 20 child-led anti-child-labour rallies (780+ participants); 4,000+ citizens sensitised on "Education for All"; digital classrooms installed in 8 schools (1,500+ students benefited); "Makkala Gram Sabha" in Kodasoge enabled 187 children from 6 villages to participate in local governance; 199 community council meetings held; 2,592 stakeholders involved; 220 active child leaders nurtured.

- **HCL Foundation Supported CSR Programme** (mentioned in table of contents; detail not captured in this pass).
- **The Pad Project – Rajasthan** (mentioned in table of contents — menstrual hygiene awareness; expanded on in the 2023–24 report as the "PAD project"/"Meri Saheli Meri Maa" campaign).

## 2. `KSCF_Annual_Report_2023-24.pdf` (25 pages)

### Structure
Overview of the Year → Our Vision, Mission, Values → Our Impact → Bal Mitra Gram™ (Guiding Principles, Key Highlights, Initiatives, Stories of Change) → Bal Mitra Mandal (Parameters, Key Highlights, Stories of Change).

### Overview of the Year — Highlights
- **1,000+ students** across **5 villages in Jharkhand** gained access to digital classrooms.
- **1,430 children** took part in **Bal Panchayat elections**, forming **130 children's councils** in Madhya Pradesh.
- **705 girl child leaders** from Uttar Pradesh took part in the **"Himmat Yatra"** campaign promoting gender equality/education.
- **"Compassion for Children's Well-Being" rally** (Uttar Pradesh): 165 participants reached **25,000 community members** on child marriage and education.
- Maharashtra BMG™ work connected communities to welfare schemes: **1,355 individuals enrolled**, securing benefits worth **₹67.75 crore**.
- **"School Chalo Abhiyan"** — school-enrolment campaign for marginalised children.
- **PAD Project** — menstrual hygiene awareness; **"Meri Saheli Meri Maa"** campaign reached **2.34 lakh (234,000) women and girls** in rural Rajasthan; amplified by **"MHM Carvaan,"** impacting **4+ lakh (400,000+) people**.
- Explicitly frames its work against **UN Sustainable Development Goal 8.7** (eliminate all forms of child labour by 2025).

### Vision, Mission, Values
- **Vision:** "A world where every child is free, safe, healthy and educated."
- **Mission (5 pillars, matching the main site):** Child Rights protection; Capacity building of stakeholders (governments, corporates, grassroots orgs); Global Voice against child violence (via movements/research); enabling Global/national policy implementation; becoming a Global Platform for best-practice dissemination and bridging trust between stakeholders.
- **Core value emphasised:** "Compassion."

### Bal Mitra Gram™ — Guiding Principles (as restated in this edition)
Describes BMG™ as **launched in 2001 by Nobel Peace Laureate Kailash Satyarthi and Sumedha Kailash**. Five guiding principles:
1. **Village Empowerment** — fostering community ownership and collective action among all local stakeholders.
2. **Integrated and Inclusive Development** — comprehensive development benefiting all, prioritising the most vulnerable.
3. **Participatory Democracy** — Bal Panchayats give children an active role in local governance.
4. **Child-Centred Decision-Making** — community actions/policies guided by children's needs and rights.
5. **Ending Centuries-Old Discriminatory Mindsets** — challenging norms that perpetuate child labour, discrimination, and gender inequality.

## 4–6. Financial Reports (`KSCF_Financial_Report_2022-23.pdf`, `_2023-24.pdf`, `_2024-25.pdf`)

### Nature of the Documents
Audited **Balance Sheet** and **Income & Expenditure Account** (consolidated), prepared under Indian trust/NGO accounting conventions, audited by **Anil S Gupta & Associates, Chartered Accountants** (New Delhi). KSCF's financial statements refer to the organisation as **"the Trust."**

### FY2024-25 Financial Report — Key Figures
- **Consolidated Balance Sheet total (as at 31 March 2025):** **₹26,40,64,040.95** (~₹26.4 crore), versus **₹26,17,32,397.63** (~₹26.2 crore) as at 31 March 2024.
- **Balance Sheet composition (31 March 2025):** Corpus Fund ₹3,05,98,841; Capital Fund ₹2,21,12,557; Programme Balance ₹21,13,52,506; small Current Liabilities.
- **Assets:** Investments in Fixed Deposits ₹15,21,39,305 (as at 31 Mar 2024 figure shown; FY25 figure appears as part of the ₹24.14 crore combined assets line); Cash & Bank Balances; Security Deposits; Tax Deducted at Source; TCS receivables.
- **Income & Expenditure (year ended 31 March 2025):**
  - **Grant Income received:** ₹12,51,74,858.75 (~₹12.5 crore) — up sharply from **₹4,90,12,423.27** (~₹4.9 crore) the prior year (FY2023-24) — indicating a major expansion in grant funding.
  - Other/interest income: ₹1,91,18,571 and ₹49,964.67 (various lines).
  - **Grant Recurring Expenditure:** ~₹14,46,74,527 (~₹14.5 crore, FY25) vs ~₹18,66,61,556 (~₹18.7 crore, FY24) — expenditure patterns vary by year depending on programme cycles.
  - Non-grant/admin expenditure and depreciation are comparatively small line items.
- **Auditor's opinion:** unqualified/standard opinion that the Balance Sheet and Income & Expenditure Account give "a true and fair view," noting an **"excess of expenditure over income" (deficit)** for the accounting year — i.e., KSCF ran a funding deficit in at least one of the audited years, which is common for grant-cycle-dependent NGOs (timing mismatches between grant receipt and programme spend).
- Signed by **Rahul Kumar Shrawat (Trustee)** and the audit partner (Anil Kumar Gupta, Partner, Firm Regn. No. 004061N).
