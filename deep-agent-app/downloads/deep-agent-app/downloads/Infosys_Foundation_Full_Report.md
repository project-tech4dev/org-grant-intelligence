## A.5 Focus Areas / Current Initiatives (India)

The Foundation organizes its work into primary and secondary focus areas:

### Primary Focus Areas

1. **Learning, Livelihoods and Sport**
   - Beneficiaries (FY26): **7,30,638**
   - Flagship program: **Infosys Springboard Livelihood Program** — launched July 2025 with an investment of **₹200+ crore**, aiming to help **half a million (5 lakh) job seekers** gain meaningful employment by **2030**. In FY26: 2,20,000+ enabled with job offers; 4,10,000+ individuals trained.
   - **Infosys Springboard** (parent digital learning platform): free platform offering 20,000+ courses; reached **11.75–13.3 million learners** globally (per various reports); aligned with India's National Education Policy 2020.
   - **Sports initiative "Gear for Gold"**: with GoSports Foundation, supports young athletes toward Olympic success — scholarships, coaching, and infrastructure upgrades across 8 academies.
   - Educational grants historically to institutions such as Chennai Mathematical Institute (₹30 crore) and Indian Institute of Science (₹20 crore).

2. **Healthcare**
   - Beneficiaries (FY26): **13,85,005**
   - Focus: affordable/accessible healthcare via technology — mobile clinics, telemedicine, digital health tools, eye-care in remote regions, maternal/newborn health.
   - Example projects: Advanced mother & neonatal care facility at PGIMER Chandigarh; Mother and Child Block equipment at AIIMS New Delhi; diabetes/obesity remission programs (Pune & Nagpur); grants to Kunigal Hospital (Karnataka) and Puttur Government Hospital (Mangaluru, via Prerna Charitable Trust); ₹48 crore commitment for maternal/child healthcare in rural Karnataka (2025, with Prashanthi Balamandira Trust).
   - Historically: hospital wards & dharmashalas at NIMHANS Bengaluru; 50+ crore donated to expand hospital capacity.

3. **Environmental Sustainability**
   - Beneficiaries (FY26): **47,01,954**
   - Focus: water conservation, lake rejuvenation, green innovation.
   - Achievements: Rejuvenated **~197 acres** of lakes (incl. Doddathogur Lake near Electronic City, Bengaluru, with Malligavad Foundation) capable of holding **3.6 billion liters** of water; restored traditional stepwells/water systems at Rashtrapati Nilayam (with SAHE) and at Osmania University, Hyderabad; captured **13 crore liters** of rainwater; planted ~50,000 trees around rejuvenated lakes. Multi-year water body program (FY24 onward) targets replenishing 10 billion liters over 5 years — 11 lakes restored so far (9 in Karnataka, 1 in Tamil Nadu, 1 in Odisha).
   - Broader Infosys ESG context: Carbon neutral since 2020 (6 consecutive years through FY25); 4.3 billion liters of added water-holding capacity via 11 lake projects (FY24–FY25); ESG Vision 2030 targets climate-positive status.

4. **Women Empowerment**
   - Beneficiaries (FY26): **7,56,622**
   - Focus: skill training, education, healthcare access, dismantling barriers to opportunity; historic emphasis on supporting the Devadasi community (led by Sudha Murty); "Women in Technology" program ran across 13 cities in FY24 (6,406 women trained, 4,186 placed in jobs).

### Secondary Focus Areas
   - Beneficiaries (FY26): **3,46,694**
   - Covers: **Art & Culture** revival, **Disaster Relief**, **Destitute Care**, **Rural Development**, and **Animal Welfare**.
   - Notable program: **Kala Dhwani – "Echoes of India's Art & Culture"** — a festival (2nd edition ongoing) celebrating India's folk and tribal heritage, in partnership with Bharatiya organizations.
   - Disaster relief examples: Flood relief support in Belagavi district (Karnataka); 2024 multi-state flood response across Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Kerala, Karnataka, West Bengal, Odisha, Tamil Nadu (e.g., 2,000 relief kits for 8,000 people via Sri Ramakrishna Sevashrama in Vijayawada/Guntur, AP).
   - Historic: ₹70 lakh contribution to an academy under Bhavan's Tiruchi Kendra for underprivileged artists; 2,262–3,000 flood-relief homes built in North Karnataka; cyclone/flood shelters and a pediatric block at Capital Hospital, Bhubaneswar, Odisha; midday-meal partnership with Akshaya Patra; ~10,000+ toilets built via the "Parishudh" initiative across rural Karnataka districts.

### Cross-cutting Program: Aarohan Social Innovation Awards
   - Recognizes technology-based social innovation across Healthcare, Education, and Women Empowerment (and more recently Environmental Sustainability) categories.
   - Prize: up to **₹50 lakh per winner**; total purse around **₹2 crore**; Jury's Special Awards of ₹10 lakh each also given.
   - 2025 was the **4th edition**; 2023 edition drew ~2,400 submissions.
   - Past winners include: nLite 360 (portable phototherapy device for jaundice), Smart Vision Glasses (AI-assisted device for visually impaired), One Home One Toilet (sanitation model by Shelter Associates).

### Employee Volunteering
   - FY25: **130,000+ volunteer hours**, **34,000+ employees participating**, via the **InfyCares** platform.
   - Example: partnership with Blue Cross of Hyderabad for Animal Birth Control & Anti-Rabies Vaccination (ABC/ARV) program (animal welfare).

**Eligibility & Compliance**

**What the Foundation explicitly states (official FAQ):**
- Supports only **apolitical and non-religious NGOs** committed to an equitable society.
- Does **not** fund religious institutions, political parties, or individuals.
- Does **not** accept public donations — it is funded solely via an annual grant from Infosys Ltd., so it operates purely as a **grant-making/CSR disbursing body**, not a fundraising charity.
- NGOs apply through a **dedicated online "Request a Grant" portal** (linked from every page of infosys.org/infosys-foundation).

**Statutory / regulatory compliance an NGO must hold** (this is a legal requirement under Indian law for *any* company routing Section-135 CSR funds — including Infosys, since Infosys Foundation is Infosys Ltd.'s CSR vehicle and its CSR disclosures are filed under Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013):
- **12A / 12AB registration** — mandatory for income-tax exemption eligibility.
- **80G registration** — typically expected so contributions/grants qualify for tax treatment and for donor/funder due diligence.
- **CSR-1 registration (Form CSR-1 with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs)** — legally mandatory since FY 2020-21 for **any** NGO to receive CSR funds from an Indian company; generates a CSR Registration Number (CRN) without which Infosys cannot report the spend.
- **FCRA registration** — only required if the NGO also receives foreign contributions; not required for Infosys CSR funding itself (Infosys funding is domestic), but often used by funders as a proxy for institutional maturity/track record.
- **Track record & documentation**: 3 years' audited financials/activity reports are a standard due-diligence bar cited across CSR/12A-80G-CSR-1 guidance for corporate funders of Infosys Foundation's scale, though Infosys has not published an itemized checklist publicly.

**How to score this criterion for a given NGO:** Confirm the NGO holds valid 12A, 80G, and (critically) **CSR-1/CRN** registration — the last is a hard legal gate for any CSR-1-bound funder like Infosys Foundation. FCRA is not required unless the NGO also seeks foreign funding.

**Relationship / Warmth (Linkage)**

This criterion cannot be pre-scored for Infosys Foundation in general — it depends entirely on the specific NGO being evaluated. What official sources reveal is **how** the Foundation builds and signals relationships, which tells you what "warmth" looks like in practice:

- **Formal channel**: All new NGO engagement is expected to route through the official **"Request a Grant" online portal** linked from every infosys.org/infosys-foundation page — i.e., cold/unsolicited outreach is structurally routed through one gateway rather than personal introductions.
- **Long-term, multi-year partnership model**: Existing relationships are typically **not one-off grants** — e.g., multi-year lake rejuvenation programs, multi-year Springboard Livelihood Program (2025–2030), and recurring partners named repeatedly across annual reports (e.g., Akshaya Patra, GoSports Foundation, SAHE, Blue Cross Hyderabad, Prashanthi Balamandira Trust, Malligavad Foundation) — repeat-named partners across 2+ annual reports is a strong observable signal of "warmth."
- **Convenings as relationship infrastructure**: The Foundation runs invite-based convenings — the **Infosys Foundation Livelihood Conclave**, the **Gear for Gold Conclave**, and the **Aarohan Social Innovation Awards** — which function as pipelines for new NGO/innovator relationships and would indicate a "recently engaged" NGO if it has participated.
- **Employee volunteering bridge (InfyCares)**: NGOs that already host Infosys employee volunteers (e.g., Blue Cross Hyderabad, reading/coaching programs referenced in the FAQ) often have an informal warm channel into the Foundation before any formal grant.
- **Trustee-level visibility**: Trustees (Salil Parekh, Sumit Virmani, etc.) are quoted by name in press releases tied to specific NGO partnerships — an NGO whose partnership has been quoted/named by a Trustee or the Head of Foundation (Manisha Saboo) in a press release is a strong warmth signal.

**How to score this criterion for a given NGO:** Check whether the NGO (a) has been named in an Infosys Foundation annual report/press release in the last 1–3 years, (b) has hosted Infosys employee volunteers, (c) has participated in an Infosys Foundation conclave/award, or (d) has an active multi-year grant. Any of these = warm/active linkage. No prior mention anywhere = cold, portal-only relationship.

**Type/structure characteristics:**
- Overwhelmingly **grants to registered institutions/NGOs**, not direct cash transfers to individuals.
- Strong preference for **infrastructure and program-based** grants (hospital wings, school buildings, digital learning platforms) over general operating support.
- Increasing share of **multi-year, outcomes-linked commitments** (Springboard Livelihood Program explicitly targets job-placement KPIs) rather than one-time donations.
- Runs its **own competitive award mechanism** (Aarohan) as an alternate entry point distinct from the standard grant portal.

**How to score this criterion for a given NGO:** Best fit = NGO seeking a project/infrastructure-based grant in the tens-of-lakhs-to-crores range, ideally multi-year, with clear outcome metrics; or an NGO/innovator eligible for the Aarohan competitive award track (≤₹50 lakh). Poor fit = NGOs seeking small, ad hoc, unrestricted, or individual-beneficiary funding.
