## 1. Overview

- **Full name:** Department of Social Welfare, Government of Manipur
- **Website:** https://socialwelfare.mn.gov.in
- **Purpose (per official "About Us"):** "One of the most important Departments which deals directly with the citizen of Manipur." Established to implement social welfare programmes and services for the upliftment of disadvantaged segments of the population — specially **Women, Children, disabled persons, old aged and infirmed** persons.
- **Objectives:** "Care, protection, welfare, development and rehabilitation of children, women, disabled persons, aged and infirm, delinquent and neglected juveniles, destitute and orphan children, ex-convicts, ex-criminals and persons released on probation."

## 4. Welfare Schemes — By Target Group

### 4.1 Welfare of Women
- **SWAYAMSIDHA Project** — centrally sponsored scheme under Integrated Women Empowerment Programme; objectives include forming self-reliant women's self-help groups, raising awareness (health, nutrition, education, sanitation, legal rights, economic upliftment), improving access to micro-credit, and involving women in local planning. Implemented in 3 blocks: Imphal East II (Keirao Bitrao), Churachandpur Tribal Development Block, Chandel Tribal Development Block. Nodal officer: Joint Director, Social Welfare.
- **Support to Training and Employment Programme (STEP)** — funded by Dept. of Women & Child Development (GoI); trains poor, asset-less women in traditional sectors (agriculture, animal husbandry, dairying, fisheries, handlooms, handicrafts, Khadi/village industries, sericulture, social forestry, wasteland development).
- **Employment-cum-Income Generating Units (NORAD-assisted)** — trains women in electronics, watch assembly, computer training, garment making, secretarial practice, embroidery, etc.
- **Working Women's Hostels with Day Care Centres** — financial assistance to voluntary orgs/PSUs/state govt./educational institutions for hostel construction.
- **Short Stay Homes for Women (SSH)** — for women with no local support system due to family problems, mental strain, social ostracism, exploitation; provides medical care, psychiatric therapy, education-cum-vocational training, recreation.
- **SWADHAR Scheme** — for women in difficult circumstances (deserted widows, released women prisoners, disaster survivors, trafficked/rescued women, terror-violence victims, mentally disordered women without family support); provides shelter, food, clothing, counselling, and social/economic rehabilitation.
- **Other Women's Homes** — run by the State and Women's Associations, with a Rs. 20,000 grant from Govt. of Manipur.
- **Vocational Trainings** for women (incl. school dropouts) in handloom weaving, typewriting/shorthand, readymade garments, mosquito net weaving, leather work, beauty culture, computers — in collaboration with NGOs.
- **Financial Assistance to Poor & Needy Widow Women** (recent scheme, per notifications).

### 4.2 Child Welfare
- **Running of Children Homes:** 90% grant-in-aid to **8 Destitute Children Homes** run by voluntary organisations; Rs. 270/child/month for care, maintenance, education, food; **320 children** housed across these homes. Locations: Kanglatongbi (Senapati), Manipur Mahila Samiti Deulahland (Imphal East), Salvation Army Mualvaphei (Churachandpur), Liwachanning (Chandel), Terakeithel Sagolband (Imphal), Ningrin Club (Ukhrul), Lei-Ingkhol Chingmeirong Mantripukhri (Imphal), Lower Lamka Women Society (Churachandpur).
- **State Bal Bhavan** — established 1989 at Khuman Lampak, Imphal; affiliated to National Bal Bhavan Society, India, New Delhi; only such institution in the entire North East. Offers dance/music/computer/drawing classes, doll museum, library, summer/winter camps. **8 children have won the National Bal Hero Award.** Bal Kendras also opened in tribal areas (Senapati, Churachandpur); a Bal Bhavan-cum-children's park planned for Moreh (Indo-Myanmar border town).
- **Sishu Greeha (Adoption Scheme):** 90% centrally sponsored "Scheme of Assistance to Homes"; regulates in-country adoption for abandoned/orphaned/destitute infants and children up to age 6. **3 NGOs recognized** by State Govt. and CARA (Central Adoption Resource Agency): Integrated Women & Children Development Centre (Lamphelpat, Imphal), Tear Fund India Committee on Relief & Rehabilitation Service (Churachandpur), Community Development Programme Centre (Thoubal).
- **Financial Assistance to Destitute Children:** Rs. 60/child/month for school-going destitute children aged 6–14 below the poverty line; **288 children** benefited.
- **Child Care Institutions (CCIs) under Child Protection Services (CPS):** An extensive network of Children Homes, Open Shelters, Observation Homes, and Special Homes (incl. PPP-model homes) run by NGOs across Imphal East, Imphal West, Thoubal, Kakching, Bishnupur districts — including specialised homes for transgender children (e.g., AURA Children Home for Transboys, Transpride for Transgirls, run by Mirage Foundation and Nirvana Foundation respectively).

### 4.3 Welfare of the Disabled
- **2 Government Special Schools** (Visual Impaired; Hearing Impaired) with hostel facilities; **5 additional NGO-run Special Schools** (4 for Mentally Retarded, 1 for Hearing Impaired).
- **Diet allowance:** Rs. 500/month per hosteller at the 2 govt. schools.
- **Scholarships:** Rs. 40–120/month for disabled students; Reader allowance + free Braille textbooks for blind students; Rs. 300/year maintenance allowance for Mentally Retarded students.
- **Employment:** 1995 survey identified **24,690 disabled persons** in the state; special employment exchange window at Langol, Imphal; **3% job reservation** in Class III/IV direct recruitment (1% Visual Impaired – 34th roster; 1% Hearing Impaired – 67th roster; 1% Locomotor Disability – 100th roster); 10-year upper age relaxation for PWDs applying to government jobs.
- **Rehabilitation Centre (Gramin Punarvas Yojna / Composite Fitment and Rehabilitation Centre):** at J.N. Hospital, Porompat, Imphal; distributes wheelchairs/tricycles; observes International Day for Disabled Persons (3rd December) with cultural shows/games and cash prizes.
- **Implementation of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (RPwD) Act, 2016** — Manipur-specific status (per official RTI-style disclosure):
  - **60+ institutions** registered under Section 50 (Registration of Institutions for Persons with Disabilities).
  - **7,573+ persons with disabilities** receiving Care-Giver Allowance under Chief Ministergi Shotharabasingi Tengbang Scheme (CMST), since 2017–18.
  - **54 persons** benefited under the new Artificial Limbs Fitting Scheme.
  - New Disability Pension Scheme launched 2022–23 (over and above the Indira Gandhi National Disabled Pension Scheme/IGNDPS).
  - Unemployment Allowance to registered PWDs (since 2006–07).
  - **4 Residential Special Schools** (Takyel, Mongshangei, Heikakpokpi) providing free education/uniform/hostel up to Class X.
  - State Fund for PwDs created (Order dated 13 Sept 2023) with a Governing Body for utilization/management.
  - District Level Committees on Disability constituted in Bishnupur, Churachandpur, Senapati, Pherzawl, Tengnoupal, Tamenglong.
  - Special Court designated (Notification dated 25 August 2020).

### 4.5 Correctional Services
- **Juvenile Justice Scheme** — financed 50:50 by Govt. of India and State Government.

## 6. Grant-in-Aid Programmes

### 6.1 Manipur State Social Welfare Advisory Board
- **Constituted:** 6th September 1954.
- Implements welfare programmes for women and children through Registered Voluntary Organisations, as the state-level agency of the Central Social Welfare Board (New Delhi).
- **Staff strength:** 25 (incl. 4 nominated by the Central Board), plus ~72 staff across 3 Border Area Welfare Extension Projects (Chandel, Singhat, Ukhrul).
- **Funding pattern:** Dept. of Social Welfare covers 50% of State Board maintenance/salaries (Non-Plan budget); Central Social Welfare Board covers the rest. For the 3 Border Area projects, the State covers via Plan Budget and the Centre bears 2/3rds.
- **Schemes run under the Board:**
  - **Creche Programme:** 219 creche units run by 217 registered voluntary organisations; Rs. 18,490/centre; 2 ayahs per unit of 25 children.
  - **Awareness Generation Project:** 35 camps by 33 voluntary organisations; 5-day camps, 25–30 women each, Rs. 10,000/camp.
  - **Condensed Course of Education for Adult Women:** 3-tier (Primary/Middle/Matric) course for socially handicapped women aged 18–35; 16 institutions sanctioned (2000–02) — Rs. 94,500 (Primary/Middle) / Rs. 1,31,100 (Matric level).
  - **Vocational Training Programme:** 6–12 month courses for women aged 18–30 (25–50 capacity) in Weaving, Tailoring, Embroidery, Computer, Handicraft, Jewellery, Cane & Bamboo — 76 institutions funded in 2001–02.
  - **Socio-Economic Programme:** economic rehabilitation for physically handicapped/widowed/destitute/deserted women via production units (handloom, handicraft, dairy, piggery, goat-rearing) — no funds released by Central Board since 1997–98 (as reported historically on site).
  - **Working Women Hostel:** 6 voluntary organisations run hostels with Central Board funding.

### 6.2 Border Area Welfare Extension Projects
- Located in **Ukhrul, Churachandpur, and Chandel** districts, under the Manipur State Welfare Board.
- **15 centres each** for: Balwadi/Pre-Primary Education (25–30 child capacity), Crèche (day care for ailing mothers), Maternity Service, and Craft Training Programme.

### 6.3 Manipur State Women Development Corporation Ltd.
- **Established:** 1995, as a pioneering institute in the state's Women's Development sector for mobilization/development of human resources across eco-social/cultural fields.
- Provides training (with Central Govt. financial assistance) in handloom weaving, typewriting/shorthand, readymade garments, mosquito net weaving, leather, beauty culture, computers — in collaboration with NGOs.
- **Special Weaving Scheme:** 500 below-poverty-line women trained; loans given for weaving yarn:

## 8. Centrally Sponsored Schemes

### 8.1 National Social Assistance Programme (NSAP)
- 100% centrally sponsored; launched in Manipur on 15th August.
- **3 Components:**
  - **National Old Pension Scheme (NOPS):** Rs. 75/month for destitute persons aged 65+; **35,061 total beneficiaries** in Manipur (historical figure per site).
  - **National Family Benefit Scheme (NFBS):** Rs. 10,000 lump sum to surviving household members when the primary breadwinner dies; 599 families benefited (2001–02).
  - **National Maternity Benefit Scheme (NMBS):** Rs. 500 lump sum for below-poverty-line pregnant women (first 2 live births, mother aged 19+); 5,635 women benefited (2001–02) — *this component has since been transferred to the Department of Family Welfare, Govt. of Manipur.*

### 8.3 Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS)
- 100% centrally sponsored; introduced in Manipur on **2nd October 1975**, piloted at Ukhrul T.D. Block.
- Now spans **34 projects** covering 9 C.D. Blocks, 24 T.D. Blocks, and 1 Urban block, with **4,501 Anganwadi Centres**.
- **Aims:** improve nutrition/health of children aged 0–6; support proper psychological/physical/social development; reduce mortality, morbidity, malnutrition, school dropouts; coordinate departments for child development; enhance mothers' capability for child health/nutrition.
- **Service package:** Supplementary nutrition, immunisation, health check-ups, referral services, treatment of minor illness, nutrition/health education, pre-school education (ages 3–6), convergence with water/sanitation services.
- **Adolescent Girls Scheme (renamed Kishori Shakti Yojana/KSY):** targets school-dropout girls aged 11–18; sanctioned in **13 blocks**.
- **UDISHA Training Programme:** 4 Anganwadi Worker Training Centres (run by NGOs and State Govt.) conducting Job/Orientation/Refresher courses.
- **PMGY Nutrition component** also delivered through ICDS projects.
