# Why Proposal Templates Differ Across Funder Types — and What Stays the Same

Having now pulled and read 20+ real templates across 12 funder categories, a clear pattern emerges: every template is shaped by **who the funder is answerable to**, not by arbitrary preference. Below is the full breakdown — what's universal, what varies, and why.

---

## 1. The Root Cause: 4 Different "Accountability Logics"

Every proposal template is really an answer to the question: *"Who does this funder have to justify this money to?"* That single factor explains almost all the variation:

| Accountability Logic | Who the funder answers to | Categories | What this forces into the template |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Regulatory/Statutory** | Government auditors, Companies Act, tax authorities | CSR (Schedule VII), Government Grants | Registration numbers, statutory certificates, legal undertakings, no-blacklisting affidavits |
| **Fiduciary/Institutional** | A Board, major donors, tax-exempt status rules | Foundations, Multilateral/UN, Bilateral Aid, International (USAID) | Theory of Change, indicators, budget narratives, compliance certifications, safeguarding |
| **Relationship/Trust** | Their own mission/community, not a regulator | INGOs, Diaspora Foundations, HNI/Family Office, Domestic Intermediaries | Little to no fixed template — replaced by relationship-building, site visits, due diligence |
| **Market/Investment** | Their own capital providers, expecting return + scale | Impact Investors/Venture Philanthropy | Business plans, unit economics, not "impact narratives" |
| **Consumer/Platform** | End retail donors, platform trust & safety | Crowdfunding Platforms | Due-diligence checklists + a public campaign page, not a written proposal |

This is why a CSR template and a Foundation template can both ask about "impact" but mean completely different things by it — one is checking a compliance box, the other is testing your theory of change.

---

## 2. What's Genuinely Common to ALL 12 Categories (The Universal Skeleton)

Strip away the jargon, and every single template — from BPCL's CSR format to USAID's 245-page guideline to World Vision's partnering framework — asks for the same **7 core things**:

1. **Who you are** (organizational background/history/registration)
2. **What problem you're solving** (need/context/rationale)
3. **What you'll do about it** (objectives + activities/methodology)
4. **Who benefits, and where** (target group + geography)
5. **What it costs** (budget, in some form — even if just a verbal "ask")
6. **How you'll know it worked** (some form of monitoring, however basic or rigorous)
7. **Who signed off** (contact person + authorized signatory)

This is the irreducible core. Every category adds its own layer *on top* of this — but never removes it.

## 3. Where They Actually Diverge — Dimension by Dimension

### A. Eligibility Gatekeeping (the biggest differentiator)
This is where the templates diverge *hardest*, because each funder type checks a different legal box:

| Category | What gates you out | Real example from the docs |
|---|---|---|
| CSR (India) | CSR-1 registration, 80G, 12A, NGO Darpan ID, GeM registration for procurement | ONGC/Coal India/PFC all require CSR-1 + no-blacklisting self-certification |
| Government Grants | FCRA No., PFMS registration, NGO Darpan ID, **Aadhaar of every key functionary** | NEC & WCD Swadhar Greh forms both demand Aadhaar numbers — no other category does this |
| Foundations | Often 501(c)(3)-equivalency (Equivalency Determination) for non-US orgs | Rockefeller's grant package explicitly requires this for international grantees |
| International/US (USAID) | Federal EIN/DUNS + Automated Directives System (ADS) compliance | The blank SF-424 form literally has an EIN/TIN field baked into a federal form — a CSR or Foundation template never asks for this |
| Bilateral (UK/Australia) | Home-country charity regulator status | FCDO requires **Fundraising Regulator** registration; DFAT requires **formal accreditation** |
| INGO/Church | **Child protection/safeguarding clearance** | World Vision treats this as a *non-negotiable prerequisite* — no proposal even proceeds without it |
| Domestic Intermediary | Deep forensic compliance (Form 10BD filing, Section 12A non-default, board tenure) | HCL Foundation's checklist checks *board member continuity* — nobody else does this |
| Diaspora Foundations | FCRA is the single make-or-break gate | Give2Asia's entire guidance page is just about FCRA Registration vs. Prior Permission |

**Why this matters:** an NGO could pass every eligibility gate for a CSR funder and be instantly disqualified from a Government grant for lacking an Aadhaar-linked functionary record, or disqualified from USAID for lacking FCRA — these aren't "extra paperwork," they're structurally different gatekeeping systems.

### B. Thematic Framing — Whose Categories Do You Have to Fit Into?
- **CSR** must map to an exact **Schedule VII clause** of India's Companies Act — a legal citation, not just a theme.
- **Government schemes** are pre-defined and non-negotiable (you fit into MGNREGS or DAY-NRLM as they exist — you don't propose a new theme).
- **Foundations** use *their own* internal strategic framework — Gates Foundation's "Global Health" logframe, MacArthur's 10-year geographic strategy — themes are funder-authored, not government-authored.
- **Multilateral/UN** use global taxonomies that exist independent of any single country — GEF's "Focal Areas," Global Fund's disease-sector indicators.
- **INGOs** frame everything around **child well-being outcomes** specifically, regardless of sector.

So "thematic fit" means something different in each case: legal citation vs. pre-set scheme vs. funder's private strategy vs. global taxonomy vs. mission-specific outcome.

### C. Budget Format — From Nonexistent to Federally Regulated
This is the widest spectrum of all:
- **Domestic Intermediaries (craft networks)**: often **no cash budget at all** — support is market linkage, not money.
- **HNI/Family Office**: an informal verbal "ask," no fixed format.
- **CSR (India)**: free-form, but must be justified against CPWD/state Schedule-of-Rates for capital items, and a **separate bank account** must open post-approval.
- **Foundations**: detailed but flexible narrative budget (Ford Foundation caps you at six categories; Gates wants it broken down by objective *and* year).
- **Multilateral (UNDP)**: must be submitted in **local currency AND USD simultaneously**.
- **USAID**: the strictest — itemized Excel, no "pooled costs" allowed, plus a **signed federal SF-424 form** with an OMB control number.

### D. Impact/Theory-of-Change Rigor
- **CSR**: "basic outcomes" — quantitative/qualitative targets, benchmarked, that's it.
- **Government**: basic M&E indicators, sustainability *question* (not a full plan).
- **Foundations**: the deepest — Rockefeller's "OneRF Indicators," Gates' full "Results Framework" (Strategy→Goal→Objectives→Results→Measurement), MacArthur explicitly states its *own* Theory of Change as the frame you must fit into.
- **Multilateral**: standardized numeric indicators tied to global frameworks (GEF Focal Areas, Global Fund Performance Framework with quarterly targets).
- **INGO**: outcomes measured specifically against *child well-being*, not generic development metrics.

### E. Risk & Safeguarding — Present or Totally Absent
This is a binary that splits the categories cleanly:
- **Absent entirely**: CSR, Government Grants, Domestic Intermediaries, HNI.
- **Present and heavy**: USAID (PSEA/IASC code of conduct, security plans), FCDO (6-category risk taxonomy: delivery/operational/context/safeguarding/fiduciary/reputational), DFAT (PSEAH), World Vision (mandatory Child Protection Standards as a hard gate).

Notice the pattern: **it's almost always the international/foreign-funded categories** that carry safeguarding requirements — a legacy of scandals in the international aid sector (Oxfam Haiti, etc.) that never touched domestic CSR or government funding the same way.

### F. Application Mechanism — How You Actually "Apply"
This might be the most practically important difference for building a system:
- **One-shot competitive RFP**: CSR (SBI, BPCL), USAID — you write it once, submit, wait.
- **Portal-based enrollment**: Government Grants — register on NGO Darpan first, *then* apply through a scheme portal.
- **Relationship-mediated, multi-stage**: Foundations (Ford/MacArthur: Concept Note → invited full Proposal), Diaspora Foundations (AIF: Concept Note → field visit → detailed proposal).
- **Continuous partnership lifecycle, no single "application"**: INGOs (World Vision's 4-stage: Scoping → Managing → Reviewing → Sustaining) — the "ask" never exists as a standalone document.
- **Digital onboarding workflow, not a document**: Crowdfunding platforms (interest survey → workbook → due diligence → live campaign page).
- **Investment pitch, not a proposal**: Impact Investors (business plan/financial model evaluated like an early-stage deal).
- **No formal application at all**: HNI/Family Office (Dasra's GivingPi is literally invite-only), Domestic Intermediaries (CInI is *embedded inside* Tata Trusts' own structure, not accepting external applications).

## 4. The Big Insight for Your System

The templates don't differ because of style preference — they differ because **each funder type is legally, financially, or relationally accountable to a different party**, and the template is just the paperwork trail that accountability leaves behind:

- Government & CSR templates are shaped by **Indian statutory law** (Companies Act, FCRA, tax code) → hence Aadhaar numbers, CSR-1, 80G/12A everywhere.
- Foundation & Multilateral/Bilateral templates are shaped by **their own board/donor fiduciary duty + foreign tax law** (IRS lobbying rules, 501(c)(3) equivalency, ADS regulations) → hence deep MEL, Theory of Change, federal forms.
- INGO, Diaspora, HNI, and Domestic Intermediary templates are shaped by **trust and ongoing relationship**, not a filing requirement → hence they often don't have a fixed template *at all*.
- Impact Investors are shaped by **return-on-capital logic** → hence a business plan replaces a proposal entirely.

**Practical implication:** if you're designing a system to auto-generate proposals, the single most important branching decision isn't "what sector is this project in" — it's **"which accountability logic does this funder operate under?"** That one flag determines whether you need a Schedule VII dropdown, an Aadhaar field, a Theory-of-Change module, a federal SF-424 generator, a safeguarding questionnaire, or — for several categories — no document at all, just a relationship/CRM workflow.
